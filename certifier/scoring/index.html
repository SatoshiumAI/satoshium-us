Certifier · Evaluation Philosophy

Evaluation Philosophy

Satoshium Certifier evaluates evidence rather than assigning arbitrary numerical scores. Certification determinations are based on published standards, documented evidence, repeatable methodology, and transparent reviewer reasoning.

This page explains how Certifier interprets evidence, confidence, determinations, and reviewer judgment when reaching certification outcomes.

Evidence Weight

Not all evidence contributes equally. Primary evidence, supporting evidence, integrity references, and contextual observations may carry different importance during review.

Evaluation Principle

Confidence

Certification reflects the level of confidence supported by available evidence rather than a numerical score. Confidence increases through corroboration, transparency, and repeatable review.

Confidence Model

Determinations

Evaluation concludes with a defined certification determination such as Pass, Conditional Pass, Fail, Expired, or Revoked rather than percentages, stars, or letter grades.

Outcome

Reviewer Judgment

Certifier combines structured standards with documented reviewer judgment. Assumptions, limitations, findings, and reasoning should remain preserved in the certification record.

Transparency

Evaluation Role

Evaluation is the reasoning layer of Certifier. Standards define expectations. Evidence provides facts. Methodology defines process. Evaluation connects those elements into a documented certification determination.

Certifier does not seek to create arbitrary scores. It seeks to preserve reviewable reasoning, visible evidence, and clear determinations that can be understood over time.
Trust is strengthened when reasoning is preserved alongside conclusions.
