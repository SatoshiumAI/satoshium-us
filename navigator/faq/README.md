# Navigator FAQ

## Purpose

This FAQ provides answers to common questions about Satoshium Navigator, its role within the Satoshium Suite, and how it interacts with Atlas and other systems.

Navigator is the query, exploration, and viewing layer of the Satoshium ecosystem. It allows users to access, analyze, compare, and interpret information contained within Atlas and related systems.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Navigator?

Navigator is a query and exploration system built to access information contained within Atlas.

It provides a structured way to search, compare, filter, and review jurisdictional intelligence, signals, profiles, and related records.

---

### What is the relationship between Navigator and Atlas?

Atlas serves as the data layer.

Navigator serves as the viewing and query layer.

Atlas stores information. Navigator helps users interact with that information.

---

### Does Navigator create data?

No.

Navigator is primarily a retrieval, exploration, and analysis system.

Records originate from Atlas and other supporting systems.

---

### Is Navigator a search engine?

Not in the traditional sense.

Navigator is designed to provide structured access to jurisdictional intelligence rather than indexing the general internet.

---

### What can Navigator query?

Navigator may query:

* Jurisdictions
* Profiles
* Signals
* Evidence
* Trust dimensions
* Comparative records
* Atlas intelligence packages
* Registry records
* Related Suite records

Subject to system capabilities and permissions.

---

### What are query types?

Query types define how information is requested.

Examples include:

* Jurisdiction lookup
* Jurisdiction comparison
* Signal review
* Risk assessment
* Trend analysis
* Record retrieval
* Filtered searches

---

### Can Navigator compare jurisdictions?

Yes.

One of Navigator's primary purposes is to allow structured comparison between jurisdictions using Atlas data and associated intelligence records.

---

### Does Navigator assign rankings?

Not necessarily.

Navigator may display rankings generated by underlying systems, but Navigator itself is not intended to function as a ranking authority.

Its primary role is information retrieval and presentation.

---

### What are signals?

Signals are observable indicators that may assist in understanding jurisdictions, institutions, policies, media environments, or related conditions.

Signals may originate from Atlas or associated systems.

---

### What are filters?

Filters allow users to narrow query results based on selected criteria.

Examples may include:

* Geography
* Region
* Category
* Trust dimensions
* Signal classifications
* Time periods
* Record types

---

### Can Navigator provide recommendations?

Navigator may present information that supports decision-making.

However, Navigator does not guarantee outcomes and should not be interpreted as legal, financial, investment, medical, or professional advice.

---

### How does Navigator relate to Certifier?

Certifier evaluates and verifies information.

Navigator retrieves and displays information.

Navigator may present records that were previously evaluated through Certifier processes.

---

### How does Navigator relate to Registry?

Registry stores records.

Navigator helps users discover and access those records.

---

### How does Navigator relate to Chronicle?

Chronicle preserves historical records.

Navigator may retrieve and display Chronicle entries when relevant to a query.

---

### How does Navigator relate to Anchor?

Anchor establishes reference points and identity structures.

Navigator may use Anchor records as part of discovery and contextualization processes.

---

### How does Navigator relate to Beacon?

Beacon assists with discovery.

Navigator assists with retrieval, exploration, and analysis.

The systems are complementary.

---

### How does Navigator relate to Attestor?

Attestor produces trust-oriented assertions and evidentiary statements.

Navigator may display Attestor records as part of a query result.

---

### Can Navigator modify Atlas records?

No.

Navigator is intended to access information, not alter it.

Changes to records occur through the appropriate systems and workflows.

---

### Are Navigator results guaranteed to be accurate?

No system can guarantee complete accuracy.

Users should review source materials, supporting evidence, and associated records when evaluating information.

---

### Is Navigator open source?

Open-source status is determined by current Satoshium licensing and repository policies.

Refer to the applicable license and repository documentation for current details.

---

### Does Navigator collect personal information?

Navigator's purpose is information retrieval and exploration.

Data handling practices are governed by applicable policies, configurations, and legal requirements.

---

### Is Navigator operational?

Refer to the Status section for current development and operational status.

Capabilities may evolve over time as the system matures.
