Navigator · Frequently Asked Questions

FAQ

This page answers common questions about Satoshium Navigator, its purpose, capabilities, scope, methodologies, and relationship to Atlas and the broader Satoshium ecosystem.

Navigator exists to help users discover, retrieve, compare, analyze, and understand information through structured exploration frameworks.

General Questions

What is Satoshium Navigator?

Satoshium Navigator is the query and exploration framework of the Satoshium ecosystem. It helps users discover, retrieve, compare, filter, analyze, and understand information originating from Atlas and related systems.

What does Navigator do?

Navigator provides structured methods for exploring jurisdictions, records, signals, trust dimensions, historical information, and related resources.

Who is Navigator designed for?

Navigator is intended for researchers, analysts, curious individuals, and anyone seeking structured exploration of available information.

Is Navigator a search engine?

Navigator includes search and retrieval capabilities, but it is broader than a traditional search engine. It is designed to support exploration, comparison, analysis, and understanding.

Atlas & Information Questions

What is the relationship between Navigator and Atlas?

Atlas serves as the jurisdiction intelligence repository of the Satoshium ecosystem. Navigator provides the mechanisms used to explore, retrieve, compare, filter, and analyze Atlas information.

Does Navigator replace Atlas?

No. Atlas remains the authoritative source repository. Navigator provides structured access and exploration capabilities for information contained within Atlas and related systems.

What information can Navigator explore?

Navigator may provide access to jurisdiction profiles, evidence, signals, trust dimensions, metadata, historical information, supporting records, and related references when available.

Can Navigator compare jurisdictions?

Yes. Comparison frameworks are one of Navigator's primary capabilities. Users may compare jurisdictions, signals, trust dimensions, records, and related information.

Query Questions

What is a query?

A query is a structured request for information. Queries serve as the primary mechanism through which users retrieve and explore information within Navigator.

What types of queries are supported?

Navigator may support jurisdiction queries, comparison queries, signal queries, historical queries, research queries, and filtered queries.

Can queries be filtered?

Yes. Filters help refine information by jurisdiction, category, signal type, trust dimension, time period, record type, and other criteria.

Do queries generate outputs?

Yes. Queries may generate search results, jurisdiction reviews, comparison reports, signal summaries, analytical outputs, and related resources.

Analysis Questions

What are signals?

Signals are observable indicators associated with jurisdictions, institutions, events, policies, trends, and related subjects. Signals help identify patterns and support analysis.

What are trust dimensions?

Trust dimensions are structured categories used to evaluate characteristics such as stability, transparency, predictability, accessibility, and consistency.

What are comparisons?

Comparisons are structured analytical reviews that evaluate similarities, differences, relationships, and context across multiple records, jurisdictions, or subjects.

Does Navigator provide recommendations?

Navigator is designed primarily to support exploration, research, comparison, and understanding. Users remain responsible for evaluating information and making their own decisions.

Scope Questions

Does Navigator provide legal advice?

No. Navigator is an informational exploration framework and should not be interpreted as legal advice.

Does Navigator provide financial or investment advice?

No. Navigator provides information and analytical tools, but users remain responsible for their own financial and investment decisions.

Does Navigator determine truth?

No. Navigator retrieves, organizes, and presents information. Users should evaluate evidence, methodology, context, and supporting records independently.

Ecosystem Questions

How does Navigator interact with the Satoshium ecosystem?

Navigator serves as the primary exploration layer of the ecosystem. It helps users access, retrieve, compare, and analyze information originating from Atlas and related systems.

Can Navigator use information from Certifier?

Yes. When available, Navigator may reference certifications, verification-oriented outputs, methodologies, and supporting records originating from Certifier.

Can Navigator use information from Registry?

Yes. Registry records may be used to support discovery, organization, categorization, and traceability of information.

Can Navigator use information from Chronicle?

Yes. Chronicle records may provide historical context, timelines, development history, and preserved documentation associated with a subject or jurisdiction.

Can Navigator use information from Attestor?

Yes. Attestations and related evidentiary records may be referenced when available and relevant to a query or output.

Can Navigator operate without Atlas?

Atlas serves as a primary information source for Navigator. While Navigator may reference additional systems, Atlas remains a foundational component of the overall framework.

Traceability over uncertainty.
