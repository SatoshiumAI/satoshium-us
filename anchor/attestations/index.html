Anchor · Attestations

Attestations and Attestation Artifact Integrity

Anchor does not create or govern attestations as a general institutional object. Within the current Suite, Attestor remains authoritative for Trust Statements and other Attestor-owned trust artifacts.

Anchor's role begins only when the integrity of an attestation, Trust Statement, endorsement, challenge, or related artifact should be preserved through an Integrity Reference.

Attestation Boundary

An attestation may express support, challenge, observation, witness, qualification, relationship, or trust-related judgment.

Anchor does not determine the meaning or validity of that statement. Anchor preserves the integrity relationship for the defined artifact representation.

Attestation Meaning ≠ Attestation Artifact Integrity

Attestation Artifact

An Attestation Artifact is a statement, endorsement, challenge, declaration, Trust Statement, or other governed representation that may become an Anchor integrity-preservation candidate.

Foundation

Attestation Source

Anchor should preserve the Source Institution, Source-System Identifier, signer, issuing role, or other attribution necessary to understand who or what governs the attestation artifact.

Foundation

Canonical Representation

Before anchoring an attestation artifact, Anchor should define the exact representation and boundary to which the Integrity Reference applies.

Foundation

Attestation Integrity

Anchor may preserve an Integrity Reference that allows later reviewers to determine whether the reviewed attestation representation remains consistent with the representation that was originally anchored.

Foundation

Relationship to Attestor

Attestor remains authoritative for:

Trust Statements · Attestor-owned trust judgments · Attestor lifecycle · Attestor publication · Attestor Corrections · Attestor Versions

Anchor may preserve the integrity of those artifacts. It does not become the trust authority.

Attestor → Trust Statement
Anchor → Integrity Reference

Attestation vs. Claim

A claim is an assertion. An attestation may support, challenge, qualify, witness, or otherwise respond to a claim. Anchor may preserve integrity for either artifact without adjudicating their relationship.

Attestation vs. Evidence

An attestation may itself be evidence, may reference evidence, or may merely express a judgment. Anchor preserves artifact integrity rather than deciding evidentiary weight.

Attestation vs. Certification

Certifier may evaluate an artifact or body of evidence under its own standards and methodology. Anchor may preserve integrity for the resulting attestation-related artifact without becoming Certifier.

Attestation vs. Trust

A Trust Statement may contain or function as an attestation, but the trust judgment remains Attestor-owned. Anchor verifies integrity, not trustworthiness.

What Anchor Preserves

Source Institution · Source-System Identifier · Attestation Artifact Type · Canonical Representation · Representation Boundary · Integrity Value · Temporal Context · Signature / Verification Material · Anchor Metadata

Anchor should preserve only the information needed to make the integrity relationship independently reviewable.

Attribution and Signatures

Attestation artifacts often depend on attribution. Anchor may preserve signer references, key references, issuing roles, Source Institution, or producing-system metadata where needed for later verification.

Signature ≠ Institutional Authority
Attribution ≠ Trust

Attestation Artifact Lifecycle

An attestation may later be withdrawn, superseded, corrected, revoked, challenged, replaced, or reissued by its Source Institution.

Anchor should preserve the earlier integrity relationship rather than silently rewriting it.

Earlier Attestation Representation → Earlier Integrity Reference
Later Attestation Representation → Later Anchor Version or New Integrity Reference where required

Attestation Correction vs. Anchor Correction

If Attestor or another Source Institution changes the attestation itself, that is a Source Artifact change.

If Anchor recorded the wrong attestation artifact, digest, representation boundary, timestamp, signer metadata, or verification material, that may require an Anchor Correction.

Source Attestation Correction ≠ Anchor Correction

Relationship to the Suite

Claims may provide assertions.
Certifier may evaluate artifacts or evidence.
Registry may catalog authoritative records.
Chronicle may preserve qualifying historical Occurrences.
Attestor may issue Trust Statements.
Anchor may preserve Integrity References for the relevant artifacts.

Each institution remains authoritative for its own canonical object.

Reference does not transfer authority.

Attestations Principle

Anchor should preserve the integrity of an attestation artifact when that preservation has durable institutional value, while leaving attestation meaning, trust judgment, dispute resolution, and lifecycle authority to the institution that owns the attestation.

Preserve the attestation artifact's integrity. Do not become the attestation authority.
