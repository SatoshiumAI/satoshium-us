# Attestations

## Overview

Attestations are statements made by an identity regarding a fact, claim, relationship, action, or condition.

Within the Satoshium ecosystem, an attestation is not proof, certification, or registration. It is an assertion made by a known or identifiable entity.

An attestation may later be verified, certified, recorded, disputed, revoked, or preserved, but the attestation itself is simply the act of declaring something to be true.

Anchor exists to establish identity. Attestations exist to establish claims made by that identity.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of attestations is to create a structured mechanism through which individuals, organizations, institutions, systems, and agents may make assertions regarding themselves or the world around them.

Examples include:

* Identity claims
* Ownership claims
* Authorship claims
* Witness statements
* Authority declarations
* Organizational representations
* Status declarations
* Historical assertions

Attestations form the foundation upon which verification and trust may later be established.

---

## What an Attestation Is

An attestation is:

* A statement made by an identity
* An assertion of a claimed fact
* A declaration that may be evaluated by others
* A record of what was claimed and by whom
* A potential input to certification, registration, or historical preservation

An attestation may be true or false.

The existence of an attestation does not imply the validity of the claim.

---

## What an Attestation Is Not

An attestation is not:

* Proof
* Verification
* Certification
* Registration
* Consensus
* Authority
* Trust

An attestation only establishes that a claim was made.

Whether that claim is accepted, rejected, verified, or disputed is determined elsewhere.

---

## Foundational Principles

### Identity Precedes Attestation

Every attestation originates from an identity.

Without attribution, an attestation cannot be evaluated, challenged, or trusted.

### Claims Exist Independently of Verification

A claim may exist before evidence is available.

Attestations preserve the claim regardless of its current verification status.

### Attestations May Be Evaluated

An attestation may later be:

* Verified
* Certified
* Registered
* Challenged
* Revoked
* Corrected

The original attestation remains part of the historical record.

### Attribution Matters

The value of an attestation is inseparable from the identity making it.

Knowing who made a claim is often as important as the claim itself.

### Records Should Preserve Assertions

Systems should preserve what was claimed, when it was claimed, and who made the claim.

History benefits from preserving assertions even when their validity changes over time.

## Relationship to Other Satoshium Systems

Attestations operate within a larger ecosystem.

* Anchor establishes identity.
* Attestations establish claims.
* Certifier evaluates authenticity.
* Registry records accepted records.
* Chronicle preserves historical context.
* Beacon enables discovery.
* Attestor provides trust signals.

Together these systems create a framework for attribution, verification, preservation, and trust.
