# Standards

## Overview

Standards provide shared definitions, structures, formats, and expectations that enable systems and participants to interact consistently.

Within the Satoshium Anchor framework, standards help establish common approaches for representing identities, claims, attestations, authority relationships, governance structures, and trust-related information.

Standards create consistency.

Consistency enables interoperability.

Interoperability enables broader participation and understanding.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of standards is to create a common foundation upon which identity-related systems may be built.

Standards help answer questions such as:

* How should identities be represented?
* How should claims be structured?
* How should attestations be recorded?
* How should authority relationships be expressed?
* How should attribution be preserved?
* How should governance information be represented?

Without standards, systems often become fragmented and difficult to evaluate.

---

## What Standards Are

Standards are:

* Shared definitions
* Shared structures
* Shared formats
* Shared expectations
* Shared conventions

Standards improve consistency while allowing implementation flexibility.

They provide guidance without necessarily dictating a single implementation.

---

## What Standards Are Not

Standards are not:

* Laws
* Regulations
* Governance decisions
* Certifications
* Identity services
* Trust determinations

A standard may describe how information should be represented without determining whether that information is accurate, trustworthy, or authoritative.

---

## Foundational Principles

### Consistency Matters

Common structures improve clarity and reduce ambiguity.

Standards help participants interpret information consistently.

### Interoperability Matters

Systems become more valuable when they can exchange information effectively.

Standards encourage compatibility across participants and platforms.

### Standards Should Be Transparent

Standards should be openly documented and understandable.

Participants should be able to evaluate how information is represented and exchanged.

### Standards Should Evolve

Identity systems, governance models, and technologies change over time.

Standards should be capable of adapting while preserving continuity.

### Standards Should Remain Technology-Neutral

Where practical, standards should focus on concepts and structures rather than dependence upon specific technologies.

This encourages flexibility and longevity.
