# Claims

## Overview

A claim is an assertion that something is true.

Claims exist throughout human society. Individuals, organizations, institutions, systems, and agents make claims regarding identity, ownership, authorship, authority, events, actions, and facts.

Within the Satoshium ecosystem, claims represent the foundational layer from which attestations, certifications, registrations, and historical records may emerge.

A claim does not require acceptance, verification, or consensus to exist.

It only requires that an assertion has been made.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of claims is to provide a structured framework for understanding assertions before they are evaluated.

Claims allow systems to distinguish between:

* What is asserted
* Who asserted it
* Whether it has been evaluated
* Whether it has been accepted
* Whether it has been recorded

By separating claims from verification, systems preserve the ability to evaluate assertions transparently and independently.

---

## What a Claim Is

A claim is:

* An assertion
* A declaration
* A statement regarding a fact, condition, event, relationship, or authority
* Information presented as true by an identity
* The starting point of potential verification

Examples include:

* I am this person.
* I own this asset.
* I authored this document.
* I witnessed this event.
* I represent this organization.
* This statement is accurate.

Claims may concern the past, present, or future.

---

## What a Claim Is Not

A claim is not:

* Evidence
* Verification
* Certification
* Registration
* Consensus
* Authority
* Truth

A claim may ultimately be true, false, incomplete, misleading, or unverifiable.

The existence of a claim does not establish its validity.

---

## Foundational Principles

### Claims Precede Verification

Before a statement can be verified, it must first be asserted.

Every verification process begins with a claim.

### Claims May Be Evaluated

Claims may later be:

* Supported
* Challenged
* Verified
* Certified
* Recorded
* Corrected
* Rejected

The claim itself remains distinct from the outcome of that evaluation.

### Claims Require Attribution

Anonymous assertions may exist, but accountability requires attribution.

Understanding who made a claim is often essential to evaluating it.

### Claims and Truth Are Separate Concepts

A claim is an assertion.

Truth is the degree to which reality corresponds with that assertion.

Systems should distinguish between the existence of a claim and the determination of its accuracy.

### Claims Form Historical Records

Claims influence decisions, institutions, communities, and civilizations.

Even incorrect claims may become historically significant.

Preserving claims helps preserve context.

---

## Claim Lifecycle

A claim may progress through multiple stages.

```text
Claim
  ↓
Attestation
  ↓
Verification
  ↓
Certification
  ↓
Registration
  ↓
Historical Preservation
```

Not all claims advance through every stage.

Some claims remain unresolved.

Others may be disputed indefinitely.

The framework exists to preserve transparency throughout the process.

## Relationship to Other Satoshium Systems

Claims serve as the starting point for several Satoshium systems.

* Anchor establishes identity.
* Claims establish assertions.
* Attestations connect claims to identities.
* Certifier evaluates authenticity and evidence.
* Registry records accepted records.
* Chronicle preserves historical context.
* Beacon enables discovery.
* Attestor contributes trust signals.

Together these systems create a structured path from assertion to preservation.
