Anchor · Claims

Claims and Claim Artifact Integrity

Anchor does not own claims as a general institutional object and does not determine whether a claim is true, false, supported, certified, or trusted.

Within Anchor, a claim matters only when a claim statement, declaration, assertion, or related artifact becomes the subject of an Integrity Reference.

Claim Boundary

A claim may be created, governed, evaluated, certified, registered, preserved historically, or attested by another institution or process.

Anchor's role begins only when the integrity of a defined claim artifact or representation should be preserved.

Claim Meaning ≠ Claim Artifact Integrity

Claim Artifact

A Claim Artifact is a statement, declaration, assertion, record, or governed representation containing a claim that may become an Anchor integrity-preservation candidate.

Foundation

Claim Source

Anchor should preserve the Source Institution, source identifier, authoring context, or other attribution necessary to understand who or what governs the claim artifact.

Foundation

Claim Representation

Anchor should define the exact Canonical Representation and Representation Boundary of the claim artifact before generating integrity material.

Foundation

Claim Integrity

Anchor may preserve an Integrity Reference that allows a later reviewer to determine whether the reviewed claim representation remains consistent with the representation that was originally anchored.

Foundation

What Anchor Preserves

Source Institution · Source-System Identifier · Claim Artifact Type · Canonical Representation · Representation Boundary · Integrity Value · Temporal Context · Signature / Verification Material · Anchor Metadata

Anchor preserves integrity context for the artifact. It does not preserve a separate claim ontology unless later production demonstrates a genuine institutional need.

Claim vs. Evidence

A claim is an assertion. Evidence may support or challenge that assertion. Anchor may preserve integrity for either artifact without deciding the evidentiary question.

Claim vs. Certification

Certifier may evaluate a claim or record under its own standards and methodology. Anchor may preserve integrity context for the resulting artifact without becoming the certification authority.

Claim vs. History

Chronicle may preserve a qualifying historical Occurrence involving a claim through a Chronicle Entry. Anchor may preserve integrity for the claim artifact or Chronicle representation without becoming the historical authority.

Claim vs. Trust

Attestor may later issue a Trust Statement concerning a claim, source, artifact, or institutional conclusion. Anchor may preserve its integrity without determining trustworthiness.

Claim Artifact Lifecycle

A claim artifact may later be revised, withdrawn, superseded, corrected, rejected, certified, or preserved historically by its Source Institution.

Anchor should preserve the integrity lineage of the representation it actually anchored rather than silently rewriting the earlier reference.

Earlier Claim Representation → Earlier Integrity Reference
Later Claim Representation → Later Anchor Version or New Integrity Reference where required

Claim Correction vs. Anchor Correction

If the Source Institution changes or corrects the claim itself, that is a Source Artifact change.

If Anchor recorded the wrong claim artifact, digest, representation boundary, timestamp, or other Anchor-owned metadata, that may require an Anchor Correction.

Source Claim Correction ≠ Anchor Correction

Attribution

Claim integrity often depends on preserving enough attribution to understand which person, institution, system, signer, or process produced the claim artifact.

Anchor should preserve only the attribution necessary for integrity review and accountability.

Preserve necessary attribution. Avoid unnecessary identity accumulation.

What Anchor Does Not Do

create a general claims registry
determine whether claims are true
score claim credibility
adjudicate disputes
certify claims
determine claim acceptance
create reputation from claims
issue trust conclusions
own claim semantics governed by other systems

Relationship to the Suite

Certifier may evaluate claims or supporting artifacts.
Registry may catalog authoritative records.
Chronicle may preserve qualifying historical Occurrences involving claims.
Attestor may issue Trust Statements.
Anchor may preserve Integrity References for the relevant artifacts.

Each institution remains authoritative for its own canonical object.

Reference does not transfer authority.

Claims Principle

Anchor should preserve the integrity of a claim artifact when that integrity has durable value, while leaving claim meaning, evaluation, certification, historical interpretation, and trust conclusions to the institutions responsible for those functions.

Preserve the claim artifact's integrity. Do not become the claim authority.
