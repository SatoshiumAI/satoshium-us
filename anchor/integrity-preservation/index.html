Anchor · Integrity Preservation

Satoshium Anchor Integrity Preservation

Integrity Preservation defines Anchor's institutional purpose: preserving evidence that important artifacts existed in a specific form at a specific time.

Anchor is not defined by blockchain or Bitcoin. Those may become implementation tools. The institution is preserving integrity.

Purpose

Integrity Preservation explains why Anchor exists as a cryptographic and temporal integrity layer for artifacts created across the Satoshium Suite.

Integrity

Integrity means an artifact can be compared against a preserved reference to determine whether its contents remain consistent with an earlier state.

Immutability

Anchor does not need to make every artifact immutable. It preserves evidence that makes later alteration detectable, reviewable, and accountable.

Tamper Evidence

Tamper evidence allows reviewers to identify whether an artifact appears to have changed after an integrity reference was created.

The Institutional Role

Anchor preserves integrity references. It does not create the artifact, certify its meaning, catalog its public status, preserve its historical narrative, or attest to its truth.

The institution is not blockchain. The institution is integrity preservation.

Cryptographic Preservation

Cryptographic preservation may include hashes, signatures, verification metadata, and other references that allow artifacts to be checked later.

Timestamps

Timestamps help establish when an integrity reference was created or preserved, allowing artifacts to be understood within time.

Signatures

Signatures may help identify who or what system created, approved, or preserved an integrity reference.

Verification

Verification compares an artifact against its preserved integrity reference to determine whether the artifact remains consistent with that reference.

What Integrity Preservation Supports

Integrity preservation supports other Suite systems by giving them durable references that can be used to review whether important artifacts have changed.

Certification Packages · SCPRs · SCRs · SCRDs · Registry Entries · Historical Events · Attestations

What Integrity Preservation Does Not Do

Integrity preservation does not determine whether a claim is true, whether a certification should be active, whether a Registry Entry should remain registered, or whether a historical interpretation is correct.

Anchor helps answer whether an artifact changed. It does not decide what the artifact means.

Implementation Neutrality

Anchor should remain institutionally defined by integrity, not by any single technical implementation. Hashing, timestamping, signatures, public logs, distributed systems, or future cryptographic tools may all support Anchor.

Blockchain and Bitcoin may be useful implementation tools. They are not the definition of Anchor.

Tools may change. Integrity preservation remains the purpose.

Integrity Philosophy

Anchor preserves confidence in artifacts by making change detectable. Its value comes from clarity, repeatability, verifiability, and long-term accountability.

The goal is not to freeze every record forever. The goal is to preserve the ability to know whether a referenced artifact still matches the preserved integrity state.
