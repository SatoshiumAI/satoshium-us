# Identities

## Overview

Identity is the foundation upon which attribution, accountability, authority, trust, and governance are built.

Before a claim can be evaluated, a record preserved, or an action attributed, there must first be an identifiable entity associated with that activity.

Within the Satoshium Anchor framework, identities represent the entities capable of participating in claims, attestations, records, governance, and trust relationships.

Anchor exists to explore the structures through which identities may be established, represented, and understood.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of identities is to provide a framework for attribution.

Identity helps answer questions such as:

* Who created this?
* Who authored this?
* Who approved this?
* Who witnessed this?
* Who owns this?
* Who is responsible for this?
* Who is acting on behalf of whom?

Without identity, accountability becomes difficult to establish and trust becomes difficult to evaluate.

---

## What an Identity Is

An identity is:

* An identifiable entity
* A source of claims and attestations
* A participant in records and governance systems
* A holder of authority, responsibility, or reputation
* A subject of attribution

An identity may be associated with actions, records, assertions, decisions, or relationships.

---

## What an Identity Is Not

An identity is not:

* Proof
* Trust
* Reputation
* Authority
* Ownership
* Verification

Identity establishes who an entity is.

Other systems may determine what that identity is permitted to do, what it owns, or whether it should be trusted.

---

## Foundational Principles

### Identity Precedes Attribution

Before an action can be attributed, an identity must exist.

Attribution depends upon the ability to associate actions with identifiable entities.

### Identity Precedes Accountability

Accountability requires responsibility.

Responsibility requires attribution.

Attribution requires identity.

### Identity Is Distinct from Reputation

An identity may possess a reputation, but the two concepts are separate.

Identity answers:

> Who is this?

Reputation answers:

> What is known about them?

### Identity Is Distinct from Authority

An identity may possess authority.

Authority may be granted, delegated, expanded, limited, or revoked.

The identity itself remains distinct from the authority it holds.

### Identity May Persist

Records, claims, attestations, and historical events may remain associated with an identity over time.

Persistence enables continuity and accountability.

## Identity Categories

Anchor currently explores several categories of identity.

### Individuals

Human participants capable of creating claims, records, attestations, and decisions.

### Organizations

Groups operating under a shared structure, mission, or governance framework.

### Institutions

Established entities that maintain authority, responsibilities, or long-term functions.

### Communities

Collections of participants organized around shared interests, goals, or principles.

### Systems

Technical infrastructures capable of producing actions, records, or outputs.

### Tools

Software or technical components operating within broader systems.

### Agents

Autonomous or semi-autonomous entities capable of performing actions on behalf of themselves or others.

### Artificial Intelligence

AI systems that may generate outputs, make decisions, perform actions, or participate in future governance frameworks.

The precise definitions and relationships between these categories remain under development.

## Identity Relationships

Identities may interact in various ways.

Examples include:

* Individual to individual
* Individual to organization
* Organization to organization
* Agent to organization
* Agent to individual
* System to system
* AI to organization

Anchor seeks to provide a framework for understanding and preserving these relationships.

## Identity Lifecycle

An identity may participate in multiple stages of the broader Satoshium ecosystem.

```text
Identity
   ↓
Claim
   ↓
Attestation
   ↓
Certification
   ↓
Registration
   ↓
Historical Preservation
```

Identity serves as the foundation for each subsequent stage.

## Relationship to Other Satoshium Systems

Identity serves as a foundational component across the Satoshium ecosystem.

* Anchor establishes identity and attribution.
* Claims establish assertions.
* Attestations connect assertions to identities.
* Certifier evaluates authenticity.
* Registry preserves records.
* Chronicle preserves historical context.
* Beacon enables discovery.
* Attestor contributes trust-related signals.

Together these systems support transparency, accountability, and preservation.
