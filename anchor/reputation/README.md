# Reputation

## Overview

Reputation is the accumulation of perceptions, experiences, observations, and historical associations connected to an identity.

Within the Satoshium Anchor framework, reputation is viewed as a consequence of actions rather than a property of identity itself.

An identity may exist without reputation.

A reputation cannot exist without identity.

Anchor explores how reputation emerges from claims, actions, records, attestations, certifications, and historical events over time.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of reputation is to provide context.

Reputation helps individuals, organizations, institutions, systems, and communities evaluate:

* Past behavior
* Historical actions
* Consistency
* Reliability
* Accountability
* Participation
* Experience

Reputation may influence trust, but it is not equivalent to trust.

---

## What Reputation Is

Reputation is:

* A collection of perceptions
* A reflection of historical behavior
* A consequence of actions and decisions
* A contextual signal
* An evolving assessment formed over time

Reputation emerges through observation, interaction, and historical experience.

---

## What Reputation Is Not

Reputation is not:

* Identity
* Authority
* Ownership
* Verification
* Certification
* Truth
* Trust

A strong reputation does not guarantee correctness.

A poor reputation does not guarantee error.

Reputation is one source of information among many.

---

## Foundational Principles

### Identity Precedes Reputation

Reputation requires an identifiable subject.

Without identity, reputation cannot accumulate.

### Reputation Is Earned

Reputation develops through actions, decisions, claims, records, and participation over time.

It cannot be declared into existence.

### Reputation Changes

Reputation is dynamic.

Positive actions may strengthen reputation.

Negative actions may weaken reputation.

Reputation evolves as new information becomes available.

### Reputation Is Contextual

An entity may possess different reputations across different communities, domains, or areas of expertise.

Reputation is not necessarily universal.

### Reputation Is Distinct from Truth

Reputation reflects perception and experience.

Truth reflects reality.

The two concepts may align, but they are not the same.

### Reputation Influences Trust

While reputation does not create trust, it often contributes to trust-related decisions.

Trust frequently incorporates reputation as one of many factors.

## Sources of Reputation

Reputation may emerge from numerous sources.

Examples include:

* Historical actions
* Published records
* Attestations
* Certifications
* Contributions
* Governance participation
* Community involvement
* Professional experience
* Institutional relationships

Anchor seeks to preserve the context through which reputation may be understood.

---

## Reputation and Accountability

Reputation becomes meaningful when actions remain attributable.

Attribution allows observers to connect actions to identities.

Without attribution, accountability weakens.

Without accountability, reputation becomes difficult to evaluate.

For this reason, identity remains foundational to reputation systems.

---

## Reputation and Trust

Reputation and trust are closely related but distinct.

Reputation answers:

> What is known about this identity?

Trust answers:

> How much confidence should be placed in this identity?

Reputation may inform trust.

It does not determine it.
