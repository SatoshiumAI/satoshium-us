# Governance

## Overview

Governance concerns the rules, responsibilities, authorities, and decision-making structures that exist within a system.

Within the Satoshium Anchor framework, governance focuses on the relationship between identity and authority.

Questions of governance often begin with questions of identity:

* Who has authority?
* Who granted that authority?
* Who is accountable?
* Who may act on behalf of another?
* Who may create, modify, approve, revoke, or challenge a record?

Anchor exists to explore these questions through the lens of attribution and accountability.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of governance is to establish clear frameworks for responsibility, representation, and authority.

Governance helps define:

* Who may act
* Under what authority
* Within what scope
* Under what limitations
* Subject to what accountability

Without governance, responsibility becomes difficult to determine and trust becomes difficult to evaluate.

---

## Identity and Governance

Governance begins with identity.

Before authority can be assigned, exercised, delegated, or revoked, the participating entities must first be identifiable.

Anchor focuses on the attribution layer that makes governance possible.

Identity precedes authority.

Authority precedes action.

Accountability follows action.

---

## What Governance Is

Governance is:

* A framework for decision-making
* A framework for accountability
* A framework for responsibility
* A framework for authority
* A framework for representation

Governance establishes how actions are authorized and how those actions may be evaluated.

---

## What Governance Is Not

Governance is not:

* Ownership
* Reputation
* Trust
* Verification
* Certification
* Consensus

While governance may interact with these concepts, each remains distinct.

Governance determines how authority is structured, not whether a particular decision is correct.

---

## Foundational Principles

### Identity Precedes Authority

Authority cannot exist independently of identity.

Every exercise of authority should be attributable to a responsible entity.

### Authority Should Be Explicit

Systems should clearly define:

* Who possesses authority
* The scope of that authority
* The duration of that authority
* The source of that authority

Ambiguity weakens accountability.

### Authority May Be Delegated

Individuals and organizations may grant authority to others.

Delegation creates new responsibilities while preserving accountability structures.

Systems should preserve clear records of delegated authority whenever possible.

### Authority May Be Revoked

Governance systems should provide mechanisms for modifying, limiting, suspending, or revoking authority.

Authority is not necessarily permanent.

### Accountability Matters

Actions should remain attributable to the entities responsible for them.

Governance systems become stronger when responsibility can be clearly traced.

### Representation Requires Clarity

Individuals, organizations, institutions, systems, and agents may act on behalf of others.

Governance should clearly distinguish between:

* Acting as oneself
* Acting on behalf of another
* Acting under delegated authority

## Governance Concepts

Anchor currently explores several governance concepts.

### Authority

The ability to perform actions within a defined scope.

### Delegation

The transfer of authority from one entity to another.

### Representation

The ability to act on behalf of another entity.

### Revocation

The removal or limitation of previously granted authority.

### Accountability

Responsibility for actions taken under authority.

### Attribution

The ability to identify who performed an action and under what authority.
