Anchor · Trust

Trust-Related Artifact Integrity

Anchor does not determine trustworthiness, issue trust judgments, calculate trust scores, or maintain trust relationships.

Within the Satoshium Suite, Attestor remains authoritative for Trust Statements. Anchor's role begins only when the integrity of a Trust Statement or other trust-related artifact should be preserved through an Integrity Reference.

Trust Boundary

Trust is a judgment or assessment made by a person, institution, community, system, or Attestor process.

Anchor does not make that judgment. Anchor may preserve the integrity of the artifact in which that judgment is expressed.

Trust Judgment ≠ Trust Artifact Integrity

Trust Statement

A Trust Statement is an Attestor-owned canonical object expressing a governed trust-related conclusion or assessment.

External Authority

Trust Artifact

A trust-related artifact may include a Trust Statement, trust signal, assessment, recommendation, warning, confidence statement, or other governed representation.

Relationship to Attestor

Attestor → Trust Statement
Anchor → Integrity Reference

Attestor remains authoritative for the meaning, trust conclusion, lifecycle, Versioning, Correction, and publication of the Trust Statement.

Anchor may preserve integrity context for a defined Trust Statement representation without becoming the trust authority.

Trust vs. Integrity

Integrity asks whether an artifact remains consistent with its anchored representation. Trust asks whether confidence should be placed in the subject, statement, source, or conclusion.

Trust vs. Verification

Anchor Integrity Verification may confirm consistency with an Integrity Reference. It does not determine whether the Trust Statement itself should be accepted.

Trust vs. Certification

Certification may inform trust, but Certifier remains authoritative for certification decisions. Anchor does not turn certification into a trust judgment.

Trust vs. Reputation

Reputation may influence trust, but Anchor calculates neither. Anchor may preserve Integrity References for artifacts produced by systems that do.

What Anchor Preserves

Source Institution · Source-System Identifier · Trust Artifact Type · Canonical Representation · Representation Boundary · Integrity Value · Temporal Context · Signature / Verification Material · Anchor Metadata

Anchor preserves integrity context. It does not copy the trust logic or trust conclusion into Anchor-owned institutional authority.

Integrity of a Trust Statement

A Trust Statement may retain perfect cryptographic integrity while remaining disputable, contextual, outdated, incomplete, or methodologically weak.

Integrity of a Trust Statement ≠ Validity of the Trust Judgment

Anchor may confirm that the representation has not changed. It does not prove that the trust conclusion is correct.

Trust Artifact Lifecycle

A Trust Statement or other trust-related artifact may later be superseded, withdrawn, corrected, revoked, replaced, or reissued by its Source Institution.

Anchor should preserve the integrity lineage of the representation it actually anchored rather than silently rewriting the earlier state.

Earlier Trust Representation → Earlier Integrity Reference
Later Trust Representation → Later Anchor Version or New Integrity Reference where required

Source Trust Change vs. Anchor Correction

If Attestor or another Source Institution changes the trust judgment, Trust Statement, methodology, or lifecycle, that is a Source Artifact change.

If Anchor recorded the wrong Source Artifact, representation, digest, timestamp, signer metadata, or other Anchor-owned information, that may require an Anchor Correction.

Source Trust Change ≠ Anchor Correction

Trust Methodology Artifacts

A trust methodology, evaluation framework, scoring model, decision rule, or governance document may itself become an Anchor candidate.

In that case Anchor preserves the integrity of the methodology artifact. It does not adopt or endorse the methodology.

Privacy and Judgment Boundary

Trust-related artifacts may contain sensitive or evaluative information. Anchor should not amplify such information merely because it can be anchored.

The minimum-necessary-data principle should remain active.

Preserve necessary integrity context. Do not amplify unnecessary trust judgments.

Relationship to the Suite

Atlas may provide authoritative intelligence.
Certifier may provide certification determinations.
Registry may catalog authoritative records.
Chronicle may preserve qualifying historical memory.
Attestor may issue Trust Statements.
Anchor may preserve Integrity References for the relevant artifacts.

These systems may inform one another while retaining separate authority.

Reference does not transfer authority.

Trust Principle

Anchor should preserve the integrity of a trust-related artifact when that preservation has durable value, while leaving trust meaning, methodology, judgment, lifecycle, and publication to the institution responsible for them.

Preserve trust artifacts. Do not become the trust authority.
