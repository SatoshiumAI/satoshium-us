Anchor · Trust

Trust

Trust is the willingness to rely upon information, identities, systems, or participants under conditions of uncertainty.

Anchor explores how trust may become more transparent, observable, evidence-based, and portable across interoperable digital environments.

Evidence

Trust often emerges from evidence rather than assertion. Evidence helps participants evaluate claims and make informed judgments.

Principle

Verification

Verification processes may strengthen confidence by examining claims, records, sources, and supporting information.

Future

Reputation

Historical behavior, participation, and prior interactions may contribute trust signals that influence future decisions.

Exploratory

Attestations

Trust may be influenced by attestations from other identities that provide support, challenge, or additional context.

Future

Trust Layer

Trust is not a record, a score, or a certificate. Trust is a judgment made by participants using available information, evidence, history, and context.

Identity → Claim → Attestation → Reputation → Trust

Trust Philosophy

Anchor does not seek to manufacture trust. Instead, it explores how systems can provide the information necessary for participants to make their own trust decisions.

Trust is strongest when it is informed, transparent, and supported by evidence rather than authority alone.

Long-Term Vision

The long-term vision of Anchor is to support trust frameworks that remain interoperable, understandable, and resilient across diverse digital systems and communities.

Trust should be portable without becoming centralized. It should be transparent without becoming coercive.
