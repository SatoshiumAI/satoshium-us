## Governing Distinction

The Suite preserves the distinction:

```text
Authority
→ who governs, owns, decides, or is institutionally responsible

Provenance
→ where information came from, how it was obtained, transformed, transmitted, or preserved
```

These concepts may be related, but they are not interchangeable.

Most importantly:

> **REFERENCE DOES NOT TRANSFER AUTHORITY.**

And:

> **AUTHORITY ≠ PROVENANCE.**

## Institutional Authority Boundaries

The reconciled institutional authority model remains:

```text
Atlas
→ authoritative jurisdiction intelligence

Navigator
→ workflow definition / orchestration

Certifier
→ certification

Registry
→ canonical registration / public catalog

Chronicle
→ historical preservation

Anchor
→ integrity preservation

Beacon
→ discovery & signals

Attestor
→ governed attestation & rule-constrained evaluation
```

Each institution retains authority over its own canonical object and institutional determinations.

---

## Reference Boundary

A reference:

```text
identifies
points to
or connects to
```

another governed object.

It does not make the referencing institution authoritative for that object.

Therefore:

```text
Reference
≠ Ownership

Reference
≠ Authority

Reference
≠ Derivation

Reference
≠ Support
```

unless the stronger relationship is explicitly and separately established.

---

## Provenance Boundary

Provenance may record:

```text
source
origin
custody
access path
derivation path
transformation
transmission
historical lineage
```

but provenance does not establish substantive institutional authority.

A provenance statement may show where information came from without making the receiving or preserving institution authoritative for the source matter.

---

## Registry Boundary

Registry may register and publicly catalog a Source Record.

That does not transfer authority over the Source Record to Registry.

```text
Source Institution
→ retains authority over Source Record

Registry
→ owns the Satoshium Registry Entry (SREG)
```

Registration records a governed relationship.

It does not create source authority.

---

## Chronicle Boundary

Chronicle may preserve the historical state of another institution's object.

That does not make Chronicle authoritative for the source object's current operational state or substantive determination.

```text
Chronicle
→ authority over Chronicle Entry

Source Institution
→ authority over source object
```

Historical preservation preserves history.

It does not transfer source authority.

---

## Anchor Boundary

Anchor may establish and preserve integrity information about a Source Artifact.

That does not make Anchor authoritative for:

```text
the source artifact's substantive meaning

certification

truth

trust

or originating institutional decision
```

Anchor authority remains bounded to integrity preservation.

---

## Beacon Boundary

Beacon may discover and reference objects governed by other institutions.

That does not make Beacon authoritative for:

```text
certification

registration

historical state

integrity

attestation

trust conclusions

or underlying intelligence
```

Beacon owns its Discovery Signal.

The referenced source institution retains source authority.

---

## Navigator Boundary

Navigator may coordinate institutional work through a Navigator Workflow Definition.

Coordination does not transfer authority.

```text
Navigator
→ owns workflow definition / orchestration

Participating Institution
→ retains authority over its own canonical output and decision
```

Workflow context does not become institutional authority.

---

## Certifier Boundary

Certifier owns certification authority for its certification process and Certification Package.

Use of Atlas intelligence, Registry entries, Chronicle records, Anchor integrity references, Beacon discovery, or Attestor outputs does not transfer those institutions' authority into Certifier.

Likewise, downstream use of a Certification Package does not transfer Certifier's certification authority to the downstream institution.

---

## Attestor Boundary

Attestor may reference governed inputs from across the Suite.

Those references remain attributable to their originating authorities.

Attestor owns:

```text
Attestation

Rule-Constrained Evaluation

Trust Statement
```

Attestor does not become authoritative source for the referenced object.

The mature boundary remains:

```text
Referenced Authority
≠ Attestor Authority

Source Provenance
≠ Attestor Ownership
```

Attestor's Trust Statement is its own bounded conclusion.

It is not a replacement for the underlying source authority.

---

## Derivation and Support

The relationship vocabulary remains distinct:

```text
references
→ points to

derived-from
→ establishes lineage / origin

supports
→ provides evidentiary or logical support
```

A derived object may preserve lineage without acquiring the full authority of its source.

A supporting object may contribute evidence without becoming authoritative for the supported object's institutional domain.

---

## Publication Boundary

Publication does not create or transfer authority.

```text
Published
→ publicly accessible

Published
≠ authoritative because published
```

Authority derives from institutional responsibility and governing architecture, not visibility.

---

## Identifier Boundary

Identifiers establish:

```text
identity
namespace
```

They do not establish:

```text
authority
provenance
relationship
status
version
validity
publication
```

Matching numeric suffixes across institutions do not imply authority, lineage, or dependency.
