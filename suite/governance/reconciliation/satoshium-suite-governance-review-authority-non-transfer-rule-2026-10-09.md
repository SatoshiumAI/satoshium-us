## Purpose

This record defines the Suite-wide non-transfer rule for governance authority.

Its purpose is to ensure that institutional authority cannot be silently acquired through:

- reference;
- orchestration;
- registration;
- interoperability;
- or Suite membership.

The rule preserves institutional sovereignty while allowing the eight formal Suite institutions to coordinate, exchange information, reference one another, and operate as one coherent Suite.

---

# Governing Rule

> **Governance authority remains with the institution to which the authority was assigned. Interaction does not transfer authority unless an explicit, authorized Suite governance decision changes the architecture.**

This applies regardless of whether institutions are connected through:

```text
reference
registration
orchestration
interoperability
discovery
preservation
integrity
certification
attestation
publication
technical dependency
Suite membership
```

The default rule is:

```text
INTERACTION
≠
AUTHORITY TRANSFER
```

---

# 1. Reference Does Not Transfer Authority

A reference identifies, relates, supports, derives from, evaluates, or otherwise connects one governed object to another.

It does not transfer governance authority.

Examples:

```text
Registry references a Certification Package
→ Registry does not acquire Certifier authority

Chronicle references a source occurrence
→ Chronicle does not acquire source authority

Anchor references a Source Artifact
→ Anchor does not acquire authority over the Source Artifact

Beacon references a source
→ Beacon does not become authority over the discovered information

Attestor references governed inputs
→ Attestor does not acquire the source institutions' authority
```

Therefore:

> **Reference establishes connection, not ownership, control, or governance authority.**

---

# 2. Orchestration Does Not Transfer Authority

Navigator may define and orchestrate workflows involving other institutions.

That coordination does not convert Navigator into a governing authority over the participating institutions.

Navigator may determine:

- workflow structure;
- workflow sequencing;
- orchestration behavior;
- Navigator-owned workflow state;
- Navigator-owned outputs.

Navigator may not determine, merely through orchestration:

- Certifier's certification decision;
- Registry's SREG decision;
- Chronicle's preservation decision;
- Anchor's integrity result;
- Beacon's Discovery Signal decision;
- Attestor's Evaluation Outcome or Trust Statement;
- Atlas's authoritative intelligence conclusions.

Therefore:

> **Orchestration coordinates institutional action. It does not absorb institutional authority.**

---

# 3. Registration Does Not Transfer Authority

Registry owns the SREG.

The originating institution retains authority over the Source Record.

Registration may establish:

- Registry identity;
- Registry classification;
- Registry metadata;
- Registry lifecycle;
- Registry publication;
- Registry relationships;
- Registry corrections;
- Registry history.

Registration does not transfer:

- Source Record ownership;
- Source Record lifecycle authority;
- Source conclusions;
- Source corrections;
- Source versioning authority.

Therefore:

```text
Registry owns the registration.
The Source Institution owns the source.
```

and:

> **Registration creates a Registry object, not a transfer of source authority.**

---

# 4. Interoperability Does Not Transfer Authority

Interoperability allows independent institutions to:

- exchange information;
- serialize objects;
- resolve identifiers;
- propagate state;
- propagate version context;
- perform handoffs;
- maintain compatibility;
- interpret governed references.

Those capabilities do not alter canonical ownership.

Interoperability may constrain how institutions exchange information.

It may not silently redefine:

- institutional roles;
- canonical objects;
- authority boundaries;
- lifecycle ownership;
- conclusions;
- Suite membership.

Therefore:

> **Interoperability connects sovereign institutions. It does not merge them.**

Technical dependence is not governance dependence.

Schema compatibility is not governance authority.

Identifier resolution is not authority transfer.

State propagation is not state ownership.

---

# 5. Suite Membership Does Not Erase Institutional Sovereignty

Membership in the Satoshium Suite creates constitutional obligations.

It does not erase institution-specific authority.

A formal Suite institution remains responsible for:

- its own canonical objects;
- its own Rules;
- its own lifecycle decisions;
- its own Corrections;
- its own Versioning;
- its own institutional determinations.

Suite Governance may constrain those responsibilities through shared constitutional rules.

It does not automatically take operational control over them.

Accordingly:

```text
Suite membership
→ creates shared obligations

Suite membership
≠ creates shared ownership
```

and:

```text
collective governance
≠ centralized operational control
```

---

# Authority Transfer Requires Explicit Constitutional Action

Authority can change only through an authorized governance process.

A real transfer or reassignment of governance authority must be explicit.

It must identify:

- the authority being changed;
- the institution currently holding it;
- the proposed new authority holder;
- the affected canonical objects;
- the affected Rules or decisions;
- the reason for change;
- the governance level required;
- the effective date;
- the preservation treatment of prior authority;
- and whether the change is constitutional.

No authority transfer may be inferred merely from:

- repeated operational practice;
- technical convenience;
- documentation wording;
- interoperability patterns;
- workflow sequencing;
- public presentation;
- cross-reference density;
- or institutional dependence.

# Governance Conflict Test

A potential authority-transfer problem exists if any process causes one institution to behave as though it may:

- revise another institution's canonical object;
- approve another institution's institutional decision;
- supersede another institution's conclusion;
- alter another institution's lifecycle state;
- issue a correction in another institution's name;
- create a version of another institution's canonical object;
- or claim source authority merely because it references or processes the source.

If any such condition appears, the matter must be classified:

```text
POTENTIAL GOVERNANCE AUTHORITY COLLISION
```

and escalated to Suite Governance.
