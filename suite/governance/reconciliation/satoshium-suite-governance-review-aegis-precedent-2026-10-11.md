## Purpose

This record reviews Aegis as a precedent for distinguishing:

```text
Satoshium application / service integration
```

from:

```text
formal Satoshium Suite institutional membership
```

The central governance question is:

> **Can a Satoshium application or service integrate with Suite institutions without thereby becoming a formal Suite institution?**

The determination is:

# YES.

Aegis demonstrates that an important, operational, technically integrated Satoshium service may remain outside the formal Suite institutional roster unless and until it is explicitly admitted through constitutional governance.

# 1. Application May Integrate with the Suite

A Satoshium application or service may interact with Suite institutions through governed interfaces, references, workflows, compatibility surfaces, inspection surfaces, or other interoperability mechanisms.

Such integration may include:

- reading Suite-owned records;
- referencing Suite identifiers;
- consuming Suite outputs;
- producing external or service-layer outputs;
- participating in Navigator-orchestrated workflows;
- exposing compatibility information;
- supplying evidence or signals to a Suite institution where governed;
- receiving Suite-originated inputs;
- providing inspection or verification-oriented service functions.

Aegis is compatible with this pattern because its current architecture is explicitly bounded around inspection, lifecycle verification, structured outputs, compatibility review, and exportable artifacts while withholding execution and mutation authority.

Therefore:

> **An external or pre-Suite application may participate in Suite workflows without becoming part of the Suite's constitutional institution set.**

---

# 2. Integration Does Not Automatically Create Suite Membership

The Day 1 non-transfer rule now extends directly to membership:

```text
INTEGRATION
≠
INSTITUTIONAL MEMBERSHIP
```

and:

```text
INTEROPERABILITY
≠
CONSTITUTIONAL ADMISSION
```

An application does not become a formal Suite institution merely because it:

- communicates with Suite institutions;
- references Suite objects;
- appears in Suite workflows;
- is linked from Suite documentation;
- consumes Suite data;
- provides data to Suite institutions;
- is technically necessary for an implementation;
- is operated by Satoshium;
- is mature;
- is operational;
- is publicly documented;
- has its own architecture;
- has its own service endpoints;
- has durable identifiers or artifacts.

Formal membership requires the admission process established during Day 2.

That does not give Aegis authority over Suite canonical objects.

