## Purpose

This record defines the boundary between:

```text
Suite Governance
```

and:

```text
Institutional Governance
```

The purpose is to ensure that governance remains strong enough to preserve Suite-wide coherence without absorbing the legitimate authority of the eight formal institutions.

The core question is:

> **What must be governed collectively because it affects the Suite as a whole, and what must remain governed by each institution because it belongs to that institution's settled responsibility?**

---

# Governing Distinction

## Suite Governance

Suite Governance governs matters whose meaning, effect, or authority is genuinely Suite-wide.

It exists to preserve:

- constitutional coherence;
- common architectural boundaries;
- shared Standards;
- shared Methodology;
- shared governance principles;
- Suite membership;
- Suite-wide controlled distinctions;
- cross-institution compatibility boundaries;
- common review and change-control rules;
- and the integrity of decisions that affect more than one institution's settled authority.

Suite Governance protects the Suite as a system of independent institutions.

It does not operate those institutions for them.

---

## Institutional Governance

Institutional Governance governs matters that fall within one institution's settled role, canonical ownership, operational responsibility, and lifecycle authority.

It exists to preserve:

- institution-specific decision rights;
- canonical object stewardship;
- institution-specific Rules;
- institution-specific Policies;
- institution-specific Procedures;
- institution-specific schemas and profiles;
- operational validation;
- lifecycle decisions;
- publication decisions;
- corrections;
- versioning;
- internal delegation;
- exceptions;
- accountability;
- and implementation controls.

Institutional Governance applies Suite-level constraints within the institution's own authorized domain.

It does not create Suite-wide authority merely because its governance model is mature or useful.

---

# What Belongs to the Suite Collectively

The following matters belong to Suite Governance because they affect the identity, coherence, or constitutional operation of the Suite as a whole.

## 1. Formal Suite Membership

Suite Governance controls:

- admission of a new formal institution;
- removal of a formal institution;
- institutional merger or division where formal Suite identity changes;
- retirement or replacement of a formal institution;
- and the formal boundary between Suite institutions and external applications.

No single institution may add another institution to the formal Suite.

No institution may declare itself outside the Suite while continuing to exercise formal Suite authority without a Suite-level governance decision.

---

## 2. Institutional Role Boundaries

Suite Governance protects the settled assignment of institutional responsibility.

Examples:

```text
Atlas
→ Authoritative Intelligence

Registry
→ Canonical Registration / Public Catalog

Anchor
→ Integrity Preservation

Attestor
→ Governed Attestation & Rule-Constrained Evaluation
```

An institution may refine how it performs its role.

It may not independently redefine its role in a way that absorbs another institution's responsibility.

A proposed role change is a Suite governance matter.

---

## 3. Canonical Object Ownership Boundaries

Suite Governance protects which institution owns which canonical object family.

Examples:

```text
Certification Package
→ Certifier

SREG
→ Registry

Chronicle Entry
→ Chronicle

Integrity Reference
→ Anchor
```

An institution may govern its own canonical objects.

It may not unilaterally claim ownership of another institution's canonical objects.

A change in canonical ownership is constitutional and requires Suite-level governance.

---

## 4. Shared Standards

Suite Standards belong collectively to the Suite.

They establish expectations that may apply across institutions.

Institutions may implement those Standards through institution-specific governance and procedures.

Institutions may not silently rewrite Suite Standards through local practice.

---

## 5. Shared Methodology

Suite Methodology belongs collectively to the Suite as a shared methodological foundation.

The existing Methodology architecture explicitly preserves institution-specific governance, methodology, validation, conformance, lifecycle, publication, and production architecture.

Where an institution has adopted more specific governed procedures, those procedures govern the institution's operation so long as they remain compatible with the Suite-level foundation.

Therefore:

> **Suite Methodology provides common methodological constraints; it does not replace institutional operating governance.**

---

## 6. Suite-Wide Controlled Distinctions

Suite Governance protects distinctions whose collapse would create cross-institution ambiguity.

Examples include:

```text
AUTHORITY ≠ PROVENANCE

REFERENCE ≠ DERIVATION ≠ SUPPORT

VALIDATION ≠ CONFORMANCE

VALIDATION ≠ EVALUATION

VERIFICATION ≠ CERTIFICATION

CANONICAL CREATION ≠ LIFECYCLE ACTIVATION ≠ PUBLICATION

CONNECTION ≠ IDENTITY
```

Institutions may define more specific local vocabulary.

They may not redefine these distinctions in a way that breaks Suite-wide meaning.

---

## 7. Cross-Institution Governance Boundaries

Suite Governance governs rules necessary to ensure that one institution cannot acquire another institution's authority through:

- reference;
- registration;
- orchestration;
- discovery;
- integrity preservation;
- certification;
- attestation;
- exchange;
- serialization;
- publication;
- or technical dependency.

The governing principle remains:

> **REFERENCE DOES NOT TRANSFER AUTHORITY.**

---

## 8. Suite-Wide Governance Change Rules

Suite Governance controls the process for:

- constitutional change;
- material cross-institution change;
- Suite-level governance revision;
- changes to Standards;
- changes to shared Methodology;
- changes to formal Suite membership;
- changes affecting multiple institutional authority boundaries;
- and changes that would alter settled Reconciliation decisions.

Institutions may propose such changes.

They may not enact them unilaterally.

---

## 9. Shared Historical and Decision Integrity

Suite Governance may require common minimum expectations for:

- decision traceability;
- effective dates;
- supersession;
- correction;
- preservation;
- historical reviewability;
- and public accountability.

Those minimums do not require all institutions to use identical internal processes.

They establish the Suite-wide floor.

---

# What Remains Institution-Specific

The following matters remain under Institutional Governance unless a specific issue crosses the Suite boundary.

## 1. Canonical Object Stewardship

Each institution governs its own canonical object family.

This includes, as applicable:

- creation;
- internal validation;
- lifecycle activation;
- publication;
- versioning;
- correction;
- supersession;
- retirement;
- archival treatment;
- and institution-specific metadata.

Suite Governance does not make individual object-level decisions unless a constitutional or cross-institution conflict exists.

---

## 2. Institution-Specific Rules

An institution may define binding Rules within its settled authority.

For example, Registry may define Rules governing SREG identity, registrability, source authority, Registry lifecycle, relationships, and corrections.

Those Rules are not automatically Rules for Atlas, Certifier, Chronicle, Anchor, Beacon, Navigator, or Attestor.

---

## 3. Institution-Specific Policies

Each institution may define Policies governing how its Rules are interpreted and applied.

Policies remain subordinate to:

- settled Suite architecture;
- applicable Suite Standards;
- applicable Suite Methodology;
- and Suite Governance constraints.

---

## 4. Institution-Specific Procedures

Institutions control their repeatable operational procedures.

Examples include:

- how Certifier organizes evidence and reaches a certification determination;
- how Registry admits a registrable source into an SREG;
- how Chronicle preserves an occurrence;
- how Anchor constructs an Integrity Reference;
- how Beacon issues or maintains a Discovery Signal;
- how Attestor evaluates eligible governed inputs.

Suite Governance should not micromanage those procedures unless they create a Suite-level conflict.

---

## 5. Institution-Specific Schemas and Profiles

Institutions govern schemas, record-type profiles, institution-specific controlled values, and representation rules for their own objects.

Suite-level interoperability requirements may constrain exchange behavior.

They do not automatically transfer schema ownership.

---

## 6. Institution-Specific Decision Rights

Institutional Governance determines which roles within that institution may:

- propose;
- review;
- approve;
- implement;
- publish;
- correct;
- supersede;
- retire;
- or maintain institution-owned materials.

The Suite may impose minimum governance expectations.

It should not replace legitimate institution-specific decision structures.

---

## 7. Institution-Specific Exceptions and Conflicts

Institutions may govern exceptions and conflicts arising inside their own authority domain.

A local exception becomes a Suite Governance matter only when it:

- conflicts with a Suite Standard;
- conflicts with shared Methodology;
- changes a settled authority boundary;
- affects another institution's canonical ownership;
- creates cross-institution incompatibility;
- or functions as a de facto constitutional change.

---

## 8. Institution-Specific Operational Evolution

An institution may improve:

- implementation;
- workflow;
- interfaces;
- validation;
- documentation;
- automation;
- representations;
- schemas;
- supporting objects;
- and operating procedures

without requiring Suite-level approval, provided the change does not alter:

- institutional role;
- canonical ownership;
- Suite membership;
- cross-institution authority;
- settled Suite semantics;
- or constitutional obligations.

---

# Where Suite Governance Must Stop

Suite Governance must stop at the boundary of legitimate institutional sovereignty.

It must not:

## 1. Operate Canonical Objects for the Institution

Suite Governance must not create, approve, correct, version, publish, supersede, or retire an institution-owned canonical object merely because the object is part of the Suite.

Object-level authority stays with the owning institution.

---

## 2. Replace Institution-Specific Governance

Suite Governance may establish minimum expectations and constitutional constraints.

It must not erase an institution's own:

- Rules;
- Policies;
- Procedures;
- schemas;
- validation architecture;
- lifecycle governance;
- publication governance;
- or internal accountability model.

The Suite Methodology already preserves this boundary by stating that Suite-level methodology does not replace institution-specific governance or production architecture. filecitefile_000000001838822f8962729236018866L53-L61

---

## 3. Convert Shared Foundations into Operational Ownership

Suite Standards and Suite Methodology constrain and guide institutional operation.

They do not become operators of:

- Certification Packages;
- SREGs;
- Chronicle Entries;
- Integrity References;
- Discovery Signals;
- Attestations;
- Trust Statements;
- Navigator Workflow Definitions;
- or Jurisdiction Intelligence Packages.

---

## 4. Absorb Authority Through Oversight

Oversight does not equal ownership.

Review does not equal operational authority.

Approval of a Suite-level rule does not make Suite Governance the owner of every institutional decision made under that rule.

---

## 5. Use Precedent as Authority Transfer

Registry currently demonstrates the most mature institution-level governance model.

That maturity does not make Registry Governance the governance authority for the rest of the Suite.

Likewise, Certifier's governance model applies to Certifier and explicitly preserves Suite Standards and Suite Methodology as higher-level shared foundations. filecitefile_00000000726081f594ab14c8e6fd1b3aL1-L20

A useful precedent may be generalized only through a formal Suite governance decision.

---

## 6. Treat Technical Dependency as Governance Dependency

An institution may depend technically on:

- a reference;
- an identifier;
- a schema;
- a workflow;
- a source;
- or an interoperability mechanism.

That dependency does not make the source institution the governance authority over the receiving institution.

Technical dependency must not become hidden constitutional authority.

---

## 7. Override Source Authority

Suite Governance cannot cause:

- Registry to own a Certifier Certification Package;
- Chronicle to own the operational state it preserves;
- Anchor to own the Source Artifact it protects;
- Beacon to become the authority over the information it discovers;
- Attestor to become the source authority for the inputs it evaluates;
- Navigator to own outcomes produced by orchestrated institutions.

The source or owning institution retains authority.

---

## 8. Create Architecture Through Documentation

Suite Governance documentation must not silently create:

- a ninth institution;
- a new canonical object;
- a new authority transfer;
- a mandatory universal pipeline;
- a new institutional role;
- or a new constitutional dependency.

Documentation must record authorized architecture.

It does not create architecture merely by describing it.

# Governance Escalation Test

A matter should remain under Institutional Governance when all of the following are true:

```text
It affects one institution's settled authority.

It does not alter Suite membership.

It does not alter institutional role.

It does not alter canonical ownership.

It does not redefine a Suite-wide controlled distinction.

It does not create authority over another institution.

It does not conflict with Suite Standards or shared Methodology.

It does not create a material cross-institution compatibility problem.
```

A matter should escalate to Suite Governance when any of the following is true:

```text
Formal Suite membership changes.

Institutional role changes.

Canonical ownership changes.

A Suite Standard changes.

Shared Methodology changes materially.

A cross-institution authority boundary changes.

A Suite-wide semantic distinction changes.

Multiple institutions become subject to one material governance decision.

A local exception effectively becomes a Suite-wide rule.

An interoperability mechanism creates de facto authority or constitutional dependency.

A settled Reconciliation decision would need to be reopened.
```

# Working Governance Model

The resulting model is:

```text
SUITE GOVERNANCE
→ protects the constitutional whole
→ defines shared constraints
→ governs material cross-institution change
→ protects institutional boundaries

INSTITUTIONAL GOVERNANCE
→ governs institution-owned responsibilities
→ governs canonical object stewardship
→ governs local Rules / Policies / Procedures
→ governs operations within settled authority
```

The relationship is:

```text
Suite Governance
        ↓ constrains
Institutional Governance
        ↓ operates
Institution-Owned Responsibilities
```

But:

```text
CONSTRAINT ≠ OWNERSHIP

OVERSIGHT ≠ OPERATION

ALIGNMENT ≠ AUTHORITY TRANSFER
```

---

# Governing Boundary Rule

> **Suite Governance governs the boundaries that make the Suite coherent. Institutional Governance governs the work that makes each institution operational. Suite Governance must stop where constitutional protection would become operational takeover.**
