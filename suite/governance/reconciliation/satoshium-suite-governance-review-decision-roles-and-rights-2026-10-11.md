## Purpose

This record defines who may perform each principal role in the Satoshium Suite governance process:

- propose;
- review;
- determine;
- approve;
- implement;
- publish.

The purpose is to create clear decision rights without inventing unnecessary permanent offices or collapsing institutional sovereignty.

The governing sequence remains:

```text
Proposal
→ Review
→ Determination
→ Approval
→ Publication
→ Implementation
→ Preservation
```

This record assigns authority to each stage.

---

# Source & Governance Boundary

The current Governance Standard establishes the need for:

- ownership;
- stewardship;
- responsibility;
- proposal;
- review;
- approval;
- publication;
- version control;
- transparency;
- reviewability.

However, the current source set does **not** define named permanent Suite governance offices such as a board, council, committee, or administrator.

Therefore this Governance Review will not invent titles or organizational bodies merely to fill that gap.

Instead, decision rights are assigned by:

```text
governed subject
+
change classification
+
affected authority
```

This preserves the Day 1 rule:

> **Governance authority follows the governed subject.**

---

# Core Separation Principle

No governance role should be interpreted as carrying more authority than the stage requires.

Therefore:

```text
PROPOSER
≠ APPROVER

REVIEWER
≠ DECISION OWNER

DETERMINER
≠ IMPLEMENTER BY DEFAULT

PUBLISHER
≠ APPROVER

IMPLEMENTER
≠ GOVERNANCE AUTHORITY
```

A person or governing actor may perform more than one role when appropriate, especially for Routine matters, but the roles remain conceptually distinct.

For Material and Constitutional matters, separation should increase with significance.

# 1. Who May Propose?

## General Rule

A governance proposal may be initiated by any legitimate actor with a direct governance interest in the governed subject.

This may include:

- the institution that owns the affected subject;
- another Suite institution affected by the matter;
- the steward of a Suite Standard or Methodology surface;
- a responsible maintainer;
- a reviewer who discovers a material governance issue;
- Suite Governance itself;
- the Satoshium founder / current constitutional steward during the present founder-governed period;
- another authorized contributor acting through the applicable governance process.

## Important Boundary

Proposal authority is intentionally broad because:

```text
ABILITY TO RAISE A QUESTION
≠
AUTHORITY TO DECIDE IT
```

An institution may propose a change affecting another institution.

It may not approve or impose that change merely because it proposed it.

## Constitutional Proposals

Constitutional proposals may originate from:

- an affected institution;
- Suite Governance;
- the current constitutional steward;
- a governance review that discovers a constitutional issue.

Proposal does not create a presumption of approval.

## Rule

> **Any authorized stakeholder may propose; only the responsible governance authority may decide.**

---

# 2. Who May Review?

## General Rule

Review must include the authority whose governed subject is affected.

Reviewers should be selected according to classification.

### Routine

```text
Owning Institution
```

Review may be performed by the institution's responsible steward, maintainer, or authorized governance reviewer.

### Material

```text
Owning Institution
+
affected Suite-level reviewer(s)
+
other materially affected institution(s), where applicable
```

Material review should include any authority whose:

- canonical object;
- interoperability boundary;
- Methodology;
- Standard;
- lifecycle;
- or operational dependency

is materially affected.

### Constitutional

```text
Formal Suite Governance Review
+
all directly affected institutional authorities
```

Constitutional review must assess the Suite as a whole, not merely the institution requesting change.

## Reviewer Limits

A reviewer may:

- test;
- challenge;
- request evidence;
- identify conflict;
- recommend;
- require reclassification;
- document findings.

A reviewer may not, by review alone:

- create authority;
- approve the change;
- alter another institution's canonical object;
- implement the proposal.

## Rule

> **Review belongs to the authorities affected by the change, in proportion to the significance of the change.**

---

# 3. Who May Determine?

## Definition

The **Determiner** converts review findings into a formal governance conclusion.

The Determiner answers:

```text
What does governance conclude?
```

## Routine

The owning institution may determine Routine matters within its settled authority.

```text
Determiner
→ Owning Institution
```

## Material

The owning institution normally remains the primary Determiner for institution-owned matters, but the determination must incorporate required Suite review.

```text
Primary Determiner
→ Owning Institution

Required governance context
→ Suite Review / concurrence where applicable
```

If the material issue is itself Suite-owned—for example, a Suite Standard—then the Suite-level responsible authority is the Determiner.

## Constitutional

For Constitutional matters:

```text
Determiner
→ Formal Suite Governance
```

No affected institution may determine constitutional change unilaterally.

## Determination Rule

> **The Determiner is the authority responsible for the governed subject at the governance level required by the classification.**

---

# 4. Who May Approve?

Approval is the strongest decision-right stage.

It creates authorized governance state.

## Routine

```text
Approver
→ Owning Institution
```

The approving authority must possess legitimate responsibility for the governed subject.

## Material

```text
Approver
→ Owning Institution
+
required Suite-level concurrence or approval
```

The exact form depends on the subject.

Examples:

- institution-owned operational governance with Suite impact  
  → institution approves after required Suite review;
- Suite Standard change  
  → Suite-level Standards governance approves;
- shared Methodology change  
  → Suite-level Methodology governance approves.

## Constitutional

```text
Approver
→ Formal Suite Governance
```

During the present founder-governed stage of Satoshium, the final constitutional approval authority remains the founder / current constitutional steward unless and until a later governance model formally delegates or supersedes that authority.

This does not convert the founder into the operational authority for institution-owned Routine matters.

It defines the present final authority for constitutional changes to the Suite itself.

## Approval Limits

Approval may not be inferred from:

- silence;
- lack of objection;
- publication;
- implementation;
- code merge;
- elapsed time;
- repeated practice.

## Rule

> **Approval must come from the authority that legitimately controls the governed subject at the required governance level.**

---

# 5. Who May Implement?

## General Rule

Implementation belongs to the actor or institution responsible for the affected operational surface.

Examples:

```text
Registry Procedure change
→ Registry implements

Suite Standards page update
→ responsible Suite Standards maintainer implements

Interoperability contract change
→ affected implementation owners implement their respective portions

Constitutional institutional restructuring
→ designated affected institutions / maintainers implement the approved transition
```

## Implementation Authority

The implementer receives authority from the approved decision.

The implementer may not enlarge that authority.

Therefore:

```text
IMPLEMENTATION SCOPE
≤
APPROVED SCOPE
```

If additional change becomes necessary:

```text
STOP
→ return to governance
```

## Implementer Limits

An implementer may not:

- change the determination;
- expand constitutional scope;
- create unapproved canonical ownership;
- reinterpret approval into broader authority.

## Rule

> **The responsible operator implements; the governance decision defines the limit of implementation authority.**

---

# 6. Who May Publish?

## General Rule

Publication should be performed by the steward or maintainer responsible for the authoritative surface being changed.

Examples:

```text
Institutional Rule
→ owning institution publishes

Suite Standard
→ Suite Standards steward publishes

Suite Governance Decision Record
→ responsible Suite Governance steward publishes

Constitutional membership change
→ Suite governance publication authority updates the formal Suite record
```

## Publisher Responsibility

The Publisher must ensure the published representation accurately reflects:

- approved determination;
- decision status;
- version;
- effective date;
- supersession;
- historical references;
- applicable terminology.

## Publisher Limits

The Publisher may not:

- alter the approved decision;
- add new authority;
- omit material conditions;
- convert a proposal into approval;
- treat publication as the decision itself.

Therefore:

```text
PUBLISHER
→ records authority

PUBLISHER
≠ creates authority
```

## Rule

> **Publication belongs to the steward of the authoritative surface, acting under an approved governance decision.**

# Decision Rights by Classification

| Role | Routine | Material | Constitutional |
|---|---|---|---|
| Propose | Authorized institutional stakeholder | Institution, affected institution, Suite stakeholder | Institution, Suite Governance, constitutional steward |
| Review | Owning institution | Owning institution + affected Suite/institutions | Formal Suite review + affected institutions |
| Determine | Owning institution | Owning authority at applicable level, with required Suite review | Formal Suite Governance |
| Approve | Owning institution | Owning authority + required Suite concurrence/approval | Formal Suite Governance / current constitutional steward |
| Implement | Responsible institutional operator | Designated affected operator(s) | Designated transition implementer(s) |
| Publish | Owning authoritative-surface steward | Appropriate institution/Suite steward | Suite governance publication authority |

# Separation of Duties

For Routine matters, one responsible actor may legitimately:

```text
review
determine
approve
implement
publish
```

when institutional governance allows it and the matter carries low governance risk.

For Material matters, preferred separation is:

```text
Proposer
≠ sole Reviewer

Reviewer
≠ sole Approver

Approver
≠ uncontrolled Implementer
```

For Constitutional matters, the process should preserve clear distinction among:

```text
proposal
review
determination
approval
implementation
publication
```

even if the present founder-governed structure means the same constitutional steward participates in more than one stage.

The purpose is traceability and accountability, not artificial bureaucracy.

---

# Conflict-of-Authority Rule

No actor may use one stage to seize authority belonging to another.

Therefore:

```text
Proposer cannot self-create authority.

Reviewer cannot silently become Approver.

Implementer cannot expand Decision.

Publisher cannot alter Determination.

Institution cannot approve constitutional change to itself unilaterally.
```

---

# Delegation Rule

Decision rights may be delegated where governance permits.

Delegation must state:

- delegating authority;
- delegated role;
- scope;
- duration where applicable;
- limitations;
- accountability.

Delegation does not erase accountability.

The delegating authority remains accountable for the legitimacy of the delegation unless governance expressly establishes otherwise.

---

# Founder / Constitutional Steward Boundary

The present Satoshium project remains founder-governed at the constitutional level.

Accordingly, until superseded by a later formally adopted governance structure:

```text
Founder / Current Constitutional Steward
→ final constitutional approval authority
```

This authority applies to:

- formal Suite membership;
- institutional creation;
- merger;
- division;
- retirement;
- removal;
- successor replacement;
- institutional role reassignment;
- canonical ownership transfer;
- foundational authority changes.

It does **not** imply that the founder must personally approve every Routine institutional Procedure, correction, schema refinement, or operational decision.

The Day 1 sovereignty model remains intact.

---

# Governing Doctrine

> **Proposal may be broad. Review follows affected authority. Determination follows the governed subject. Approval follows the required governance level. Implementation follows operational responsibility. Publication follows stewardship of the authoritative surface.**

Expanded:

> **No governance role receives authority merely by participating in another stage. Decision rights remain bounded by subject ownership, change classification, and explicit delegation.**
