## Purpose

This principal artifact consolidates Day 2 of the Satoshium Suite Governance Review.

It defines how the Suite may legitimately change without allowing documentation, implementation, interoperability, or operational convenience to silently rewrite institutional architecture.

Day 2 establishes:

- what constitutes an institutional change;
- the distinction among documentation, operational, methodology, governance, canonical-object, institutional-role, and constitutional change;
- when an institution may evolve within its existing authority;
- the threshold between ordinary institutional evolution and Suite-level architectural change;
- governance for creation of a new Suite institution;
- governance for merger, division, retirement, removal, and successor replacement;
- the Aegis precedent;
- the constitutional test for Suite membership;
- the prohibition on implicit constitutional change;
- and the Governance Change Classification Model.

The closing objective is:

> **Permit institutions to evolve. Require the Suite to govern architectural change. Require formal constitutional action when the Suite itself changes.**

---

# I. Governing Day 2 Doctrine

The foundational rule is:

> **Institutions are free to evolve inside the architecture. They are not free to redefine the architecture.**

This creates the core distinction:

```text
CHANGE WITHIN SETTLED AUTHORITY
→ Institution may govern

CHANGE TO SETTLED AUTHORITY
→ Suite review required

CHANGE TO THE CONSTITUTIONAL STRUCTURE OF THE SUITE
→ Formal Suite Governance Decision required
```

---

# II. Definition of Institutional Change

An **institutional change** is:

> **A governed change that alters the formal identity, settled role, authority boundary, canonical-object ownership, constitutional position, or institutional existence of a Satoshium Suite institution.**

Institutional change is not determined by:

- implementation size;
- number of files;
- development effort;
- public visibility;
- code volume.

It is determined by whether the change alters what the institution:

- is;
- owns;
- controls;
- may decide;
- or represents constitutionally.

---

# III. Change-Type Classification Framework

The following change types are formally distinguished.

## 1. Documentation Correction

Changes the description of already-settled reality.

Examples:

- correcting stale terminology;
- fixing outdated status;
- aligning README language with current architecture;
- correcting a factual documentation error.

```text
Default classification
→ Routine
```

unless the proposed wording actually changes architecture.

---

## 2. Operational Refinement

Changes how an institution performs existing authorized work.

Examples:

- automation;
- workflow improvement;
- validation improvement;
- publication tooling;
- internal process refinement;
- UI changes;
- maintenance improvements.

```text
Default classification
→ Routine
```

unless the implementation creates new authority, ownership, or constitutional dependency.

---

## 3. Methodology Change

Changes how governed work is performed or evaluated.

It may be:

```text
Institution-specific
or
Suite-level
```

A methodology change remains below constitutional level when it preserves:

- role;
- ownership;
- authority;
- institutional sovereignty.

---

## 4. Governance Change

Changes how authority is exercised or controlled.

Examples:

- approval thresholds;
- review processes;
- delegation;
- accountability;
- change control;
- exception handling;
- reconsideration;
- governance record requirements.

A governance change may be Routine, Material, or Constitutional depending on effect.

---

## 5. Canonical-Object Change

Changes the Suite-recognized canonical object architecture.

Examples:

- adding a canonical object family;
- replacing a canonical object;
- splitting or merging canonical object families;
- transferring canonical ownership.

```text
Default classification
→ Material

Ownership transfer
→ Constitutional
```

---

## 6. Institutional-Role Change

Changes what an institution exists to do.

Examples:

```text
Beacon gains certification authority

Anchor begins issuing Trust Statements

Registry gains source authority
```

```text
Default classification
→ Constitutional
```

---

## 7. Constitutional Change

Changes the foundational architecture of the Suite.

Includes:

- membership;
- institutional existence;
- role allocation;
- canonical ownership assignment;
- authority boundaries;
- mandatory constitutional dependencies;
- merger;
- division;
- retirement;
- removal;
- successor replacement.

```text
Classification
→ Constitutional
```

---

# IV. Highest-Order Change Rule

A proposed change may contain multiple change types.

The controlling classification is:

> **The highest material effect of the proposal.**

Therefore:

```text
README edit
+
new authority claim
→ not Documentation Correction
→ Governance / Role / Constitutional Change

schema edit
+
new canonical object
→ not Operational Refinement
→ Canonical-Object Change
```

The anti-evasion rule is:

```text
Documentation
cannot conceal Architecture

Implementation
cannot conceal Authority Transfer

Methodology
cannot conceal Role Change

Governance
cannot conceal Constitutional Change

Schema
cannot conceal Canonical-Object Creation
```

# V. Existing Institution Change Authority

An existing institution may act independently when the change remains within settled authority.

## Expand Capabilities

Permitted when expansion:

- remains inside the settled role;
- preserves canonical ownership;
- does not absorb another institution's authority;
- does not create mandatory constitutional dependency.

```text
Classification
→ Routine or Material
```

depending on cross-Suite effect.

---

## Narrow Capabilities

Permitted when the institution can still perform its settled role and maintain its canonical responsibilities.

If narrowing makes the institution unable to fulfill its constitutional role:

```text
Classification
→ Material or Constitutional
```

---

## Change Procedures

Permitted when Procedures remain consistent with:

- Rules;
- Policies;
- Suite Standards;
- Suite Methodology;
- settled authority boundaries.

```text
Classification
→ Routine
```

unless new authority is created.

---

## Add Supporting Objects

Permitted when objects remain:

- noncanonical;
- subordinate;
- institution-owned;
- non-duplicative.

```text
Classification
→ Routine
```

Elevating a supporting object into canonical status:

```text
→ Material
```

or:

```text
→ Constitutional
```

if canonical ownership changes.

---

## Change Canonical Object

An institution may not unilaterally alter canonical object architecture.

```text
Canonical-object architecture change
→ Material

Canonical ownership transfer
→ Constitutional
```

---

## Change Institutional Role

An institution may clarify wording around its role.

It may not unilaterally change the role itself.

```text
Role clarification
→ Routine

Role modification
→ Constitutional
```

# VI. Threshold Between Ordinary Evolution and Suite-Level Architectural Change

The threshold is:

```text
CHANGE WITHIN SETTLED AUTHORITY
→ ordinary institutional evolution

CHANGE TO SETTLED AUTHORITY
→ Suite-level architectural change
```

A change crosses the architectural threshold when it alters:

- institutional role;
- canonical object architecture;
- canonical ownership;
- authority boundary;
- institutional sovereignty;
- constitutional dependency;
- Suite membership;
- settled Suite semantics;
- settled Reconciliation decisions.

Cross-institution impact alone is not sufficient.

Therefore:

```text
CROSS-INSTITUTION IMPACT
≠
ARCHITECTURAL CHANGE

CROSS-INSTITUTION AUTHORITY CHANGE
=
ARCHITECTURAL CHANGE
```

# VII. Governance for Creation of a New Suite Institution

A new formal Suite institution requires:

```text
Demonstrated need
Unique responsibility
Non-duplication
Authority boundary
Canonical ownership
Interoperability review
Sovereignty review
Constitutional review
Formal admission
```

The governing rule is:

```text
NEW CAPABILITY
≠
NEW INSTITUTION
```

A new institution must solve a distinct constitutional problem, not merely provide a new operational capability.

---

# VIII. Governance for Structural Institutional Change

The following are constitutional acts:

- institutional merger;
- institutional division;
- institutional retirement;
- removal from the Suite;
- successor replacement.

All require explicit treatment of:

- role;
- canonical ownership;
- authority;
- lifecycle;
- corrections;
- versioning;
- identifiers;
- interoperability;
- existing records;
- effective date;
- historical continuity.

The preservation rule is:

> **Change prospectively. Preserve retrospectively.**

And:

```text
If authority is not explicitly transferred
→ AUTHORITY DOES NOT TRANSFER
```

# XI. Prohibition on Implicit Constitutional Change

The following may never occur implicitly:

```text
new institutional authority
new canonical ownership
new institutional role
new Suite membership
new constitutional dependency
authority transfer
canonical-object reassignment
institutional creation
institutional merger
institutional division
institutional retirement
institutional replacement
```

Therefore:

```text
documentation wording
→ cannot create authority

implementation convenience
→ cannot create ownership

reference relationship
→ cannot expand jurisdiction

interoperability
→ cannot transfer authority

workflow sequence
→ cannot create hierarchy

technical dependency
→ cannot create governance authority

new tool
→ cannot create a new institution

repository restructure
→ cannot alter constitutional architecture

inactivity
→ cannot create retirement

new implementation
→ cannot create successor status
```

Any apparent change of this kind without a governance record must be classified:

```text
POTENTIAL IMPLICIT CONSTITUTIONAL CHANGE
```

# XII. Governance Change Classification Model

The Suite Governance Review establishes three governance classes:

---

## ROUTINE

### Governing Level

```text
Institution-Level
```

### Definition

A Routine change remains wholly within settled institutional authority and does not materially affect another institution or the Suite architecture.

Typical examples:

- documentation correction;
- Procedure update;
- implementation refinement;
- noncanonical supporting object;
- schema refinement preserving canonical identity;
- internal automation;
- local operational capability expansion;
- local capability narrowing that preserves institutional purpose.

### Authority

```text
Responsible Institution
```

### Suite Review

```text
Not normally required
```

### Governing Rule

> **Routine change exercises existing authority.**

---

## MATERIAL

### Governing Level

```text
Institution + Suite Review
```

### Definition

A Material change remains short of constitutional restructuring but has significant consequences for:

- interoperability;
- shared Methodology;
- cross-institution implementation;
- canonical-object architecture;
- institutional governance;
- shared Standards;
- or multiple institutions.

Typical examples:

- substantial institution-specific methodology change with cross-Suite effect;
- significant governance change;
- canonical-object architecture modification that preserves ownership;
- material change to lifecycle semantics with interoperability impact;
- major capability change near an institutional boundary;
- new supporting architecture with Suite-wide implications.

### Authority

```text
Owning Institution
+
Suite Review
```

### Suite Review Purpose

Suite review determines whether the change:

- remains inside settled authority;
- preserves canonical ownership;
- preserves institutional sovereignty;
- preserves interoperability;
- requires reclassification as Constitutional.

### Governing Rule

> **Material change may remain institution-owned, but the Suite must verify that the architecture remains intact.**

---

## CONSTITUTIONAL

### Governing Level

```text
Formal Suite Governance Decision
```

### Definition

A Constitutional change alters the foundational architecture of the Suite.

Includes:

- new formal Suite institution;
- removal of an institution;
- merger;
- division;
- retirement;
- successor replacement;
- institutional-role change;
- canonical ownership transfer;
- formal Suite membership change;
- foundational authority change;
- new mandatory constitutional dependency;
- change to institutional sovereignty.

### Authority

```text
Formal Suite Governance
```

### Required Process

At minimum:

```text
Proposal
→ Review
→ Constitutional Determination
→ Formal Decision
→ Approval
→ Publication
→ Implementation
→ Historical Preservation
```

### Governing Rule

> **Constitutional change changes the architecture itself and therefore cannot be approved solely by the institution affected.**

---

# XIII. Classification Summary

| Classification | Governing Level | Core Test | Typical Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routine | Institution-Level | Does it exercise existing authority? | Institution may act |
| Material | Institution + Suite Review | Does it materially affect shared architecture without changing the constitution? | Institution acts with Suite review |
| Constitutional | Formal Suite Governance Decision | Does it change role, ownership, membership, sovereignty, or foundational authority? | Formal constitutional decision required |

---

# XIV. Escalation Rule

A change starts at the lowest plausible classification but must be escalated if its actual effect reaches a higher level.

The escalation path is:

```text
ROUTINE
↓
MATERIAL
↓
CONSTITUTIONAL
```

A lower classification may never be used to avoid a higher one.

---

# XV. Classification Questions

Use the following decision sequence:

```text
1. Does this remain entirely within existing institutional authority?
   YES → ROUTINE candidate

2. Does it materially affect another institution, interoperability,
   shared Methodology, Standards, or canonical architecture
   without changing foundational authority?
   YES → MATERIAL

3. Does it change role, canonical ownership, Suite membership,
   institutional sovereignty, constitutional dependency,
   or institutional existence?
   YES → CONSTITUTIONAL
```

If uncertain between Routine and Material:

```text
prefer Material review
```

If uncertain whether authority, ownership, role, or membership changes:

```text
escalate to Constitutional review
```

# XVI. Day 2 Constitutional Doctrine

Day 2 establishes the following constitutional doctrine:

> **The Suite may evolve without rewriting itself. Institutions may improve extensively within settled authority. Material change may require shared review. Constitutional change requires formal Suite Governance.**

Expanded:

> **Routine change belongs to the institution. Material change belongs to the institution with Suite review. Constitutional change belongs to formal Suite Governance. No lower-level process may silently create authority, ownership, membership, or institutional identity.**
