## Purpose

This record defines the governance required when an existing Satoshium Suite institution is:

- merged with another institution;
- divided into multiple institutions;
- retired;
- removed from the formal Suite;
- or replaced by a successor institution.

Each of these actions changes the constitutional structure of the Suite.

They therefore require formal Suite-level governance and may not occur through ordinary institutional evolution, documentation edits, repository restructuring, implementation convenience, or de facto operational practice.

---

# Governing Principle

> **Institutional existence is a constitutional matter. No formal Suite institution may be merged, divided, retired, removed, or replaced without an explicit Suite Governance decision that preserves authority, canonical identity, historical continuity, and interoperability.**

The baseline presumption is:

```text
INSTITUTIONAL CONTINUITY
→ PRESUMED

STRUCTURAL CHANGE
→ MUST BE JUSTIFIED
```

The burden is on the proposed change to demonstrate why the existing institutional structure is no longer coherent or sufficient.

---

# Common Constitutional Requirements

Before any merger, division, retirement, removal, or replacement may occur, the governance record must establish:

```text
1. Reason for change
2. Constitutional classification
3. Current institutional role
4. Current canonical object ownership
5. Current authority boundaries
6. Current lifecycle / correction / versioning responsibilities
7. Existing interoperability relationships
8. Proposed future allocation of every responsibility
9. Proposed future allocation of every canonical object
10. Identifier and historical-continuity treatment
11. Effect on existing production records
12. Effect on references and provenance
13. Effect on Suite Standards / Methodology / Interoperability
14. Effective date
15. Formal approval
16. Public documentation
17. Historical preservation
```

No responsibility or canonical object may become ownerless through constitutional restructuring.

No authority may transfer implicitly.

# 1. Institutional Merger

## Definition

An **Institutional Merger** combines two or more formal Suite institutions into a single formal institution.

Example:

```text
Institution A
+
Institution B
→
Merged Institution C
```

A merger changes:

- formal Suite membership;
- institutional identity;
- role allocation;
- canonical ownership;
- authority boundaries;
- interoperability relationships.

Therefore:

```text
Institutional Merger
→ CONSTITUTIONAL CHANGE
```

---

## Merger Justification

A merger must demonstrate that maintaining separate institutions is no longer architecturally coherent.

Acceptable grounds may include:

- responsibilities have become genuinely inseparable;
- the distinction no longer produces meaningful independent authority;
- continued separation creates unresolved constitutional contradiction;
- a formally reviewed architecture demonstrates that one institution is no longer independently institution-worthy.

The following are insufficient by themselves:

- reducing repositories;
- simplifying navigation;
- reducing development workload;
- reducing the number of applications;
- branding convenience;
- shared implementation infrastructure.

---

## Required Merger Determinations

The merger record must determine:

### Institutional Identity

```text
Which institution survives?

OR

Is an entirely new institutional identity created?
```

### Role

Every pre-merger role must be:

```text
retained
reallocated
retired
or explicitly superseded
```

### Canonical Objects

Every canonical object family must be assigned explicitly.

A merger does **not** automatically mean the object families must merge.

Example:

```text
Institutional merger
≠
automatic canonical-object merger
```

The merged institution may legitimately retain multiple independently governed canonical object families if formally approved.

### Existing Records

Existing objects remain historically attributable to the institution that created them.

They must not be retroactively rewritten as though the merged institution originally created them.

### Identifiers

Existing identifiers remain valid historical identities unless a formal migration rule explicitly provides otherwise.

### Authority

All transferred authority must be enumerated.

Nothing transfers merely because the institutions merge organizationally.

---

## Merger Doctrine

> **Merge institutions only when their constitutional responsibilities have become inseparable—not merely because their implementations are related.**

# 2. Institutional Division

## Definition

An **Institutional Division** splits one formal Suite institution into two or more formal institutions.

Example:

```text
Institution A
→
Institution B
+
Institution C
```

A division changes:

- formal membership;
- responsibility allocation;
- canonical ownership;
- authority;
- institutional identity;
- interoperability.

Therefore:

```text
Institutional Division
→ CONSTITUTIONAL CHANGE
```

---

## Division Justification

A division must demonstrate that one institution now contains multiple durable responsibilities that require independent governance.

The review must show that:

- the responsibilities are genuinely distinct;
- each proposed institution is institution-worthy;
- each has clear authority;
- canonical ownership can be cleanly allocated;
- separation improves constitutional clarity rather than merely implementation organization.

A large institution is not automatically a candidate for division.

---

## Required Division Determinations

The decision must establish:

```text
Original role
→ allocation across successor institutions

Original canonical objects
→ allocation across successor institutions

Rules / Policies / Procedures
→ inheritance or replacement

Lifecycle authority
→ allocation

Correction authority
→ allocation

Versioning authority
→ allocation

Existing records
→ continued historical ownership

New records
→ prospective ownership

Interoperability
→ revised relationships
```

---

## Existing Object Rule

A division must not retroactively falsify historical ownership.

If the original institution created an object:

```text
historical creator
→ original institution
```

even if future maintenance authority is formally transferred to a successor.

The governance record must distinguish:

```text
ORIGINAL AUTHORITY
```

from:

```text
SUCCESSOR MAINTENANCE AUTHORITY
```

where applicable.

---

## Division Doctrine

> **Divide an institution only when its responsibilities have become constitutionally distinct enough to require independent authority—not merely because its implementation has become complex.**

---

# 3. Institutional Retirement

## Definition

**Institutional Retirement** is the governed cessation of an institution as an active formal Suite institution while preserving its historical existence, records, identifiers, decisions, and constitutional history.

Retirement means:

```text
institution no longer performs new active institutional work
```

It does **not** mean:

```text
institution never existed
```

or:

```text
institutional history is deleted
```

---

## Retirement Conditions

Retirement may be appropriate when:

- the institutional responsibility is no longer needed;
- the function has legitimately ceased;
- the role has been constitutionally reallocated;
- a successor institution has been approved;
- the institution has become structurally obsolete.

---

## Retirement Requirements

The retirement decision must determine:

- effective retirement date;
- disposition of canonical objects;
- maintenance responsibility for existing records;
- correction responsibility for historical records;
- versioning limits after retirement;
- public status representation;
- archive location;
- reference resolution;
- interoperability treatment;
- whether a successor exists.

---

## Retirement State

The preferred constitutional treatment is:

```text
ACTIVE INSTITUTION
→ RETIRED INSTITUTION
```

not:

```text
ACTIVE INSTITUTION
→ DELETED
```

Retirement preserves institutional identity as history.

---

## Historical Records

Published canonical objects created before retirement remain valid within their historical context unless separately corrected, superseded, or withdrawn under applicable governance.

Retirement alone does not invalidate prior objects.

---

## Retirement Doctrine

> **Retirement ends future institutional operation; it does not erase institutional history.**

---

# 4. Removal from the Suite

## Definition

**Removal from the Suite** ends an institution's formal Suite membership.

Removal differs from retirement.

```text
RETIREMENT
→ institution ceases active institutional operation

REMOVAL
→ institution ceases formal Suite membership
```

An institution could theoretically:

```text
remain operational externally
but no longer be a formal Suite institution
```

Accordingly, removal is primarily a membership action.

---

## Grounds for Removal

Removal requires formal constitutional justification.

Potential grounds may include:

- the institution no longer satisfies the Suite membership standard;
- its responsibility no longer belongs inside the Suite;
- its role has been reclassified as external, service, subsystem, or other non-institutional status;
- constitutional reorganization places the function outside the formal Suite.

Removal must not be used casually to resolve ordinary operational disagreement.

---

## Removal Requirements

The governance decision must determine:

```text
new classification
→ External / Service / Subsystem / Retired / other

canonical object treatment
→ preserved / transferred / closed

authority treatment
→ retained externally / transferred / terminated

existing reference treatment
→ preserved

interoperability treatment
→ revised

Suite documentation
→ updated prospectively

historical Suite membership
→ preserved
```

---

## No Retroactive Erasure

If an institution was formerly a Suite member, historical documentation may accurately state that fact.

Removal changes current membership.

It does not rewrite past membership.

---

## Removal Doctrine

> **Removal changes constitutional membership prospectively; it does not erase historical membership or silently reassign institutional authority.**

---

# 5. Replacement by a Successor Institution

## Definition

**Successor Replacement** occurs when an existing institution is formally superseded by a new institution that assumes some or all of its responsibilities prospectively.

Example:

```text
Institution A
→ retired / removed / superseded

Institution B
→ formally admitted successor
```

Replacement is not merely a rename.

A true successor relationship requires a constitutional determination.

---

## Successor Requirements

The governance record must establish:

- why succession is required;
- whether the successor is genuinely a new institution;
- which responsibilities transfer;
- which responsibilities do not transfer;
- which canonical objects transfer prospectively;
- how existing objects remain attributed;
- which Rules / Policies / Procedures are inherited;
- which governance decisions remain applicable;
- how references resolve;
- how historical lineage is expressed.

---

## Successor Does Not Rewrite History

A successor institution may acquire future responsibility.

It does not become the historical creator of predecessor objects.

Therefore:

```text
PREDECESSOR OBJECT
→ remains historically a predecessor object

SUCCESSOR AUTHORITY
→ may govern future maintenance if explicitly transferred
```

---

## Identifier Continuity

Existing predecessor identifiers should remain durable.

The successor may receive a new identifier family if its institutional identity or canonical object model changes.

Automatic identifier rewriting is prohibited.

---

## Successor Relationship

The relationship must be explicit:

```text
successor_of
predecessor_of
supersedes_institution
or equivalent governed relationship
```

The relationship must not be inferred from chronology alone.

---

## Replacement Doctrine

> **A successor may inherit responsibility, but it does not inherit history by rewriting it.**

# Canonical Object Continuity Rules

For every structural change:

```text
Existing canonical identity
→ PRESERVE

Historical creator
→ PRESERVE

Existing identifier
→ PRESERVE unless formally superseded

Existing provenance
→ PRESERVE

Existing governance history
→ PRESERVE

Future authority
→ ASSIGN EXPLICITLY

Future canonical ownership
→ ASSIGN EXPLICITLY
```

No constitutional restructuring permits silent mutation of historical identity.

---

# Authority Transfer Rule

Structural change may require authority transfer.

That transfer must specify:

```text
FROM
→ predecessor institution

TO
→ successor / merged / divided institution

SUBJECT
→ exact responsibility or canonical object

EFFECTIVE DATE
→ explicit

HISTORICAL TREATMENT
→ preserved
```

If the transfer is not explicitly authorized:

```text
AUTHORITY DOES NOT TRANSFER
```

---

# Interoperability Continuity Rule

Before structural change becomes effective, Suite Governance must determine whether existing references continue to resolve.

The plan must address:

- canonical identifiers;
- public URLs where relevant;
- machine-readable references;
- relationship semantics;
- provenance;
- lifecycle references;
- correction references;
- version references;
- external consumers.

The Suite should prefer durable resolution over wholesale rewriting of historical records.

---

# Prohibited Structural Practices

The following are prohibited:

- deleting an institution from current documentation without a governance decision;
- merging repositories and treating that as institutional merger;
- creating two applications and treating that as institutional division;
- ceasing development and treating that as constitutional retirement;
- renaming an institution and treating that as successor replacement;
- moving files and treating that as canonical ownership transfer;
- allowing authority to migrate through practice rather than decision;
- rewriting historical objects to reflect successor ownership;
- breaking durable references without a governed continuity plan.

---

# Constitutional Preservation Principle

The Suite must preserve two truths simultaneously:

```text
THE ARCHITECTURE MAY CHANGE
```

and:

```text
THE HISTORY OF THE FORMER ARCHITECTURE REMAINS TRUE
```

This produces the governing preservation rule:

> **Change prospectively. Preserve retrospectively.**

---

# Governing Doctrine

> **Institutional restructuring must reallocate future authority explicitly while preserving past authority accurately.**

Expanded:

> **Merger, division, retirement, removal, and successor replacement are constitutional acts. Each requires explicit treatment of role, canonical ownership, authority, identifiers, existing records, interoperability, effective date, and historical continuity. The Suite may change its institutional structure, but it may not rewrite the history of the structure that previously existed.**
