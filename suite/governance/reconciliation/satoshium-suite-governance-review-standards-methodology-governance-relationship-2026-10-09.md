# Source-Derived Findings

## 1. Suite Standards

The Suite Standards documentation defines the Standards surface as:

```text
the canonical rule layer used across the Satoshium Suite
```

Standards establish what the Suite:

- expects;
- recognizes;
- evaluates;
- records;
- publishes;
- preserves;
- exchanges;
- and interprets across institutional implementations.

The Standards documentation expressly states:

```text
Standards define what is expected.

Methodology defines how expectations are implemented.

Institutions perform their own governed operations.

Canonical objects remain owned by their originating institutions.
```

It also states that Standards do not replace institutional authority.

Therefore, the source-supported role of Suite Standards is:

> **Suite Standards define shared expectations and rule boundaries.**

---

## 2. Suite Methodology

The Suite Methodology documentation defines Methodology as the repeatable processes used to apply Suite Standards consistently, transparently, and objectively.

Its central distinction is:

```text
Standards define what must be achieved.

Methodology defines how those standards are implemented,
evaluated,
and maintained.
```

The same source expressly establishes an architectural boundary:

> The Suite-level Methodology surface does not replace institution-specific governance, methodology, Validation, Conformance, lifecycle, publication, or production architecture.

It further states that where an institution has adopted more specific governed procedures, those institution-specific requirements govern that institution's operation.

Therefore, the source-supported role of Suite Methodology is:

> **Suite Methodology defines shared implementation principles and repeatable methods without replacing institutional operating authority.**

## 3. Suite Governance — What the Current Source Actually Establishes

The supplied current Governance Standard is located at:

```text
/suite/standards/governance/
```

Its stated purpose is:

```text
to define the policies,
responsibilities,
review processes,
and change controls
that guide the evolution of standards
throughout the Satoshium Suite
```

The page identifies four principal governance concerns:

- Standards Governance;
- Change Management;
- Version Control;
- Transparency.

It further states that governance protects the integrity of standards by ensuring they evolve through transparent, documented, and repeatable processes.

### Important Source Boundary

The current public source does **not** establish a separate, fully developed Suite Governance layer parallel to Standards and Methodology.

What it establishes directly is:

```text
Governance Standard
→ governance of official Satoshium standards
→ ownership / stewardship / responsibility
→ change management
→ version control
→ transparency
```

Accordingly, this review must distinguish:

```text
SOURCE-ESTABLISHED CURRENT STATE
→ Governance Standard governs the evolution of Suite Standards

CURRENT GOVERNANCE REVIEW
→ may determine whether broader Suite Governance should be formalized
```

The broader constitutional governance model being developed in this review must therefore be understood as a **Governance Review determination**, not something already fully established by the current Governance Standard page.

This distinction prevents the review from retroactively reading a broader authority into a source that presently speaks primarily to standards governance.

# What Suite Standards May Do

Suite Standards may:

- establish shared expectations;
- define common terms and boundaries;
- define requirements that institutions must respect;
- establish shared expectations for governance, interoperability, schemas, terminology, versioning, evidence, and other Suite-wide concerns.

Suite Standards may not:

- operate institution-owned canonical objects;
- make institution-specific operational decisions;
- absorb institutional authority;
- replace institution-specific Rules, Policies, or Procedures where those remain compatible with Suite-wide expectations.

---

# What Suite Methodology May Do

Suite Methodology may:

- define shared implementation principles;
- define repeatable Suite-level methodological approaches;
- provide common process expectations;
- support consistency across institutions.

Suite Methodology may not:

- replace institution-specific governance;
- replace institution-specific Validation or Conformance;
- replace institution-specific lifecycle or publication architecture;
- substitute itself for production procedures already governed by an institution.

---

# What the Current Governance Standard May Do

Based strictly on the supplied current source, the Governance Standard may:

- define ownership and stewardship for official Suite Standards;
- govern how Standards are proposed;
- govern how Standards are reviewed;
- govern how Standards are approved;
- govern how Standards are published;
- govern Standard versioning;
- govern supersession and preservation of Standards;
- require governance decisions to be documented and reviewable.

The current page does not yet establish detailed procedures for broader constitutional governance of:

- Suite membership;
- institutional role changes;
- canonical-object reassignment;
- institution-level operational decisions;
- cross-institution authority disputes.

Those broader matters are being examined by the present Governance Review.

---

# What Institution-Specific Rules May Do

Institution-specific Rules may:

- establish binding requirements within the institution's settled domain;
- define object-specific requirements;
- define identity rules;
- define authority boundaries;
- define lifecycle constraints;
- define Validation requirements;
- define institutional eligibility or admission requirements where applicable.

Institution-specific Rules may not:

- redefine Suite-wide Standards;
- change another institution's authority;
- redefine another institution's canonical object;
- override settled Suite architecture.

---

# What Institution-Specific Policies May Do

Institution-specific Policies may:

- interpret and operationalize institutional Rules;
- define decision criteria;
- define operating conditions;
- define institution-specific governance expectations;
- guide institution-owned lifecycle, correction, publication, or maintenance decisions.

Policies may not silently become Suite-wide Standards.

---

# What Institution-Specific Procedures May Do

Institution-specific Procedures may:

- define repeatable operational steps;
- turn Rules and Policies into executable work;
- specify institutional production sequences;
- define review, Validation, publication, maintenance, correction, and other operational actions.

Procedures may not create new constitutional authority.

Operational procedure remains below institutional authority boundaries.

# Important Architectural Clarification

A previously convenient stack such as:

```text
Suite Standards
↓
Suite Methodology
↓
Suite Governance
↓
Institutional Governance
```

is **too simple to treat as source-established architecture**.

The current sources support a more nuanced relationship:

- Standards are the Suite-wide rule layer.
- Methodology is the Suite-wide implementation-method layer.
- The current Governance Standard governs the evolution of Standards.
- Institution-specific Rules, Policies, and Procedures govern institution-owned operations.
- The broader Suite Governance model is now being formalized through this Governance Review.

Therefore:

> **Governance should not be treated as merely another linear layer beneath Methodology. It is a control function that governs change and accountability across the relevant governed surface.**

This is a clarification, not a reopening of institutional architecture.
