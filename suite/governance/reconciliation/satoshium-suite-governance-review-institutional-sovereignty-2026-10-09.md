## Purpose

This review tests whether each formal Satoshium Suite institution retains a distinct and governable sphere of authority consistent with:

- its settled institutional role;
- its settled canonical object ownership;
- its own lifecycle and publication responsibilities;
- the authority of source institutions it references;
- and the completed Suite Interoperability model.

The question for each institution is:

> **Does the institution retain authority over its own role and canonical objects without improperly absorbing the authority of other institutions?**

The source basis for this review is limited to the supplied current public Suite and institutional surfaces.

---

## Suite-Level Sovereignty Standard

The current Suite Interoperability surface provides the clearest common sovereignty rule:

```text
Each institution preserves its own canonical object,
identifier,
lifecycle,
governance,
and authority.

Reference does not transfer authority.
```

The Suite Status surface likewise reports status institution by institution because each formal Suite institution has its own:

```text
canonical object
authority
lifecycle
production evidence
release condition
```

The Suite Methodology surface preserves the same boundary by stating that shared methodology does not replace institution-specific authority and that more specific governed institutional procedures govern institutional operation.

Accordingly, institutional sovereignty is evaluated using five questions:

1. Is the institution's role distinct?
2. Is its canonical object ownership distinct?
3. Does it retain authority over its own object lifecycle and institutional decisions?
4. Does it preserve the authority of referenced or participating institutions?
5. Does the current source material reveal any sovereignty collision requiring architectural reopening?

# 1. Atlas — Institutional Sovereignty Review

## Source-Derived Position

Atlas is identified as the Suite institution for:

```text
Authoritative Intelligence
```

Its canonical object is:

```text
Jurisdiction Intelligence Package
```

The Atlas surface states that it organizes jurisdiction intelligence and supports the other Suite institutions through structured intelligence.

The same surface also distinguishes Navigator from Atlas by stating that Navigator may structure exploration and workflows without assuming Atlas authority.

The Suite Interoperability surface confirms:

```text
Atlas
→ Authoritative Intelligence
→ Jurisdiction Intelligence Package
```

and further confirms that Atlas retains authority over the underlying jurisdiction intelligence when downstream Suite objects participate in production relationships.

## Sovereignty Boundary

Atlas governs:

- authoritative jurisdiction intelligence;
- Jurisdiction Intelligence Packages;
- Atlas-owned evidence, signal, metadata, framework, and source layers;
- Atlas-specific representation and maintenance of those intelligence packages.

Atlas does not gain authority over:

- Navigator Workflow Definitions;
- Certification Packages or certification decisions;
- SREGs;
- Chronicle Entries;
- Integrity References;
- Discovery Signals;
- Attestations;
- Trust Statements.

Likewise, downstream use of Atlas intelligence does not transfer Atlas authority to another institution.

## Determination

```text
Distinct role
→ YES

Distinct canonical ownership
→ YES

Authority preserved across downstream use
→ YES

Cross-institution sovereignty collision
→ NONE IDENTIFIED
```

# Cross-Institution Review Finding

Across the supplied sources, the sovereignty model is unusually consistent.

The institutions repeatedly preserve the same pattern:

```text
Own the institutional action.
Own the institution's canonical object.
Own the institution-specific lifecycle.
Own the institution-specific publication decision.
Own institution-specific correction and versioning.

Do not absorb source authority.
Do not absorb neighboring institutional authority.
Do not interpret reference as ownership.
```

The completed Interoperability surface reinforces this explicitly:

```text
Each institution preserves:
→ its own canonical object
→ its own identifier
→ its own lifecycle
→ its own governance
→ its own authority
```

No supplied source presents one of the eight institutions as a meta-institution with general authority over the other seven.
