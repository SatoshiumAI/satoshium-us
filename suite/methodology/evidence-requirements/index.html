Evidence Requirements

Evidence Requirements define the minimum information necessary to support objective, transparent, and repeatable certification decisions.

Every certification should be supported by sufficient evidence to allow independent review and long-term verification.

Identity Evidence

Evidence establishing the identity of the certification target, issuer, or responsible authority.

Primary Evidence

Original records, official documents, first-hand observations, or authoritative sources directly supporting certification.

Supporting Evidence

Supplementary documentation providing additional context, corroboration, or clarification.

Independent Verification

Evidence originating from independent sources capable of confirming or strengthening certification confidence.

Evidence Integrity

Evidence should remain authentic, complete, unaltered, and suitable for long-term preservation.

Evidence Traceability

Every evidence item should be traceable to its source, allowing future reviewers to verify authenticity.

Evidence Sufficiency

Certification decisions should be based upon sufficient evidence rather than the minimum evidence available.

Evidence Preservation

Evidence should remain available for future review, auditing, appeals, and historical reference whenever practical.

Methodology Role

Evidence Requirements define what information is necessary before certification may proceed. They establish the minimum evidentiary foundation upon which evaluation criteria, certification logic, scoring, and trust determinations are applied.

Strong certification begins with strong evidence.
Evidence preserves truth long after memory fades.
