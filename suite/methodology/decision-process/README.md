# Decision Process

**Path:** `/suite/methodology/decision-process/`  
**Surface:** Satoshium Suite · Methodology  
**Status:** Current repository documentation

## Purpose

This directory documents the Satoshium Suite **Decision Process** methodology surface.

The Decision Process describes how governed certification inputs are brought together to produce documented, reviewable certification determinations.

The public page for this directory is:

- `index.html`

This README documents the repository role and architectural boundaries of the directory. It does not replace the public presentation in `index.html`.

## Methodology Role

The Decision Process is part of the Satoshium Suite Methodology.

Its role is to explain how applicable standards, eligible evidence, evaluation results, scoring, and certification logic participate in a governed certification determination.

The Decision Process should support outcomes that are:

- understandable;
- repeatable;
- transparent;
- attributable;
- reviewable; and
- reproducible under the same governing conditions.

The Decision Process does not itself create institutional authority. Certification authority remains with Satoshium Certifier under the established Suite architecture.

## Decision Inputs

A certification decision may consume governed inputs defined by the applicable standards, methodology, evidence requirements, evaluation process, scoring model, and certification logic.

Each input retains its own meaning and authority boundary.

In particular:

- eligibility is not an evaluation outcome;
- validation is not evaluation;
- validation is not eligibility;
- validation is not conformance;
- evidence validity does not itself determine certification;
- scoring does not independently create a certification determination; and
- reference to another governed object does not transfer authority over that object.

## Decision Reasoning

Decision reasoning should be sufficiently documented so that an independent reviewer can understand how the governed inputs produced the certification determination.

The reasoning should preserve:

- the applicable standards and methodology;
- the evidence considered;
- the evaluation result;
- any governed scoring;
- the certification logic applied;
- the resulting determination; and
- the provenance necessary to review the decision.

A certification decision should not appear arbitrary or depend upon undocumented reasoning.

## Human Review

The methodology may provide for documented human review where professional judgment, exception handling, appeals, or procedural review are required.

Human review remains governed by the applicable standards and methodology.

Human involvement does not permit undocumented alteration of canonical records, authority boundaries, or lifecycle semantics.

## Automated Review

Where the governing methodology permits deterministic execution, software may apply certification logic consistently.

Automation is an implementation mechanism.

It does not independently acquire Certifier authority, and software execution must preserve the same evidentiary, provenance, relationship, and lifecycle boundaries that apply to human review.

## AI Assistance

Artificial intelligence may assist with functions such as evidence review, summarization, or recommendation where permitted by the governing methodology.

AI assistance must remain subordinate to the governed certification process.

An AI-generated recommendation is not, by itself, a certification determination.

## Appeals and Corrections

The Decision Process may support review when additional evidence, corrections, or procedural concerns are presented.

Appeals and corrections must preserve the established historical and lifecycle disciplines:

- correction is not deletion;
- correction is not automatically a new version;
- correction does not itself determine a versioning decision;
- supersession is not mutation; and
- changed canonical conclusions require appropriately governed canonical treatment.

Historical records should remain historically accurate.

## Decision Documentation

Every certification determination should contain or be accompanied by sufficient documentation to support independent review.

Decision documentation should make clear:

- what was evaluated;
- what evidence was used;
- which standards and methodology applied;
- what logic was executed;
- what determination resulted; and
- which institution owns the authoritative record.

Documentation does not transfer authority.

## Relationship to Certifier

Satoshium Certifier is the Suite institution responsible for certification operations and the Certification Package.

This Decision Process methodology surface supports that institution by documenting the reasoning framework under which certification determinations are reached.

The methodology does not replace Certifier and does not independently issue certification objects.

## Relationship to Other Suite Institutions

Other Suite institutions may reference certification records or related outputs according to their own canonical responsibilities.

Those references must preserve institutional boundaries.

In particular:

- Registry reference does not make Registry the certification authority.
- Chronicle records chronology rather than certification authority.
- Anchor establishes integrity references rather than certification authority.
- Beacon provides discovery signals and discovery metadata.
- Attestor may consume eligible governed certification objects as referenced inputs while preserving Certifier as the source authority for those objects.

Reference does not equal derivation, support, identity, or authority transfer.

## Repository Expectations

Changes to this directory should preserve:

1. the distinction between methodology and institutional authority;
2. the distinction between inputs, evaluation, decision, lifecycle state, and publication;
3. the distinction between certification determinations and later trust-related conclusions;
4. evidence and provenance boundaries;
5. documented and reproducible reasoning;
6. historical accuracy; and
7. consistency with the governing Suite standards, methodology, and Certifier architecture.

Changes that would redefine certification outcomes, lifecycle semantics, trust relationships, or institutional authority require the appropriate governed architectural review rather than a documentation-only edit.

## Governing Principle

**A certification determination should be explainable, reviewable, reproducible, and governed by the methodology that produced it.**
