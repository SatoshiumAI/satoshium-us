# Certification Decision Process **Path:** `/suite/methodology/decision-process/` **Surface:** Satoshium Suite · Methodology **Status:** Current repository documentation ## Purpose This directory documents the Satoshium Suite **Certification Decision Process** methodology surface. The Certification Decision Process describes how governed **Certifier / certification-domain inputs** are brought together to produce documented, reviewable certification determinations. It is not the governance decision process for the Satoshium Suite and does not govern Governance Decision Records (GDRs). The public page for this directory is: - `index.html` This README documents the repository role and architectural boundaries of the directory. It does not replace the public presentation in `index.html`. ## Methodology Role The Certification Decision Process is part of the Satoshium Suite Methodology as a **certification-specific methodology surface**. Its role is to explain how applicable standards, eligible evidence, evaluation results, explicitly adopted scoring, and certification logic participate in a governed Certifier determination. The Decision Process should support outcomes that are: - understandable; - repeatable; - transparent; - attributable; - reviewable; and - reproducible under the same governing conditions. The Certification Decision Process does not itself create institutional authority. Certification authority remains with Satoshium Certifier under the established Suite architecture. It also does not create Suite Governance authority, institutional approval authority, or a Governance Decision Record. ## Scope Boundary This methodology surface governs **certification determinations only**. ```text Certification Decision Process → Certifier / certification-domain methodology Governance Decision Lifecycle → Suite Governance Governance Decision Record (GDR) → formal governance decision record ``` Therefore: ```text CERTIFICATION DECISION ≠ GOVERNANCE DECISION CERTIFICATION DETERMINATION ≠ GDR CERTIFIER REVIEW ≠ SUITE GOVERNANCE REVIEW CERTIFIER APPROVAL ≠ CONSTITUTIONAL APPROVAL ``` A certification determination may be important across the Suite, but it does not become a Suite Governance decision merely because other institutions reference, register, preserve, discover, or evaluate it. ## Decision Inputs A certification decision may consume governed inputs defined by the applicable standards, methodology, evidence requirements, evaluation process, explicitly adopted scoring model, and certification logic. Each input retains its own meaning and authority boundary. In particular: - eligibility is not an evaluation outcome; - validation is not evaluation; - validation is not eligibility; - validation is not conformance; - evidence validity does not itself determine certification; - scoring does not independently create a certification determination; and - reference to another governed object does not transfer authority over that object. ## Decision Reasoning Decision reasoning should be sufficiently documented so that an independent reviewer can understand how the governed inputs produced the certification determination. The reasoning should preserve: - the applicable standards and methodology; - the evidence considered; - the evaluation result; - any governed scoring; - the certification logic applied; - the resulting determination; and - the provenance necessary to review the decision. A certification decision should not appear arbitrary or depend upon undocumented reasoning. Within this surface, "decision" means a **Certifier certification determination**. It does not mean a Suite Governance determination or constitutional approval. ## Human Review The methodology may provide for documented human review where professional judgment, exception handling, appeals, or procedural review are required. Human review remains governed by the applicable certification standards and methodology. Human review does not become Suite Governance review merely because the determination may have cross-institution consequences. Human involvement does not permit undocumented alteration of canonical records, authority boundaries, or lifecycle semantics. ## Automated Review Where the governing methodology permits deterministic execution, software may apply certification logic consistently. Automation is an implementation mechanism. It does not independently acquire Certifier authority, governance authority, or approval authority. Software execution must preserve the same evidentiary, provenance, relationship, lifecycle, and institutional boundaries that apply to human review. ## AI Assistance Artificial intelligence may assist with functions such as evidence review, summarization, or recommendation where permitted by the governing methodology. AI assistance must remain subordinate to the governed certification process. An AI-generated recommendation is not, by itself, a certification determination. ## Appeals and Corrections The Certification Decision Process may support certification-domain review when additional evidence, corrections, or procedural concerns are presented. Appeals and corrections must preserve the established historical and lifecycle disciplines: - correction is not deletion; - correction is not automatically a new version; - correction does not itself determine a versioning decision; - supersession is not mutation; and - changed canonical conclusions require appropriately governed canonical treatment. Historical records should remain historically accurate. ## Decision Documentation Every certification determination should contain or be accompanied by sufficient documentation to support independent review. Decision documentation should make clear: - what was evaluated; - what evidence was used; - which standards and methodology applied; - what logic was executed; - what determination resulted; and - which institution owns the authoritative record. Documentation does not transfer authority. ## Relationship to Certifier Satoshium Certifier is the Suite institution responsible for certification operations and the Certification Package. This Certification Decision Process methodology surface supports that institution by documenting the reasoning framework under which certification determinations are reached. The methodology does not replace Certifier and does not independently issue certification objects. ## Relationship to Suite Governance This surface must remain distinct from the governance lifecycle established during the Satoshium Suite Governance Review. Suite Governance follows its own lifecycle: ```text Proposal → Classification → Review → Determination → Approval → Governance Decision Record (GDR) → Publication → Implementation → Preservation ``` That lifecycle governs governance decisions. The Certification Decision Process governs Certifier determinations. ```text Certification Decision Process ≠ Governance Decision Lifecycle Certification Determination ≠ Governance Determination Certification Record ≠ Governance Decision Record ``` If a certification-related matter proposes a change to institutional role, canonical ownership, Suite membership, authority allocation, or another constitutional boundary, the matter must leave this certification methodology surface and enter the applicable Suite Governance / Architecture Reconciliation process. ## Relationship to Other Suite Institutions Other Suite institutions may reference certification records or related outputs according to their own canonical responsibilities. Those interactions do not make the Certification Decision Process a cross-institution decision authority. Those references must preserve institutional boundaries. In particular: - Registry reference does not make Registry the certification authority. - Chronicle records chronology rather than certification authority. - Anchor establishes integrity references rather than certification authority. - Beacon provides governed Discovery Signals; Discovery Metadata remains supporting Beacon structure. - Attestor may consume eligible governed certification objects as referenced inputs while preserving Certifier as the source authority for those objects. Reference does not equal derivation, support, identity, or authority transfer. ## Repository Expectations Changes to this directory should preserve: 1. the distinction between methodology and institutional authority; 2. the distinction between inputs, evaluation, decision, lifecycle state, and publication; 3. the distinction between certification determinations and later trust-related conclusions; 4. evidence and provenance boundaries; 5. documented and reproducible reasoning; 6. historical accuracy; and 7. consistency with the governing Suite standards, methodology, and Certifier architecture; 8. explicit certification-domain scope; 9. separation of certification determinations from governance determinations; 10. separation of Certifier review from Suite Governance review; 11. separation of certification records from Governance Decision Records (GDRs); and 12. escalation of constitutional matters to Suite Governance / Architecture Reconciliation. Changes that would redefine certification outcomes, lifecycle semantics, trust relationships, or institutional authority require the appropriate governed architectural review rather than a documentation-only edit. ## Governing Principle **A certification determination should be explainable, reviewable, reproducible, and governed by the certification methodology that produced it; it is not a Suite Governance decision.**
