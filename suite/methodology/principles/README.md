# Methodology Principles

**Path:** `/suite/methodology/principles/`  
**Surface:** Satoshium Suite · Methodology  
**Status:** Current repository documentation

## Purpose

This directory documents the Satoshium Suite **Methodology Principles** surface.

Methodology Principles establish the enduring philosophical foundation beneath Suite methodologies. They guide how processes are designed, evidence is handled, evaluations are performed, and certification decisions are reached.

The public page for this directory is:

- `index.html`

This README documents the repository role and architectural boundaries of the directory. It does not replace the public presentation in `index.html`.

## Methodology Role

The Methodology Principles sit beneath individual methodologies and implementation procedures.

Their role is to provide durable guidance so that Suite processes remain:

- evidence-based;
- transparent;
- repeatable;
- consistent;
- reviewable;
- historically disciplined; and
- capable of documented improvement.

Principles guide methodology. They do not independently create institutional authority, certification outcomes, lifecycle states, publication states, or trust statements.

## Current Principles

The current public methodology surface identifies the following principles.

### Evidence Before Assertion

Conclusions should be supported by verifiable evidence rather than opinion or unsupported assumption.

Evidence must retain provenance and source authority.

Reference does not transfer authority.

### Transparency Before Authority

Processes should be understandable and reviewable rather than dependent upon hidden or unexplained authority.

Transparency does not erase institutional authority boundaries. It makes the basis for governed action visible and reviewable.

### Repeatability Over Subjectivity

Equivalent evidence evaluated under equivalent governing conditions should produce equivalent outcomes.

Repeatability should preserve context, provenance, applicable versions, and the methodology in force at the time of evaluation.

### Truth Before Trust

The current public methodology surface states that trust should arise from faithful application of truth rather than declaration alone.

Within the broader Suite architecture, this principle must be interpreted carefully so that methodology does not claim universal truth determination and does not collapse evidence, validity, evaluation outcome, or Attestor Trust Statements into a single concept.

### Consistency Across the Suite

Suite institutions and supporting systems should apply shared standards and methodologies consistently while preserving their distinct institutional responsibilities.

Consistency does not imply identical authority or interchangeable canonical objects.

### Continuous Improvement

Methodologies should evolve through documented review while preserving historical integrity.

Improvement should not silently rewrite prior historical states, canonical records, or governed decisions.

## Authority and Relationship Discipline

These principles must be applied consistently with the Suite's established authority and relationship boundaries.

In particular:

- authority is not eligibility;
- eligibility is not an evaluation outcome;
- validation is not evaluation;
- validation is not eligibility;
- validation is not conformance;
- valid does not mean true;
- valid does not mean supported;
- valid does not mean published;
- evaluation outcome is not a Trust Statement;
- canonical creation is not lifecycle activation;
- lifecycle activation is not publication;
- reference does not equal derivation;
- reference does not equal support; and
- reference does not transfer authority.

Methodology principles guide governed behavior without superseding these distinctions.

## Historical Discipline

Principles should remain stable enough to support long-term institutional coherence while allowing procedures and implementation details to evolve.

Where methodologies change:

- historical records should remain historically accurate;
- earlier governed decisions should not be retroactively rewritten merely to match later terminology;
- corrections should follow the applicable governed correction process; and
- supersession should remain distinguishable from mutation.

## Relationship to Standards and Methodology

Standards define expectations.

Methodology defines implementation.

Methodology Principles provide the enduring conceptual foundation that helps keep those implementations coherent over time.

The principles do not replace the standards themselves and do not independently define institutional authority.

## Relationship to Suite Institutions

Each Suite institution applies standards and methodology according to its own canonical responsibility.

Methodology Principles therefore guide Suite-wide discipline without collapsing institutional roles among Atlas, Navigator, Certifier, Registry, Chronicle, Anchor, Beacon, and Attestor.

Shared principles do not imply shared authority.

## Repository Expectations

Changes to this directory should preserve:

1. enduring methodological guidance rather than transient implementation detail;
2. evidence and provenance discipline;
3. transparency and reviewability;
4. repeatability under equivalent governing conditions;
5. institutional and authority boundaries;
6. historical integrity; and
7. compatibility with the governing Suite standards and methodology.

Changes that would redefine the meaning of truth, trust, institutional authority, or Attestor Trust Statements require the appropriate governed architectural review rather than a documentation-only edit.

## Governing Principle

**Principles endure longer than procedures. Procedures may evolve, but they should remain faithful to the governed principles from which they derive.**
