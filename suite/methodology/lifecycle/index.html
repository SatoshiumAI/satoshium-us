Certification Lifecycle

The Certification Lifecycle defines the complete progression of a certification from initial submission through validation, evaluation, issuance, monitoring, renewal, retirement, and archive.

The lifecycle provides temporal structure for Certifier and certification-domain activity. Other Suite institutions may reference or interact with certification records while retaining independent authority over their own canonical object lifecycles.

Scope Boundary

The Certification Lifecycle is a certification-specific methodology surface. It does not govern the canonical lifecycle of Registry, Chronicle, Anchor, Beacon, Navigator, Atlas, or Attestor objects.

Certification Lifecycle → Certifier / certification-domain methodology.
Institutional Lifecycle → owned by the institution governing the canonical object.

REFERENCE ≠ LIFECYCLE IMPORT.
HANDOFF ≠ LIFECYCLE TRANSFER.
INTEROPERABILITY ≠ LIFECYCLE OWNERSHIP.

Submission

A certification target is identified, submitted, or selected for review. Initial metadata, scope, and intended certification standard are recorded.

Validation

The submission is checked for completeness, identity, scope, required fields, and eligibility before entering formal evaluation.

Evidence Collection

Required evidence is gathered, organized, referenced, and prepared for review according to the applicable evidence requirements.

Evaluation

Evidence is reviewed against evaluation criteria, scoring rules, trust signals, and the relevant certification standard.

Decision

A certification determination is reached, including pass, conditional pass, fail, suspended, revoked, expired, or other defined status outcomes.

Issuance

Approved certifications are issued with structured records, identifiers, metadata, evidence references, and verification-ready schema fields.

Publication

Certification records may be published under Certifier authority and may later be referenced by independently governed registration, integrity-preservation, discovery, indexing, or public-verification mechanisms.

Monitoring

Active certifications may be monitored for changes, expiration, corrections, disputes, updated evidence, or conditions requiring review.

Renewal

Certifications may be renewed when updated evidence, continued validity, or periodic review supports continued certification status.

Retirement and Archive

Certifications that are no longer active may be retired while remaining preserved as part of the permanent institutional record.

Institutional Lifecycle Independence

Downstream institutions may interact with certification objects without adopting Certifier lifecycle semantics.

Certification Lifecycle ≠ SREG Lifecycle.
Certification Lifecycle ≠ Chronicle Entry Lifecycle.
Certification Lifecycle ≠ Integrity Reference Lifecycle.
Certification Lifecycle ≠ Discovery Signal Lifecycle.
Certification Lifecycle ≠ Attestation / Trust Statement Lifecycle.
Certification Lifecycle ≠ Navigator Workflow Definition Lifecycle.
Certification Lifecycle ≠ Atlas Intelligence Lifecycle.

Methodology Role

The Certification Lifecycle establishes the chronological framework for certification-domain activity. It does not establish the lifecycle model for every Suite institution or create a mandatory cross-institution pipeline.

Conceptual Sequence ≠ Mandatory Pipeline.
Exercised Lineage ≠ Mandatory Architecture.
Every institution retains lifecycle authority over its own canonical objects.
Certification becomes trustworthy when every stage is visible, documented, and preserved.
