Certification Logic

Certification Logic defines the decision rules used to transform evidence, evaluation, and scoring into objective certification outcomes.

Rather than relying upon subjective judgment, certification decisions follow documented logical conditions that can be reviewed, reproduced, and implemented consistently by both people and software.

Eligibility Rules

Determine whether a certification target satisfies the minimum conditions required to begin formal evaluation.

Evidence Rules

Verify that required evidence exists, meets integrity requirements, and satisfies the applicable evidence methodology.

Evaluation Rules

Apply evaluation criteria consistently to all submitted evidence, producing repeatable assessment results.

Decision Rules

Translate evaluation outcomes into certification decisions, including pass, conditional pass, fail, suspension, revocation, expiration, or renewal.

Consistency Rules

Ensure equivalent evidence evaluated under equivalent standards produces equivalent certification outcomes.

Exception Handling

Document how incomplete evidence, conflicting information, appeals, corrections, and exceptional circumstances are handled without compromising methodological integrity.

Machine Execution

Certification Logic should be expressed in a structured form that can eventually be executed by software while remaining understandable to human reviewers.

Future Automation

Provides the foundation for deterministic certification, automated verification, AI-assisted review, and interoperable certification engines.

Methodology Role

Certification Logic serves as the reasoning layer of the Satoshium Methodology. It transforms standards, evidence, evaluation criteria, and scoring into consistent certification decisions while preserving transparency, repeatability, and long-term institutional trust.

Certification logic should explain every decision well enough that an independent reviewer—or a future software implementation—can reach the same conclusion using the same evidence.
Logic transforms methodology into repeatable decisions.
