# Evidence

## Purpose

Evidence provides the supporting information that may help users understand, evaluate, review, or contextualize attestations.

Within Attestor, evidence serves as one of the primary mechanisms through which attestations can be examined and interpreted.

Evidence contributes context.

It does not automatically establish truth.

---

## What Is Evidence?

Evidence is information that may support, challenge, clarify, or contextualize a statement, claim, attestation, event, or record.

Examples may include:

* Documents
* Records
* Publications
* Photographs
* Videos
* Datasets
* Historical records
* Witness statements
* Certifications
* Public records

Evidence provides information that may assist interpretation.

---

## Why Evidence Matters

Attestations often involve statements regarding facts, events, qualifications, identities, relationships, or conditions.

Without supporting information, users may have difficulty evaluating those statements.

Evidence helps answer questions such as:

* Why was this attestation made?
* What information supports it?
* What information challenges it?
* What context exists?

---

## Evidence Philosophy

Evidence informs understanding.

Evidence does not guarantee correctness.

Evidence may be:

* Strong
* Weak
* Complete
* Incomplete
* Reliable
* Uncertain

Interpretation remains the responsibility of users and related systems.

---

## Types of Evidence

### Documentary Evidence

Written materials and records.

Examples:

* Reports
* Agreements
* Publications
* Certifications
* Official records

---

### Digital Evidence

Electronic records and artifacts.

Examples:

* Digital records
* Metadata
* Transaction logs
* System records
* Digital signatures

---

### Observational Evidence

Information derived from direct observation.

Examples:

* Witness observations
* Recorded observations
* Inspection reports

---

### Historical Evidence

Information preserved from past events.

Examples:

* Historical records
* Archives
* Timelines
* Prior attestations

---

### Supporting Evidence

Materials offered in support of a claim or attestation.

Examples:

* References
* Citations
* Documentation
* Independent records

---

### Contradictory Evidence

Information that may challenge or conflict with an attestation.

Examples:

* Alternative records
* Conflicting reports
* Corrective information

Contradictory evidence may be valuable for understanding context.

---

## Evidence Attributes

Evidence may vary according to:

* Source
* Availability
* Completeness
* Reliability
* Relevance
* Timeliness
* Traceability

Different evidence may contribute different levels of context.

---

## Evidence Lifecycle

### Collection

Evidence is gathered.

### Documentation

Evidence is recorded.

### Association

Evidence is linked to an attestation, claim, or record.

### Review

Evidence may be evaluated.

### Preservation

Evidence may be retained for future reference.

---

## Evidence and Attestations

Evidence may support an attestation.

Evidence may challenge an attestation.

Evidence may provide context for an attestation.

A simplified relationship may be represented as:

```text id="fj2nv4"
Evidence → Attestation
```

Evidence informs understanding.

The attestation remains a separate statement.

---

## Evidence and Verification

Evidence contributes to verification processes.

Verification evaluates available evidence.

Verification is generally associated with Certifier.

A simplified distinction may be represented as:

```text id="o9x7er"
Evidence → Verification
```

Evidence provides information.

Verification evaluates information.

---

## Evidence and Trust

Evidence may influence trust.

Trust involves broader judgments that may include:

* Reputation
* Historical performance
* Transparency
* Context
* Verification outcomes

Evidence contributes to trust decisions but does not determine them.

---

## Evidence and Sources

Evidence should remain connected to its sources whenever possible.

Users should be able to understand:

* Where evidence originated
* Who created it
* When it was produced
* How it relates to an attestation

Source attribution remains important.

---

## Relationship to Chronicle

Chronicle preserves historical information.

Evidence used within Attestor may also become part of the historical record preserved by Chronicle.

This supports long-term traceability.

---

## Relationship to Registry

Registry may maintain references to evidence records.

Evidence may be cataloged, classified, and referenced throughout the ecosystem.

---

## Relationship to Beacon

Beacon may help users discover evidence.

Attestor helps organize evidence within trust-related contexts.

A simplified relationship may be represented as:

```text id="m87q2y"
Beacon → Discovery
Attestor → Evidence Context
```

---

## Guiding Principles

### Transparency

Evidence origins should remain visible.

### Attribution

Sources should remain identifiable.

### Traceability

Evidence should support review and investigation.

### Preservation

Evidence should remain available whenever practical.

### Context

Evidence should be presented with sufficient information for interpretation.

---

## Long-Term Vision

As Attestor evolves, evidence may become a foundational component of trust frameworks, attestation systems, verification activities, governance structures, and interoperability standards.

Evidence helps answer a simple question:

```text id="w8o7ra"
What information supports this statement?
```
