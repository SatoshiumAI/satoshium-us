Attestor · Evidence

Evidence

Evidence is supporting material or an authoritative reference used by Satoshium Attestor to evaluate an Attestation and support a Trust Statement.

Evidence informs a conclusion; it does not become proof merely because it is referenced. Its origin, provenance, status, relevance, and authority must remain distinguishable from the Attestor assertion that relies upon it.

Governing principle: Reference does not transfer authority.

Supporting Material

Evidence may include documents, records, datasets, media, source material, observations, authoritative references, or other information admitted through Attestor's governed Eligibility architecture. Evidence is not a separate canonical Attestor object class merely because it participates in evaluation.

Established Role

Authoritative References

Attestor may reference canonical Suite objects when they are relevant to an Attestation, including certifications, registered records, Chronicle entries, integrity references, discovery metadata, intelligence, or workflow context. Those objects remain authoritative in their originating institutions.

Established

Provenance

Evidence preserves sufficient provenance to identify its source or origin, attribution, relationship, relevant state, derivation where applicable, and material limitations. Attestor uses the adopted provenance modes direct, referenced, and derived.

Established Requirement

Relevance & Scope

Evidence should be evaluated in relation to the specific assertion and scope under review. Evidence relevant to one bounded question should not silently support a broader conclusion.

Established Requirement

Supporting & Conflicting Evidence

Attestor preserves material that supports, limits, qualifies, contextualizes, or conflicts with an assertion. Conflict and uncertainty remain visible within the Evaluation basis rather than being discarded to produce a favorable result.

Established Requirement

Evidence Status

Evidence may change in availability, authority, interpretation, provenance, or relevance over time. Attestor preserves the relevant source state at Evaluation so later changes do not silently rewrite the basis of an earlier Trust Statement.

Established Context

Evidence Is Not the Attestation

Evidence and Attestations are separate architectural concerns. Evidence supplies support or context. An Attestation expresses the governed assertion that Attestor evaluates.

Eligible Governed Evidence / References → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

Evidence Does Not Become Attestor Authority

When Attestor references a Certifier Certification Package, Registry record, Chronicle Entry, Anchor Integrity Reference, Beacon Discovery Signal or Discovery Metadata, Atlas intelligence, Navigator workflow context, or another governed source, Attestor does not redefine that source or become its authority.

Source authority remains with the source. Attestor owns the Trust Statement it produces from governed evaluation.

Established Evidence Architecture

Evidence participates in Attestor through Eligibility, Provenance, Relationships, Evaluation, Validation, and the applicable schema/profile architecture. Evidence does not independently determine an Evaluation Outcome and is not converted into a Trust Statement.

Availability ≠ Eligibility
Authority ≠ Eligibility
Reference ≠ Eligibility
Evidence ≠ Attestation ≠ Trust Statement

Attestor does not establish a universal evidence weighting system, confidence score, or majority-source rule. Sufficiency is governed by the applicable evaluation purpose, rules, scope, conflicts, limitations, and eligible basis.

Exact machine serialization, executable evidence-profile validation, retention mechanics, and production-specific evidence handling remain implementation work.
