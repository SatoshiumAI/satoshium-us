Principles

Attestor is guided by foundational principles intended to support transparency, accountability, traceability, historical preservation, and trust-related understanding.

Trust cannot be imposed. It must be earned, documented, and evaluated.

Transparency

Trust-related information should remain visible, understandable, and open to review whenever practical.

Attribution

Statements, attestations, corrections, and trust signals should remain connected to identifiable sources whenever possible.

Traceability

Relationships among evidence, sources, records, and attestations should support investigation and review.

Accountability

Trust becomes stronger when actions, statements, and corrections remain attributable.

Historical Preservation

Corrections should improve understanding without erasing the historical record.

Context Matters

Trust-related information should remain connected to supporting evidence, sources, and historical circumstances.

Interoperability

Trust information should remain portable across systems without losing important context.

Humility

Trust systems should acknowledge uncertainty. Documentation should support inquiry rather than certainty.

Principle Chain

The principles of Attestor build upon one another:

Transparency → Accountability → Reputation → Trust

Trust emerges from information, behavior, history, and context rather than from authority alone.

Guiding Philosophy

Attestor does not seek to determine truth. Its purpose is to preserve information relevant to trust, allowing observers to make informed evaluations.
