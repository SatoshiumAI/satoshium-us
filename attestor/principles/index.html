Attestor · Principles

Principles

Attestor is guided by foundational principles that constrain how governed assertions are formed, how eligible inputs are evaluated, and how bounded Trust Statements preserve attribution, provenance, scope, limitations, traceability, and institutional authority.

These principles do not manufacture trust or establish universal truth. They define the conditions under which an Attestor conclusion can remain accountable and reviewable.

Canonical responsibility: Attestor → Trust Statement
Governing principle: Reference does not transfer authority.

Bounded Conclusions

A Trust Statement should say no more than the applicable Attestation, eligible inputs, evidence, provenance, scope, status, limitations, and evaluation support.

Attribution

Attestations, Trust Statements, corrections, and other Attestor-governed changes should remain attributable to the responsible authority, process, or actor recognized by Attestor architecture.

Provenance

The origin, attribution, source relationships, relevant state, and history necessary to understand an input, assertion, evaluation, or output should remain traceable.

Scope

Assertions and conclusions should preserve the boundaries within which they apply so that a limited finding is not silently interpreted as universal.

Traceability

Relationships among Attestations, Evidence, authoritative references, evaluations, Trust Statements, and governed changes should support review and reconstruction of the relevant basis.

Evidence Context

Evidence should remain connected to its relevance, provenance, status, scope, limitations, and source authority. Referencing evidence does not automatically make it proof or determine the conclusion.

Preserve Uncertainty

Incomplete, conflicting, qualified, or insufficient information should remain visible where material. Attestor should not manufacture certainty that its governed inputs do not support.

Governed Change

Corrections, clarifications, withdrawals, supersession, and other changes to Attestor-owned objects should preserve provenance and historical traceability while allowing the current state to remain identifiable.

Authority Boundaries

Attestor may reference objects governed by other Suite institutions without assuming their canonical responsibility. Source authority remains with the source; Attestor remains responsible for its own Attestations, evaluation, and Trust Statements.

Interoperability

Governed information should be able to participate across institutional boundaries without losing the meaning, provenance, scope, status, relationships, limitations, or authority necessary for correct interpretation.

No Automatic Conversion

No Certification Package, Registry record, Chronicle Entry, Integrity Reference, Discovery Signal, evidence item, or other input automatically becomes an Attestation or determines a Trust Statement.

Reviewability

An Attestor conclusion should preserve enough information for an authorized reviewer or system to understand what was concluded, what supported it, where it applies, what limits it, and where its authority ends.

Principles in Operation

The principles constrain Attestor throughout the path from governed input to institutional output:

Eligible Governed Inputs → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

The sequence does not imply that every available input is eligible, that every Attestation warrants a Trust Statement, or that a Trust Statement extends beyond its defined scope.

Principles and Rules

Principles express the foundational commitments that should guide Attestor. Rules translate those commitments into institutional constraints. Advanced architecture should later express applicable rules as normative requirements, validation behavior, schemas, controlled values, and conformance tests.

Principle → Rule → Normative Requirement → Validation → Conformance

Guiding Philosophy

Attestor does not seek to establish universal truth, generic reputation, or an automatic trust score. It produces governed, attributable, bounded Trust Statements whose basis, scope, limitations, provenance, and authority remain visible.
