Eligibility

Eligibility determines what may legitimately enter Attestor evaluation. It formalizes the word eligible in the governing relationship Eligible Governed Inputs → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement.

An input becomes eligible because it satisfies the requirements of a particular Attestor evaluation—not merely because it exists, is public, is authoritative, or can be technically retrieved.

Eligibility principle: Availability ≠ Eligibility. Authority ≠ Eligibility. Reference ≠ Eligibility.

Eligibility Purpose

Eligibility is Attestor's governed determination that a proposed input is permissible for use in a particular evaluation. It is contextual rather than universal: an input may be eligible for one evaluation and ineligible for another.

Governed Identity

An input must be identifiable or otherwise distinguishable enough to be referenced, traced, and reviewed. Anonymous, ambiguous, or unresolvable material cannot be treated as eligible when its identity is material to the evaluation.

Relevant Relationship

The input must have a material relationship to the subject, assertion, scope, or evaluation question. Mere availability, proximity, or mention does not make information eligible.

Traceable Provenance

Material provenance must be sufficient to understand where the input came from and how it reached Attestor. Provenance may be direct, referenced, or derived, but must be adequate for the intended evaluation.

Authority Context

Where authority is material, the source or Attesting Authority must be attributable and its legitimate scope understandable. Authority supports eligibility analysis but does not automatically establish eligibility.

Scope Compatibility

The input must be usable within the scope of the evaluation. Information outside the relevant subject, jurisdiction, condition, time, state, or question may be excluded or bounded rather than treated as generally applicable.

State & Currency

The source state and relevant time must be sufficiently known when they affect interpretation. Superseded, withdrawn, historical, or later-modified material may still be eligible for some purposes, but its state must not be concealed.

Integrity & Reviewability

The input must be sufficiently intact, resolvable, or reviewable for the evaluation purpose. Eligibility does not require that every source be independently verified by Attestor, but Attestor must be able to understand what material it is actually evaluating.

Eligibility Requirements

A proposed input is eligible when Attestor can responsibly admit it to a defined evaluation under the applicable purpose, scope, provenance, authority, state, relationship, and reviewability requirements. Exact machine validation belongs to later architecture.

Eligibility Is Evaluation-Specific

Eligibility is not a permanent quality attached to a source. The same governed object may be eligible for one evaluation, ineligible for another, or eligible only for a limited historical or contextual purpose. Eligibility therefore belongs to the relationship between an input and a particular evaluation.

Input + Evaluation Purpose + Scope + Provenance + Authority Context + State + Relationship + Rules → Eligibility Determination

Eligible Here ≠ Eligible Everywhere

Eligible Does Not Mean Favorable

Eligibility permits an input to be considered. It does not determine how much weight the input receives, whether it supports the assertion, or what evaluation outcome follows. Eligible inputs may support, weaken, contradict, complicate, or leave unresolved the assertion under review.

Eligible → May enter evaluation
Evaluation → Determines treatment and conclusion

Eligible ≠ Supporting
Eligible ≠ Sufficient
Eligible ≠ Correct
Eligible ≠ Favorable

Ineligible Inputs

An input should not enter the governed evaluation basis when its identity or provenance is materially inadequate, its relationship to the evaluation is immaterial, its scope is incompatible, its state cannot be responsibly interpreted, it is materially unusable, or an applicable Attestor rule prohibits its use. Exclusion must not be used to hide material conflict or inconvenient evidence.

Material provenance failure → Ineligible
Material irrelevance → Ineligible
Unresolvable scope incompatibility → Ineligible
Materially uninterpretable state → Ineligible
Applicable rule prohibition → Ineligible

Unfavorable to assertion → Not a reason for ineligibility

Excluded and Considered Inputs

Where an input is considered for eligibility but excluded and that exclusion is material to understanding the evaluation, Attestor should preserve enough context to make the decision reviewable. The later Evaluation and Methodology architecture will determine the exact record requirements.

Proposed Input → Eligibility Review
Eligible → May enter Evaluation Basis
Ineligible → Excluded from Evaluation Basis
Material Exclusion → Preserve reviewable reason/context

Source Institutions and Eligibility

Canonical Suite objects are governed inputs, but their institutional authority does not grant automatic eligibility for every Attestor purpose. Attestor evaluates their relevance and admissibility while preserving the source institution's meaning and authority.

External Inputs

External information may be eligible when it can be governed sufficiently for the evaluation purpose. External origin neither disqualifies nor privileges an input. Attestor must preserve attribution, provenance, authority context, scope, relevant state, and limitations to the degree required by the evaluation.

Suite Source ≠ Automatically Eligible
External Source ≠ Automatically Ineligible
Public Source ≠ Automatically Eligible
Private / Restricted Source ≠ Automatically Ineligible

Eligibility depends on governed evaluation requirements.

Eligibility and Attestations

An Attestation organizes the governed assertion presented for evaluation. The evidence, source references, and other governed inputs supporting or challenging that assertion must satisfy applicable eligibility requirements before they are treated as part of the evaluation basis. An Attestation's existence does not make all of its references eligible.

Attestation → Presents governed assertion
Referenced Inputs → Undergo eligibility determination
Eligible Inputs → Enter evaluation basis
Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Produces bounded conclusion

What Eligibility Does Not Establish

Eligibility is an admission decision, not an evaluation result. It does not establish truth, source superiority, evidence weight, structural validity, conformance, publication, or trustworthiness.

Eligibility ≠ Truth
Eligibility ≠ Authority Ranking
Eligibility ≠ Evidence Weight
Eligibility ≠ Validation
Eligibility ≠ Conformance
Eligibility ≠ Publication
Eligibility ≠ Trust Statement Outcome

Advanced Architecture Position

Eligibility now gives formal meaning to “Eligible Governed Inputs.” It creates the governed admission boundary between available information and the material Attestor is permitted to use as an evaluation basis. The next Evaluation architecture can therefore define how admitted inputs are treated and how a bounded Trust Statement conclusion is reached.
