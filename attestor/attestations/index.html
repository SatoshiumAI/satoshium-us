Attestations

An Attestation is a governed, attributable assertion used by Satoshium Attestor to express a bounded statement about a subject, record, relationship, condition, event, or other trust-relevant matter.

An Attestation preserves what is being asserted, who or what is responsible for the assertion, the scope in which it applies, the evidence and authoritative references that support it, and the provenance necessary for review.

Boundary: An Attestation is an Attestor object. Referencing an authoritative Suite object does not transfer that object's authority to Attestor.

Assertion

Every Attestation expresses a specific assertion. The assertion should be sufficiently bounded to identify what is being stated without implying a broader conclusion than the available authority and evidence support.

Core

Attribution

An Attestation must preserve the responsible attesting actor, system, or institutional process so that the assertion remains attributable and reviewable.

Required

Subject & Scope

The subject identifies what the Attestation concerns. Scope defines the boundary within which the assertion is intended to apply and helps prevent a narrow statement from being interpreted as a universal conclusion.

Required

Evidence & References

An Attestation may reference evidence, certifications, registered records, Chronicle entries, integrity references, discovery metadata, or other governed sources. Those references retain their original authority and meaning.

Provenance

Status & Lifecycle

Attestations use the adopted Lifecycle States draft, active, superseded, withdrawn, and retired. Correction and review are governed activities rather than lifecycle states.

Governed

Trust Statement Relationship

An Attestation is not itself Attestor's final canonical output. Eligible Attestations, evaluated with their evidence, provenance, status, and limitations, support the production of Attestor Trust Statements.

Output Path

Attestation Structure

The Attestation architecture establishes the normative structure and canonical identifier ATT-YYYY-NNNN. Canonical machine-readable serialization, executable validation, governed review, conformance evaluation, lifecycle control, and publication have now been exercised in production.

ATT-YYYY-NNNN → Type → Attesting Authority → Subject → Assertion → Scope → Eligible References → Provenance → Relationships → Lifecycle / Publication State

Adopted Attestation Types

Attestor uses six controlled Attestation Types: identity, evidence, source-provenance, verification-related, relationship-condition, and correction-supersession.

Type classifies the assertion. Type does not establish Eligibility, transfer authority, or predetermine an Evaluation Outcome.

Attestation and Evidence

An Attestation and its supporting evidence are not the same object. The Attestation expresses the assertion. Evidence and authoritative references provide the basis against which that assertion may be evaluated.

Evidence supports an Attestation. It does not become true merely because it is referenced, and the Attestation does not become the authority for the evidence.

Attestation and Trust Statement

Attestations provide structured inputs to Attestor's trust-specific evaluation. A Trust Statement is the canonical Attestor output produced when eligible inputs have been evaluated under Attestor rules with appropriate provenance, scope, status, and limitations preserved.

Eligible Governed Inputs → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

Authority Boundary

Attestor may attest to matters involving objects governed elsewhere in the Satoshium Suite, but an Attestation does not certify on behalf of Certifier, register on behalf of Registry, preserve history on behalf of Chronicle, establish integrity on behalf of Anchor, or create discovery authority on behalf of Beacon.

Reference does not transfer authority.
