ATTESTOR · TRUST STATEMENTS

Satoshium Attestor Trust Statements

A Trust Statement is the canonical output of Satoshium Attestor: a governed, attributable, bounded conclusion produced through Attestor evaluation of eligible inputs.

A Trust Statement preserves the basis and limits of its conclusion without replacing the authority of the evidence, records, certifications, or other governed objects it references.

Canonical responsibility: Attestor → Trust Statement
Governing principle: Reference does not transfer authority.

Purpose

Trust Statements provide Attestor's governed means of expressing a conclusion about a defined subject or matter after applicable assertions, evidence, authoritative references, provenance, scope, status, and limitations have been evaluated.

Canonical Output

The Trust Statement is distinct from an Attestation. An Attestation expresses a governed assertion; a Trust Statement expresses Attestor's bounded conclusion after evaluation.

Attribution

A Trust Statement should identify the Attestor authority, process, or other responsible actor recognized by the eventual Attestor authority model so the conclusion remains accountable and reviewable.

Traceability

A Trust Statement should preserve traceable relationships to the Attestations, evidence, authoritative references, and relevant source states that support the conclusion.

Foundational Trust Statement Content

A Trust Statement should preserve enough information to make the conclusion identifiable, attributable, scoped, reviewable, and traceable to its governed basis.

Identity · Subject · Conclusion · Scope · Attribution · Supporting Attestations · Evidence / Authoritative References · Provenance · Status · Limitations · Relevant Time / State · Relationships

These are foundational information requirements, not yet an adopted machine schema or final controlled field set.

Identity

A Trust Statement should be uniquely distinguishable so it can be referenced, reviewed, related, corrected, withdrawn, superseded, or otherwise governed through its lifecycle. The identifier format remains advanced architecture.

Subject

The subject identifies what the Trust Statement is about. The final subject model and supported subject classes remain unresolved.

Conclusion

The Trust Statement should express the bounded conclusion reached by Attestor. Its wording must not claim more than the applicable evaluation supports.

Scope

The scope identifies the conditions and boundaries within which the conclusion applies so a limited conclusion is not interpreted as universal.

Supporting Basis

Supporting Attestations, evidence, and authoritative references should remain identifiable and traceable, including material conflicting or qualifying information where applicable.

Provenance & Authority

The origin, attribution, source relationships, relevant state, and authority of the inputs used in evaluation should remain distinguishable from Attestor's authority over the Trust Statement itself.

Status & Relevant State

A Trust Statement should support a governed lifecycle and preserve enough temporal or source-state context to understand the basis on which the conclusion was produced.

Limitations & Uncertainty

Material limitations, uncertainty, incomplete information, conflicting evidence, or other qualifications should remain visible when they affect interpretation of the conclusion.

From Attestation to Trust Statement

A Trust Statement is not produced merely because an Attestation exists. Attestor must apply its governing rules to the relevant assertion and eligible inputs before a conclusion is warranted.

Eligible Inputs → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

Exact evaluation criteria, sufficiency rules, validation sequence, and generation conditions remain part of advanced Attestor architecture.

Relationship to Source Authority

A Certification Package may support a Trust Statement without becoming the Trust Statement. The same principle applies to Registry records, Chronicle Entries, Integrity References, Discovery Signals, Atlas intelligence, Navigator workflow context, and other eligible governed sources.

Source Object → Retains Source Authority
Trust Statement → Retains Attestor Authority

Relationship to the Suite

Trust Statements may reference governed outputs from across the Satoshium Suite while preserving each institution's canonical responsibility.

Atlas → Authoritative Intelligence
Navigator → Workflow Definition / Orchestration
Certifier → Certification Package
Registry → Satoshium Registry Record
Chronicle → Chronicle Entry
Anchor → Integrity Reference
Beacon → Discovery Signal / Discovery Metadata
Attestor → Trust Statement

What a Trust Statement Does Not Do

A Trust Statement does not establish universal truth, issue a certification, create or redefine Registry status, rewrite Chronicle history, establish an Anchor Integrity Reference, create a Beacon Discovery Signal, or otherwise assume the canonical authority of a referenced institution.

It is not an automatic reputation score or a universal declaration that a person, organization, record, or subject is trustworthy.

A Trust Statement is authoritative as an Attestor conclusion within its defined scope — not as universal authority over the objects it references.

Lifecycle and Change

A Trust Statement may require review when its supporting basis, source state, scope, or other material conditions change. Advanced architecture should define creation, review, correction, withdrawal, supersession, versioning, publication, and other lifecycle behavior.

Historical traceability should remain compatible with a clearly identifiable current state.

Trust Statement Architecture Ahead

Advanced Attestor architecture should establish the Trust Statement identifier, schema, controlled values, conclusion model, scope representation, authority model, evidence and Attestation relationships, status vocabulary, lifecycle, validation, generation criteria, correction model, publication behavior, and conformance requirements.

This foundational page establishes what a Trust Statement is and what it must preserve without prematurely declaring those machine rules.
