Attestor · Advanced Architecture

Authority

The Attestor Authority architecture defines Attesting Authority, Attestor authority, referenced authority, and the exact institutional boundaries that determine where each begins and ends.

Authority is preserved, attributed, and bounded. Attestor may evaluate governed assertions and authoritative references, but evaluation does not cause the authority of a source object, institution, or external party to migrate into Attestor.

Governing principle: Reference does not transfer authority.

Attesting Authority

The Attesting Authority is the attributable party or governed source responsible for the assertion carried by an Attestation. Its authority applies only to the assertion, subject matter, jurisdiction, role, or source context it is legitimately positioned to address. Attribution identifies the authority; it does not enlarge it.

Attestor Authority

Attestor is authoritative for its own governed evaluation process, Attestor-owned objects, and the Trust Statements it produces. A Trust Statement is authoritative as an Attestor conclusion within its defined scope, basis, limitations, status, and relevant time or state. Attestor does not thereby become universally authoritative over the underlying subject.

Referenced Authority

A referenced authority remains authoritative according to its own institution, source system, jurisdiction, role, or governing framework. Attestor may preserve and evaluate information from that authority without adopting, replacing, or extending the source authority.

Source Authority

Canonical Suite objects retain the authority of the institution that owns them. Certifier remains authoritative for Certification Packages, Registry for Satoshium Registry Records, Chronicle for Chronicle Entries, Anchor for Integrity References, Beacon for Discovery Signals and Discovery Metadata, Atlas for Authoritative Intelligence, and Navigator for Workflow Definition / Orchestration.

External Authority

External authorities may be referenced where eligible, but their authority must be attributable and bounded to the role or source under which the referenced assertion or evidence was produced. External status alone does not make an input eligible or determinative.

Authority Scope

Authority must be interpreted together with scope. An authority may be legitimate for one assertion, jurisdiction, role, period, or subject and not for another. Attestor must not infer broader authority from a narrower source.

Authority Preservation

Attestor preserves the source identity, provenance, scope, status, and relevant limitations needed to understand what a referenced authority actually supports. Removing that context can create an authority claim the source itself never made.

Authority Conflict

When authorities or governed sources conflict, Attestor preserves the conflict as evaluation context rather than silently selecting one source as universally controlling. Evaluation determines what bounded conclusion, if any, Attestor can responsibly produce.

Authority Layers

Attestor distinguishes the authority behind an assertion from the authority exercised by Attestor when producing its own conclusion. These layers may be connected through evaluation, but they are not interchangeable.

Attesting Authority → authority for the attributable assertion within its legitimate scope

Referenced Authority → authority retained by a cited institution, source, or external party

Attestor Authority → authority for Attestor's governed evaluation and resulting Trust Statement

Connection through evaluation → Does not merge authority

Attesting Authority Requirements

An Attestation must identify enough authority context to make the assertion accountable and interpretable. The exact machine representation is reserved for Schemas, but the conceptual model requires the authority to be identifiable and its relationship to the assertion to be understandable.

Attesting Authority → Identifiable
Authority Context → Preserved
Relationship to Assertion → Understandable
Scope / Role / Jurisdiction when material → Preserved
Provenance → Traceable
Limitations → Visible when material

Attestor's Authority Boundary

Attestor's authority begins with its governed institutional process: receiving or forming eligible Attestations, preserving their governed basis, applying Attestor rules, recording evaluation, and producing and maintaining Trust Statements. Its authority ends where another institution or source owns the underlying canonical fact, record, certification, history, integrity determination, discovery object, intelligence product, workflow, or external assertion.

Attestor may:
govern Attestations · evaluate eligible inputs · produce Trust Statements · maintain Attestor lifecycle · correct Attestor-owned objects

Attestor may not:
reissue source authority · rewrite source objects · certify on behalf of Certifier · register on behalf of Registry · rewrite Chronicle history · establish Anchor integrity references · create Beacon Discovery Signals under Attestor authority · redefine Atlas intelligence · assume Navigator workflow authority

Authority Does Not Equal Eligibility

A source may be authoritative for its own object or assertion and still not be eligible for a particular Attestor evaluation. Eligibility is a separate governed determination based on the evaluation purpose, scope, provenance, source state, relationship, and applicable Attestor rules.

Authority → Who legitimately stands behind the source assertion or object
Eligibility → Whether that input may enter a particular Attestor evaluation
Evaluation → What Attestor concludes from eligible governed inputs

Authority ≠ Eligibility ≠ Evaluation Outcome

Authority Does Not Equal Agreement

Preserving an authority does not require Attestor to adopt that authority's assertion as its own conclusion. Attestor can accurately attribute a source, preserve its legitimate authority, and still produce a supported, partially-supported, not-supported, contradicted, or indeterminate evaluation outcome when the governing evaluation rules justify it.

Preserve Authority → Preserve who stands behind the assertion
Evaluate Assertion → Apply Attestor rules to eligible governed inputs
Produce Trust Statement → State Attestor's bounded conclusion

Attribution ≠ Adoption

Authority Conflict and Multiple Authorities

An Attestation or evaluation may involve more than one authority. Where their assertions, records, or interpretations differ, Attestor must preserve attribution and material disagreement rather than erase the conflict. No generic hierarchy of authority is created by this page.

Multiple authorities → Preserve separately
Material conflict → Preserve as evaluation context
Automatic authority ranking → Not adopted
Universal controlling source → Not assumed
Bounded resolution → Evaluation architecture

What Authority Does Not Establish

Authority identifies the legitimate source or institutional basis for an assertion, object, process, or conclusion. It does not by itself establish that the content is correct, current, sufficient, eligible, validated, conformant, published, or favorable.

Authority ≠ Truth
Authority ≠ Evidence Sufficiency
Authority ≠ Eligibility
Authority ≠ Validation
Authority ≠ Conformance
Authority ≠ Publication
Authority ≠ Favorable Trust Statement

Advanced Architecture Position

The Authority architecture establishes the boundary conditions needed for Provenance, Eligibility, Evaluation, Relationships, Schemas, Validation, and eventual production. Later architecture may formalize representation and rules, but it must preserve these authority boundaries.
