Attestor · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Satoshium Attestor, Attestations, Evidence, Trust Statements, authority boundaries, corrections, interoperability, and the current state of Attestor architecture.

Canonical responsibility: Attestor → Trust Statement
Governing principle: Reference does not transfer authority.

What is Attestor?

Attestor is the Satoshium Suite institution responsible for producing governed, attributable, bounded Trust Statements through evaluation of eligible inputs. It preserves the scope, provenance, limitations, relationships, and authority boundaries necessary to make those conclusions reviewable.

What is a Trust Statement?

A Trust Statement is Attestor's canonical output: a governed, attributable, bounded conclusion produced through Attestor evaluation. It should preserve the basis and limits of its conclusion without replacing the authority of the objects it references.

Does Attestor determine truth?

No. Attestor does not establish universal truth. It produces a bounded conclusion within a defined scope under Attestor rules. The conclusion should say no more than its eligible inputs, evidence, provenance, status, limitations, and applicable evaluation support.

What is an Attestation?

An Attestation is a governed, attributable, bounded assertion about a subject, record, relationship, condition, event, or other trust-relevant matter. An Attestation may participate in evaluation, but it is not itself the final Trust Statement.

What is the difference between an Attestation and a Trust Statement?

An Attestation expresses an assertion. A Trust Statement expresses Attestor's conclusion after applicable rule-constrained evaluation. The two are related but distinct Attestor objects.

Eligible Governed Inputs → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

What role does Evidence play?

Evidence provides supporting material or authoritative references used to evaluate an Attestation and support a Trust Statement. Evidence informs a conclusion; it does not become proof merely because Attestor references it. Its provenance, relevance, scope, status, limitations, and source authority should remain visible where applicable.

What is a trust signal in Attestor?

“Trust signal” remains descriptive language for trust-relevant evaluation context. It is not presently established as a separate canonical Attestor object, controlled record type, reputation score, or automatic trust indicator. It must not be confused with Beacon's canonical Discovery Signals.

How does Attestor relate to Certifier?

Certifier owns the Certification Package and certification process. Attestor may reference a Certification Package as an authoritative input when relevant and eligible, but it does not certify or recertify the subject. Certifier retains authority over the certification; Attestor retains authority over its own Trust Statement.

How does Attestor relate to Anchor?

Anchor owns the Integrity Reference. Attestor may reference an Anchor Integrity Reference when integrity context is relevant to an evaluation, but Attestor does not establish or redefine Anchor's integrity determination.

How does Attestor relate to Beacon?

Beacon owns Discovery Signals and Discovery Metadata. Attestor may reference those objects when discovery context is relevant, but it does not create or redefine Beacon's canonical discovery architecture.

Can Attestor reference Registry and Chronicle records?

Yes, when they are relevant and eligible. Registry retains authority over Satoshium Registry records, and Chronicle retains authority over Chronicle Entries. Attestor may use those objects as governed references without assuming their institutional authority.

What does “Reference does not transfer authority” mean?

It means that Attestor may rely upon or reference an object governed elsewhere without becoming the authority for that object. The source institution remains authoritative for its canonical object; Attestor is responsible for how the eligible reference participates in its own evaluation and Trust Statement.

Can Attestor-owned information be corrected?

Yes. Attestor requires governed mechanisms for addressing errors, clarifications, withdrawals, supersession, and other changes affecting its own Attestations and Trust Statements while preserving provenance and historical traceability. The exact lifecycle mechanics remain advanced architecture.

Does Attestor maintain a reputation system or trust score?

Not under the current foundational architecture. Reputation is not established as an Attestor canonical object, and Attestor does not presently define an automatic trust score. Its canonical output is the bounded Trust Statement.

Are Trust Statements automatically public?

Not yet determined. Traceability is foundational, but publication eligibility, visibility, publication states, Registry relationships, Beacon discovery relationships, and Anchor relationships remain matters for advanced architecture.

Is Attestor operational?

Not yet. Attestor is completing foundational reconciliation. Advanced schemas, controlled values, evaluation rules, lifecycle architecture, validation, conformance, and a governed first production Trust Statement remain ahead.

What remains to be designed?

Advanced Attestor architecture still needs to establish identifiers, schemas, controlled values, Attestation Types, subject and authority models, scope fields, evidence eligibility, evaluation and sufficiency rules, uncertainty representation, lifecycle states, validation behavior, publication rules, conformance tests, reference vectors, and production procedures.

Foundational Principle

Attestor does not manufacture trust from a sequence of signals. It forms governed Attestations, evaluates eligible inputs under bounded rules, and produces Trust Statements whose basis, scope, limitations, provenance, and authority remain visible.
