Attestor · Operational Evaluation

Evaluation

Evaluation defines the heart of Attestor: how Attestations and eligible governed inputs undergo Rule-Constrained Evaluation to produce a bounded Trust Statement.

Attestor does not convert sources directly into conclusions. It evaluates a defined assertion against an eligible, attributable, provenance-preserving basis under governed rules and states only the conclusion that basis can responsibly support.

Canonical evaluation path: Attestation + Eligible Governed Inputs → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

Evaluation Purpose

Rule-Constrained Evaluation is the governed process by which Attestor examines an Attestation against its eligible evaluation basis and determines what bounded conclusion Attestor can responsibly state. Evaluation is the institutional bridge between assertion and Trust Statement.

Evaluation Subject

Every evaluation must identify the Attestation and assertion being evaluated. Attestor evaluates a defined assertion within a defined scope; it does not perform an unbounded search for whether a subject is generally trustworthy or true.

Evaluation Basis

The evaluation basis consists only of eligible governed inputs admitted for the particular evaluation. Material supporting, limiting, conflicting, contradictory, and indeterminate information must remain available to the evaluation rather than being filtered according to the desired result.

Applicable Rules

Evaluation must operate under identifiable Attestor rules and methodology. Rules constrain what may be considered, how scope and authority are preserved, how uncertainty is handled, and what conclusion the available basis permits.

Reasoned Treatment

Attestor must preserve enough reasoning context to make the treatment of material inputs reviewable. Evaluation is not a mechanical vote among sources, an authority popularity contest, or a hidden confidence calculation.

Bounded Conclusion

The evaluation conclusion must be no broader or stronger than the eligible basis, applicable scope, provenance, authority, relevant state, limitations, and uncertainty support. Where the basis cannot justify a stronger conclusion, Attestor must preserve that limitation.

Evaluation Outcome

The controlled outcome classifies the evaluation result as supported, partially-supported, not-supported, contradicted, or indeterminate. The outcome summarizes the bounded evaluation; it does not replace the Trust Statement conclusion.

Trust Statement Production

A completed evaluation provides the governed basis for a Trust Statement. The Trust Statement remains a distinct Attestor-owned canonical object with its own identity, scope, attribution, provenance, limitations, status, and relationships.

Evaluation Sequence

The evaluation process is governed so that Attestor cannot reach a conclusion before the assertion, scope, eligible basis, authority and provenance context, and applicable rules are known. Methodology now defines the standing operational sequence, while Validation supplies machine-testable controls for the governed objects and context used in production.

1. Identify Attestation and Assertion
2. Confirm Evaluation Purpose and Scope
3. Establish Eligible Governed Inputs
4. Preserve Authority and Provenance Context
5. Apply Applicable Attestor Rules
6. Consider Supporting, Limiting, Conflicting, and Contradictory Material
7. Preserve Uncertainty and Material Limitations
8. Determine Evaluation Outcome
9. Form Bounded Attestor Conclusion
10. Produce or Update Governed Trust Statement

Outcome Semantics

The Controlled Values architecture establishes five evaluation outcomes. Their semantics govern production Evaluation. Methodology defines how the evaluation is conducted, while Validation tests applicable structural and normative requirements without replacing the Evaluation itself.

supported → the eligible evaluation basis supports the assertion within the defined scope without a material unresolved conflict that prevents that conclusion

partially-supported → the basis supports a bounded or material portion of the assertion, but not the assertion in full as presented

not-supported → the eligible basis does not provide sufficient support for the assertion within the defined scope, without establishing the stronger conclusion that it is contradicted

contradicted → the eligible basis materially supports a conclusion inconsistent with the assertion within the defined scope

indeterminate → the eligible basis, conflict, uncertainty, provenance, state, or limitations do not permit Attestor to responsibly determine support or contradiction

Outcome and Conclusion Are Distinct

The controlled outcome is a classification of the evaluation result. The Trust Statement conclusion is the bounded human- and machine-interpretable statement Attestor actually makes. Two evaluations may share an outcome class while requiring different conclusions because their subjects, scopes, evidence, limitations, or relevant states differ.

Evaluation Outcome → Controlled classification
Bounded Conclusion → Attestor's substantive conclusion
Trust Statement → Canonical object carrying conclusion + context

Outcome ≠ Conclusion ≠ Trust Statement Identity

No Automatic Source Conversion

No source object, certification, registry record, historical entry, integrity reference, discovery signal, intelligence product, workflow result, or external assertion automatically becomes an Attestor conclusion. Each remains within its source authority and may enter evaluation only when eligible.

Source Object → Preserve Source Authority
Eligible Reference / Input → Enter Evaluation Basis
Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Attestor Conclusion
Trust Statement → Attestor Authority

Reference does not transfer authority.

No Hidden Weighting Model

This architecture does not adopt a universal numeric weighting system, confidence percentage, reputation score, majority-source rule, or automatic authority hierarchy. If future specialized methodology requires weighting for a defined evaluation type, that weighting must be explicitly governed, explainable, bounded to that methodology, and separately adopted.

Source Count ≠ Truth
Authority Count ≠ Outcome
Confidence Percentage → Not adopted
Reputation Score → Not adopted
Universal Weighting Formula → Not adopted
Hidden Ranking → Prohibited

Conflict and Uncertainty

Material disagreement, incomplete evidence, unresolved provenance, temporal mismatch, source-state change, and other uncertainty must influence the conclusion when relevant. Attestor does not force every evaluation into a positive or negative answer. indeterminate is a legitimate governed outcome when the basis cannot responsibly support a stronger determination.

Material Conflict → Preserve and evaluate
Material Limitation → Bound conclusion
Insufficient Basis → Do not overstate
Unresolvable Determination → indeterminate

Uncertainty is governed context, not a defect to hide.

Evaluation-Time State

The evaluation must remain tied to the relevant state and time of its inputs. A later change to a source object does not retroactively rewrite what Attestor evaluated. Material later changes may instead trigger review, correction, supersession, or a new evaluation under Lifecycle and Versioning rules.

Evaluation Basis at Time T → Preserved
Trust Statement Conclusion at Time T → Traceable to that basis
Later Material Change → Review under governed lifecycle
Historical Evaluation → Not silently rewritten

Evaluation Reviewability

A governed evaluation must preserve enough context for a later reviewer to understand what was evaluated, what basis was admitted, what material information was excluded and why when relevant, what rules applied, what limitations existed, and how the resulting conclusion remained within those bounds.

Assertion → Known
Scope → Known
Eligible Basis → Traceable
Material Exclusions → Reviewable when required
Rules / Methodology → Identifiable
Outcome → Recorded
Conclusion → Bounded
Provenance → Preserved

What Evaluation Does Not Do

Attestor Evaluation does not declare universal truth, inherit another institution's authority, certify on behalf of Certifier, create generic reputation, produce a trust score, guarantee future conditions, or erase uncertainty. Its responsibility is narrower and more defensible: produce an attributable, reviewable, bounded Attestor conclusion from an eligible governed basis.

Evaluation ≠ Universal Truth Determination
Evaluation ≠ Certification
Evaluation ≠ Source Authority Transfer
Evaluation ≠ Reputation
Evaluation ≠ Trust Score
Evaluation ≠ Guarantee

Evaluation → Governed basis for a bounded Trust Statement
