Rules

Attestor is governed by foundational rules that constrain how assertions, evidence, authoritative references, evaluation, corrections, and Trust Statements may be handled.

These rules preserve provenance, scope, traceability, institutional authority, and reviewability while preventing Attestor from claiming authority it does not own.

Governing principle: Reference does not transfer authority.

Rule 1: Preserve Attribution

An Attestation, evidence source, authoritative reference, evaluation, correction, or Trust Statement should preserve sufficient attribution to identify who or what is responsible for the relevant assertion, source, or action.

Rule 2: Preserve Provenance

Attestor should preserve sufficient provenance to explain the origin, relationship, status, and relevant history of the inputs and Attestor objects used in an evaluation.

Rule 3: Preserve Scope

Assertions, evidence relationships, evaluations, and Trust Statements must remain bounded by the scope in which they are supported. A narrow conclusion must not silently become a universal one.

Rule 4: Preserve Authority Boundaries

Attestor may reference authoritative objects governed elsewhere, but it does not inherit, replace, or redefine their authority. The originating institution remains authoritative for its own canonical objects.

Rule 5: Preserve Evidence Context

Evidence should remain connected to the assertion and evaluation for which it is relevant, including material limitations, conflicting information, and source status where applicable.

Rule 6: Preserve Traceability

Relationships among Attestations, evidence, authoritative references, evaluations, corrections, and Trust Statements should remain sufficiently traceable for review and validation.

Rule 7: Preserve Governed Change

Corrections, clarifications, withdrawals, supersession, and other changes to Attestor-owned objects must not silently erase the prior state or the provenance of the change.

Rule 8: Distinguish Current and Historical State

Attestor should preserve historically relevant prior states while clearly distinguishing them from the currently effective state of an Attestation or Trust Statement.

Rule 9: Do Not Claim Universal Truth

A Trust Statement is a bounded Attestor conclusion produced under Attestor rules. It does not establish universal truth, and its language should preserve the scope, evidence, limitations, and authority supporting the conclusion.

Rule 10: Do Not Convert Inputs into Conclusions

No single evidence item, verification result, certification, discovery signal, historical event, or other input automatically determines a Trust Statement. Attestor must perform its own governed evaluation.

Rule 11: Preserve Uncertainty

When eligible inputs are incomplete, conflicting, qualified, or insufficient, Attestor should preserve that uncertainty rather than manufacture a stronger conclusion than the evidence supports.

Rule 12: Support Interoperability Without Authority Transfer

Attestor should exchange and reference governed information across the Satoshium Suite without changing the canonical ownership, meaning, identifiers, lifecycle, or authority of the referenced objects.

Rule Application

The rules operate together rather than as a simple trust-producing chain. A valid Attestor evaluation should preserve the relevant attribution, provenance, scope, authority, evidence context, traceability, lifecycle state, and uncertainty before a Trust Statement is produced.

Eligible Governed Inputs → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

Rules and Normative Architecture

These rules establish Attestor's institutional operating constraints. Advanced Architecture now carries those constraints into the applicable normative requirements, controlled values, schemas and profiles, lifecycle and versioning rules, Validation architecture, and Conformance architecture.

Not every institutional rule requires an identical machine check. Executable validation rules and complete conformance-test mechanics remain implementation work and must preserve the meaning of the governing rule rather than replace it.

Principle → Rule → Normative Requirement → Validation → Conformance
Validation ≠ Evaluation
Validation ≠ Conformance
Valid ≠ Published

Governance Boundary

Attestor may require procedures for review, disputes, correction authorization, publication, and other institutional operations. Those procedures should be defined only where Attestor's own responsibility requires them and should not silently create a reputation system, generic trust framework, or Suite-wide governance authority.
