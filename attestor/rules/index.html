Rules

Attestor is guided by operational rules intended to support transparency, accountability, consistency, traceability, and responsible trust-related recordkeeping.

Rules help preserve integrity without attempting to replace judgment.

Rule 1: Preserve Attribution

Whenever practical, attestations, evidence, sources, and trust signals should remain connected to identifiable origins.

Rule 2: Preserve Context

Trust-related information should remain linked to supporting evidence, historical records, and relevant sources.

Rule 3: Preserve Transparency

Records should remain understandable, reviewable, and accessible whenever practical.

Rule 4: Preserve Traceability

Relationships among records should support investigation, review, and verification.

Rule 5: Preserve Corrections

Corrections should remain visible and connected to original records whenever practical.

Rule 6: Preserve History

Historical records should not be silently removed when trust-related context depends upon them.

Rule 7: Separate Trust and Truth

Attestor documents information relevant to trust. It does not claim authority over truth.

Rule 8: Support Interoperability

Trust-related records should be capable of interacting with other systems without losing context.

Operational Rule Chain

The rules build upon one another:

Attribution → Transparency → Accountability → Trust

When records remain visible and traceable, observers can make more informed trust evaluations.

Future Governance

Additional operational standards may emerge as Attestor evolves. Future versions may include trust frameworks, dispute processes, reputation guidance, and governance-related procedures.
