# Satoshium Attestor — Methodology

**Path:** `/attestor/methodology/`  
**Institution:** Satoshium Attestor  
**Current Stage:** Operational Methodology  
**Canonical Responsibility:** `Attestor → Trust Statement`

## Purpose

Methodology establishes Attestor's complete repeatable institutional process.

It assembles the established Attestor architecture into an end-to-end governed sequence from a bounded trust-relevant purpose through Attestation, eligibility, evaluation, Trust Statement formation, governance, and publication where authorized.

## Governing Model

`Governed Purpose → Governed Inputs / Eligibility → Attestation → Evaluation Basis → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement → Governed Continuation`

## Canonical Institutional Sequence

1. Establish Purpose and Evaluation Scope
2. Identify Subject
3. Define Governed Assertion
4. Establish Attesting Authority / Attribution
5. Form Attestation
6. Identify Potential Governed Inputs
7. Preserve Source Authority and Provenance
8. Determine Input Eligibility for This Evaluation
9. Establish Evaluation Basis
10. Apply Rule-Constrained Evaluation
11. Preserve Conflict, Uncertainty, and Limitations
12. Determine Evaluation Outcome
13. Form Bounded Attestor Conclusion
14. Create Trust Statement
15. Apply Validation and Required Review
16. Establish Lifecycle / Version State
17. Determine Conformance Where Applicable
18. Authorize Publication Where Applicable
19. Preserve Provenance, Relationships, and History
20. Re-review on Material Trigger

## Stage 1 — Establish the Matter

The process begins with:

- institutional purpose;
- subject;
- assertion context;
- scope;
- relevant time/state; and
- known limitations.

Attestor does not begin with a desired conclusion.

## Stage 2 — Form the Attestation

Conceptually:

`Purpose Established → Subject Identified → Assertion Defined → Scope Bounded → Attribution Preserved → Provenance Preserved → Governed References Associated → Attestation Formed`

The Attestation is the governed attributable assertion.

## Stage 3 — Determine Eligibility

Potential inputs are evaluated for admission into the specific evaluation.

`Potential Input + Evaluation Context → Eligibility Determination`

Key distinctions:

`Available ≠ Eligible`

`Authoritative ≠ Automatically Eligible`

`Unfavorable ≠ Ineligible`

## Stage 4 — Establish Evaluation Basis

The governed evaluation basis includes, as applicable:

- eligible governed inputs;
- source authority context;
- provenance;
- evaluation scope;
- relevant time/state;
- applicable rules;
- material exclusions; and
- material limitations.

## Stage 5 — Rule-Constrained Evaluation

`Attestation + Governed Evaluation Basis + Applicable Rules → Rule-Constrained Evaluation`

Controlled Evaluation Outcomes:

- `supported`
- `partially-supported`
- `not-supported`
- `contradicted`
- `indeterminate`

The methodology does not adopt:

- hidden trust scores;
- generic reputation rankings;
- automatic majority-source rules;
- automatic authority hierarchies; or
- universal numeric weighting.

## Stage 6 — Form Trust Statement

`Evaluation Outcome → Bounded Conclusion → Trust Statement`

The Trust Statement preserves, as applicable:

- identity;
- subject;
- bounded conclusion;
- scope;
- Attestor attribution;
- supporting Attestation(s);
- evaluation basis;
- provenance;
- relevant state;
- limitations/uncertainty;
- relationships; and
- lifecycle context.

## Stage 7 — Validate and Review

Validation tests the resulting object against applicable normative requirements.

Review determines whether the object/process is ready for the next governed action.

`Validation ≠ Evaluation`

`Validation ≠ Review`

`NOT-TESTED ≠ PASS`

`Review ≠ Automatic Approval`

## Stage 8 — Lifecycle, Versioning, and Conformance

Attestor establishes:

- lifecycle state;
- governed version identity;
- correction/supersession behavior; and
- conformance where applicable.

Material meaning changes require new canonical identity where Versioning requires it.

Lifecycle activation remains distinct from Publication.

## Stage 9 — Publication

Publication is an affirmative governed public-representation decision.

`Creation ≠ Activation ≠ Validation ≠ Conformance ≠ Publication`

`Published ≠ Supported`

## Stage 10 — Continuing Governance

Material triggers may cause later review.

Possible governed responses include:

- confirm;
- correct;
- revise;
- re-evaluate;
- supersede;
- withdraw; or
- retire.

`Later State ≠ Silent Rewrite of Earlier State`

The twenty-step Canonical Institutional Sequence remains the durable methodological model. Production may express that model through more granular records and control points without changing its institutional meaning.

## Reviewability

The methodology preserves enough context to reconstruct:

- the assertion;
- eligible inputs;
- material exclusions where required;
- applicable rules/method;
- evaluation basis;
- outcome;
- bounded conclusion;
- provenance;
- lifecycle/version history; and
- publication history where material.

## Authority Boundaries

`Source Object → Retains Source Authority`

`Governed Reference → Preserves Source Context`

`Attestation → Preserves Assertion Attribution`

`Evaluation → Attestor-governed process`

`Trust Statement → Attestor Authority`

**Reference does not transfer authority.**

## Repeatability

Repeatability means consistent governed process, not automatic identical conclusions.

Profile-specific requirements may vary when explicitly governed.

`Repeatability ≠ Universal Numeric Weighting`

`Repeatability ≠ Automatic Conclusion`

## What Methodology Does Not Establish

`Method Followed ≠ Assertion True`

`Method Followed ≠ Supported Outcome`

`Method Followed ≠ Publication`

`Method Followed ≠ Source Authority Transfer`

`Method Followed ≠ Permanent Conclusion`

Methodology establishes a repeatable governed institutional process.

The first-production operation establishes that Attestor's standing methodology can govern a real end-to-end operation.
