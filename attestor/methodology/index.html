Attestor · Operational Methodology

Methodology

Methodology establishes Attestor's complete repeatable institutional process—from a bounded trust-relevant purpose through governed assertion, input eligibility, Rule-Constrained Evaluation, Trust Statement formation, and continuing governance.

The methodology turns Attestor's architecture into a disciplined sequence while preserving attribution, provenance, scope, uncertainty, institutional authority, and historical state at every material step.

Methodology principle: Governed Purpose → Governed Inputs / Eligibility → Attestation → Evaluation Basis → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement → Governed Continuation

Methodology Purpose

Methodology establishes Attestor's complete repeatable institutional process: how a trust-relevant matter moves from defined purpose and governed inputs through Attestation, eligibility, evaluation, Trust Statement formation, review, lifecycle governance, and—when authorized—publication.

Process Boundary

The methodology governs Attestor activity only. It may consume or reference canonical Suite and external objects, but it does not perform another institution's canonical responsibility or convert source authority into Attestor authority.

Purpose & Subject

Every Attestor operation begins by establishing the evaluation purpose, identifying the subject, and defining the trust-relevant question or assertion context. The process must be bounded before evidence or sources are interpreted.

Attestation Formation

The Attestation is formed as a governed attributable assertion with subject, assertion, scope, Attesting Authority, provenance, and governed references. Inputs are not silently converted into conclusions.

Eligibility Determination

Potential inputs are evaluated for admission into the specific evaluation basis. Availability, authority, and reference alone do not establish eligibility. Material exclusions remain reviewable where required.

Rule-Constrained Evaluation

Eligible governed inputs are evaluated under applicable Attestor rules. Supporting, limiting, conflicting, contradictory, and uncertain material must be considered without hidden weighting or automatic source conversion.

Trust Statement Formation

The evaluation produces a bounded Attestor conclusion carried by a canonical Trust Statement. The Trust Statement preserves its supporting Attestation(s), evaluation basis, provenance, relevant state, limitations, and relationships.

Governed Continuation

After creation, Attestor applies validation, review, lifecycle, versioning, conformance, and publication governance as applicable. Later material change can trigger review without silently rewriting the historical evaluation.

Canonical Institutional Sequence

The Attestor methodology is organized as a repeatable governed sequence. Individual profiles may add requirements, but they must preserve this institutional logic unless later architecture explicitly establishes a bounded exception.

4. Establish Attesting Authority / Attribution

6. Identify Potential Governed Inputs

9. Establish Evaluation Basis
10. Apply Rule-Constrained Evaluation
11. Preserve Conflict, Uncertainty, and Limitations
12. Determine Evaluation Outcome
13. Form Bounded Attestor Conclusion
14. Create Trust Statement

Stage 1 — Establish the Matter

Attestor begins with a bounded institutional purpose, not with a desired conclusion. The subject, question, assertion context, scope, relevant time/state, and known limitations must be sufficiently defined to make later eligibility and evaluation meaningful.

Purpose → What is Attestor being asked to evaluate?
Subject → What does the matter concern?
Scope → What is inside and outside the evaluation?
Relevant State / Time → What state is being evaluated?

Desired Outcome → Must not determine the process

Stage 2 — Form the Attestation

The governed assertion is expressed through an Attestation. Formation preserves who or what stands behind the assertion, what is asserted, the subject and scope, provenance, evidence/source references, relevant state, limitations, and relationships required by the applicable profile.

Purpose Established → Subject Identified → Assertion Defined → Scope Bounded → Attribution Preserved → Provenance Preserved → Governed References Associated → Attestation Formed

Stage 3 — Establish Eligible Governed Inputs

Attestor identifies potential inputs and determines which may legitimately enter this particular evaluation. Eligibility remains contextual. Material exclusions must not be based merely on whether an input favors or disfavors the assertion.

Potential Input → Identity / Relevance / Provenance / Authority Context / Scope / State / Integrity / Applicable Rules → Eligibility Determination

Available ≠ Eligible
Authoritative ≠ Automatically Eligible
Unfavorable ≠ Ineligible

Stage 4 — Establish the Evaluation Basis

The evaluation basis is the governed set of eligible inputs, source/provenance context, applicable rules, scope, relevant state, and material exclusions necessary to understand what Attestor actually evaluated.

Eligible Governed Inputs
+ Source Authority Context
+ Provenance
+ Evaluation Scope
+ Relevant Time / State
+ Applicable Rules
+ Material Exclusions / Limitations where required
→ Governed Evaluation Basis

Stage 5 — Rule-Constrained Evaluation

Attestor evaluates the Attestation against the governed evaluation basis. The process must consider material supporting, limiting, conflicting, and contradictory information and preserve uncertainty rather than forcing a favorable or binary result.

Attestation + Governed Evaluation Basis + Applicable Rules → Rule-Constrained Evaluation

Permitted outcomes:
supported · partially-supported · not-supported · contradicted · indeterminate

No hidden trust score
No generic reputation ranking
No automatic majority-source rule
No automatic authority hierarchy

Stage 6 — Form the Trust Statement

The evaluation outcome informs a bounded Attestor conclusion. That conclusion becomes the substantive core of a canonical Trust Statement, which preserves enough context to remain attributable, traceable, reviewable, and historically interpretable.

Evaluation Outcome → Bounded Conclusion → Trust Statement

Trust Statement preserves, as applicable:
Identity · Subject · Conclusion · Scope · Attestor Attribution · Supporting Attestation(s) · Evaluation Basis · Provenance · Relevant State · Limitations / Uncertainty · Relationships · Lifecycle Context

Stage 7 — Validate and Review

The resulting Attestor objects must satisfy applicable normative structural and rule requirements. Validation tests the object against adopted architecture; review considers whether the object and process are ready for the next governed action.

Object + Normative Requirements → Validation
Validation Results + Required Institutional Review → Governance Decision

Validation ≠ Evaluation
Validation ≠ Review
NOT-TESTED ≠ PASS
Review ≠ Automatic Approval

Stage 8 — Lifecycle, Versioning, and Conformance

Attestor establishes the object's governed lifecycle state and preserves version identity. Where a conformance claim is applicable, the target is assessed only against its declared requirements set and tested scope.

Canonical Object → Lifecycle State
Governed Revision → Version Identity
Material Meaning Change → New Canonical Object where required
Declared Requirements + Evidence → Conformance Determination where applicable

Lifecycle Activation ≠ Publication
Revision ≠ Historical Erasure

Stage 9 — Publication

Publication occurs only when Attestor affirmatively authorizes a governed public representation. Publication is downstream of canonical creation and remains distinct from lifecycle, validation, conformance, and evaluation outcome.

Canonical Object + Applicable Requirements + Publication Authorization → Published Representation

Creation ≠ Activation ≠ Validation ≠ Conformance ≠ Publication
Published ≠ Supported

Stage 10 — Continuing Governance

Attestor's responsibility does not end at publication. Material new evidence, changed source state, authority changes, provenance defects, corrections, contradictions, scope changes, or applicable rule changes may trigger review. Historical evaluation context must not be silently replaced by current source state.

Material Trigger → Review
Review → Confirm / Correct / Revise / Re-evaluate / Supersede / Withdraw / Retire as applicable

Later State ≠ Silent Rewrite of Earlier State

Methodological Reviewability

A repeatable process must be reviewable. Attestor should preserve enough governed context to reconstruct what was asserted, what was eligible, what was evaluated, which rules applied, what conclusion was reached, and how the resulting object moved through later governance.

Assertion → Traceable
Eligible Inputs → Traceable
Material Exclusions → Reviewable where required
Rules / Method → Identifiable
Evaluation Basis → Preserved
Outcome / Conclusion → Distinguishable
Provenance → Preserved
Lifecycle / Version History → Preserved
Publication History → Preserved where material

Institutional Authority Boundaries

The methodology may use Suite and external objects as eligible governed inputs without absorbing their authority. Attestor evaluates and concludes under Attestor authority; source institutions remain authoritative for their own canonical objects.

Source Object → Retains Source Authority
Governed Reference → Preserves Source Context
Attestation → Preserves Assertion Attribution
Evaluation → Attestor-governed process
Trust Statement → Attestor Authority

Reference does not transfer authority.

Repeatability Without Mechanical Overreach

Repeatability requires consistent governed steps, not an invented universal scoring formula. Different Attestation profiles may require different evidence, rules, or review depth while remaining conformant to the same institutional methodology.

Repeatable Process → Consistent governance and decision path
Profile-Specific Requirements → Permitted when explicitly governed
Human / Machine Review → May coexist where architecture permits

Repeatability ≠ Universal numeric weighting
Repeatability ≠ Automatic conclusion

What Methodology Does Not Establish

Methodology defines how Attestor operates. It does not guarantee a favorable conclusion, universal truth, source correctness, permanent currency, publication, or authority beyond Attestor's institutional boundary.

Method Followed ≠ Assertion True
Method Followed ≠ Supported Outcome
Method Followed ≠ Publication
Method Followed ≠ Source Authority Transfer
Method Followed ≠ Permanent Conclusion

Methodology → Repeatable governed institutional process
