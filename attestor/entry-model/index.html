Attestor · Operational Entry Model

Entry Model

The Attestor Entry Model defines the complete conceptual structure of an Attestation and a Trust Statement, and the governed relationship between the assertion being evaluated and the conclusion Attestor produces.

The model establishes the conceptual requirements and authority boundaries that the rest of Attestor architecture formalizes through identifiers, controlled values, schemas and profiles, lifecycle and versioning, Validation, Conformance, Publication, Methodology, and Production.

Core relationship: Eligible Governed Inputs → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

Attestation

An Attestation is a governed, attributable assertion presented for an Attestor purpose. It identifies the subject, assertion, scope, Attesting Authority, supporting references, provenance, relevant context, and limitations necessary for evaluation. It is not Attestor's final trust conclusion.

Trust Statement

A Trust Statement is Attestor's canonical output: a governed, attributable, bounded conclusion produced through rule-constrained evaluation of an Attestation and its eligible governed inputs. Its authority is limited to Attestor's conclusion within the statement's defined scope.

Attesting Authority

Every Attestation preserves the identity and authority context of the party or governed source responsible for the assertion. Attestor may evaluate that assertion without assuming the authority of the attesting party or referenced objects.

Subject

The subject identifies what the Attestation and resulting evaluation concern. Referencing a subject does not transfer ownership or canonical authority to Attestor.

Assertion

The assertion is the bounded proposition presented for evaluation. It remains distinct from supporting evidence and from the Trust Statement that may result.

Scope

Scope constrains interpretation by identifying the relevant subject, question, conditions, time or state, and limitations needed to prevent a bounded conclusion from being treated as universal.

Evidence & Governed References

Evidence and governed references provide the basis Attestor may use during evaluation. They retain their source meaning, status, provenance, and authority. Their presence does not automatically establish correctness, sufficiency, verification, certification, or a favorable Trust Statement.

Provenance

Provenance preserves where assertions, evidence, references, and evaluation inputs came from and how they entered the Attestor process. A Trust Statement remains traceable to the governed basis on which its conclusion was produced.

Limitations & Uncertainty

Known limitations, unresolved conditions, conflicting information, uncertainty, and other material constraints remain visible where they affect interpretation.

Relevant Time & State

An evaluation may depend on the state of a source, subject, or condition at a particular time. The Entry Model preserves enough temporal and state context to distinguish the basis evaluated from later changes.

Assertion and Conclusion Are Distinct

The Entry Model preserves a deliberate separation between what is presented to Attestor and what Attestor concludes. An Attestation carries an attributable assertion. Evaluation tests that assertion and its eligible governed basis under Attestor rules. The resulting Trust Statement records Attestor's bounded conclusion.

Attestation: governed assertion presented for evaluation
Evaluation: rule-constrained institutional assessment
Trust Statement: governed Attestor conclusion

Authority Model

The Entry Model does not collapse source authority into Attestor authority. Atlas, Navigator, Certifier, Registry, Chronicle, Anchor, Beacon, and attributable external sources remain authoritative for their own governed objects and responsibilities. Attestor is authoritative for its own governed Attestations, its institutional evaluation process and resulting conclusions, and its Trust Statements, but reference alone does not make Attestor authoritative over a referenced source object.

Source Object → Retains Source Authority
Governed Reference → Preserves Source Context
Attestation → Preserves Attribution and Assertion Context
Trust Statement → Retains Attestor Authority

Governing principle: Reference does not transfer authority.

Relationship Model

The foundational relationship is directional. Eligible governed inputs form the governed basis available to an Attestation and its evaluation; an Attestation enters rule-constrained evaluation; and evaluation may produce a Trust Statement. A Trust Statement remains traceable backward to its Attestation and governed basis without converting references into support claims or Attestor-owned source records.

Eligible Governed Inputs → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

Trust Statement → derived-from / traces to → Attestation
Attestation → references → Evidence / Governed Sources
Referenced Source → retains → Source Authority

Reference ≠ Support
Reference ≠ Authority Transfer

First Production Entry-Model Demonstration

The first controlled production operation exercised the Entry Model end to end. A bounded verification-related Attestation was canonically created as ATT-2026-0001, evaluated against six eligible governed source inputs through an established Evaluation Basis and Rule-Constrained Evaluation, and produced the bounded Trust Statement TRST-2026-0001.

The operation preserved the distinction between assertion, evidence/reference, evaluation outcome, and Trust Statement. The final Trust Statement did not become a certification, a universal truth claim, or a transfer of authority from any referenced Suite institution.

Entry Model Boundary

This Entry Model establishes conceptual object structure rather than duplicating the normative detail governed by Attestor's dedicated architecture. Identifiers, controlled vocabularies, formal Attestation Types, Authority, Provenance, Eligibility, Evaluation, Relationships, Lifecycle, Versioning, Validation, Conformance, Publication, Methodology, and Production are established by their respective pages. Schemas and Validation govern applicable executable representation and machine requirements; implementation-specific mechanics remain subordinate to those contracts.
