Satoshium Attestor

Satoshium Attestor is the Satoshium Suite institution for governed Attestation and Rule-Constrained Evaluation. It creates canonical Attestations and Trust Statements while preserving the authority, provenance, relevant state, scope, and meaning of the governed inputs and references used in evaluation.

Attestor does not determine universal truth, replace certification, redefine registered records, rewrite history, establish integrity, or perform discovery. It produces Trust Statements through Rule-Constrained Evaluation of eligible governed inputs while maintaining explicit authority boundaries across the Suite.

Governing principle: Reference does not transfer authority.

Canonical Responsibility

Within the Satoshium Suite, Attestor owns governed Attestations and Trust Statements. Rule-Constrained Evaluation is the institutional process that transforms eligible governed inputs and an Attestation into a bounded Attestor conclusion expressed through a Trust Statement. Referenced certifications, Registry records, Chronicle events, Anchor integrity references, Beacon discovery metadata, Atlas intelligence, Navigator workflow context, and other governed inputs remain authoritative within their originating institutions.

Attestor → Attestation + Rule-Constrained Evaluation + Trust Statement

Authority Boundary

Attestor interprets governed inputs for a trust-specific purpose. It does not become the source of truth for the objects it references and does not silently inherit the authority of another Suite institution.

Certifier owns certification.
Registry owns registered record identity and state.
Chronicle owns preserved event history.
Anchor owns integrity references.
Beacon owns discovery signals and discovery metadata.
Attestor owns Attestations and Trust Statements.

Suite Method

Attestor follows the Suite's evidence-first, authority-bounded method: identify the subject and applicable authority, reference eligible evidence and canonical records, evaluate the attestation under Attestor rules, preserve provenance and limitations, and produce a scoped Trust Statement.
