Attestor · Corrections

Corrections

Corrections provide governed mechanisms for addressing errors, clarifications, withdrawals, supersession, and other changes affecting Attestor's own Attestations and Trust Statements.

A correction should change the current interpretation or status of an Attestor object without silently erasing what previously existed, why it changed, or who authorized the change.

Authority boundary: Attestor corrects Attestor-owned objects. It does not correct authoritative objects owned by other Suite institutions.

Correction

A correction addresses an identified error in an Attestor assertion or Trust Statement while preserving the relationship between the prior and corrected state.

Required Capability

Clarification

A clarification may refine wording, scope, context, or interpretation without necessarily reversing the underlying Attestor conclusion. The architecture must distinguish clarification from substantive correction.

Lifecycle Concern

Withdrawal

An Attestation or Trust Statement may require withdrawal when Attestor can no longer stand behind the assertion or conclusion under its governing rules. Withdrawal should remain attributable and historically traceable.

Lifecycle Concern

Supersession

A later Attestor object may supersede an earlier one when new evidence, changed conditions, corrected information, or a new evaluation produces a replacement state. The relationship between versions must remain explicit.

Lifecycle Concern

Provenance of Change

Corrections and related changes should preserve who or what authorized the change, what changed, why it changed, when it changed, and which Attestor object or version was affected.

Required Principle

Historical Traceability

Prior states should remain traceable according to Attestor's eventual retention and publication rules so reviewers can distinguish the historical record from the currently effective state.

Required Principle

Correction Is Not a New Canonical Object

This reconciliation does not establish a separate “Correction Record” as an additional Attestor canonical object. Correction may ultimately be implemented through lifecycle state, versioning, relationships, replacement Attestations, replacement Trust Statements, or another governed mechanism established during advanced architecture.

Attestor's canonical responsibility remains: Trust Statement.

Correction Does Not Rewrite Source Authority

If a referenced Certification Package, Registry record, Chronicle Entry, Anchor Integrity Reference, Beacon Discovery Signal or Discovery Metadata, or another governed source changes, Attestor may update its own evaluation or issue a correction, withdrawal, or superseding Trust Statement as appropriate.

Attestor does not alter the originating object. The source institution remains responsible for its own correction and lifecycle rules.

Reference does not transfer authority.

Current State and Historical State

Correction architecture should allow a reviewer to identify both the currently effective Attestor state and the historically relevant prior state. Preserving history does not require presenting a superseded or withdrawn conclusion as current.

Prior State → Governed Change → Current State
Provenance and relationship remain visible across the transition.

Advanced Architecture Required

The exact correction model remains to be established. Advanced Attestor architecture should determine correction triggers, lifecycle states, versioning, supersession relationships, withdrawal treatment, authorization, validation, publication behavior, and how corrected or superseded Trust Statements are represented.
