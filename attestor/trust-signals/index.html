Attestor · Legacy / Descriptive Trust Signals

Trust Signals

Within Attestor, a trust signal is a descriptive concept for information or an indicator that may be relevant to the evaluation of an Attestation or the support of a Trust Statement.

“Trust signal” is retained only as historical and descriptive language for trust-relevant evaluation context. It is not a canonical Attestor object, controlled record type, Evaluation Outcome, score, or final conclusion.

Namespace boundary: Attestor trust signals are not Beacon Discovery Signals. Beacon owns Discovery Signals and Discovery Metadata.

Attestation Context

The existence, attribution, status, scope, or relationship of an Attestation may provide information relevant to an Attestor evaluation. The Attestation remains the governed assertion; the signal is only the trust-relevant meaning considered in context.

Evaluation Context

Evidence Context

Evidence may contribute trust-relevant information through its provenance, relevance, authority, consistency, limitations, or relationship to an assertion. The evidence itself remains distinct from any interpretation derived from it.

Evaluation Context

Authoritative Outcome Context

Certification, validation, review, registration, integrity, history, discovery, or other governed outcomes may be relevant to Attestor evaluation when properly referenced. Their original institutional meaning and authority must remain intact.

Authority-Bounded

Historical & Relationship Context

Historical events, repeated conditions, relationships, changes, and other preserved context may affect how an assertion is evaluated when those facts are supported by eligible evidence or authoritative records.

Evaluation Context

Accountability Context

Attribution, transparent correction, supersession, withdrawal, responsibility, and reviewable provenance may be relevant to an Attestor evaluation without automatically establishing a favorable or unfavorable conclusion.

Evaluation Context

Conflicting or Uncertain Context

Trust-relevant information may support, limit, conflict with, or leave uncertainty around an assertion. Attestor should preserve ambiguity where the governed inputs do not justify a stronger conclusion.

Established Requirement

Not a Beacon Discovery Signal

Beacon and Attestor use information for different institutional purposes. Beacon produces Discovery Signals and Discovery Metadata so authoritative information can be discovered and navigated. Attestor evaluates trust-relevant inputs in support of Trust Statements.

Beacon → Discovery Signal / Discovery Metadata
Attestor → Trust Statement

No Automatic Direction or Score

The earlier Attestor model described trust signals as positive, negative, neutral, mixed, or uncertain. Those directional labels are not part of the adopted Attestor architecture. Attestor does not establish a generic scoring, reputation, weighting, or directional signal model.

Trust-relevant information must be evaluated in context; it does not become a score merely because Attestor considers it.

Relationship to Trust Statements

Trust-relevant indicators may contribute to an Attestor evaluation only through governed evidence, authoritative references, provenance, scope, status, and applicable Attestor rules. They do not independently produce a Trust Statement.

Eligible Governed Inputs → Attestation → Rule-Constrained Evaluation → Trust Statement

The production operation referenced BEAC-2026-0001 while preserving Beacon's authority over its Discovery Signal / Discovery Metadata. The Beacon object was not converted into an Attestor Trust Signal, and Attestor did not acquire Beacon's source authority.
