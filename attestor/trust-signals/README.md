# Trust Signals

## Purpose

Trust signals are pieces of information that may influence how users interpret, evaluate, or understand a statement, attestation, identity, record, organization, or event.

Within Attestor, trust signals provide contextual information that may contribute to trust-related judgments.

Trust signals do not determine trust.

They provide information relevant to trust.

---

## What Is a Trust Signal?

A trust signal is an observation, indicator, record, relationship, event, or piece of information that may influence trust-related evaluations.

Examples include:

* Attestations
* Verification outcomes
* Historical performance
* Reputation indicators
* Evidence references
* Corrections
* Source transparency
* Accountability records

Trust signals help provide context.

---

## Why Trust Signals Matter

Trust decisions often involve incomplete information.

Users frequently ask:

* Why should this statement be trusted?
* What information supports it?
* What historical context exists?
* What reputation information is available?

Trust signals help provide information relevant to those questions.

---

## Trust Signal Philosophy

Trust signals inform judgment.

Trust signals do not replace judgment.

A trust signal may:

* Increase confidence
* Decrease confidence
* Clarify context
* Raise questions
* Support investigation

Trust signals are informational rather than authoritative.

---

## Types of Trust Signals

### Attestation Signals

Signals derived from attestations.

Examples:

* Identity attestations
* Qualification attestations
* Participation attestations
* Ownership attestations

Attestations may provide insight into trust-related relationships.

---

### Evidence Signals

Signals derived from supporting evidence.

Examples:

* Documented support
* Independent references
* Source materials
* Verification artifacts

Evidence may strengthen or weaken confidence.

---

### Reputation Signals

Signals derived from historical behavior and participation.

Examples:

* Historical activity
* Prior contributions
* Community observations
* Historical performance

Reputation provides long-term context.

---

### Verification Signals

Signals derived from verification activities.

Examples:

* Certifications
* Verification outcomes
* Review findings
* Validation records

Verification signals may contribute important context.

---

### Accountability Signals

Signals related to responsibility and transparency.

Examples:

* Clear attribution
* Identifiable attestors
* Transparent records
* Public corrections

Accountability often influences trust evaluations.

---

### Historical Signals

Signals derived from historical records.

Examples:

* Prior attestations
* Historical events
* Correction history
* Reputation development

History provides valuable trust context.

---

## Positive and Negative Signals

Trust signals are not inherently positive.

Some signals may strengthen confidence.

Others may reduce confidence.

Examples:

### Potentially Positive

* Strong evidence
* Consistent history
* Transparent attribution
* Successful verification

### Potentially Negative

* Missing evidence
* Contradictory records
* Frequent corrections
* Unclear attribution

Interpretation remains context dependent.

---

## Trust Signals and Trust

Trust signals contribute information.

Trust is a judgment.

A simplified relationship may be represented as:

```text id="g6m4yx"
Trust Signal → Trust Evaluation
```

Signals influence understanding.

They do not determine outcomes.

Attestations contribute information that may later influence trust evaluations.

## Trust Signals and Evidence

Evidence may generate trust signals.

* Supporting documentation

* Historical records
* Source references

---

## Trust Signals and Reputation

Reputation may be viewed as the accumulation of trust signals over time.

Examples include:

* Historical activity
* Prior attestations
* Verification outcomes
* Community interactions

Trust signals contribute to reputation development.

---

## Trust Signals and Corrections

Corrections may themselves become trust signals.

* Transparent corrections
* Retractions
* Error acknowledgments
* Revision histories

The willingness to document corrections may strengthen credibility.

---

## Guiding Principles

### Transparency

Trust signals should remain visible whenever practical.

### Attribution

Signal origins should remain identifiable.

### Traceability

Signals should support review and investigation.

### Context

Signals should remain connected to relevant information.

### Preservation

Historical trust signals should remain accessible.

---

## Long-Term Vision

As Attestor evolves, trust signals may become foundational components of broader trust frameworks, reputation systems, governance structures, interoperability standards, and digital trust networks.

The goal is not to automate trust.

The goal is to preserve information relevant to trust.

---

## Guiding Statement

The purpose of a trust signal may be summarized as:

```text id="w7c5kn"
Trust signals do not tell us what to believe.

They help explain why belief may exist.
```
