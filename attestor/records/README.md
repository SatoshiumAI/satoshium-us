# Records

## Purpose

Records provide the structured foundation through which attestations, evidence, trust-related information, reputation signals, corrections, and associated relationships can be documented and preserved.

Within Attestor, records serve as the persistent representation of trust-related information.

Records help ensure that information remains traceable, reviewable, and historically accessible.

---

## What Is a Record?

A record is a structured representation of information maintained within Attestor.

Records may document:

* Attestations
* Evidence
* Reputation signals
* Trust relationships
* Corrections
* Sources
* Claims
* Historical events

Records help preserve context over time.

---

## Why Records Matter

Trust-related information often depends upon historical context.

Without records it becomes difficult to understand:

* Who made a statement
* When it was made
* Why it was made
* What evidence supports it
* What changes occurred later

Records help preserve these relationships.

---

## Record Philosophy

Records should preserve information.

Records should preserve context.

Records should preserve history.

Records should support review.

Attestor seeks to document trust-related information rather than obscure it.

---

## Types of Records

### Attestation Records

Records documenting attestations made by an attestor.

Examples:

* Identity attestations
* Qualification attestations
* Participation attestations
* Ownership attestations

---

### Evidence Records

Records documenting supporting evidence.

Examples:

* Source references
* Supporting documents
* Verification references
* Historical materials

---

### Reputation Records

Records documenting reputation-related information.

Examples:

* Reputation events
* Historical participation
* Reputation indicators
* Community observations

---

### Trust Records

Records documenting trust-related relationships and assessments.

Examples:

* Trust relationships
* Confidence indicators
* Trust signals
* Trust observations

---

### Correction Records

Records documenting modifications, corrections, amendments, or retractions.

Examples:

* Administrative corrections
* Evidence corrections
* Attestation corrections
* Retractions

---

### Relationship Records

Records documenting relationships among entities.

Examples:

* Identity relationships
* Organizational relationships
* Evidence relationships
* Record relationships

---

## Record Components

Most records may include:

### Identifier

A unique record identifier.

---

### Record Type

The classification of the record.

---

### Subject

The entity, claim, event, or object associated with the record.

---

### Creator

The party responsible for creating the record.

---

### Timestamp

The date and time associated with the record.

---

### Supporting Information

Relevant context, evidence, references, or related records.

---

### Status

Current record status.

Examples:

* Active
* Corrected
* Archived
* Retracted
* Superseded

---

## Record Lifecycle

### Creation

A record is created.

### Documentation

Relevant information is recorded.

### Association

Relationships to other records are established.

### Review

Records may be examined or updated.

### Preservation

Records are retained for future reference.

## Guiding Principles

### Transparency

Records should remain understandable.

### Attribution

Creators should remain identifiable whenever practical.

### Traceability

Records should support review and investigation.

### Preservation

Historical information should remain available.

### Accountability

Records should support responsible documentation.
