# Atlas Record

## Purpose

The Atlas Record serves as the trust-related reference connecting Attestor to jurisdiction intelligence, media intelligence, and contextual information maintained within Atlas.

Atlas provides data.

Attestor provides trust context.

Atlas Records help preserve relationships between trust-related information and the underlying jurisdictional, geographic, media, and contextual records that may influence trust evaluations.

---

## Why Atlas Records Exist

Trust-related decisions often depend upon context.

Questions frequently arise such as:

* Where did this event occur?
* Which jurisdiction is involved?
* What regulatory environment exists?
* What media sources reported the information?
* What contextual information may influence interpretation?

Atlas Records help connect trust-related information to relevant contextual data.

---

## Relationship to Atlas

Atlas serves as the data layer of the Satoshium ecosystem.

A simplified relationship may be represented as:

```text id="v8j4ka"
Atlas → Data
Attestor → Trust
```

Atlas helps provide context.

Attestor helps preserve trust-related interpretations and relationships associated with that context.

## What an Atlas Record Contains

An Atlas Record may contain references to:

* Jurisdiction records
* Media records
* Geographic information
* Regulatory context
* Economic indicators
* Historical jurisdiction data
* Related trust records

Atlas Records function primarily as contextual references.

Authority over Atlas data remains with Atlas.

## Guiding Principles

### Context Matters

Trust-related information should preserve relevant context.

### Attribution

Referenced Atlas records should remain identifiable.

### Traceability

Relationships should support review and investigation.

### Transparency

Contextual information should remain understandable.

### Separation of Responsibilities

Atlas remains responsible for data.
Attestor remains responsible for trust context.

---

## Long-Term Vision

As Attestor evolves, Atlas Records may become foundational components for documenting how jurisdictional, geographic, regulatory, and media contexts influence trust-related information across digital ecosystems.

The goal is not to merge data and trust.

The goal is to preserve their relationship.
