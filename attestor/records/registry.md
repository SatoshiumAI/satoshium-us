# Registry Record

## Purpose

The Registry Record serves as the trust-related reference connecting Attestor to structured records maintained within Registry.

Registry organizes records.

Attestor provides trust context.

Registry Records help preserve relationships between attestations, evidence, trust signals, corrections, certifications, identities, and the structured records that represent them.

---

## Why Registry Records Exist

Trust-related information often depends upon records that can be:

* Located
* Referenced
* Reviewed
* Preserved
* Audited

Users frequently want to know:

* Does a record exist?
* What record supports this claim?
* What information has been documented?
* Has the record changed over time?

Registry Records help answer these questions.

---

## Relationship to Registry

Registry serves as the records layer of the Satoshium ecosystem.

A simplified relationship may be represented as:

```text id="v7m4dk"
Registry → Records
Attestor → Trust
```

Registry preserves structured records.

Attestor preserves trust-related context associated with those records.

## What a Registry Record Contains

A Registry Record may contain references to:

* Registry identifiers
* Record classifications
* Record status
* Related attestations
* Related evidence
* Related trust signals
* Associated corrections
* Historical references

Registry Records function primarily as record-context references.

Authority over record management remains with Registry.

## Guiding Principles

### Records Should Be Traceable

Users should be able to review record relationships.

### Attribution Matters

Record origins should remain identifiable.

### Transparency Matters

Record context should remain understandable.

### Preservation Matters

Historical records should remain accessible.

### Separation of Responsibilities

Registry manages records.

Attestor preserves trust context.

---

## Long-Term Vision

As Attestor evolves, Registry Records may become foundational components for documenting how structured records contribute to trust evaluations, accountability frameworks, reputation systems, and historical understanding across digital ecosystems.

The goal is not merely to preserve records.

The goal is to preserve their trust significance.
