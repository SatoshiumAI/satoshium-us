# Satoshium Registry Purpose

## Overview

The `registry/purpose/` directory contains the public Purpose page and supporting documentation that explain why Satoshium Registry exists, what institutional problem it solves, what authority it holds, and how it serves the broader Satoshium Suite.

The public page is published through:

```text
registry/purpose/index.html
```

This `README.md` serves as the directory-level documentation for that page.

---

## Purpose of This Directory

The Purpose directory explains the institutional reason for Satoshium Registry.

Registry exists to create and maintain durable, structured, publicly discoverable Satoshium Registry Entries, or SREGs, for authoritative records produced across the Satoshium Suite.

Registry preserves:

- identity;
- classification;
- source attribution;
- public references;
- relationships;
- versions;
- Registry Status;
- Registry Lifecycle State;
- correction history;
- supersession history;
- revocation history;
- archival continuity;
- the path back to the Source Institution.

---

## Why Registry Exists

The Satoshium Suite creates many authoritative records, including:

- jurisdiction intelligence;
- canonical jurisdiction records;
- certifications;
- certified records;
- historical events;
- integrity references;
- discovery signals;
- trust statements;
- workflow definitions;
- media resources;
- institutional artifacts.

Without a dedicated catalog institution, these records may remain scattered across repositories, websites, packages, schemas, and public pages.

Registry exists to reduce that fragmentation.

It provides a durable institutional layer through which authoritative records can remain identifiable, classifiable, referenceable, related, version-aware, and discoverable over time.

---

## The Institutional Problem

Creation alone does not guarantee continuity.

As the Suite grows, users must still be able to determine:

- what record exists;
- who created it;
- where its authoritative source is located;
- which Registry Record Type applies;
- what Registry Identifier identifies the SREG;
- what Source-System Identifier identifies the Source Record;
- what its current Registry Status is;
- what its current Registry Lifecycle State is;
- what the Source-Record Status is;
- which versions apply;
- what other records are related;
- how prior states remain discoverable.

Registry exists to answer those questions consistently.

---

## Registry Mission

The mission of Satoshium Registry is to maintain the public catalog layer of the Satoshium Suite through durable, attributable, version-aware, relationship-aware SREGs.

Registry does not merely collect information.

Registry creates structured Registry objects that preserve the public context surrounding authoritative records.

---

## Canonical Operational Object

Registry's canonical operational object is the:

```text
Satoshium Registry Entry
```

Abbreviation:

```text
SREG
```

A SREG identifies, classifies, references, connects, versions, and preserves the discoverability of an Authoritative Source Record without transferring authority away from the institution that created it.

---

## Canonical Operational Hierarchy

Registry is organized through four distinct layers:

```text
Satoshium Registry
  ↓
Registry Entry (SREG)
  ↓
Registry Record Type
  ↓
Authoritative Source Record
```

### Satoshium Registry

The institution responsible for Registry operations and the public catalog.

### Registry Entry (SREG)

The canonical Registry operational object.

### Registry Record Type

The controlled primary classification assigned to the SREG.

### Authoritative Source Record

The record created and maintained by the originating institution.

---

## Core Registry Purposes

### Identity

Registry assigns and preserves stable Registry Identifiers for SREGs.

### Classification

Registry assigns approved Registry Record Types and controlled classifications.

### Source Attribution

Registry identifies the Source Institution and preserves the Source-System Identifier and canonical source reference.

### Relationships

Registry preserves typed relationships among SREGs, Source Records, institutions, certifications, events, integrity references, attestations, discovery signals, and workflows.

### Status and Lifecycle

Registry preserves the current institutional condition of the SREG while keeping it separate from the status and lifecycle of the Source Record.

### Versions and Corrections

Registry preserves versions, updates, corrections, supersession, revocation, retirement, and archival history.

### Publication

Registry publishes consistent human-readable and machine-readable forms of the SREG.

### Preservation

Registry preserves identity, provenance, relationships, versions, and discoverability after records change or leave active use.

---

## Registry Authority

Registry is authoritative for Registry-owned information, including:

- Registry Identifier;
- Registry Record Type;
- Registry Status;
- Registry Lifecycle State;
- Registry Entry Version;
- Registry relationships;
- Registry correction history;
- Registry publication history;
- Registry catalog presentation.

Registry authority applies to the SREG.

It does not extend to the substantive content or institutional meaning of the Source Record.

---

## Source Authority

The Source Institution remains authoritative for:

- Source Record content;
- Source-System Identifier;
- Source-Record version;
- Source-Record Status;
- Certification Outcome;
- Certification Status;
- attestation conclusion;
- historical event meaning;
- integrity reference;
- discovery signal;
- jurisdiction intelligence;
- workflow definition;
- ownership and licensing.

Registry may report or reference source-controlled values.

Registry does not redefine them.

## Relationship to Suite Institutions

### Atlas

Creates jurisdiction intelligence, canonical jurisdiction records, evidence resources, machine-readable packages, and generation manifests.

### Certifier

Creates Certification Packages, Certification Process Reports, Certification Receipts, and structured certified records.

### Registry

Creates SREGs that catalog and connect authoritative institutional records.

### Chronicle

Creates and preserves historical events and institutional chronology.

### Anchor

Creates and preserves integrity references, hashes, timestamps, signatures, and durable verification points.

### Beacon

Creates discovery signals and metadata that help records be found.

### Attestor

Creates trust statements, attestations, validations, and supporting references.

### Navigator

Creates workflow definitions and coordinates cross-system operational activity.

Each institution retains authority over its own canonical object.

---

## Interoperability Purpose

Registry allows Suite institutions to remain independent while their records become consistently:

- identifiable;
- referenceable;
- classifiable;
- related;
- version-aware;
- discoverable.

Registry does not operate another institution's canonical object.

It creates the Registry object that catalogs that source.

---

## First Operational Path

The first operational certification-to-Registry path is:

```text
Atlas Resource
  ↓
Certifier Evaluation
  ↓
Certification Package
  ↓
SCRD
  ↓
SREG
```

The Certification Package remains Certifier's canonical certification object.

The SCRD remains a Certifier-owned certified record.

The SREG remains Registry's canonical catalog object.

This is one interoperability path.

Registry may catalog authoritative records from every approved Suite institution.

---

## Long-Term Purpose

Registry exists so records remain understandable even when:

- public pages move;
- repositories change;
- schemas evolve;
- Source Records receive new versions;
- status changes;
- institutions mature;
- records are corrected;
- records are superseded;
- records are revoked;
- records are retired;
- records are archived;
- new Suite systems are introduced.

Registry preserves the durable identity and structured context needed to find and interpret those records later.

---

## What Registry Does Not Do

Registry does not:

- certify subjects;
- create Certification Outcomes;
- create attestations;
- rewrite Source Records;
- replace Source-System Identifiers;
- create ownership;
- create legal rights;
- create regulatory approval;
- establish truth merely by registration;
- absorb institutional authority;
- require blockchain publication;
- guarantee continued availability of external sources.

Registration creates a Registry Entry.

It does not create the authority of the source.

## Guiding Principles

- Registry exists to preserve discoverability.
- SREG is Registry's canonical operational object.
- Registry catalogs Authoritative Source Records.
- Source Institutions retain source authority.
- Registry Identifier and Source-System Identifier remain distinct.
- Registry Status and Source-Record Status remain distinct.
- Registry Lifecycle and Source Lifecycle remain distinct.
- Relationships should be typed and attributable.
- Versions should remain independently traceable.
- Human-readable and machine-readable forms should agree.
- Prior states should remain discoverable.
- Registration does not create authority.

---

## Disclaimer

The Registry Purpose documentation explains the institutional role of Satoshium Registry.

It does not itself create:

- registration;
- certification;
- attestation;
- verification;
- ownership;
- legal rights;
- regulatory approval;
- endorsement;
- affiliation;
- Source Institution authority.

Those forms of authority remain with the applicable Source Institution, Source Record, rights holder, governing authority, or responsible external system.
