# Satoshium Registry FAQ

## Frequently Asked Questions

---

### What is Satoshium Registry?

Satoshium Registry is the public record layer of the Satoshium ecosystem.

Its purpose is to identify, organize, classify, catalog, and preserve records across Satoshium tools, jurisdictions, media, signals, certifications, and future attestations.

---

### Why does Registry exist?

Registry exists because information becomes difficult to maintain when it is scattered across multiple systems.

Registry provides structure, organization, discoverability, and continuity.

---

### Does Registry create authority?

No.

Registry records information and relationships.

Creating a Registry entry does not create authority, ownership, certification, legal rights, or verification.

---

### Is Registry the same as Certifier?

No.

Certifier evaluates information against standards and produces certification records.

Registry catalogs those records and makes them discoverable.

---

### Is Registry the same as Chronicle?

No.

Chronicle records historical events, milestones, and ecosystem development.

Registry catalogs records and references.

---

### Is Registry the same as Anchor?

No.

Anchor focuses on preservation and integrity references.

Registry focuses on organization and discoverability.

---

### Is Registry the same as Attestor?

No.

Attestor is expected to support future verification and attestation activities.

Registry maintains structured records and references.

---

### What kinds of records can Registry catalog?

Initial categories include:

- Tools
- Jurisdictions
- Media
- Signals
- Certifications
- Attestations

Additional categories may be added in future versions.

---

### What is a Registry Record?

A Registry Record is a structured entry maintained within Registry.

Records contain references, classifications, metadata, and relationships.

---

### What is a Registry Entry?

A Registry Entry is the public representation of a Registry Record.

Entries help users discover and understand information within the ecosystem.

---

### What is a classification?

A classification assigns a record to a defined category.

Classifications improve organization and discoverability.

---

### What is a schema?

A schema defines the structure of a Registry record.

Schemas help maintain consistency across different record types.

---

### Can records change over time?

Yes.

Records may be updated, superseded, retired, archived, or expanded as information evolves.

Registry seeks to preserve continuity during those changes.

---

### Does Registry preserve history?

Yes.

Registry supports historical references and record continuity whenever practical.

Historical context is an important part of long-term record management.

---

### Can Registry reference external resources?

Yes.

Registry may reference documents, websites, repositories, reports, media, certifications, and other resources.

Registry does not necessarily control or own those resources.

---

### Is Registry decentralized?

The long-term architecture of Registry may evolve over time.

Current versions are focused on establishing record structures, classifications, and interoperability standards.

---

### Is Registry complete?

No.

Registry is an evolving framework and is currently in an early development phase.

Additional record types, schemas, policies, and interoperability features are expected in future releases.

---

### What is the ultimate goal of Registry?

The ultimate goal of Registry is to ensure that records remain discoverable, understandable, connected, and preservable across time.

---

## Guiding Statement

> Information may be created anywhere.
>
> Registry exists so it can be found again later.
