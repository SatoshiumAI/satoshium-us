Satoshium Registry Lifecycle

Registry Lifecycle defines how Registry Entries move through registration, update, supersession, revocation, and archival.

Lifecycle states answer a simple question: what is the current institutional condition of a Registry Entry?

Purpose

The Registry Lifecycle provides a common model for understanding the status of Registry Entries over time.

Not Synchronization

Synchronization describes technical alignment between systems. Lifecycle describes the institutional state of a Registry Entry.

Version Awareness

Registry Entries may change over time. Lifecycle states help preserve which version is current, which version has been replaced, and which records remain historically important.

Preservation

Registry does not erase the history of a record merely because it changes. Lifecycle states preserve continuity across updates, corrections, revocations, and archival decisions.

Lifecycle Overview

A Registry Entry may pass through several states during its institutional life. These states provide visibility into whether the entry is awaiting registration, currently recognized, updated, superseded, revoked, or archived.

Pending Registration → Registered → Active → Updated → Superseded → Revoked → Archived

Pending Registration

The entry has been identified for registration but has not yet been formally entered into the Registry.

Registered

The entry has been formally created within the Registry and assigned its Registry Identifier.

Active

The entry is current, discoverable, and recognized as the active Registry representation of the referenced record.

Updated

The entry has been modified to reflect new information, corrected references, revised metadata, or other Registry-maintained changes.

Superseded

The entry has been replaced by a newer Registry Entry or newer version while remaining preserved for historical reference.

Revoked

The entry has been withdrawn from active recognition due to invalidation, reversal, material error, loss of source authority, or another stated reason.

Archived

The entry is no longer active but remains preserved as part of the historical Registry record.

Archived does not mean deleted. It means preserved outside active operational use.

Updates and Corrections

Registry Entries may require updates when public references change, metadata improves, relationships are added, identifiers are corrected, or source records evolve.

Updates should preserve continuity. The Registry should make clear whether an entry was corrected, revised, superseded, or revoked.

Revocation and Supersession

Revocation and supersession serve different purposes. A superseded entry has been replaced by a newer version or entry. A revoked entry has been withdrawn from active recognition.

Superseded records preserve succession. Revoked records preserve accountability.

Relationship to SREG

Lifecycle states apply to Satoshium Registry Entries. The SREG remains the Registry’s canonical object, while lifecycle states describe the current condition of that object within the Registry.

This allows Registry to preserve records without claiming authority over the systems that produced them.
