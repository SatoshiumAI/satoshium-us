# Registry Records

## Overview

The records directory contains Registry records that identify, classify, catalog, and preserve information across the Satoshium ecosystem.

Records are the primary operational artifacts maintained by Registry.

They provide structured references that support discoverability, continuity, organization, and interoperability.

---

## Purpose

Records exist to answer several important questions:

- What exists?
- What category does it belong to?
- What is its current status?
- What is related to it?
- How can it be found later?

The records directory serves as the foundation for answering those questions consistently.

---

## Record Categories

Current Registry record categories include:

### Tools

Records describing ecosystem applications, services, frameworks, and systems.

Examples:

- Atlas
- Certifier
- Registry
- Chronicle
- Anchor
- Attestor

---

### Jurisdictions

Records describing geographic and governmental entities.

Examples:

- Countries
- States
- Provinces
- Territories
- Regions

---

### Media

Records describing public media resources.

Examples:

- Videos
- Images
- Thumbnails
- Audio resources
- Media pages

---

### Signals

Records documenting public signals, announcements, and informational references.

---

### Certifications

Records documenting certification activities and outputs.

---

### Attestations

Records documenting future verification and attestation activities.

---

### Historical Records

Records preserving milestones, releases, and significant ecosystem events.

## Record Structure

Individual records are generally expected to include:

- Identifier
- Title
- Record Type
- Classification
- Status
- Metadata
- References
- Related Records

Record structures may be refined through future schema development.

---

## Record Lifecycle

Records may progress through stages such as:

```text
Create
   ↓
Classify
   ↓
Catalog
   ↓
Maintain
   ↓
Preserve
```

The objective is to maintain useful records over time.

---

## Relationship to Registry

Registry provides the framework.

Records provide the content.

Together they create an organized public record system.
