Satoshium Registry Corrections

Corrections improve Registry records while preserving continuity, transparency, and historical context whenever practical.

Registry recognizes that information evolves, understanding improves, and occasional errors occur. The correction process exists to support accuracy without unnecessarily erasing history.

Why Corrections Exist

Registry records may contain incomplete information, outdated references, metadata issues, classification errors, or other inaccuracies that become apparent over time.

Transparency

Corrections should be understandable. Users should be able to determine that information was improved and, when practical, understand why.

Continuity

Registry seeks to preserve historical context whenever practical. Corrections should improve records without unnecessarily removing meaningful historical information.

Accuracy

The primary objective of a correction is to improve the quality, usefulness, and reliability of Registry records.

Metadata Corrections

Corrections may involve identifiers, titles, classifications, statuses, references, dates, or other metadata elements.

Reference Corrections

Corrections may also involve broken references, missing links, relationship updates, or improved cross-referencing between records.

Correction Workflow

Registry follows a simple correction sequence designed to balance accuracy and continuity.

Issue Identified → Review → Correction → Documentation → Publication

Minor and Major Corrections

Minor corrections may include typographical issues, formatting improvements, metadata updates, and link repairs.

Major corrections may include classification changes, structural revisions, record mergers, record splits, or substantial updates to existing records.

The larger the change, the more important documentation and transparency become.

Correction Philosophy

Registry does not view corrections as a means of erasing history. Corrections are intended to improve understanding while preserving continuity whenever practical.

The goal is not perfection. The goal is improvement.
