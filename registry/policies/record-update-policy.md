# Registry Record Update Policy

## Purpose

This policy establishes how Registry records should be updated after creation.

The objective is to support accuracy, continuity, discoverability, and long-term preservation while maintaining consistency across the Registry framework.

---

## Policy Statement

Registry records may be updated when new information becomes available, existing information changes, errors are corrected, relationships evolve, or additional context improves the usefulness of a record.

Updates should improve the quality of the Registry without unnecessarily disrupting continuity.

---

## Guiding Principles

Record updates should be guided by the following principles:

### Accuracy

Updates should improve the accuracy of Registry records.

### Transparency

Significant updates should be understandable.

### Continuity

Updates should preserve historical context whenever practical.

### Consistency

Updates should follow established Registry structures and policies.

### Preservation

Important information should not be removed unnecessarily.

---

## Reasons for Updates

A record may be updated for reasons including:

- New information
- Metadata improvements
- Classification changes
- Additional references
- Relationship updates
- Structural improvements
- Error correction
- Status changes

Additional update scenarios may emerge over time.

---

## Update Workflow

```text
Update Need Identified
          ↓
Review
          ↓
Modification
          ↓
Validation
          ↓
Publication
```

This workflow provides a consistent framework for record maintenance.

---

## Common Update Types

### Metadata Updates

Examples include:

- Title changes
- Identifier improvements
- Date updates
- Status modifications
- Category updates

Metadata updates help maintain record quality.

---

### Reference Updates

Reference updates may include:

- New links
- Updated references
- Additional cross-references
- Relationship corrections

Reference updates improve discoverability.

---

### Classification Updates

Classification updates occur when a record requires revised categorization.

Examples include:

- New taxonomy placement
- Hierarchy adjustments
- Category refinements

Classification updates should preserve continuity whenever practical.

---

### Content Updates

Content updates may include:

- Expanded descriptions
- Additional context
- Clarified explanations
- Documentation improvements

Content updates should improve usability and understanding.

---

## Status Updates

Records may move between statuses such as:

- Draft
- Active
- Published
- Superseded
- Retired
- Archived

Status changes should be documented clearly whenever practical.

---

## Historical Continuity

Registry should preserve historical context whenever practical.

Examples may include:

- Update notes
- Historical references
- Change logs
- Version histories

The objective is to improve transparency and continuity.

---

## Major Updates

Major updates may include:

- Structural revisions
- Classification redesigns
- Record mergers
- Record splits
- Significant metadata changes

Major updates should be documented whenever practical.

---

## Minor Updates

Minor updates may include:

- Typographical corrections
- Formatting improvements
- Link updates
- Minor metadata adjustments

Minor updates generally require less documentation.

---

## Validation Considerations

Before publication, updates should be reviewed for:

- Structural consistency
- Classification accuracy
- Metadata quality
- Reference integrity

Validation improves long-term reliability.

---

## Relationship to Other Policies

This policy works alongside:

- Record Creation Policy
- Correction Policy
- Record Retirement Policy

Together these policies support the Registry record lifecycle.

---

## Future Enhancements

Future Registry capabilities may support:

- Version control systems
- Automated update tracking
- Change histories

- Workflow automation

Future enhancements should remain aligned with Registry's continuity mission.

---

## Policy Summary

Updates should improve records while preserving continuity whenever practical.

The objective is not simply to change records.

The objective is to improve them.
