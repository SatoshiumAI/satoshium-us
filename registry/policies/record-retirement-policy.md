# Registry Record Retirement Policy

## Purpose

This policy establishes how Registry records should be retired when they are no longer active, relevant, maintainable, or appropriate for continued operational use.

The objective is to preserve continuity while clearly communicating the status of retired records.

---

## Policy Statement

Retirement is the process of changing the operational status of a Registry record while preserving its historical existence.

Retirement does not necessarily imply deletion.

Whenever practical, retired records should remain discoverable as part of the historical record.

---

## Guiding Principles

Record retirement should be guided by the following principles:

### Continuity

Historical context should be preserved whenever practical.

### Transparency

Retirement decisions should be understandable.

### Preservation

Retired records may still possess historical value.

### Discoverability

Users should be able to determine that a record previously existed.

---

## Reasons for Retirement

A record may be retired for reasons including:

- Obsolescence
- Replacement by a newer record
- Structural reorganization
- Project discontinuation
- Classification changes
- Historical preservation purposes

Additional reasons may arise as Registry evolves.

---

## Retirement Workflow

```text
Retirement Need Identified
             ↓
Review
             ↓
Status Change
             ↓
Reference Updates
             ↓
Historical Preservation
             ↓
Publication
```

This workflow provides a consistent approach to retirement activities.

---

## Retirement Status

Retired records should generally receive a status such as:

- Retired
- Superseded
- Archived

The selected status should communicate the record's condition clearly.

---

## Superseded Records

A record may be superseded when a newer record replaces it.

Whenever practical, the retired record should reference the replacement.

Example:

```text
Record A
   ↓
Superseded By
   ↓
Record B
```

This supports continuity and navigation.

---

## Archived Records

Archived records remain preserved for historical purposes.

Archived records are generally not considered active operational records.

However, they may continue to provide historical value.

---

## Retirement Metadata

When practical, retired records should include:

- Retirement date
- Retirement status
- Retirement reason
- Replacement references
- Historical notes

These elements improve transparency and future understanding.

---

## Historical Preservation

Retirement should not unnecessarily remove historical context.

Whenever practical, retired records should remain available for:

- Research
- Historical review
- Cross-reference support
- Continuity purposes

Preservation is a core Registry objective.

---

## Deletion Considerations

Deletion should be approached cautiously.

In most situations, retirement is preferred over removal.

Deletion may be appropriate for:

- Duplicate records
- Invalid records
- Test records
- Erroneous entries

When practical, significant deletions should be documented.

---

## Relationship to Other Policies

This policy works alongside:

Together these policies support the Registry record lifecycle.

---

## Future Enhancements

Future Registry capabilities may support:

- Automated retirement workflows
- Historical version tracking
- Retirement notifications
- Relationship mapping
- Expanded archival support

Future enhancements should remain aligned with Registry's continuity mission.

---

## Policy Summary

Retirement is not the same as disappearance.

A retired record may still contribute value through historical context, references, and continuity.

Registry seeks to preserve that value whenever practical.

---

## Guiding Statement

> Records may cease to be active.
>
> Their history may still matter.
>
> Registry seeks to preserve both realities.
