# Registry Record Retirement Policy

## Policy Status

**Policy Name:** Registry Record Retirement Policy  
**Policy Type:** Institutional Registry Policy  
**Applies To:** Satoshium Registry Entries (SREGs) leaving active operational use  
**Initial Policy Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Active  
**Effective Date:** 2026-08-01  

---

## Purpose

This policy establishes the binding institutional requirements for retiring a Satoshium Registry Entry, or SREG, when it is no longer appropriate for active operational use.

The purpose of retirement is to communicate a change in Registry condition while preserving:

- Registry identity;
- Source Institution attribution;
- Authoritative Source Record references;
- prior versions;
- relationships;
- status history;
- lifecycle history;
- correction history;
- supersession or revocation history;
- long-term discoverability.

Retirement does not mean disappearance.

Retirement changes operational use while preserving historical continuity.

## Policy Statement

Registry may retire a SREG when the entry should no longer remain in active operational use.

A retirement action must:

- identify the reason;
- identify the applicable lifecycle action;
- preserve the Registry Identifier;
- preserve prior SREG versions;
- preserve source attribution;
- preserve material references and relationships;
- distinguish retirement from deletion;
- distinguish Registry action from Source Institution action;
- remain consistent across official human-readable and machine-readable forms;
- preserve historical discoverability.

Registry must not silently remove an operational SREG merely because it is obsolete, replaced, revoked, or no longer publicly available.

---

## Scope

This policy applies to Registry actions involving:

- supersession;
- revocation;
- retirement;
- archival;
- source discontinuation;
- source removal;
- institutional discontinuation;
- obsolete Registry Entries;
- invalid Registry Entries;
- duplicate Registry Entries;
- discontinued Record Types;
- entries no longer suitable for active catalog use.

This policy also governs:

- retirement review;
- lifecycle transition;
- Registry Status change;
- replacement references;
- retirement records;
- publication notices;
- historical preservation;
- deletion review.

---

## Outside Scope

This policy does not authorize Registry to:

- retire or revoke a Source Record;
- revoke a certification;
- alter an attestation;
- retire an Atlas resource;
- remove a Chronicle event;
- invalidate an Anchor reference;
- deactivate a Beacon signal;
- terminate a Navigator workflow;
- control an external institution;
- delete source-controlled content.

Registry governs the SREG.

The Source Institution governs the Source Record.

---

## Retirement Terminology

Retirement is an umbrella policy category.

The final Registry action should use the most accurate institutional designation.

### Superseded

The SREG or SREG version has been replaced by a newer Registry Entry or version.

### Revoked

Registry has withdrawn the SREG from active recognition for a documented reason.

### Retired

The SREG is no longer used operationally under Registry policy but does not require revocation or supersession.

### Archived

The SREG is preserved outside active operational use.

These terms must not be used interchangeably.

---

## Guiding Principles

### Continuity

Retirement must preserve the historical path from the prior SREG to any replacement, successor, archival record, or Source Record.

### Transparency

The reason and date of retirement should be understandable.

### Preservation

Prior versions, references, and material history should remain preserved.

### Discoverability

Users should be able to determine that the SREG previously existed and why it is no longer active.

### Authority Separation

Registry retirement must not imply that the Source Institution retired or invalidated the Source Record unless the source says so.

### Proportionality

The documentation and review required should reflect the significance of the retirement action.

### Consistency

All official representations must agree on the resulting Registry condition.

## Reasons for Retirement

A SREG may be retired because of:

- replacement by a newer SREG;
- replacement by a newer SREG version;
- obsolete classification;
- structural reorganization;
- discontinued Registry Record Type;
- invalid registration;
- duplicate registration;
- material Registry error;
- loss of source authority;
- Source Record retirement;
- Source Record removal;
- Source Record revocation;
- project discontinuation;
- institution discontinuation;
- migration to a successor system;
- preservation-only status;
- another documented governance reason.

The retirement reason must identify whether the cause originates in Registry, the Source Institution, or an external condition.

## Retirement Need Identification

A retirement need may be identified through:

- Registry review;
- Source Institution notice;
- Source Record status change;
- correction review;
- duplicate detection;
- schema migration;
- Record Type reorganization;
- project discontinuation;
- invalidation finding;
- public-reference failure;
- lifecycle review;
- governance action.

Identification does not itself authorize retirement.

## Authority and Cause Determination

Before retirement, Registry must determine:

- whether the issue belongs to Registry or the Source Institution;
- whether the SREG remains valid;
- whether the Source Record still exists;
- whether a successor Source Record exists;
- whether a replacement SREG exists;
- whether the correct action is supersession, revocation, retirement, archival, correction, or update;
- whether legal, rights, privacy, or access restrictions apply.

The selected action must match the actual institutional condition.

## Supersession Policy

A SREG may be superseded when:

- a newer SREG replaces it;
- a new Registry Identifier is required;
- the Source Record is replaced by a successor record;
- the original classification can no longer accurately represent the object;
- institutional restructuring creates a distinct successor object;
- a replacement entry is required for architectural coherence.

A superseded SREG must preserve:

- original Registry Identifier;
- final SREG version;
- supersession date;
- supersession reason;
- replacement Registry Identifier or version;
- prior Source Record reference;
- successor Source Record reference, when applicable;
- prior relationships;
- replacement relationships;
- final Registry Status;
- final Lifecycle State.

```text
Prior SREG
  ↓
Superseded By
  ↓
Replacement SREG
```

Supersession means replaced.

It does not necessarily mean invalid.

---

## Revocation Policy

A SREG may be revoked when:

- registration was invalid;
- source authority was materially misidentified;
- the SREG materially misrepresented the Source Record;
- the Source Record was fraudulent or invalid and Registry policy requires withdrawal;
- a material error cannot be corrected while preserving the same Registry identity;
- an approved governance action requires withdrawal from active recognition.

A revocation must preserve:

- Registry Identifier;
- affected SREG version;
- revocation reason;
- revocation date;
- responsible Registry action;
- Source Record impact;
- resulting Registry Status;
- resulting Lifecycle State;
- correction history;
- replacement or archival path;
- public revocation notice.

Revocation preserves accountability.

It does not require deletion.

---

## Retirement Policy

A SREG may be designated Retired when:

- it is no longer used operationally;
- it is not invalid;
- it does not require a replacement;
- it is not merely being archived following supersession or revocation;
- Registry policy requires removal from active catalog use.

A retired SREG should preserve:

- Registry identity;
- retirement reason;
- retirement date;
- final operational version;
- source references;
- related records;
- final Registry Status;
- final Lifecycle State;
- successor information, if any.

---

## Archival Policy

A SREG may be archived when it should remain preserved outside active operational use.

Archived records should remain available through appropriate:

- archival indexes;
- supersession indexes;
- revocation indexes;
- historical catalogs;
- version-history pages;
- machine-readable archival records.

Archival must preserve:

- Registry Identifier;
- prior versions;
- Source Institution;
- Authoritative Source Record;
- source references;
- Registry Status history;
- Lifecycle State history;
- relationships;
- correction history;
- supersession history;
- revocation history;
- archival date;
- archival reason.

Archived does not mean deleted.

## Registry Status and Source-Record Status

Registry Status must remain separate from Source-Record Status.

Example:

```text
Source-Record Status: Retired
Registry Status: Active Historical Entry
Registry Lifecycle State: Active
```

Registry may preserve the SREG as an active historical catalog entry while accurately reporting that the Source Record has been retired.

Another example:

```text
Source-Record Status: Active
Registry Status: Revoked
Registry Lifecycle State: Revoked
```

Registry may revoke the SREG because of a Registry defect even though the Source Record remains active.

## Replacement References

When a successor exists, the retired SREG must reference it.

The successor should also reference the prior SREG when the relationship is relevant.

Examples:

```text
SREG A → superseded by → SREG B
```

```text
SREG B → supersedes → SREG A
```

```text
Retired SREG → replaced by → Successor Source Record
```

Replacement references improve continuity, navigation, and machine-readable relationship integrity.

---

## Relationship Updates

Retirement may require updates to:

- source relationships;
- successor relationships;
- parent-child relationships;
- certification relationships;
- attestation relationships;
- historical relationships;
- integrity references;
- discovery signals;
- workflow references;
- catalog indexes.

Retirement must not silently leave active relationships pointing to an obsolete current object when a successor exists.

Historical relationships should remain preserved where appropriate.

## Version Requirements

Retirement must preserve version distinctions.

Registry should distinguish among:

- Registry Entry Version;
- SREG schema version;
- Record-Type Profile version;
- Registry specification version;
- Source-Record version;
- Source Institution publication version.

A retirement action should preserve the final active SREG version and may produce a new version containing the retirement state.

Example:

```text
SREG Version 1.2.0 — Active
  ↓
Retirement Record
  ↓
SREG Version 1.3.0 — Superseded
```

## Source Removal or Unavailability

A Source Record may become:

- removed;
- unavailable;
- private;
- inaccessible;
- relocated;
- retired;
- superseded;
- revoked.

Source unavailability does not automatically require SREG deletion.

Registry may preserve:

- prior source location;
- last known Source-Record Status;
- date availability changed;
- integrity references;
- Chronicle relationships;
- replacement source reference;
- archival context;
- appropriate Registry Status and Lifecycle State.

## Deletion Policy

Deletion is exceptional.

Retirement, revocation, supersession, or archival should normally be preferred.

Deletion may be considered for:

- unregistered test fixtures;
- accidental duplicates with no independent history;
- malicious content;
- unlawful publication;
- sensitive information published in error;
- security-critical information;
- entries created without valid Registry authority;
- data that cannot lawfully or safely remain published.

Deletion must not be used merely to simplify history.

---

## Deletion Review

Before deleting an official or previously public SREG, Registry should determine:

- whether archival is sufficient;
- whether redaction is sufficient;
- whether revocation is sufficient;
- whether the Registry Identifier should remain reserved;
- whether a deletion notice can remain;
- whether legal or privacy requirements require full removal;
- whether historical metadata can be preserved safely;
- whether related records require updates.

Significant deletion should be documented unless prohibited by law, safety, privacy, or security requirements.

---

## Duplicate Records

When duplicates exist, Registry should determine whether:

- one SREG is authoritative;
- the records represent distinct Source Records;
- a merger is required;
- one entry should be superseded;
- one entry should be revoked;
- one entry is an unregistered test record eligible for deletion.

Duplicate resolution must preserve any meaningful historical activity.

---

## Record Mergers and Splits

Retirement may occur as part of a merger or split.

### Merger

Multiple SREGs may be replaced by one successor SREG.

### Split

One SREG may be replaced by multiple successor SREGs.

The retirement action must preserve:

- all prior Registry Identifiers;
- successor identifiers;
- relationship mapping;
- reason;
- effective date;
- source impact;
- version history;
- public continuity.

---

## Validation Requirements

Before publication, Registry should validate that:

- retirement authority is established;
- the selected action is accurate;
- required metadata exists;
- status and lifecycle transitions are valid;
- affected versions are identified;
- successor references are valid;
- Source Record impact is documented;
- relationships are updated;
- human-readable and machine-readable forms agree;
- historical preservation requirements are satisfied;
- the applicable schema validates.

---

## Prohibited Retirement Practices

Registry must not:

- silently delete an official SREG;
- use retirement to conceal a correction;
- use retirement to conceal a revocation;
- mark a Source Record retired when only the SREG is retired;
- erase prior versions solely for convenience;
- remove successor references;
- reuse a retired Registry Identifier;
- publish inconsistent retirement states;
- collapse Registry Status and Source-Record Status;
- imply that retirement revokes source authority;
- remove historical relationships without documented reason.

---

## Review and Approval

The level of review required should correspond to:

- retirement action;
- reason;
- historical significance;
- Source Institution impact;
- interoperability impact;
- legal or privacy implications;
- whether deletion is proposed;
- whether a replacement exists;
- whether multiple records are affected.

The reviewing role and approval requirements should be defined by Registry Procedure.

## Policy Summary

Retirement changes the operational condition of a SREG while preserving its institutional history.

A compliant retirement action should make clear:

- which SREG is affected;
- why it is leaving active use;
- whether it is superseded, revoked, retired, or archived;
- which version is affected;
- whether the Source Record changed;
- whether a successor exists;
- how references and relationships were updated;
- how history remains discoverable.

Retirement is not the same as disappearance.

---

## Disclaimer

This policy governs Registry-owned retirement actions.

It does not authorize Registry to retire or invalidate:

- Source Records;
- certifications;
- attestations;
- external legal records;
- ownership;
- licensing;
- regulatory determinations;
- Source Institution authority.

Those remain controlled by the applicable Source Institution or external authority.
