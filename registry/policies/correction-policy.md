# Registry Correction Policy

## Purpose

This policy establishes how corrections should be handled within Satoshium Registry.

The objective is to improve accuracy while preserving continuity, transparency, and historical context whenever practical.

---

## Policy Statement

Errors may occur.

Records may contain:

- Incorrect information
- Outdated information
- Incomplete information
- Broken references
- Classification issues
- Metadata errors

When errors are identified, Registry should seek to correct them in a transparent and consistent manner.

---

## Guiding Principles

Corrections should be guided by the following principles:

### Transparency

Corrections should be understandable.

### Continuity

Corrections should avoid unnecessary loss of historical context.

### Accuracy

Corrections should improve the quality of Registry records.

### Preservation

Important historical information should not be discarded unnecessarily.

---

## Types of Corrections

### Metadata Corrections

Examples include:

- Titles
- Identifiers
- Categories
- Status values
- Dates

Metadata corrections generally have minimal impact on record continuity.

---

### Classification Corrections

Classification corrections occur when a record has been assigned to an incorrect category.

Examples include:

- Incorrect record type
- Incorrect hierarchy placement
- Incorrect taxonomy assignments

Classification updates should preserve references whenever practical.

---

### Reference Corrections

Reference corrections address issues involving:

- Broken links
- Incorrect relationships
- Missing cross-references
- Invalid resource references

Reference corrections improve discoverability and usability.

---

### Content Corrections

Content corrections address factual, structural, or descriptive issues within a Registry record.

Content corrections should preserve continuity whenever practical.

---

## Correction Workflow

```text
Issue Identified
       ↓
Review
       ↓
Correction
       ↓
Documentation
       ↓
Publication
```

This workflow provides a consistent process for managing corrections.

---

## Historical Context

When practical, Registry should preserve evidence that a correction occurred.

Possible approaches include:

- Change logs
- Update notes
- Historical references
- Version tracking

The objective is to support transparency and continuity.

---

## Minor Corrections

Minor corrections may include:

- Typographical errors
- Formatting issues
- Minor metadata updates
- Link corrections

Minor corrections generally do not require extensive documentation.

---

## Major Corrections

Major corrections may include:

- Significant classification changes
- Structural changes
- Record mergers
- Record splits
- Historical corrections

Major corrections should be documented whenever practical.

---

## Record Status During Corrections

Records may remain active while corrections are made.

In certain situations, records may be temporarily designated as:

- Draft
- Under Review
- Superseded

Status changes should be applied consistently.

---

## Relationship to Other Policies

This policy works alongside:

- Record Creation Policy
- Record Update Policy
- Record Retirement Policy

Together these policies support the Registry record lifecycle.

---

## Future Enhancements

Future versions of Registry may support:

- Formal version histories
- Change tracking systems
- Automated correction workflows
- Expanded audit references

These capabilities may improve transparency and continuity over time.

---

## Policy Summary

The purpose of a correction is not to erase history.

The purpose of a correction is to improve accuracy while preserving continuity whenever practical.

---

## Guiding Statement

> Errors may occur.
>
> Corrections improve records.
>
> Transparency preserves trust.
>
> Registry seeks to support all three.
