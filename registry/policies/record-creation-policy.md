# Registry Record Creation Policy

## Purpose

This policy establishes how Registry records should be created within the Satoshium Registry framework.

The objective is to promote consistency, organization, discoverability, and continuity across all Registry record types.

---

## Policy Statement

Registry records should be created whenever information, resources, references, or relationships require structured documentation within the Registry framework.

Record creation is intended to improve organization and long-term discoverability.

---

## Guiding Principles

Record creation should be guided by the following principles:

### Clarity

Records should be understandable.

### Consistency

Records should follow established structures whenever practical.

### Discoverability

Records should support efficient navigation and retrieval.

### Continuity

Records should contribute to long-term organizational continuity.

### Preservation

Records should help preserve important information and relationships.

---

## Eligible Record Types

Registry records may be created for categories including:

- Tools
- Jurisdictions
- Media
- Signals
- Certifications
- Attestations
- Historical Records
- Reference Records
- Future approved categories

Additional categories may be introduced over time.

---

## Record Creation Workflow

```text
Need Identified
       ↓
Classification
       ↓
Record Creation
       ↓
Metadata Assignment
       ↓
Cross-Reference Review
       ↓
Publication
```

This workflow provides a consistent framework for introducing new records.

---

## Required Elements

Whenever practical, a Registry record should include:

### Identifier

A unique identifier for the record.

### Title

A descriptive record title.

### Record Type

The primary classification assigned to the record.

### Status

The current status of the record.

### Metadata

Relevant descriptive information.

### References

Associated internal or external references when appropriate.

---

## Classification Requirements

Each record should be assigned to at least one primary record category.

Examples include:

```text
Tool
Jurisdiction
Media
Signal
Certification
Attestation
Historical Record
Reference Record
```

Consistent classification improves discoverability.

---

## Metadata Requirements

Metadata should be sufficient to support future discovery and interpretation.

Examples may include:

- Identifier
- Title
- Category
- Creation date
- Status
- Related records
- References

Metadata requirements may expand over time.

---

## Cross-Reference Considerations

When practical, records should document relevant relationships.

Examples include:

- Related tools
- Associated jurisdictions
- Related certifications
- Historical references
- Media references

Cross-references improve navigation and continuity.

---

## Record Status Assignment

New records should receive an initial status.

Common statuses include:

- Draft
- Active
- Published
- Archived

Status values may evolve as Registry develops.

---

## Publication Considerations

Before publication, records should be reviewed for:

- Classification accuracy
- Metadata completeness
- Reference quality
- Structural consistency

The objective is to improve long-term usability.

---

## Relationship to Other Policies

This policy works alongside:

- Correction Policy
- Record Update Policy
- Record Retirement Policy

Together these policies support the Registry record lifecycle.

---

## Future Enhancements

Future versions of Registry may support:

- Automated record creation
- Schema validation
- Metadata enforcement
- Workflow automation
- Expanded interoperability

Future enhancements should remain consistent with Registry's organizational mission.

---

## Policy Summary

A Registry record should be created whenever structured documentation improves discoverability, continuity, organization, or preservation.

The goal is not simply to record information.

The goal is to make information easier to find and understand later.

---
