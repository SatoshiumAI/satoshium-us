# Satoshium Registry Transparency

## Overview

The `registry/transparency/` directory contains the public institutional page and supporting documentation for the Satoshium Registry Transparency framework.

The public page is published through:

```text
registry/transparency/index.html
```

This `README.md` serves as the directory-level documentation for that page.

---

## Purpose of This Directory

The purpose of this directory is to define how Satoshium Registry makes its authority, requirements, decisions, conditions, limitations, changes, and history visible and accountable.

The Transparency framework applies to:

- institutional authority;
- Registry scope;
- Registry limitations;
- Registry Rules;
- Registry Policies;
- Registry Procedures;
- Governance Decisions;
- schemas;
- Record-Type Profiles;
- Controlled Values;
- Source Authority review;
- Registrability decisions;
- identifier assignment and resolution;
- relationships;
- provenance;
- Validation;
- Publication;
- Versioning;
- Corrections;
- Registry Status;
- Registry Lifecycle;
- Catalog inclusion and presentation;
- Registry History;
- exceptions;
- conflicts;
- restrictions;
- withdrawals;
- archival preservation.

Transparency allows users and systems to understand not only what Registry publishes, but how Registry reached, maintained, qualified, corrected, restricted, or withdrew that publication.

---

## Constitutional Position

Transparency operates across the entire Registry architecture.

```text
Authority and Rules
  ↓
Decisions and Procedures
  ↓
Validation and Publication
  ↓
Notices, Corrections, and History
  ↓
Public Accountability
```

Transparency reveals Registry conduct.

It does not require disclosure of information that must remain restricted.

---

## Core Principle

```text
Authority must be clear.
Limitations must be visible.
History must remain discoverable.
```

A transparent Registry should make material institutional conditions understandable without violating legitimate privacy, rights, safety, security, contractual, lawful, or institutional restrictions.

## Why Transparency Matters

Registry trust depends on:

- visible authority;
- clear institutional boundaries;
- published Rules and Policies;
- understandable Procedures;
- attributable Governance Decisions;
- accurate Source Authority representation;
- disclosed Validation outcomes;
- visible Publication conditions;
- transparent Corrections;
- clear version distinctions;
- preserved historical states;
- honest limitations and uncertainty.

Transparency supports responsible interpretation of Registry records and institutional actions.

## Transparency Characteristics

### Visible

Material Registry authority, conditions, and changes should be discoverable.

### Attributable

Decisions and actions should identify the responsible authority, role, process, or workflow.

### Qualified

Warnings, restrictions, uncertainty, conditions, and limitations should be disclosed.

### Historically Preserved

Current presentation should not erase prior states or material institutional history.

### Proportionate

Disclosure should match the legitimate public need while respecting lawful and institutional restrictions.

## Institutional Authority Transparency

Registry should make clear:

- Registry owns SREGs;
- Registry owns Registry-controlled metadata;
- Source Institutions retain authority over Source Records;
- Certifier controls certification outcomes and certification artifacts;
- Attestor controls attestations and trust statements;
- Chronicle controls Chronicle-created event records;
- Anchor controls Anchor-created integrity records;
- Beacon controls Beacon-created signals;
- Navigator controls Navigator-created workflow definitions and execution records;
- other Suite institutions retain authority within their domains;
- registration does not transfer ownership;
- registration does not transfer Source Authority.

## Transparency Levels

### Public Disclosure

Fully visible through Registry Publication.

### Public Summary

General facts are visible while sensitive supporting detail remains restricted.

### Controlled Access

Available only under defined authorization or access conditions.

### Internal Record

Preserved for institutional operation, audit, Governance, and review.

### Non-Disclosure

Withheld when disclosure would create unlawful or material harm.

The selected level should be proportionate to:

- the information;
- the risk;
- the authority;
- the public need;
- privacy;
- rights;
- safety;
- security;
- contractual obligations;
- lawful restrictions.

## Transparency Validation

Validation should confirm:

- required public fields are present;

## Invalid Transparency Conditions

Invalid conditions include:

- material limitation omitted;
- Source Authority misrepresented;
- Registry Validation represented as Certification;
- Correction applied without notice;
- historical version presented as current;
- restriction hidden while materially affecting interpretation;
- restricted information exposed;
- public summary materially misleading;
- decision authority omitted;
- superseded Rule presented as current;
- superseded Policy presented as current;
- superseded Procedure presented as current;
- superseded record presented as current;
- Catalog prominence presented as institutional rank;
- human-readable and machine-readable disclosure mismatch.

- Restricted information must remain protected.
