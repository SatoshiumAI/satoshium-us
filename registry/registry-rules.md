# Satoshium Registry Rules

## Purpose

These rules establish the foundational operating principles for Satoshium Registry.

The objective of Registry is to create a structured, discoverable, and maintainable public record framework across the Satoshium ecosystem.

---

# Rule 1 — Registry Records Information

Registry records information.

Registry does not create authority, ownership, certification, verification, rights, or truth.

Registry documents and organizes records.

---

# Rule 2 — Records Must Be Identifiable

Every Registry record should be identifiable.

Records should contain sufficient information to distinguish them from other records.

Examples may include:

- Titles
- Identifiers
- Categories
- Status information
- Associated references

---

# Rule 3 — Records Must Be Classifiable

Each record should belong to at least one defined record type.

Examples include:

- Tools
- Jurisdictions
- Media
- Signals
- Certifications
- Attestations

Classification improves discoverability and organization.

---

# Rule 4 — Records Should Be Discoverable

Registry should prioritize discoverability.

Records should be structured so users can locate, understand, and navigate information efficiently.

---

# Rule 5 — References Should Be Preserved

Registry should preserve meaningful references whenever practical.

References provide context and improve continuity.

---

# Rule 6 — Historical Continuity Matters

When records change, Registry should preserve historical context whenever practical.

Updates should not unnecessarily erase important historical information.

---

# Rule 7 — Corrections Should Be Transparent

Errors may occur.

When corrections are made, Registry should strive to document those corrections openly and transparently.

Transparency improves trust and continuity.

---

# Rule 8 — Cross-References Are Encouraged

Relationships between records should be documented whenever useful.

Cross-references improve navigation and contextual understanding.

---

# Rule 9 — Schemas Promote Consistency

Schemas should be used whenever practical.

Consistent record structures improve interoperability, maintenance, and future automation.

---

# Rule 10 — Registry Should Remain Flexible

Registry is intended to evolve.

New record types, classifications, schemas, and policies may be added as ecosystem requirements develop.

---

# Rule 11 — Preservation Over Perfection

A useful record is generally better than no record.

Registry prioritizes continuity and preservation while remaining open to future refinement.

---

# Rule 12 — Documentation Over Memory

Important information should be documented whenever practical.

Registry seeks to reduce dependence on institutional memory alone.

---

# Rule 13 — Interoperability Matters

Registry should remain compatible with other Satoshium systems whenever practical.

Examples include:

- Atlas
- Certifier
- Chronicle
- Anchor
- Attestor

Interoperability improves long-term ecosystem cohesion.

---

# Rule 14 — Future Compatibility

Registry decisions should consider future expansion whenever practical.

The framework should support growth without requiring unnecessary restructuring.

---

# Rule 15 — Records Should Outlive Their Creation

Registry is designed to preserve information beyond the moment of creation.

The long-term usefulness of a record should be considered whenever possible.

---

## Registry Philosophy

Registry exists to create structure.

Structure improves discovery.

Discovery improves understanding.

Understanding improves continuity.

Continuity improves preservation.

---

## Guiding Statement

> Record what exists.
>
> Organize what is recorded.
>
> Preserve what is organized.
>
> Allow future generations to find it again.
