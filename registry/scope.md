# Satoshium Registry Scope

## Purpose

This document defines the intended scope of Satoshium Registry.

Scope establishes what Registry is designed to manage, what responsibilities it supports, and what activities fall outside its intended role.

---

## Registry Scope Statement

Satoshium Registry exists to identify, organize, classify, catalog, cross-reference, and preserve records across the Satoshium ecosystem.

Registry serves as the public record layer connecting information, resources, and references into a structured framework that can be maintained over time.

---

## Included Within Scope

Registry is intended to support the following activities.

### Record Identification

Registry records that a resource exists.

Examples include:

- Tools
- Pages
- Reports
- Media
- Signals
- Certifications
- Attestations
- Historical records

---

### Record Classification

Registry assigns records to structured categories.

Classification supports organization and discoverability.

---

### Cataloging

Registry maintains structured collections of records.

Cataloging improves navigation and long-term management.

---

### Cross-Referencing

Registry documents relationships between records.

Cross-references provide context and improve understanding.

---

### Historical Continuity

Registry supports historical references and continuity across time.

Records may retain links to previous versions, related resources, and significant milestones.

---

### Metadata Management

Registry may maintain descriptive metadata associated with records.

Examples include:

- Titles
- Categories
- Identifiers
- Status
- Dates
- References

---

### Schema Support

Registry may define and maintain schemas used to structure records consistently.

Schemas improve interoperability and future automation.

---

### Ecosystem Interoperability

Registry is intended to support interoperability across Satoshium systems.

Examples include:

- Atlas
- Certifier
- Chronicle
- Anchor
- Attestor

---

## Outside Scope

The following activities are outside the intended scope of Registry.

### Certification

Registry does not certify information.

Certification is expected to be performed by Certifier.

---

### Verification

Registry does not independently verify information.

Future verification activities may be supported by Attestor.

---

### Integrity Preservation

Registry may reference integrity records but does not serve as the primary preservation mechanism.

Anchor is expected to support preservation-related functions.

---

### Historical Narratives

Registry may catalog historical records but is not primarily responsible for maintaining ecosystem history.

Chronicle is expected to support historical documentation.

---

### Ownership Determination

Registry does not determine ownership.

A Registry entry does not create ownership rights or authority.

---

### Legal Authority

Registry is not a governmental, legal, regulatory, or authoritative registry.

Registry records information for organizational purposes.

---

### Truth Determination

Registry does not determine objective truth.

Registry records information, classifications, references, and relationships.

---

## Scope Boundaries

Registry focuses on structure rather than evaluation.

Registry focuses on organization rather than certification.

Registry focuses on references rather than verification.

Registry focuses on continuity rather than authority.

These boundaries help maintain a clear separation of responsibilities across the ecosystem.

---

## Future Expansion

Registry is designed to evolve.

Additional record categories, schemas, policies, and interoperability features may be introduced over time.

Future expansion should remain consistent with Registry's organizational mission.

---

## Scope Summary

Registry exists to answer several fundamental questions:

- What exists?
- Where is it located?
- What category does it belong to?
- What is related to it?
- What is its status?
- How can it be found later?

The scope of Registry is defined by those objectives.

---

## Guiding Statement

> Registry does not create information.
>
> Registry does not certify information.
>
> Registry does not verify information.
>
> Registry organizes information so it can be found again.
