Satoshium Registry Definitions

Satoshium Registry Definitions establishes the canonical institutional vocabulary used across Registry architecture, operations, schemas, publications, records, and public documentation.

Definitions preserve shared meaning. They distinguish similar terms, prevent authority drift, and ensure that human-readable and machine-readable Registry materials describe the same institutional concepts.

Constitutional Position

Registry Definitions operates beneath Suite Standards and Suite Methodology and supports every Registry framework, including Scope, Rules, Policies, Procedures, Schemas, Controlled Values, Validation, Publication, Governance, Catalog, History, and Transparency.

Suite Standards → Suite Methodology → Registry Definitions → Registry Frameworks and Records
Definitions explain what institutional terms mean. Controlled Values identify which governed values may be used.

Why Definitions Matter

Registry depends on precise distinctions between institutions, objects, identifiers, authority, status, lifecycle, versions, validation, publication, and historical records.

The Core Question

Definitions asks:

What does this term mean within Satoshium Registry, and how is it distinct from related terms?

Registry's Responsibility

Registry must define its canonical vocabulary, maintain those definitions, preserve prior meanings, and prevent silent semantic change.

Registry's Limitation

Registry Definitions must not redefine Source Institution terminology or another Suite institution's authoritative concepts outside Registry scope.

Canonical Definition Model

Term → Canonical Definition → Authority Boundary → Approved Usage → Versioned History

Definition Requirements

A governed Registry definition should identify:

  • canonical term;
  • canonical definition;
  • institutional authority;
  • approved usage;
  • distinction from related terms;
  • aliases or deprecated terminology;
  • effective date;
  • definition version;
  • related Registry frameworks;
  • change history.

Canonical

One current institutional definition governs Registry usage.

Distinct

Similar terms remain separated when their authority or meaning differs.

Versioned

Material definition changes preserve prior meanings and effective periods.

Operational

Definitions support schemas, Procedures, Validation, Publication, and records.

Foundational Architecture

Satoshium Registry ↓ Satoshium Registry Entry (SREG) ↓ Registry Record Type ↓ Authoritative Source Record

This layered model distinguishes the Registry institution, its canonical operational object, the Registry classification applied to that object, and the Source Record whose authority remains with its originating institution.

Institutional Terms

Satoshium Suite

The coordinated institutional environment containing Satoshium Registry and the other Suite institutions, governed by Suite Standards, Suite Methodology, and Suite Interoperability.

Satoshium Registry

The Suite institution responsible for canonical registration, SREG identity, Registry classification, Registry-owned metadata, Registry Relationships, Validation, Publication, Versioning, Corrections, Status, Lifecycle, Catalog presentation, History, Transparency, and related institutional Governance.

Registry Institution

Satoshium Registry understood as an enduring institutional authority, distinct from any individual SREG, web page, schema, Catalog, or software implementation.

Suite Institution

A defined institution within Satoshium Suite that holds authority over a distinct functional domain, such as Atlas, Certifier, Registry, Chronicle, Anchor, Beacon, Attestor, or Navigator.

Source Institution

The institution, organization, authority, system, or recognized origin responsible for the authoritative meaning of a Source Record.

External Institution

A Source Institution or governing authority outside Satoshium Suite whose records, identifiers, rights, or decisions may be referenced by Registry without becoming Registry-owned.

Registry Object Terms

Satoshium Registry Entry (SREG)

The canonical Registry operational object used to identify, classify, describe, relate, version, publish, correct, preserve, and resolve a registrable Source Record or Registry-governed object.

Registry Entry

The full-form name for a SREG. Within Registry architecture, “Registry Entry” and “SREG” refer to the same canonical operational object.

Registry Record

A general term for a record maintained by Registry. Depending on context, it may refer to a SREG or to a Registry-owned supporting record such as a Validation Record, Publication Record, Correction Record, History Record, Governance Decision Record, Scope Record, or Transparency Record.

Registry Record Type

A governed Registry classification assigned to a SREG, such as Tool, Jurisdiction, Media, Certification, Attestation, Signal, Event, Integrity Reference, Workflow, Institution, Schema, Policy, Procedure, or Governance Decision.

Record-Type Profile

A governed schema specialization defining the additional fields, Controlled Values, relationships, Validation rules, and Publication requirements applicable to a particular Registry Record Type.

Registry Object

Any object governed or maintained directly by Registry, including SREGs, supporting Registry Records, schemas, profiles, Controlled-Value Sets, Catalog indexes, Policies, Procedures, and Governance materials.

Source Record Terms

Source Record

A record, artifact, resource, decision, event, package, statement, signal, or other source-domain object created or controlled by a Source Institution and represented by Registry without transferring its authority.

Authoritative Source Record

The Source Record recognized as the authoritative source-domain object to which a SREG points. Its substantive meaning remains under Source Institution authority.

Canonical Source

The preferred authoritative source location or representation identified by the Source Institution or supported by Registry provenance.

Derived Artifact

An artifact generated, transformed, summarized, converted, or otherwise derived from an original or canonical Source Record.

Generated Artifact

A machine-created or process-created artifact produced through an approved generation method, often accompanied by a generation manifest or integrity reference.

Source Representation

The Registry's recorded description or reference to a Source Record. A Source Representation is not the Source Record itself and does not transfer Source Authority.

Authority Terms

Source Authority

The institutional authority responsible for the substantive meaning, status, outcome, rights, or source-domain lifecycle of a Source Record.

Registry Authority

The authority held by Satoshium Registry over SREG identity, Registry-owned fields, classification, Validation, Publication, Versioning, Corrections, Status, Lifecycle, Catalog presentation, History, and Governance.

Relationship Authority

The institution, Governance decision, source evidence, or authorized process responsible for establishing or maintaining a Registry Relationship.

Decision Authority

The role, institution, governing body, or authorized process empowered to approve a Registry decision.

Delegated Authority

Authority exercised by a role, process, or institution under a documented delegation from the authority that retains ultimate accountability.

Authority Boundary

The defined limit separating Registry authority from Source Institution, other Suite institution, external legal, regulatory, governmental, or rights-holder authority.

Scope and Admission Terms

Registry Scope

The institutional, operational, technical, object, field, action, geographic, temporal, and authority boundaries within which Registry may act.

Scope Determination

A formal or operational decision establishing whether an object, responsibility, field, or action falls within Registry authority.

Scope Outcome

A governed result of Scope Determination, such as Within Scope, Within Scope with Conditions, Shared or Coordinated Scope, Restricted Scope, Outside Scope, or Indeterminate.

Shared or Coordinated Scope

A condition in which Registry has a defined registration, cataloging, publication, or preservation role while another institution retains related substantive authority.

Restricted Scope

A condition in which an object remains within Registry's institutional domain but access, Publication, indexing, or disclosure is limited.

Registrability

The determination of whether a specific object satisfies Registry requirements for registration as a SREG.

Registrability Outcome

The governed result of Registrability review, such as Registrable, Conditionally Registrable, Provisionally Registrable, Restricted Registrable, Historical Registrable, Not Registrable, Indeterminate, or Not Evaluated.

Identifier Terms

Registry Identifier

The permanent, unique, non-reused identifier assigned by Registry to a SREG or other approved Registry object.

SREG Identifier

A Registry Identifier assigned specifically to a Satoshium Registry Entry.

Source-System Identifier

An identifier assigned by the Source Institution or source system to the Authoritative Source Record.

Identifier Domain

The institutional namespace or system within which an identifier is assigned and interpreted.

Identifier State

The governed condition of an identifier, such as Reserved, Assigned, Published, Superseded, Revoked, or Archived.

Canonical Resolution

The process by which a Registry Identifier resolves to the official current Registry context while preserving access to prior and historical states.

Alias

A non-canonical alternate label, identifier, title, code, or historical reference preserved for discovery, compatibility, or interpretation.

Classification and Schema Terms

SREG Base Schema

The common structural schema defining fields and requirements shared across all Satoshium Registry Entries.

Registry Schema Specification

The Registry-level specification governing how schemas, fields, types, constraints, versions, and machine-readable representations are defined.

Schema Version

The version assigned to a schema definition used to interpret and validate Registry records.

Controlled Value

A governed canonical value, code, label, status, outcome, type, or notice approved for use within a defined Registry field or value set.

Controlled-Value Set

A governed collection of related Controlled Values, definitions, codes, labels, usage rules, versions, aliases, and historical replacements.

Canonical Definition

The current authoritative Registry meaning assigned to a term.

Relationship Terms

Registry Relationship

A typed, directed or governed association between Registry Identifiers, Source-System Identifiers, Source Records, institutions, versions, or related objects.

Relationship Type

The governed meaning assigned to a Registry Relationship, such as sourced from, references, certifies, attests to, supersedes, derived from, or archived as.

Relationship Status

The governed current condition of a Registry Relationship, such as Active, Historical, Provisional, Disputed, Superseded, Revoked, or Invalid.

Inverse Relationship

The governed reciprocal expression of a directed relationship, such as “sourced from” and “source of.”

Symmetric Relationship

A relationship whose meaning applies identically in both directions when explicitly defined as symmetric by Registry Governance.

Relationship Context

The temporal, version, authority, evidence, or scope conditions under which a Registry Relationship applies.

Provenance and Custody Terms

Provenance

The recorded origin, authority, source lineage, custody, derivation, transformation, publication, and preservation history of a Registry object or Source Record representation.

Custody

The possession, storage, stewardship, or preservation of a record or artifact. Custody does not by itself establish Source Authority.

Original Custody

Custody held by the original creator or originating institution.

Archival Custody

Custody held for preservation of a record or artifact after or apart from its original operational use.

Generation Manifest

A machine-readable or human-readable record describing how a generated artifact was produced, including source inputs, process, date, version, and integrity information.

Integrity Reference

A record or value used to support integrity verification of an artifact, record, version, or publication.

Validation Terms

Registry Validation

The process of evaluating whether a SREG or Registry object conforms to applicable Registry Rules, Policies, Procedures, schemas, profiles, Controlled Values, and Publication requirements.

Validation Record

A Registry-owned record documenting the object validated, requirements applied, date, findings, exceptions, outcome, and related action.

Validation Outcome

The governed result of Registry Validation, such as Valid, Valid with Warnings, Conditionally Valid, Invalid, Indeterminate, or Not Evaluated.

Blocking Error

A Validation finding that prevents approval, Publication, or continued operation until resolved or governed through an authorized process.

Warning

A non-blocking Validation finding that must remain visible or be reviewed but does not necessarily prevent Publication.

Validation Exception

A documented and authorized departure from an otherwise applicable Validation requirement.

Informational Finding

A Validation observation recorded for context, future review, or transparency without constituting an error or warning.

Publication Terms

Registry Publication

The authorized release of a SREG or Registry object as an official human-readable, machine-readable, downloadable, indexed, or historical Registry representation.

Publication Record

A Registry-owned record documenting publication authority, date, version, canonical URLs, Validation outcome, notices, restrictions, and related history.

Publication Readiness

The governed determination of whether a Registry object satisfies the requirements for Publication.

Publication Readiness Outcome

The governed result of Publication review, such as Ready, Ready with Notices, Restricted, or Not Ready.

Canonical Human-Readable Record

The official Registry presentation designed for human interpretation.

Canonical Machine-Readable Record

The official Registry representation designed for structured processing by systems.

Publication Notice

A visible Registry statement identifying a material condition, warning, restriction, Correction, supersession, revocation, archival state, or limitation.

Version Terms

Registry Entry Version

The version assigned by Registry to a distinct state of a SREG.

Source-Record Version

The version assigned by the Source Institution or source system to the Authoritative Source Record.

Catalog Version

The version assigned to a generated or published Registry Catalog collection, distinct from the versions of individual SREGs.

Definition Version

The version assigned to a canonical Registry definition or Definitions set.

Major Change

A material change that alters structure, meaning, compatibility, authority, or interpretation sufficiently to require a major version increment.

Minor Change

A backward-compatible material addition or refinement that does not replace the prior fundamental meaning or structure.

Patch Change

A limited correction or refinement that preserves the existing meaning, structure, and compatibility of the record.

Supersession

The governed replacement of a Registry object, version, decision, Policy, Procedure, schema, profile, or value by a successor while preserving the prior object historically.

Correction Terms

Registry Correction

An authorized change to a Registry-owned field, record, representation, or historical statement made to remedy an identified error or omission.

Correction Record

A Registry-owned record documenting the affected object or version, prior value, corrected value, reason, authority, evidence, date, and resulting version.

Correction Authority

The Registry role, institution, Governance decision, or authorized process empowered to approve a Correction.

Source Correction

A Correction made under Source Institution authority to the Source Record itself, distinct from a Registry Correction.

Silent Correction

A material change made without preserving or disclosing the prior state, Correction authority, or change history. Silent material Correction is not permitted within Registry.

Status and Lifecycle Terms

Registry Status

The current Registry condition of a SREG, such as Pending, Active, Restricted, Disputed, Superseded, Revoked, Retired, or Archived.

Registry Lifecycle State

The stage occupied by a SREG within the Registry lifecycle, such as Proposed, Under Review, Registered, Published, Updated, Superseded, Revoked, Retired, or Archived.

Source-Record Status

The source-domain status assigned to the Authoritative Source Record by the Source Institution.

Lifecycle Transition

An authorized movement from one Registry Lifecycle State to another.

Revocation

The authorized withdrawal of a SREG, relationship, identifier use, exception, or other Registry object from valid active operation.

Retirement

The intentional removal of a Registry object from active operational use without necessarily declaring it invalid.

Archival

The transition of a Registry object or artifact into a preserved historical condition with continued contextual resolution.

Governance Terms

Registry Governance

The institutional framework through which Registry authority, decision rights, roles, approvals, changes, exceptions, conflicts, delegations, reviews, and accountability are controlled.

Governance Decision

An authorized institutional decision affecting Registry Rules, Policies, Procedures, schemas, profiles, Controlled Values, Scope, operations, or other governed materials.

Governance Decision Record

A Registry-owned record documenting a Governance Decision, authority, rationale, evidence, date, scope, conditions, implementation, and history.

Registry Rule

A binding Registry requirement governing institutional or operational conduct.

Registry Policy

A governed statement defining institutional principles, requirements, decision criteria, or operating conditions under Registry Rules.

Registry Procedure

A governed operational sequence describing how Registry implements an approved Rule, Policy, framework, or decision.

Governance Exception

An authorized, limited, documented departure from an applicable Registry requirement that does not silently amend the underlying Rule or Policy.

Delegation

A documented grant of defined decision or action authority to a role, process, or institution while accountability remains traceable to the delegating authority.

Catalog Terms

Registry Catalog

The public discovery and resolution layer through which published SREGs are organized, browsed, searched, filtered, related, versioned, and accessed.

Catalog Entry

The discovery-oriented representation of a published SREG within the Registry Catalog.

Catalog Metadata

Registry-owned metadata used to support discovery, filtering, ordering, indexing, notices, and Catalog presentation without changing canonical SREG meaning.

Catalog Collection

A governed grouping of SREGs by Record Type, Source Institution, Status, Lifecycle, publication period, historical condition, or other approved classification.

Catalog Inclusion

The governed act of placing a published SREG into current or historical Catalog views.

History Terms

Registry History

The chronological, versioned, attributable, and resolvable institutional history of Registry objects, actions, decisions, versions, Corrections, transitions, Publications, Catalog releases, and Governance changes.

History Record

A Registry-owned record documenting a material Registry event, prior state, new state, date, authority, version context, evidence, and historical resolution.

History Event Type

A governed classification describing the kind of institutional event recorded in Registry History.

Historical State

A prior state of a Registry object preserved for interpretation, accountability, provenance, or archival resolution.

Historical Artifact

A preserved prior publication, record, schema, profile, Policy, Procedure, Controlled-Value Set, Catalog, or other artifact retained as part of Registry History.

Historical Resolution

The ability to locate and interpret a prior Registry state or artifact through stable identifiers, versions, relationships, or archival paths.

Transparency Terms

Registry Transparency

The institutional framework through which Registry makes its authority, requirements, decisions, conditions, limitations, versions, Corrections, restrictions, and history visible and accountable.

Transparency Record

A Registry-owned record documenting the subject, Disclosure Level, public fields, restricted fields, authority, basis, effective date, review date, and change history of a transparency decision.

Disclosure Level

The governed visibility condition applied to information, such as Public Disclosure, Public Summary, Controlled Access, Internal Record, or Non-Disclosure.

Public Summary

A limited public disclosure that communicates material facts while withholding restricted supporting information.

Restriction

A governed limitation on access, Publication, indexing, reuse, disclosure, or resolution arising from privacy, rights, safety, security, contractual, legal, Source Institution, or institutional requirements.

Withdrawal

The authorized removal of a Registry Publication or public representation from current public availability while preserving appropriate historical accountability.

Interoperability Terms

Suite Interoperability

The Suite-level framework defining how institutions exchange, reference, preserve, and coordinate records while maintaining authority boundaries.

Registry Interoperability

The Registry implementation of Suite Interoperability through identifiers, schemas, profiles, Controlled Values, relationships, provenance, mappings, Publications, and workflows.

Mapping

A documented correspondence between Registry values, fields, identifiers, or concepts and those of another system or institution.

Semantic Mapping

A mapping that identifies corresponding or related meanings while preserving known differences and authority boundaries.

Integration

A governed technical or institutional connection through which Registry exchanges, references, receives, publishes, or coordinates records with another Suite or external system.

Terms That Must Remain Distinct

  • Registry is not the same as SREG.
  • SREG is not the same as the Source Record.
  • Registry Record Type is not the same as the Source Institution's classification.
  • Registry Identifier is not the same as the Source-System Identifier.
  • Registry Entry Version is not the same as the Source-Record Version.
  • Registry Status is not the same as Registry Lifecycle State.
  • Registry Status is not the same as Source-Record Status.
  • Validation is not the same as Certification.
  • Registration is not the same as endorsement.
  • Publication is not the same as Source Authority.
  • Custody is not the same as Authority.
  • Catalog inclusion is not the same as affiliation.
  • Registry History is not the same as Chronicle event records.
  • Controlled Values are not the same as Definitions.
  • Scope is not the same as Registrability.

Definitions and Controlled Values

Definitions and Controlled Values serve related but distinct purposes.

  • Definitions establish what institutional terms mean.
  • Controlled Values establish which approved values may be used in governed fields.
“Registry Status” is a defined concept. “Active,” “Restricted,” and “Archived” are Controlled Values within that concept.

Aliases and Deprecated Terms

Registry may preserve:

  • historic terms;
  • prior names;
  • abbreviations;
  • common aliases;
  • deprecated terminology;
  • replacement terms;
  • effective periods;
  • migration guidance.

Aliases and deprecated terms should not replace current canonical terminology in new Registry materials.

Definition Versioning

A material definition change should preserve:

  • prior Definition Version;
  • new Definition Version;
  • prior definition;
  • new definition;
  • change summary;
  • reason;
  • approval authority;
  • effective date;
  • affected Registry materials;
  • migration or interpretation guidance;
  • historical publication.
Definitions may evolve. Their historical meanings must remain interpretable.

Definitions Validation

Validation should confirm:

  • canonical term is used consistently;
  • definition matches current Registry authority;
  • related terms remain distinct;
  • deprecated terminology is identified;
  • aliases are not presented as current canonical terms;
  • human-readable and machine-readable materials use compatible meanings;
  • Source Institution terminology is not silently redefined;
  • definition versions are identified when required;
  • historical meanings remain discoverable;
  • affected schemas, Policies, Procedures, and Controlled Values are aligned.

Invalid Definition Conditions

  • one term used for multiple conflicting institutional meanings;
  • Registry and Source Institution authority combined into one definition;
  • SREG treated as identical to the Source Record;
  • Registry Status confused with Registry Lifecycle State;
  • Validation represented as Certification;
  • Catalog inclusion represented as endorsement;
  • custody represented as Source Authority;
  • deprecated term presented as current without notice;
  • definition changed without version or history;
  • human-readable and machine-readable meanings conflict;
  • another Suite institution's term redefined outside Registry authority.

Definitions Governance

Registry Governance should control:

  • creation of canonical terms;
  • definition approval;
  • authority boundaries;
  • aliases;
  • deprecated terminology;
  • replacement terms;
  • Definition Versioning;
  • definition conflicts;
  • Suite terminology alignment;
  • Source Institution terminology boundaries;
  • historical preservation;
  • human-readable and machine-readable consistency.

Definition Review Triggers

Definitions should be reviewed when:

  • Suite Standards change;
  • Suite Methodology changes;
  • Suite Interoperability changes;
  • Registry Scope changes;
  • a new Registry Record Type is approved;
  • a new schema or Profile introduces a new concept;
  • a Controlled-Value Set reveals ambiguity;
  • Validation identifies inconsistent usage;
  • Source Institution terminology changes;
  • a new Suite institution is created;
  • authority conflicts expose semantic ambiguity;
  • historical records become difficult to interpret.

Relationship to Scope

Scope defines Registry authority and boundaries.

Definitions provides the canonical meanings required to describe those boundaries consistently.

Relationship to Schemas

Schemas define fields, types, structure, and constraints.

Definitions explains the institutional meaning of the concepts represented by those fields.

Schemas define structure. Definitions preserve semantic meaning.

Relationship to Policies and Procedures

Policies and Procedures should use canonical Registry terminology.

They may apply Definitions but should not silently redefine foundational terms through operational language.

Relationship to Transparency

Transparency depends on definitions that make authority, limitations, conditions, and historical changes understandable.

Public materials should not require users to infer the difference between Registry-owned concepts and source-owned concepts.

What Definitions Do Not Establish

  • Definitions do not create Source Authority.
  • Definitions do not make an object registrable.
  • Definitions do not replace Rules, Policies, or Procedures.
  • Definitions do not replace schemas or Controlled Values.
  • Definitions do not certify a Source Record.
  • Definitions do not create an attestation.
  • Definitions do not transfer ownership or rights.
  • Definitions do not override Suite Standards or Methodology.
  • Definitions do not redefine another institution's authority.
  • Definitions do not eliminate the need for context, version, or Governance.

Definitions Principle

Registry Definitions should provide stable, precise, versioned institutional meanings that preserve authority boundaries, operational consistency, historical interpretation, and semantic alignment across every Registry form.

Shared language creates coherence. Precise distinctions preserve authority. Versioned definitions preserve meaning through time.
