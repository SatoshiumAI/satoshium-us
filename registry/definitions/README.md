# Satoshium Registry Definitions

## Overview

The `registry/definitions/` directory contains the public institutional page and supporting documentation for the Satoshium Registry Definitions framework.

The public page is published through:

```text
registry/definitions/index.html
```

This `README.md` serves as the directory-level documentation for that page.

---

## Purpose of This Directory

The purpose of this directory is to establish and preserve the canonical institutional vocabulary used across Satoshium Registry.

Registry Definitions supports:

- Registry architecture;
- Registry Scope;
- Registry Rules;
- Registry Policies;
- Registry Procedures;
- Registry Schemas;
- Record-Type Profiles;
- Controlled Values;
- Source Authority;
- Registrability;
- Identifiers;
- Relationships;
- Provenance;
- Validation;
- Publication;
- Versioning;
- Corrections;
- Registry Status;
- Registry Lifecycle;
- Governance;
- Catalog;
- History;
- Transparency;
- Interoperability.

Definitions preserve shared meaning, prevent authority drift, and ensure that human-readable and machine-readable Registry materials describe the same institutional concepts.

---

## Constitutional Position

Registry Definitions operates beneath Suite Standards and Suite Methodology.

```text
Suite Standards
  ↓
Suite Methodology
  ↓
Registry Definitions
  ↓
Registry Frameworks and Records
```

Definitions provide semantic meaning.

They do not replace:

- Registry Rules;
- Registry Policies;
- Registry Procedures;
- Registry Schemas;
- Record-Type Profiles;
- Controlled Values;
- Governance Decisions.

---

## Core Principle

```text
Shared language creates coherence.
Precise distinctions preserve authority.
Versioned definitions preserve meaning through time.
```

A canonical definition should remain stable, attributable, versioned, and operationally usable across Registry materials.

---

## Why Definitions Matter

Registry depends on precise distinctions between:

- institution and record;
- Registry object and Source Record;
- Registry authority and Source Authority;
- Registry Identifier and Source-System Identifier;
- Registry Status and Registry Lifecycle State;
- Registry Entry Version and Source-Record Version;
- Validation and Certification;
- Publication and Source Authority;
- Catalog inclusion and affiliation;
- Registry History and Chronicle event records;
- Definitions and Controlled Values;
- Scope and Registrability.

Without canonical definitions, the same term may be interpreted differently across pages, schemas, workflows, publications, and historical records.

## Definition Characteristics

### Canonical

One current institutional definition governs Registry usage.

### Distinct

Similar terms remain separated when their authority or meaning differs.

### Versioned

Material definition changes preserve prior meanings and effective periods.

### Operational

Definitions support schemas, Procedures, Validation, Publication, Governance, and records.

### Historically Preserved

Prior definitions remain discoverable for interpretation of historical Registry materials.

## Foundational Architecture

```text
Satoshium Registry
  ↓
Satoshium Registry Entry (SREG)
  ↓
Registry Record Type
  ↓
Authoritative Source Record
```

This layered model distinguishes:

- the Registry institution;
- the canonical Registry operational object;
- the Registry classification applied to that object;
- the Source Record whose authority remains with its originating institution.

## Institutional Terms

### Satoshium Suite

The coordinated institutional environment containing Satoshium Registry and the other Suite institutions, governed by Suite Standards, Suite Methodology, and Suite Interoperability.

### Satoshium Registry

The Suite institution responsible for canonical registration, SREG identity, Registry classification, Registry-owned metadata, Registry Relationships, Validation, Publication, Versioning, Corrections, Registry Status, Registry Lifecycle, Catalog presentation, History, Transparency, and related institutional Governance.

### Registry Institution

Satoshium Registry understood as an enduring institutional authority, distinct from any individual SREG, web page, schema, Catalog, or software implementation.

### Suite Institution

A defined institution within Satoshium Suite that holds authority over a distinct functional domain, such as:

- Atlas;
- Certifier;
- Registry;
- Chronicle;
- Anchor;
- Beacon;
- Attestor;
- Navigator.

### Source Institution

The institution, organization, authority, system, or recognized origin responsible for the authoritative meaning of a Source Record.

### External Institution

A Source Institution or governing authority outside Satoshium Suite whose records, identifiers, rights, or decisions may be referenced by Registry without becoming Registry-owned.

## Registry Object Terms

### Satoshium Registry Entry

The canonical Registry operational object used to identify, classify, describe, relate, version, publish, correct, preserve, and resolve a registrable Source Record or Registry-governed object.

### SREG

The canonical abbreviation for Satoshium Registry Entry.

### Registry Entry

The full-form name for a SREG.

Within Registry architecture, `Registry Entry` and `SREG` refer to the same canonical operational object.

### Registry Record

A general term for a record maintained by Registry.

Depending on context, it may refer to:

- a SREG;
- Validation Record;
- Publication Record;
- Correction Record;
- History Record;
- Governance Decision Record;
- Scope Record;
- Transparency Record;
- another Registry-owned supporting record.

### Registry Record Type

A governed Registry classification assigned to a SREG.

Examples may include:

- Tool;
- Jurisdiction;
- Media;
- Certification;
- Attestation;
- Signal;
- Event;
- Integrity Reference;
- Workflow;
- Institution;
- Schema;
- Policy;
- Procedure;
- Governance Decision.

### Record-Type Profile

A governed schema specialization defining the additional fields, Controlled Values, relationships, Validation rules, and Publication requirements applicable to a particular Registry Record Type.

### Registry Object

Any object governed or maintained directly by Registry, including:

- SREGs;
- supporting Registry Records;
- schemas;
- Record-Type Profiles;
- Controlled-Value Sets;
- Catalog indexes;
- Policies;
- Procedures;
- Governance materials.

## Source Record Terms

### Source Record

A record, artifact, resource, decision, event, package, statement, signal, or other source-domain object created or controlled by a Source Institution and represented by Registry without transferring its authority.

### Authoritative Source Record

The Source Record recognized as the authoritative source-domain object to which a SREG points.

Its substantive meaning remains under Source Institution authority.

### Canonical Source

The preferred authoritative source location or representation identified by the Source Institution or supported by Registry provenance.

### Derived Artifact

An artifact generated, transformed, summarized, converted, or otherwise derived from an original or canonical Source Record.

### Generated Artifact

A machine-created or process-created artifact produced through an approved generation method, often accompanied by a generation manifest or integrity reference.

### Source Representation

The Registry's recorded description or reference to a Source Record.

A Source Representation is not the Source Record itself and does not transfer Source Authority.

## Authority Terms

### Source Authority

The institutional authority responsible for the substantive meaning, status, outcome, rights, or source-domain lifecycle of a Source Record.

### Registry Authority

The authority held by Satoshium Registry over:

- SREG identity;
- Registry-owned fields;
- classification;
- Validation;
- Publication;
- Versioning;
- Corrections;
- Registry Status;
- Registry Lifecycle;
- Catalog presentation;
- History;
- Governance.

### Relationship Authority

The institution, Governance Decision, source evidence, or authorized process responsible for establishing or maintaining a Registry Relationship.

### Decision Authority

The role, institution, governing body, or authorized process empowered to approve a Registry decision.

### Delegated Authority

Authority exercised by a role, process, or institution under a documented delegation from the authority that retains ultimate accountability.

### Authority Boundary

The defined limit separating Registry authority from:

- Source Institution authority;
- another Suite institution's authority;
- external legal authority;
- regulatory authority;
- governmental authority;
- rights-holder authority.

## Scope and Admission Terms

### Registry Scope

The institutional, operational, technical, object, field, action, geographic, temporal, and authority boundaries within which Registry may act.

### Scope Determination

A formal or operational decision establishing whether an object, responsibility, field, or action falls within Registry authority.

### Scope Outcome

A governed result of Scope Determination.

Possible outcomes may include:

- Within Scope;
- Within Scope with Conditions;
- Shared or Coordinated Scope;
- Restricted Scope;
- Outside Scope;
- Indeterminate.

### Shared or Coordinated Scope

A condition in which Registry has a defined registration, Catalog, Publication, or preservation role while another institution retains related substantive authority.

### Restricted Scope

A condition in which an object remains within Registry's institutional domain but access, Publication, indexing, or disclosure is limited.

### Registrability

The determination of whether a specific object satisfies Registry requirements for registration as a SREG.

### Registrability Outcome

The governed result of Registrability review.

Possible outcomes may include:

- Registrable;
- Conditionally Registrable;
- Provisionally Registrable;
- Restricted Registrable;
- Historical Registrable;
- Not Registrable;
- Indeterminate;
- Not Evaluated.

## Identifier Terms

### Registry Identifier

The permanent, unique, non-reused identifier assigned by Registry to a SREG or other approved Registry object.

### SREG Identifier

A Registry Identifier assigned specifically to a Satoshium Registry Entry.

### Source-System Identifier

An identifier assigned by the Source Institution or source system to the Authoritative Source Record.

### Identifier Domain

The institutional namespace or system within which an identifier is assigned and interpreted.

### Identifier State

The governed condition of an identifier.

Possible values may include:

- Reserved;
- Assigned;
- Published;
- Superseded;
- Revoked;
- Archived.

### Canonical Resolution

The process by which a Registry Identifier resolves to the official current Registry context while preserving access to prior and historical states.

### Alias

A non-canonical alternate label, identifier, title, code, or historical reference preserved for discovery, compatibility, or interpretation.

## Classification and Schema Terms

### SREG Base Schema

The common structural schema defining fields and requirements shared across all Satoshium Registry Entries.

### Registry Schema Specification

The Registry-level specification governing how schemas, fields, types, constraints, versions, and machine-readable representations are defined.

### Schema Version

The version assigned to a schema definition used to interpret and validate Registry records.

### Controlled Value

A governed canonical value, code, label, status, outcome, type, or notice approved for use within a defined Registry field or value set.

### Controlled-Value Set

A governed collection of related Controlled Values, definitions, codes, labels, usage rules, versions, aliases, and historical replacements.

### Canonical Definition

The current authoritative Registry meaning assigned to a term.

- Registry Identifiers;
- Source-System Identifiers;
- Source Records;

### Relationship Type

The governed meaning assigned to a Registry Relationship.

Examples may include:

- sourced from;
- references;
- certifies;
- attests to;
- supersedes;
- derived from;
- archived as.

### Relationship Status

The governed current condition of a Registry Relationship.

Possible values may include:

- Active;
- Historical;
- Provisional;
- Disputed;
- Superseded;
- Revoked;
- Invalid.

### Inverse Relationship

The governed reciprocal expression of a directed relationship.


### Relationship Context

The temporal, version, authority, evidence, or scope conditions under which a Registry Relationship applies.

## Provenance and Custody Terms

### Provenance

The recorded origin, authority, source lineage, custody, derivation, transformation, Publication, and preservation history of a Registry object or Source Record representation.

### Custody

The possession, storage, stewardship, or preservation of a record or artifact.

Custody does not by itself establish Source Authority.

### Original Custody

Custody held by the original creator or originating institution.

### Archival Custody

Custody held for preservation of a record or artifact after or apart from its original operational use.

### Generation Manifest

A machine-readable or human-readable record describing how a generated artifact was produced, including:

- source inputs;
- process;
- date;
- version;
- integrity information.

### Integrity Reference

A record or value used to support integrity verification of an artifact, record, version, or Publication.

## Validation Terms

### Registry Validation

The process of evaluating whether a SREG or Registry object conforms to applicable:

- Registry Rules;
- Registry Policies;
- Registry Procedures;
- schemas;
- Record-Type Profiles;
- Controlled Values;
- Publication requirements.

### Validation Record

A Registry-owned record documenting:

- the object validated;
- requirements applied;
- validation date;
- findings;
- exceptions;
- Validation Outcome;
- related action.

### Validation Outcome

The governed result of Registry Validation.

Possible values may include:

- Valid;
- Valid with Warnings;
- Conditionally Valid;
- Invalid;
- Indeterminate;
- Not Evaluated.

### Blocking Error

A Validation finding that prevents approval, Publication, or continued operation until resolved or governed through an authorized process.

### Warning

A non-blocking Validation finding that must remain visible or be reviewed but does not necessarily prevent Publication.

### Validation Exception

A documented and authorized departure from an otherwise applicable Validation requirement.

### Informational Finding

A Validation observation recorded for context, future review, or Transparency without constituting an error or warning.

## Version Terms

### Registry Entry Version

The version assigned by Registry to a distinct state of a SREG.

### Source-Record Version

The version assigned by the Source Institution or source system to the Authoritative Source Record.

### Catalog Version

The version assigned to a generated or published Registry Catalog collection, distinct from the versions of individual SREGs.

### Definition Version

The version assigned to a canonical Registry definition or Definitions set.

### Major Change

A material change that alters structure, meaning, compatibility, authority, or interpretation sufficiently to require a major version increment.

### Minor Change

A backward-compatible material addition or refinement that does not replace the prior fundamental meaning or structure.

### Patch Change

A limited Correction or refinement that preserves the existing meaning, structure, and compatibility of the record.

### Supersession

The governed replacement of a Registry object, version, decision, Policy, Procedure, schema, profile, or value by a successor while preserving the prior object historically.

---

## Correction Terms

### Registry Correction

An authorized change to a Registry-owned field, record, representation, or historical statement made to remedy an identified error or omission.

### Correction Record

A Registry-owned record documenting:

- affected object;
- affected version;
- prior value;
- corrected value;
- reason;
- Correction Authority;
- supporting evidence;
- correction date;
- resulting version.

### Correction Authority

The Registry role, institution, Governance Decision, or authorized process empowered to approve a Correction.

### Source Correction

A Correction made under Source Institution authority to the Source Record itself, distinct from a Registry Correction.

### Silent Correction

A material change made without preserving or disclosing:

- the prior state;
- Correction Authority;
- change history.

Silent material Correction is not permitted within Registry.

## Terms That Must Remain Distinct

The following terms must not be treated as interchangeable:

- Registry and SREG;
- SREG and Source Record;
- Registry Record Type and Source Institution classification;
- Registry Identifier and Source-System Identifier;
- Registry Entry Version and Source-Record Version;
- Registry Status and Registry Lifecycle State;
- Registry Status and Source-Record Status;
- Validation and Certification;
- Registration and endorsement;
- Publication and Source Authority;
- Custody and Authority;
- Catalog inclusion and affiliation;
- Registry History and Chronicle event records;
- Controlled Values and Definitions;
- Scope and Registrability.

These distinctions protect institutional meaning and authority.

## Definitions and Controlled Values

Definitions and Controlled Values serve related but distinct purposes.

### Definitions

Establish what institutional terms mean.

### Controlled Values

Establish which approved values may be used in governed fields.

Example:

```text
Registry Status
```

is a defined concept.

Values such as:

```text
Active
Restricted
Archived
```

are Controlled Values within that concept.

## Invalid Definition Conditions

Invalid conditions include:

- one term used for multiple conflicting institutional meanings;
- Registry and Source Institution authority combined into one definition;
- SREG treated as identical to the Source Record;
- Registry Status confused with Registry Lifecycle State;
- Validation represented as Certification;
- Catalog inclusion represented as endorsement;
- custody represented as Source Authority;
- deprecated term presented as current without notice;
- definition changed without version or history;
- human-readable and machine-readable meanings conflict;
- another Suite institution's term redefined outside Registry authority.

## Relationship to Scope

Scope defines Registry authority and institutional boundaries.

---

## Relationship to Schemas

Schemas define:

- fields;
- types;
- structure;
- constraints.

Definitions explains the institutional meaning of the concepts represented by those fields.

```text
Schemas define structure.
Definitions preserve semantic meaning.
```

---

## Relationship to Policies and Procedures

Policies and Procedures should use canonical Registry terminology.

They may apply Definitions but should not silently redefine foundational terms through operational language.

---

## Relationship to Controlled Values

Definitions establishes the meaning of a concept.

Controlled Values establishes the approved values permitted within that concept.

Definitions and Controlled Values should remain aligned but separate.

---

## Relationship to Transparency

Transparency depends on definitions that make authority, limitations, conditions, versions, and historical changes understandable.

Public materials should not require users to infer the difference between Registry-owned concepts and source-owned concepts.
