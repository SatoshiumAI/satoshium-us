# Satoshium Registry Governance

## Overview

The `registry/governance/` directory contains the public institutional page and supporting documentation for the Satoshium Registry Governance framework.

The public page is published through:

```text
registry/governance/index.html
```

This `README.md` serves as the directory-level documentation for that page.

---

## Purpose of This Directory

The purpose of this directory is to define the authority, decision rights, approvals, accountability, change control, exception handling, conflict resolution, review, and institutional stewardship governing:

- Satoshium Registry Entries;
- Registry Rules;
- Registry Policies;
- Registry Procedures;
- schemas;
- Record-Type Profiles;
- controlled values;
- identifiers;
- Record Types;
- relationships;
- provenance;
- validation;
- publication;
- versioning;
- corrections;
- Registry Status;
- Registry Lifecycle;
- restricted access;
- archival preservation;
- governance records.

Governance ensures that Registry decisions remain authorized, consistent, reviewable, versioned, transparent, and aligned with the wider Satoshium Suite.

---

## Constitutional Position

Registry Governance operates within Suite authority.

```text
Suite Standards
  ↓
Suite Methodology
  ↓
Suite Interoperability
  ↓
Registry Governance
  ↓
Registry Rules, Policies, Procedures, and Operations
```

Registry may govern its own implementation.

It must not redefine Suite-wide authority or absorb the authority of Source Institutions or other Suite institutions.

---

## Core Principle

```text
Authority makes decisions legitimate.
Governance makes them accountable.
History makes them enduring.
```

Every material Registry decision should be attributable to an appropriate authority and preserved as part of institutional history.

## Governance Characteristics

### Authorized

Material decisions must be made by an appropriate Registry authority or approved process.

### Documented

Decisions should preserve:

- authority;
- rationale;
- evidence;
- date;
- scope;
- effect;
- affected materials.

### Versioned

Governance-controlled materials should preserve current and historical versions.

### Reviewable

Decisions, exceptions, conflicts, and reversals should remain reconstructable and open to appropriate review.

### Transparent

Material governance conditions should be visible when they affect public interpretation or Registry operation.

## Governance Authority

Governance authority may be exercised through:

- the Registry institution;
- the Founder or designated institutional steward;
- approved Registry governance roles;
- approved review bodies or councils;
- documented automated controls operating under approved authority;
- delegated roles with defined scope;
- Suite-level authority where constitutional alignment is required.

Delegation permits another role or process to act.
It does not remove accountability from the governing institution.

## Governance Roles

### Authority

Holds final decision responsibility.

### Approver

Formally approves a proposal, material, or action.

### Reviewer

Evaluates consistency, evidence, risk, and readiness.

### Operator

Executes approved Procedures.

### Maintainer

Preserves records, schemas, documentation, and publication.

### Custodian

Preserves artifacts or records without necessarily controlling institutional meaning.

### Observer

Monitors compliance or reports concerns without final decision authority.

## Exception Governance

Exceptions should be approved only when:

- the governing requirement is identified;
- the requesting need is documented;
- the approving authority is competent within scope;
- risk is considered;
- impact is considered;
- scope is limited;
- duration is limited or reviewable;
- compensating controls are documented;
- material effects are visible in Validation;
- material effects are visible in Publication;
- expiration or review is defined.

```text
An exception changes application in a governed case.
It does not silently change the governing Rule or Policy.
```

## Conflict Resolution

When Registry materials or decisions conflict, Governance should:

1. Identify the conflicting provisions or decisions.
2. Determine the governing authority hierarchy.
3. Identify current and historical versions.
4. Apply the more specific requirement when appropriate.
5. Apply clear supersession when documented.
6. Pause material action when ambiguity creates risk.
7. Issue an interim decision when necessary.
8. Approve correction, amendment, or supersession.
9. Publish the resolution.
10. Preserve the conflict and decision history.

## Emergency Governance

Temporary emergency action may be appropriate when delay would create material:

- security risk;
- safety risk;
- privacy harm;
- rights violation;
- publication deception;
- integrity failure;
- systemic Registry damage.

Emergency action should preserve:

- emergency authority;
- action taken;
- reason;
- date and time;
- affected records;
- affected systems;
- temporary duration;
- required post-action review;
- final ratification, modification, or reversal.

Emergency action does not eliminate the requirement for later accountability.

## Guiding Principles

- Governance must remain within Registry authority.
- Registry Governance must remain aligned with Suite authority.
- Material decisions must be authorized.
- Material decisions must be documented.
- Material decisions must be reviewable.
- Governance-controlled materials must be versioned.
- Delegation does not eliminate accountability.
- Separation of duties should match risk and significance.
- Exceptions must be limited and visible.
- Conflicts must be resolved through documented authority.
- Appeals and reconsideration must preserve prior decisions.
- Emergency action requires post-action review.
- Historical Governance materials must remain discoverable.
- Governance must not absorb Source Authority.
- Governance must not transfer authority from other Suite institutions.
- Governance must not permit silent change or historical erasure.
