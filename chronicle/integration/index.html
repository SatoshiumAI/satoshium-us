Satoshium Chronicle Integration

Chronicle Integration defines how Chronicle interoperates with Certifier, Registry, Atlas, Anchor, Beacon, Attestor, Navigator, and the broader Satoshium Suite while preserving clear institutional authority boundaries.

Integration answers three questions: how does Chronicle reference authoritative Suite objects without duplicating them, how are those references carried through Verification and Validation, and how does Chronicle preserve the historical Occurrences associated with those objects through canonical Chronicle Entries?

Purpose

Chronicle Integration establishes the boundary between authoritative operational objects and Chronicle’s historical-preservation responsibility. Chronicle collects and references authoritative Suite records during Entry production, verifies its representation of them, validates required reference requirements, and preserves qualifying Occurrences through Chronicle Entries rather than absorbing another system’s operational record.

Core Distinction

Certifier creates the canonical Certification Package. Registry creates independent SREG Registry Entries. Chronicle creates Chronicle Entries that preserve qualifying historical occurrences while referencing those authoritative Suite objects.

Not a Certification Store

Chronicle does not become the authoritative home of Certification Packages, SCPRs, SCRs, SCRDs, receipts, SREGs, Integrity References, Discovery Signals, Trust Statements, Workflow Definitions, Atlas records, or other Suite objects. Those objects remain authoritative within the systems responsible for them.

Historical Layer

Chronicle serves as the historical-preservation layer that records qualifying Occurrences, their temporal context, Relationships, Provenance, Historical Context, Verification, Validation context, publication lineage, and historical significance across the Suite without replacing the operational systems that produced the underlying records or actions.

Suite Interoperability Alignment

Suite Interoperability requires each system to preserve its own authority, object, and responsibility while referencing authoritative objects maintained elsewhere. Chronicle follows that reference-based design and does not duplicate, replace, or reinterpret the objects it references.

Stable Objects · Clear Boundaries · Durable References · Explicit Validation · Repeatable Workflows

The governing interoperability rule is:

Reference does not transfer authority.

Integration Relationship

Chronicle integrates through stable references to authoritative Suite objects. In the certification-centered Suite flow, Chronicle may preserve qualifying certification Occurrences by referencing the canonical Certification Package and, where a corresponding SREG exists and is materially relevant, the related Registry Entry. Chronicle preserves its own historical Entry without assuming authority over those referenced objects.

Certifier → Certification Package
Registry → SREG Registry Entry
Chronicle → Chronicle Entry preserving a qualifying occurrence

Chronicle’s Institutional Object

Chronicle’s canonical object is the Chronicle Entry. The historical occurrence is what happened; the Chronicle Entry is Chronicle’s structured preservation record of that qualifying occurrence.

Within Suite interoperability, references to Chronicle “historical events” should therefore be understood as Chronicle’s responsibility to preserve historical occurrences, not as a separate canonical object competing with the Chronicle Entry.

Occurrence → Preservation Eligibility → Chronicle Entry

Certifier Relationship

Certifier remains authoritative for certification evaluation, determinations, lifecycle actions, certification status, and the canonical Certification Package, including its related public outputs.

Registry Relationship

Registry remains authoritative for independent SREG Registry Entries, registration and cataloging, Registry metadata, Registry relationships, and Registry lifecycle state.

Chronicle Relationship

Chronicle creates and maintains Chronicle Entries that preserve qualifying historical occurrences, including certification creation, renewal, suspension, revocation, expiration, and other approved historical event classes.

Suite Relationship

Chronicle participates in Suite interoperability as an independent institution. Its published Entries may be referenced by other Suite systems where useful, while Chronicle itself may reference Atlas, Certifier, Registry, Anchor, Beacon, Attestor, Navigator, and other authoritative Suite records for historical context.

Suite-Wide Relationships

Certification is the current canonical interoperability flow of the Suite, but Chronicle’s historical-preservation role may also apply to qualifying occurrences originating in other Suite systems when those occurrences satisfy Chronicle Preservation Eligibility.

Atlas → source intelligence, jurisdiction data, evidence, metadata, source material
Certifier → Certification Package and certification authority
Registry → SREG Registry Entry and catalog authority
Anchor → Integrity Reference
Beacon → Discovery Signal / Discovery Metadata
Attestor → Trust Statement / attestation
Navigator → Workflow Definition / orchestration
Chronicle → Chronicle Entry preserving qualifying historical occurrence

A Chronicle Entry about an Anchor, Attestor, Registry, Beacon, Navigator, Atlas, or Certifier occurrence does not transfer that system’s authority to Chronicle.

Integration in the Entry Production Procedure

Integration is applied throughout the formal Chronicle Entry Production Procedure, not as a separate post-production step.

Identify Occurrence → Assess Preservation Eligibility → Collect Authoritative References → Classify Event Type → Select Event-Type Profile → Create Entry → Establish Relationships → Record Sources / Evidence / Provenance → Verify → Validate → Publish → Preserve

The first explicit authority checkpoint occurs when authoritative references are collected. Additional checkpoints occur during Relationship review, Verification, Validation, and Publication.

Certification Events

Within certification-centered interoperability, Chronicle may preserve certification occurrences that satisfy Preservation Eligibility. The Chronicle Entry provides historical context without duplicating or reinterpreting the authoritative Certification Package.

Certification Created → Certification Renewed → Certification Suspended → Certification Revoked → Certification Expired

Certification-Centered Source Object

In the canonical certification interoperability flow, the Certification Package is the shared source object produced by Certifier and referenced by downstream Suite systems. Chronicle should use that package as the authoritative certification reference when preserving certification-related occurrences.

This does not mean every future Chronicle occurrence must originate from a Certification Package. A Registry milestone, Anchor event, governance change, or other approved occurrence may originate from a different authoritative Suite object or institutional source.

Why Chronicle Preserves Events

Certifications may change over time through actions and lifecycle states governed by Certifier. The Certification Package and Certifier lifecycle remain authoritative for certification state. Chronicle preserves qualifying historical occurrences associated with those changes through Chronicle Entries.

Authoritative records establish institutional state. Chronicle Entries preserve what happened and how it fits history.

Preservation Eligibility and Integration

Interoperability does not automatically make every Suite action a Chronicle Entry. Chronicle applies its own Preservation Eligibility rules before creating a historical record.

Suite Activity ≠ Automatic Chronicle Entry

This prevents Chronicle from becoming a duplicate activity log or operational ledger for the rest of the Suite.

Integration and Validation

Chronicle Validation tests whether required authoritative references and institutional boundaries are correctly represented in the current Entry Version.

Required authoritative reference present · Owning system identified · Reference structure valid · Relationship semantics coherent · Authority not transferred · Event-Type Profile requirements satisfied

For the Certification Event-Type Profile, at least one structured authoritative reference must identify system: certifier. A related SREG is conditional rather than a substitute for the Certifier reference.

A Validation failure establishes that Chronicle's current representation does not yet conform. It does not invalidate the referenced authoritative object.

Authority Boundaries

Chronicle does not determine whether a certification is valid, active, suspended, revoked, expired, or otherwise in a Certifier-defined state. Certification meanings remain with Certifier. Registry separately governs its own cataloging and Registry lifecycle state.

Chronicle preserves its own historical representation of a qualifying occurrence and references the authoritative systems or records that establish the underlying action, state, or institutional fact. Chronicle does not convert those references into Chronicle authority.

Integration and the Publication Gate

Required authoritative references are part of Chronicle's publication-readiness requirements.

Required Verification State + Validation PASS + Required Authoritative References + Publication Prerequisites = Eligible for Publication

Publication does not convert referenced Suite objects into Chronicle-owned objects. Chronicle publishes its own historical-preservation Entry while preserving the authority of the referenced institution.

Integration Philosophy

Chronicle should preserve history without absorbing the responsibilities of other systems. Its role is not to certify, catalog, anchor, signal, attest, orchestrate, or replace Atlas source intelligence. Its role is to preserve qualifying historical memory through structured, durable Chronicle Entries.

Interoperability connects Chronicle to the Suite. It does not erase institutional boundaries.
