Chronicle Preservation Eligibility Model

Preservation Eligibility is Chronicle's institutional admission rule. It determines whether an Occurrence should become part of the Chronicle historical record through a canonical Chronicle Entry.

Historical Significance is treated within this model as a principal rationale for preservation rather than as a separate institutional system.

Eligibility Question

Should Chronicle preserve this Occurrence?

Preservation Eligibility is assessed before a production Chronicle Entry is created. It prevents Chronicle from becoming a universal activity log while preserving room for historically meaningful Occurrences that deserve institutional memory.

Canonical Sequence

Occurrence → Preservation Eligibility → Chronicle Entry → Historical Preservation

The eligibility decision determines admission to Chronicle. The Entry Model determines the structure of the record after admission.

Institutional Relevance

The Occurrence materially relates to Satoshium, a Suite institution, an authoritative Suite object, governance, architecture, public operation, or another recognized area of institutional history.

Eligibility Factor

Historical Significance

The Occurrence has enduring value for understanding how Satoshium developed, changed, operated, or established institutional continuity.

Eligibility Factor

Lifecycle Significance

The Occurrence represents a meaningful creation, renewal, suspension, revocation, expiration, transition, completion, or other historically relevant lifecycle milestone.

Eligibility Factor

First, Last, or Milestone

The Occurrence is the first, last, largest, earliest, inaugural, terminal, threshold-crossing, or otherwise historically distinctive milestone of its kind.

Eligibility Factor

Governance or Architectural Change

The Occurrence materially changes rules, standards, institutional responsibilities, architecture, interoperability, policy, or another foundational element.

Eligibility Factor

Relationship Significance

The Occurrence establishes, changes, or terminates a historically meaningful relationship among Suite institutions, records, systems, or authoritative objects.

Eligibility Factor

Evidentiary or Interpretive Importance

Preserving the Occurrence materially improves later understanding of another historical Entry, institutional development, disputed interpretation, or major historical sequence.

Eligibility Factor

Continuity Value

The Occurrence fills a meaningful gap needed to reconstruct Chronicle history across time, even if the event appears less significant when viewed in isolation.

Eligibility Factor

Eligibility Standard

Chronicle does not require every eligible Occurrence to satisfy every factor. Eligibility should depend on an approved preservation basis appropriate to the Event Type and historical circumstances.

Eligibility is reasoned admission, not a numerical score.

Automatic Preservation Classes

Chronicle may approve limited classes of Occurrences for which preservation is presumed or automatically required because their historical value is already institutionally established.

Automatic classes should remain narrow and governed. They should be created only after operational experience demonstrates that repeated individual eligibility review would add little value.

Approved Automatic Class → Preservation Eligible unless an exclusion rule applies

Automatic Does Not Mean Unreviewed

Automatic preservation removes the need to re-decide historical importance, but the resulting Chronicle Entry must still satisfy Entry Model, authority, Provenance, Verification, Validation, publication, and other production rules.

Initial Automatic Classes

No broad automatic preservation catalog should be frozen yet. Certification operational experience should determine whether specific certification milestones warrant automatic treatment.

Discretionary Consideration

Occurrences outside an automatic class may still qualify through discretionary Preservation Eligibility review.

Discretionary review should document the preservation rationale rather than relying on intuition alone.

Occurrence → Eligibility Factors → Historical-Significance Rationale → Eligibility Decision

Historical-Significance Rationale

When Historical Significance supports preservation, Chronicle should preserve a concise rationale explaining why the Occurrence has enduring historical value.

The rationale should identify the relevant significance basis without attempting to create an artificial universal importance score.

Historical Significance = Why this Occurrence is worth preserving

Exclusion Criteria

An Occurrence should ordinarily be excluded when preservation would add little or no durable historical value or would duplicate information already adequately represented elsewhere.

Routine Activity · Trivial Change · Exact Duplicate · Purely Administrative Noise · Failed Eligibility · Unsupported Speculation · Out-of-Scope Activity

Routine Activity

Normal operational actions should not become Chronicle Entries merely because they occurred or generated system records.

Trivial Occurrences

Minor changes with no meaningful historical, institutional, lifecycle, relational, or continuity value should ordinarily remain outside Chronicle.

Exact Duplicates

Chronicle should not create multiple Entries for the same Occurrence merely because multiple Sources, records, or references describe it.

Administrative Noise

Routine formatting changes, metadata maintenance, internal processing actions, automated checks, and similar administrative events ordinarily belong in logs or operational records rather than Chronicle.

Unsupported Speculation

Predictions, rumors, or imagined future developments should not become historical Entries merely because they may later prove important.

Out-of-Scope Activity

An Occurrence outside Chronicle's institutional scope should not be admitted unless a legitimate historical relationship to Satoshium brings it within Scope.

Duplicate Occurrence Handling

Multiple records, Sources, Evidence items, or Suite references may describe the same underlying Occurrence. Chronicle should ordinarily preserve one canonical Chronicle Entry for that Occurrence and attach the relevant supporting relationships.

One Occurrence → One Canonical Chronicle Entry → Multiple Supporting References

A later development should receive a separate Entry only when it represents a distinct qualifying Occurrence rather than additional information about the original one.

Trivial Occurrence Handling

Triviality should be assessed in historical context rather than by event size alone. A small technical action may become historically important if it represents a first, final transition, governance milestone, or necessary continuity point.

Small does not always mean trivial. Routine does not always mean historical.

Eligibility and Event Type

Event Type classifies the Occurrence. Preservation Eligibility decides whether Chronicle should preserve it.

Event Type = What kind of Occurrence is this?
Preservation Eligibility = Should Chronicle preserve it?

Matching an approved Event Type does not automatically guarantee preservation unless Chronicle has explicitly designated that Event Type or class as automatic.

Eligibility and Evidence

Preservation Eligibility is distinct from Evidence sufficiency. An Occurrence may be historically important even when Evidence is incomplete, disputed, or limited.

Chronicle should preserve those limitations transparently rather than using evidentiary uncertainty as an automatic substitute for the eligibility decision.

Retrospective Eligibility

An Occurrence may become eligible later when subsequent history reveals significance that was not apparent at the time.

Later Significance → Retrospective Eligibility → Chronicle Entry preserving original Event Date

Eligibility Decision

The Preservation Eligibility decision should result in a clear institutional outcome.

Eligible → Proceed to Chronicle Entry creation
Not Eligible → Do not create a Chronicle Entry
Deferred → Preserve review context until sufficient basis exists

Final decision-state terminology should be established through Chronicle Controlled Values.

Chronicle should preserve enough history to maintain institutional memory, but not so much activity that history becomes indistinguishable from operational noise.
