Satoshium Chronicle Corrections

Corrections improve Chronicle’s own preservation records while preserving transparency, historical continuity, provenance, and traceability.

Chronicle recognizes that new evidence may emerge, sources may become available, and errors or omissions may be discovered over time. Corrections improve Chronicle’s representation of history without silently replacing the record that was previously published.

Why Corrections Exist

Chronicle Entries may require correction when factual errors, metadata defects, incomplete sourcing, broken references, omitted context, or other documented issues are identified. A correction applies to Chronicle’s own preservation record and does not alter an authoritative record maintained by another Suite system.

Transparency

Corrections should remain visible, attributable, and understandable. Historical reviewers should be able to identify what changed, why it changed, when the change occurred, and which Chronicle version contains the correction.

Historical Context

Chronicle preserves historical continuity by retaining the relationship between prior and corrected states. Corrections should improve accuracy without erasing material context or obscuring the fact that an earlier representation existed.

Accuracy

The primary objective of a correction is to improve the accuracy, reliability, and reviewability of a Chronicle Entry while preserving the integrity of its historical lineage.

Authority Boundary

Chronicle may correct its own Entry, metadata, references, context, or supporting records. It does not correct, supersede, or reinterpret an authoritative object owned by another Suite system. If an originating system changes its own authoritative record, Chronicle may preserve that later occurrence and update its references according to Chronicle rules.

Evidence Updates

Newly discovered evidence or documentation may justify a correction, supplemental context, or a new version of a Chronicle Entry. Evidence must be handled according to applicable Suite evidence standards and Chronicle procedures rather than being silently substituted into the historical record.

Source Updates

Corrections may involve improved source attribution, additional references, archival discoveries, repaired links, or clarified provenance. Authoritative Suite objects remain authoritative within the systems that created them; Chronicle corrects only its own references or historical representation.

Suite Alignment

Chronicle Corrections follow Satoshium Suite standards for stable objects, evidence handling, terminology, schema discipline, version preservation, validation-ready records, and reference-based interoperability.

Correction Workflow

Chronicle follows a documented, repeatable correction process designed to improve accuracy while preserving provenance, prior states, and institutional authority boundaries.

Issue Identified → Scope & Authority Review → Source / Evidence Review → Correction Decision → Versioned Update → Validation → Publication → Preservation

Minor and Major Corrections

Minor corrections may include typographical errors, non-substantive metadata fixes, repaired references, formatting corrections, or clarification notes that do not materially change the historical meaning of the Entry.

Major corrections may include changes to event dates, event descriptions, authoritative references, relationships, provenance, or other substantive elements that materially change Chronicle’s representation of the preserved occurrence.

The greater the effect on the meaning of a Chronicle Entry, the stronger the requirements for documented rationale, source support, validation, versioning, and preservation of the prior state.

Corrections and Versioning

Corrections and versioning are related but not identical. Non-substantive editorial fixes may be handled according to Chronicle publication rules. Substantive corrections that change the historical meaning, authoritative references, provenance, relationships, or other material fields should create a traceable versioned state.

Prior published states should remain historically preservable so later reviewers can understand what Chronicle recorded before the correction and how the record changed.

Correction Philosophy

Chronicle does not use corrections to erase prior history. A substantive correction becomes part of the Chronicle Entry’s own historical lineage and must remain traceable to the state it corrected.

The goal is to maintain an accurate, reviewable, and durable historical record while preserving how Chronicle’s own record changed over time.
