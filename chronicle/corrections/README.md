# Chronicle Corrections

## Purpose

Chronicle Corrections preserve the integrity, transparency, and historical lineage of Chronicle's own preservation records.

Chronicle is designed to preserve qualifying historical occurrences through Chronicle Entries and related supporting records. As new evidence emerges, sources become available, errors are identified, or context improves, Chronicle may need to correct its own representation of the historical record.

Chronicle does not use corrections to alter authoritative records owned by Certifier, Registry, Anchor, Beacon, Attestor, Navigator, Atlas, or another Suite system. Those systems remain authoritative for the objects and actions within their respective institutional boundaries.

Rather than silently replacing prior Chronicle states, Chronicle favors transparent, traceable, and version-aware correction.

The goal is not to create an ahistorical appearance of perfection. The goal is to maintain an accurate, auditable, reviewable, and durable record of both the preserved occurrence and Chronicle's own record of it.

## Suite Alignment

Chronicle Corrections operate within the Satoshium Suite architecture.

Corrections should follow Suite-wide expectations for:

* Stable and durable objects
* Clear institutional authority boundaries
* Reference-based interoperability
* Evidence handling
* Provenance and traceability
* Structured records and schema discipline
* Version preservation
* Validation-ready workflows
* Documented and repeatable procedures

Chronicle corrects its own preservation record. It does not replace, supersede, or reinterpret the authority of another Suite system.

## Principles

### Preserve Historical Lineage

Prior Chronicle states should remain historically preservable whenever practical.

Substantive corrections should supplement and version the Chronicle record rather than erase the state that previously existed.

### Transparency

Corrections should clearly identify, as applicable:

* What was corrected
* Why the correction was made
* Who or what initiated the correction
* When the correction occurred
* Which Chronicle Entry or supporting record was affected
* Which version contains the correction
* Which prior version or state the correction supersedes

### Evidence-Based Updates

Corrections should be supported by evidence, authoritative records, documentation, or other verifiable information whenever available.

New evidence may justify a correction, supplemental context, or a new version of a Chronicle Entry. Evidence should be handled according to applicable Suite evidence standards and Chronicle procedures.

Assertions that remain unverified should not be presented as established fact merely because they appear in a correction process.

### Traceability

Every substantive correction should preserve a relationship to the affected Chronicle record and its prior state.

Future reviewers should be able to reconstruct the lineage of a Chronicle Entry and understand how Chronicle's representation changed over time.

### Authority Boundaries

A Chronicle correction applies only to Chronicle's own Entry, metadata, references, context, relationships, provenance, or supporting records.

If another Suite system changes an authoritative object, Chronicle may preserve that later occurrence and update its own references according to Chronicle rules, but Chronicle does not itself correct the originating authoritative object.

## Types of Corrections

### Factual Correction

A correction to information within a Chronicle record that is demonstrably inaccurate.

Examples:

* Incorrect event date
* Incorrect identifier
* Incorrect attribution
* Incorrect originating system
* Incorrect relationship reference

### Evidence Update

New evidence or documentation that expands, clarifies, challenges, or materially changes the support for Chronicle's representation of an occurrence.

### Source or Provenance Correction

A correction to source attribution, authoritative references, archival references, provenance, or the documented origin of information.

### Clarification

Additional context that improves understanding without materially changing the underlying historical representation.

### Administrative Correction

Formatting, non-substantive metadata, repaired references, schema-aligned formatting, or other editorial changes that do not materially alter the historical meaning of the Chronicle record.

### Substantive Correction

A correction that materially changes the historical meaning, event description, event date, authoritative reference, provenance, relationship, or other significant element of a Chronicle Entry.

Substantive corrections should create a traceable versioned state.

## Correction Lifecycle

Chronicle should follow a documented and repeatable correction workflow.

1. Issue identified
2. Scope and authority reviewed
3. Sources and evidence reviewed
4. Correction decision documented
5. Corrected Chronicle state prepared
6. Version relationship established when required
7. Validation performed
8. Correction published
9. Prior state preserved
10. Historical linkage maintained

---

## Corrections and Versioning

Corrections and versioning are related but are not identical.

Minor editorial or administrative fixes may be handled according to Chronicle publication and maintenance rules.

Substantive corrections that change the meaning, authoritative references, provenance, relationships, or other material fields of a Chronicle Entry should result in a new traceable version or equivalent preserved state.

Prior published states should remain historically preservable so future reviewers can determine what Chronicle recorded before the correction and how the record changed.

Chronicle should not silently overwrite substantive historical content.

---

## Record Preservation

Chronicle favors correction and version preservation over deletion.

When practical:

* Original or prior Chronicle states remain preserved
* Corrected states identify their relationship to earlier states
* Correction records reference affected Chronicle Entries
* Historical relationships remain auditable
* Published substantive changes remain reviewable
* Authoritative external Suite records remain referenced rather than duplicated

Deletion should be rare and limited to circumstances involving legal, privacy, security, integrity, or operational requirements.

A deletion requirement should not be treated as permission to rewrite unrelated historical context.

---

## Relationship to Verification

Verification and correction are separate institutional functions.

Verification assesses the support, consistency, provenance, references, or confidence associated with Chronicle's representation of an occurrence.

Corrections modify Chronicle's own record when a documented issue warrants change.

A correction does not automatically invalidate an entire Chronicle Entry. It may affect one field, one relationship, one evidence item, one interpretation, or the Entry as a whole depending on the nature of the issue.

Verification results should remain distinguishable from the authority of the originating Suite system.

---

## Relationship to Authoritative Suite Records

Chronicle may reference authoritative objects created by other Suite systems.

Examples include:

* Certification Packages produced by Certifier
* SREG Registry Entries maintained by Registry
* Integrity references produced by Anchor
* Discovery signals produced by Beacon
* Trust statements produced by Attestor
* Workflow definitions maintained by Navigator
* Other authoritative Suite records as the architecture develops

If one of those systems corrects, supersedes, or versions its own authoritative object, Chronicle may preserve that later occurrence and update its own references where appropriate.

Chronicle does not independently alter the authoritative object.

---

## Validation

Substantive corrections should be validated before publication.

Validation may include:

* Identifier integrity
* Schema conformance
* Controlled-value conformance
* Required-field checks
* Authoritative reference checks
* Relationship integrity
* Provenance completeness
* Evidence linkage
* Version linkage
* Publication readiness

Detailed validation requirements will be defined by Chronicle's operational validation architecture.
