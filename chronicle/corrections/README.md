# Chronicle Corrections

## Purpose

Corrections exist to preserve the integrity of the historical record.

Chronicle is designed to document events, records, evidence, and observations as they are known at a given point in time. As new information emerges, mistakes may be identified, evidence may be updated, or additional context may become available.

Rather than removing historical records, Chronicle favors transparent correction.

The goal is not to create a perfect record. The goal is to create an auditable record.

---

## Principles

### Preserve History

Original records should remain accessible whenever possible.

Corrections should supplement the historical record rather than erase it.

### Transparency

All corrections should clearly identify:

* What was corrected
* Why the correction was made
* Who initiated the correction
* When the correction occurred

### Evidence-Based Updates

Corrections should be supported by evidence, documentation, or verifiable information whenever available.

Assertions without supporting evidence may be recorded but should be identified as unverified.

### Traceability

Every correction should maintain a reference to the original record.

Future reviewers should be able to reconstruct the complete history of a record and understand how it evolved over time.

---

## Types of Corrections

### Factual Correction

A correction to information that is demonstrably inaccurate.

Examples:

* Incorrect date
* Incorrect identifier
* Incorrect attribution
* Incorrect location

### Evidence Update

New evidence that expands, clarifies, or supersedes previously available evidence.

### Clarification

Additional context that improves understanding without changing the underlying facts.

### Administrative Correction

Formatting, categorization, schema, or metadata changes that do not alter substantive content.

---

## Correction Lifecycle

1. Correction identified
2. Supporting information gathered
3. Correction reviewed
4. Correction recorded
5. Historical linkage established
6. Correction published

---

## Record Preservation

Chronicle favors correction over deletion.

When practical:

* Original records remain preserved
* Corrected records receive updated status
* Correction records reference affected entries
* Historical relationships remain auditable

Deletion should be rare and limited to situations involving legal, privacy, security, or operational requirements.

---

## Relationship to Verification

Verification determines the confidence level associated with a record.

Corrections update the record when new information becomes available.

A correction does not automatically invalidate a record. Instead, it becomes part of the record's continuing history.
