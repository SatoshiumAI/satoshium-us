Satoshium Chronicle Evidence

Evidence provides support, context, traceability, and transparency for Chronicle Entries. Chronicle handles evidence within the Satoshium Evidence Standard so future reviewers can understand what materials were available, how they relate to the preserved occurrence, and what limitations they carry.

Evidence does not transfer authority to Chronicle and does not determine another Suite system’s official outcome. Chronicle references and preserves evidence so its own historical representation remains reviewable, verifiable, and understandable over time.

Supporting Information

Evidence may support, challenge, clarify, contradict, or contextualize a Chronicle Entry or a claim represented within it. Chronicle should identify the evidence relationship explicitly rather than treating every evidence item as uniformly supportive.

Transparency

Preserving evidence references and material context allows future reviewers to examine the basis of Chronicle’s historical representation rather than relying solely on summaries, conclusions, or interpretations.

Recognized Evidence

Chronicle may reference evidence categories recognized by the Satoshium Evidence Standard, including primary sources, secondary sources, institutional records, self-attestations, cryptographic records, metadata, screenshots, archives, media, receipts, witness statements, and other approved evidence types.

Suite Alignment

Chronicle Evidence follows the Satoshium Evidence Standard for evidence type, quality, integrity, sufficiency, limitations, traceability, preservation, and structured evidence records. Chronicle does not create a competing evidence standard.

Authority Boundary

Evidence referenced by Chronicle does not transfer authority from the Suite system that owns the underlying determination or record. Chronicle may preserve and assess evidence relevant to its Entry while leaving certification, registration, anchoring, attestation, discovery, and other authoritative actions with their originating systems.

Historical Context

Evidence may preserve contemporary context surrounding an occurrence, including what was known, documented, disputed, or available at the time. Contextual evidence should remain distinguishable from evidence that directly establishes an authoritative action or fact.

Verification Support

Chronicle verification may use evidence to assess support, consistency, provenance, traceability, authenticity, and limitations within Chronicle’s own historical representation. Verification does not re-adjudicate an outcome owned by another Suite system.

Evidence Integrity

Evidence should remain traceable, authentic where determinable, complete where practical, time-associated, version-aware, and suitable for long-term review. Chronicle should preserve durable references and preservation information rather than silently substituting later material for what was originally reviewed.

Evidence Categories

Evidence may take many forms depending on the preserved occurrence. Chronicle uses Suite-recognized evidence categories and should record evidence type and relationship consistently through controlled values and structured records.

Primary Sources · Secondary Sources · Institutional Records · Cryptographic Records · Metadata · Screenshots · Archives · Media · Receipts · Witness Statements

Evidence Quality and Limitations

Chronicle should preserve the qualities and limitations relevant to later review rather than reducing evidence to a simple present/absent state.

Authority · Independence · Completeness · Authenticity · Timeliness · Reproducibility · Traceability · Resistance to Alteration or Misinterpretation

Conflicting, stale, incomplete, unverifiable, or unsupported material should be disclosed as such. Uncertainty is part of the evidence record and should not be hidden.

Evidence Record Structure

Evidence referenced by Chronicle should ultimately use the Suite Evidence Standard’s structured record concepts so evidence can be described consistently and validated over time.

Source · Evidence Type · Authority · Date · Relationship to Entry or Claim · Preservation Status · Review Notes · Public Reference

Evidence and Sources

Sources identify where information originated or from whom or what it was obtained.

Evidence is the material or record used to support, challenge, contradict, clarify, or contextualize a claim or Chronicle Entry. Provenance records how the evidence originated, was obtained, and entered the Chronicle record.

Source = Where information originated.
Evidence = What bears on the claim or occurrence.
Provenance = How that information or evidence came into the record.

Evidence, Eligibility, and Sufficiency

Preservation Eligibility and evidence sufficiency are separate questions. An occurrence may be historically significant enough to warrant preservation even when evidence is incomplete, disputed, or limited, provided Chronicle discloses that condition according to its rules.

Evidence sufficiency should be assessed according to the applicable Suite standard and the purpose for which the evidence is being used. Chronicle should not borrow a certification sufficiency threshold automatically when preserving historical context.

Evidence Philosophy

Chronicle preserves evidence references, integrity information, and historical context so Chronicle Entries can be reviewed, examined, and reassessed over time.

The goal is not to convert evidence into Chronicle authority. The goal is to preserve the evidentiary basis, limitations, and context that make the historical record reviewable.
