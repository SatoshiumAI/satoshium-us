Chronicle · Evidence

Satoshium Chronicle Evidence

Evidence is material that bears on Chronicle's historical representation of a qualifying Occurrence. It may support, challenge, contradict, clarify, corroborate, contextualize, or limit confidence in a Chronicle Entry or claim. Evidence supports Verification and may also be subject to applicable Validation requirements.

Evidence is not the authoritative event itself and does not transfer authority to Chronicle. Where another Suite system owns the underlying action, determination, or record, that authoritative object remains authoritative and Chronicle references it separately.

Role Within Chronicle

Evidence supports review of Chronicle's own historical representation. It does not replace the Occurrence, the Chronicle Entry, an authoritative Suite object, Verification, or Validation.

Occurrence → Chronicle Entry
Evidence → bears on the Entry or claim
Authoritative object → remains authoritative in its originating system

Support

Evidence may support a factual assertion, temporal detail, relationship, provenance statement, or other part of Chronicle's historical representation.

Challenge

Evidence may challenge Chronicle's current representation or indicate that a claim requires further review.

Contradiction

Conflicting Evidence should remain visible rather than being removed merely because it complicates the historical record.

Clarification

Evidence may clarify ambiguous dates, identities, relationships, terminology, or historical context without independently establishing authority.

Corroboration

Multiple independent items may corroborate the same historical representation. Corroboration should not be confused with duplication.

Context

Evidence may preserve contemporary context showing what was known, documented, disputed, or available at the time of the Occurrence.

Verification Support

Chronicle Verification may use Evidence to assess support, consistency, authenticity, Provenance, Relationships, limitations, and temporal coherence.

Historical Limitations

Incomplete, stale, conflicting, unavailable, derivative, or otherwise limited Evidence should remain explicitly described where material to later review.

Controlled Evidence Types

Evidence Type should use the Chronicle Controlled Values Registry rather than ad hoc category labels.

Authoritative Evidence · Documentary Evidence · Repository Evidence · Archival Evidence · Machine-Generated Evidence · Testimonial Evidence · Contextual Evidence · Other

These categories describe the form or institutional character of the Evidence. They do not determine its relationship to the Entry or its evidentiary strength.

Authoritative Evidence

Material drawn from or directly reflecting an authoritative record or system. Authority belongs to the originating system, not to Chronicle.

Documentary Evidence

Documents, reports, letters, publications, filings, or other documentary material that bears on the Entry or claim.

Repository Evidence

Repository records, commits, releases, structured artifacts, or other preserved repository material relevant to the historical representation.

Archival Evidence

Archived webpages, snapshots, preserved documents, historical collections, or other materials accessed through an archive.

Machine-Generated Evidence

Logs, hashes, timestamps, system outputs, generated metadata, receipts, or other machine-produced material used in historical review.

Testimonial Evidence

Statements, interviews, declarations, witness accounts, or similar testimonial material relevant to the Entry.

Contextual Evidence

Material that primarily helps explain the surrounding historical circumstances rather than directly establishing the underlying authoritative action.

Other

Used only when an approved existing Evidence Type cannot accurately represent the material. Repeated use should trigger review for a possible new Controlled Value.

Evidence Linkage to Chronicle Entries

Evidence should be linked explicitly to the Chronicle Entry or specific claim it bears upon.

Evidence Item / Evidence Record → Evidence Relationship → Chronicle Entry or Claim

Linkage should make clear whether the Evidence supports, challenges, contradicts, clarifies, corroborates, contextualizes, or limits confidence.

Evidence Relationship Semantics

Evidence relationship terms should remain distinct from Evidence Type.

Evidence Type = What kind of Evidence is this?
Evidence Relationship = How does it bear on the Entry or claim?

Candidate relationship terms include Supports, Challenges, Contradicts, Clarifies, Corroborates, Contextualizes, and Limits Confidence. These should become a formal Controlled Value set only when schema and production use demonstrate the need.

Evidence and Authoritative Objects

A Certification Package, SREG Registry Entry, Integrity Reference, Trust Statement, or other authoritative Suite object may provide Evidence relevant to a Chronicle Entry. Its authoritative status still belongs to the originating system.

Evidence may reference authority.
Evidence does not become the authority.
Reference does not transfer authority.

Evidence, Sources, Provenance, and Verification

Source = Where information came from.
Evidence = What bears on the Entry or claim.
Provenance = How the information or Evidence originated, moved, and entered Chronicle.
Verification = Chronicle's review of its own historical representation.
Validation = Chronicle's conformance test of the current Entry Version.

Evidence Quality and Limitations

Chronicle should preserve qualities and limitations relevant to later review rather than reducing Evidence to a simple present/absent state.

Authority · Independence · Completeness · Authenticity · Timeliness · Reproducibility · Traceability · Resistance to Alteration or Misinterpretation

These are quality considerations, not a universal numerical trust score.

Evidence Record Expectations

A distinct Evidence Record should be created only when its additional structure provides durable operational value. Where one is used, it should preserve enough structure to identify the Evidence and connect it to its Source, Provenance, Entry linkage, limitations, and review context.

Evidence Type · Source Reference · Provenance · Relationship to Entry or Claim · Date / Temporal Context · Limitations · Integrity Information when applicable · Review Notes

Evidence, Eligibility, and Sufficiency

Preservation Eligibility and Evidence sufficiency remain separate questions. An Occurrence may be historically significant enough to preserve even when Evidence is incomplete, disputed, or limited.

Chronicle should disclose those conditions rather than automatically importing certification-specific Evidence thresholds into historical preservation.

Evidence Within Chronicle Validation

Phase VIII establishes Chronicle Validation as an active production function. Evidence-related requirements are evaluated through the applicable Chronicle Validation Rules rather than through a separate Evidence-validation system.

Validation should confirm, where applicable:

Approved Evidence Type · Required Entry or claim linkage · Valid Source reference · Required Provenance · Known limitations preserved · Relationship semantics valid · Event-Type Profile requirements satisfied · Evidence authority not overstated

An Evidence defect may cause the current Entry Version to fail Validation when the affected Evidence information is required by the Base Schema, Event-Type Profile, Provenance rules, Verification dependency, or publication prerequisites.

Evidence in the Entry Production Procedure

Evidence handling occurs during assembly of Sources, Evidence, Provenance, and Relationships before Verification and Validation.

Create Entry → Establish Relationships → Record Sources / Evidence / Provenance → Perform Verification → Perform Validation

Chronicle should preserve enough Evidence structure to support review and traceability without creating unnecessary supporting objects when a direct, durable reference is sufficient.

First Production Evidence Application

The first production Chronicle Entry confirmed that Evidence structure should remain proportional to the needs of the Entry.

For CHR-2026-0001, the supporting Certifier artifacts had evidentiary value during Verification, but Chronicle did not create separate Evidence Records merely to duplicate the same material already preserved as traceable Sources and authoritative references.

A Source may have evidentiary value without requiring a duplicate Evidence object or Evidence reference.

This production precedent preserves Chronicle's distinction among Source, Evidence, Authoritative Record, and Provenance while applying the principle: Minimum necessary structure first.

Evidence Philosophy

Chronicle preserves Evidence so the historical representation can remain inspectable, challengeable, and understandable over time.

Evidence supports the historical representation. It does not become the event.
