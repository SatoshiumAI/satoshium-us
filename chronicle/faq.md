# Chronicle FAQ

## What is Chronicle?

Chronicle is the historical preservation layer of the Satoshium ecosystem.

It exists to preserve records, entries, sources, evidence, verification activities, and corrections in a structured and transparent manner.

---

## Why does Chronicle exist?

Information is often scattered, forgotten, altered, or lost over time.

Chronicle exists to preserve historical context and improve long-term accountability by documenting what was recorded, what evidence existed, and how understanding evolved.

---

## Does Chronicle determine truth?

No.

Chronicle does not determine absolute truth.

Chronicle preserves information and supports review.

Verification activities may assess confidence in records, but historical interpretation remains open to future review.

---

## What is a Chronicle Entry?

An entry is a historical record.

Entries may document:

* Events
* Decisions
* Publications
* Observations
* Investigations
* Milestones

Entries serve as the primary records within Chronicle.

---

## What is the difference between a Source and Evidence?

A source identifies where information originated.

Evidence represents information that supports, challenges, clarifies, or contextualizes a claim or historical record.

In simple terms:

```text
Source   = Where information came from
Evidence = What supports the claim
```

---

## What is Verification?

Verification is the process of evaluating confidence in a record using available evidence, sources, and context.

Verification documents confidence levels.

It does not establish absolute certainty.

---

## What are Corrections?

Corrections are documented updates made to improve accuracy, clarity, completeness, or context.

Chronicle treats corrections as part of the historical record rather than evidence that the historical record failed.

---

## Why preserve corrections?

Historical transparency improves when reviewers can see how understanding changed over time.

Chronicle favors visible corrections over hidden revisions whenever practical.

---

## What types of information can Chronicle preserve?

Chronicle may preserve:

* Historical events
* Publications
* Observations
* Evidence
* Sources
* Verification records
* Corrections
* Supporting documentation

---

## Can Chronicle preserve conflicting information?

Yes.

Historical records often contain uncertainty, disagreement, or conflicting accounts.

Chronicle may preserve differing perspectives while allowing verification activities to assess confidence levels.

---

## Is Chronicle a database?

Not necessarily.

Chronicle is a historical preservation framework.

Future implementations may use databases, archives, distributed storage systems, or other technologies.

---

## Is Chronicle a certification system?

No.

Certification and attestation functions belong to Certifier.

Chronicle preserves historical information related to those activities.

---

## Is Chronicle a registry?

No.

Registry organizes and identifies records.

Chronicle preserves historical context associated with those records.

---

## How does Chronicle relate to Certifier?

Certifier creates attestations.

Chronicle preserves historical information surrounding those attestations.

A certification may become part of the historical record, but the systems serve different purposes.

---

## How does Chronicle relate to Registry?

Registry identifies and organizes records.

Chronicle preserves historical information associated with those records.

The systems complement one another but remain separate.

---

## Can verification results change?

Yes.

New evidence, additional sources, or improved context may lead to updated confidence assessments.

Chronicle recognizes that historical understanding evolves over time.

---

## Does Chronicle preserve deleted information?

Not necessarily.

Chronicle seeks to preserve historical information whenever practical, but preservation capabilities depend upon implementation, availability, legal requirements, and operational constraints.

---

## What is Chronicle's long-term goal?

The long-term goal is to create a durable historical preservation system capable of maintaining records, evidence, sources, verification activities, and corrections across time.

The objective is transparency, accountability, and historical continuity.

---

## What is Chronicle's guiding principle?

Chronicle seeks to preserve:

* What was recorded
* What evidence existed
* What sources were available
* How understanding evolved

The goal is not perfect certainty.

The goal is transparent historical preservation.
