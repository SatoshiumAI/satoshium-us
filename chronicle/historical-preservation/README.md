# Chronicle Historical Preservation

## Purpose

Chronicle Historical Preservation defines the principles by which Satoshium Chronicle preserves qualifying historical occurrences as durable, reviewable Chronicle Entries.

Chronicle exists not merely to note that something happened, but to preserve the context, relationships, provenance, and historical continuity needed to understand how the Satoshium ecosystem evolved through time.

Historical Preservation is a constitutional Chronicle function. It defines what Chronicle is trying to preserve, how that preservation remains institutionally bounded, and why historical continuity matters across the Satoshium Suite.

---

## Suite Alignment

Chronicle Historical Preservation operates within the Satoshium Suite architecture.

Historical preservation should follow Suite-wide expectations for:

* Stable objects
* Canonical terminology
* Durable identifiers and references
* Structured records
* Version preservation
* Provenance and traceability
* Validation-ready artifacts
* Documented and repeatable procedures
* Reference-based interoperability
* Clear institutional authority boundaries

Chronicle preserves its own canonical Chronicle Entry while referencing authoritative objects maintained by the Suite systems that created them.

Chronicle does not duplicate, replace, or reinterpret those authoritative objects.

---

## Canonical Preservation Object

The canonical object of Chronicle is the Chronicle Entry.

The historical occurrence is what happened.

The Chronicle Entry is Chronicle's structured preservation record representing that qualifying occurrence.

Conceptually:

> Occurrence → Preservation Eligibility → Chronicle Entry

This distinction is fundamental.

Events, decisions, releases, certifications, registry milestones, anchor actions, attestations, governance changes, and other historical developments may become the subject of Chronicle preservation.

They do not become separate competing canonical Chronicle objects.

They are represented through Chronicle Entries and classified through Event Type or other controlled Chronicle classifications.

---

## Historical Preservation

Historical Preservation is the institutional function by which Chronicle maintains durable historical memory of qualifying occurrences.

Chronicle aims to preserve:

* What happened
* When it happened
* Which system, institution, or record established the occurrence
* Why the occurrence mattered
* Which authoritative records relate to it
* Which sources and evidence provide context
* Which relationships connect it to other occurrences
* How Chronicle's own representation evolved over time

The goal is not merely retention.

The goal is durable historical understanding.

---

## Preservation Eligibility

An occurrence does not become a Chronicle Entry merely because it happened.

Chronicle applies Preservation Eligibility to determine whether the occurrence belongs in the historical record.

Preservation Eligibility asks:

> Should Chronicle preserve this occurrence?

Eligibility may be established through:

* An approved Event Type or preservation class
* Historical Significance
* Other Chronicle preservation rules adopted through the institutional architecture

Preservation Eligibility is distinct from:

* Authority
* Evidence quality
* Verification confidence
* Publication status
* Operational importance within another Suite system

An occurrence may be operationally important but not historically preservable.

Likewise, an occurrence may become historically significant only later.

---

## Historical Significance

Historical Significance describes why an occurrence matters within the continuing history of Satoshium.

Potential significance factors may include:

* Institutional change
* Lifecycle significance
* First or last occurrence
* Major milestone
* Material architectural change
* Governance change
* Relationship significance
* Historical continuity value
* Evidentiary importance
* Interpretive importance
* Long-term institutional relevance

Historical Significance supports Preservation Eligibility but does not replace the formal eligibility mechanism.

Chronicle should avoid arbitrary or purely intuitive preservation decisions.

---

## Institutional Memory

Chronicle provides an institutional-memory layer for the Satoshium Suite.

This does not mean Chronicle owns the history of every participating system.

Each Suite system remains authoritative for the objects, records, determinations, and actions within its own institutional scope.

Chronicle preserves qualifying occurrences that help future reviewers understand how the wider Satoshium ecosystem changed over time.

Examples may include:

* Certification Events
* Registry Milestones
* Governance Changes
* Attestation Events
* Anchor Events
* Public Releases
* Institutional Milestones
* Other approved Event Types or historically significant occurrences

Chronicle's preservation function should remain selective.

---

## Authority Boundaries

Historical preservation does not transfer authority.

Examples:

* Certifier remains authoritative for Certification Packages and certification determinations.
* Registry remains authoritative for SREG Registry Entries and Registry lifecycle state.
* Anchor remains authoritative for Integrity References.
* Beacon remains authoritative for Discovery Signals and Discovery Metadata.
* Attestor remains authoritative for Trust Statements and attestations.
* Navigator remains authoritative for Workflow Definitions and orchestration.
* Atlas remains authoritative for its own source intelligence, jurisdiction data, evidence, metadata, and other Atlas records.

Chronicle may preserve that an authoritative action or transition occurred.

Chronicle does not recreate, supersede, or reinterpret the originating authority.

---

## References Over Duplication

Chronicle should preserve history through durable references to authoritative Suite records.

It should not duplicate authoritative objects merely to make a historical Entry self-contained.

A Chronicle Entry may reference:

* Certification Packages
* SREG Registry Entries
* Integrity References
* Discovery Signals
* Trust Statements
* Workflow Definitions
* Atlas records
* Public releases
* Archival sources
* Evidence records
* Related Chronicle Entries

Referenced objects remain authoritative within the systems that created them.

---

## Historical Continuity

Historical value emerges from connected context.

Chronicle should preserve relationships among:

* Chronicle Entries
* Authoritative Suite records
* Related occurrences
* Source systems
* Evidence
* Provenance
* Public references
* Institutional decisions
* Later historical developments

This allows future reviewers to understand sequence and context.

Chronicle should distinguish relationship from causation.

Temporal or structural proximity does not automatically establish that one event caused another.

---

## Historical Relationships

Chronicle Entries may preserve relationships such as:

* Precedes
* Follows
* Related To
* References
* Originated From
* Registered As
* Anchored By
* Attested By
* Superseded By
* Corrected By
* Version Of
* Associated With

The final relationship vocabulary will be governed by Chronicle controlled values and relationship rules.

Relationships should remain directional where direction matters.

They should not transfer institutional authority between Suite systems.

---

## Temporal Integrity

Historical preservation depends on accurate temporal distinction.

Chronicle should distinguish, where applicable:

* Event Date
* Entry Creation Date
* Verification Date
* Validation Date
* Publication Date
* Correction Date
* Version Date
* Preservation Date

The date an occurrence happened must not be confused with the date Chronicle created or published its Entry.

This is especially important for retrospective preservation.

---

## Retrospective Preservation

Chronicle may preserve an occurrence after the fact when later context reveals historical significance that was not apparent at the time of occurrence.

Retrospective preservation should not rewrite history.

Chronicle should preserve:

* The original occurrence date
* The later Chronicle Entry creation date
* The basis for retrospective Preservation Eligibility
* The historical context that made the occurrence significant later
* Any relevant authoritative references and evidence

Later recognition of significance is itself part of the historical context.

---

## Preservation Over Deletion

Chronicle favors durable historical preservation over silent deletion.

When practical, Chronicle should preserve:

* Prior Entry states
* Version lineage
* Correction history
* Historical relationships
* Source references
* Evidence references
* Provenance
* Publication history

Deletion should be rare and governed by legal, privacy, security, integrity, or operational requirements.

Chronicle should not use deletion to create an artificial appearance that earlier states or interpretations never existed.

---

## Corrections and Historical Lineage

Historical Preservation and Corrections are closely related.

When Chronicle corrects its own Entry, the correction should preserve the historical lineage of the record.

Substantive corrections should remain traceable to prior states.

Chronicle should not silently overwrite material historical content.

If another Suite system changes its own authoritative object, Chronicle may preserve that later occurrence and update references according to Chronicle rules.

Chronicle does not correct the originating authoritative object.

---

## Historical Preservation Is Not Activity Logging

Chronicle is not a universal activity log.

Suite systems may generate routine operational activity that is important to their own workflows but does not require independent historical preservation.

Chronicle should not preserve every:

* Status check
* Internal update
* Metadata adjustment
* Routine validation
* System log event
* Background operation
* Minor administrative action

unless that occurrence independently satisfies Preservation Eligibility.

Conceptually:

> Logs preserve activity.  
> Chronicle preserves history.

This distinction protects Chronicle from becoming a duplicated operational ledger.

---

## Preservation and Event Types

Approved Event Types may define classes of occurrences that are presumptively eligible for preservation.

Examples may include certain:

* Certification lifecycle events
* Registry milestones
* Anchor events
* Attestation events
* Governance events
* Major releases

Automatic or presumptive eligibility should be used carefully.

Chronicle should not convert every operational action in a Suite system into a Chronicle Entry.

Event-Type Profiles should define specialized historical requirements while preserving the canonical Chronicle Entry model.

---

## Preservation and Evidence

Historical Significance and evidence strength are separate dimensions.

An occurrence may be historically significant while its evidence is incomplete, disputed, or limited.

Chronicle may preserve such an occurrence when permitted by its rules, provided uncertainty, evidence limitations, and verification state remain visible.

Evidence does not create historical significance by itself.

Historical Significance does not eliminate the need for evidence transparency.

---

## Preservation and Verification

Verification reviews Chronicle's own historical representation.

Verification may examine:

* Authoritative references
* Sources
* Evidence
* Provenance
* Temporal consistency
* Relationship consistency
* Internal consistency
* Evidence limitations

Verification does not transfer authority to Chronicle.

It also does not re-adjudicate determinations owned by another Suite system.

---

## Preservation and Validation

Validation determines whether a Chronicle Entry conforms to Chronicle's structural and procedural rules.

Validation may include:

* Identifier integrity
* Schema conformance
* Required fields
* Controlled values
* Relationship integrity
* Provenance requirements
* Authoritative reference checks
* Version linkage
* Publication readiness

A historically meaningful occurrence is not automatically a valid production Chronicle Entry.

The Entry must still satisfy Chronicle's operational requirements.

---

## Timeline and Historical Discovery

Timeline should function as a public chronological discovery mechanism derived from published Chronicle Entries.

Timeline is not a second historical record system.

Chronology is the ordering principle behind Timeline.

Conceptually:

> Occurrence → Chronicle Entry → Context & Relationships → Timeline → Historical Continuity

Timeline should remain downstream of Chronicle Entries rather than becoming an independent canonical layer.

---

## Long-Term Perspective

Historical value may become clearer over time.

Chronicle therefore preserves for future understanding, not merely immediate operational use.

A durable Chronicle should allow later reviewers to determine:

* What Chronicle knew at the time
* Which records were authoritative
* Which evidence was available
* How relationships were understood
* How the Entry changed
* Why the occurrence was preserved
* How later history changed its significance

Long-term reviewability is a core historical-preservation objective.

---

## Historical Philosophy

Chronicle preserves historical memory without replacing the Suite systems responsible for creating, certifying, cataloging, anchoring, signaling, attesting, orchestrating, or otherwise maintaining authoritative objects.

History remains reviewable when Chronicle Entries stay connected to:

* Authoritative records
* Institutions
* Evidence
* Provenance
* Relationships
* Decisions
* Versions
* Corrections
* Temporal context

Chronicle's role is not to own every fact in the Suite.

Its role is to preserve qualifying historical memory in a structured, durable, and institutionally coherent form.
