Chronicle Relationship Model

The Chronicle Relationship Model defines how a Chronicle Entry connects to authoritative records, Source Records, Registry Entries, other Chronicle Entries, originating systems, and preceding or following historical Occurrences.

Relationships describe connection and sequence. They do not create authority, causation, ownership, or equivalence unless those meanings are explicitly supported.

Core Relationship Principle

Relationship describes connection.
Authority remains with the institution that owns the referenced object.

Chronicle may reference and relate external objects without absorbing their institutional authority.

Authoritative Source Records

A Chronicle Entry may reference authoritative Source Records or other records that establish where historical information originated.

References Derived From

Registry Entries

A Chronicle Entry may relate to an SREG Registry Entry when the registered object, registration action, or Registry relationship is historically relevant.

Other Chronicle Entries

Entries may relate to one another when they preserve distinct but connected historical Occurrences.

Preceding / Following Events

Directional relationships may establish historical sequence where one qualifying Occurrence clearly occurs before or after another.

Originating Systems

Chronicle may identify the Suite system or external institution from which an authoritative object or historical action originated.

Originating System is represented through governed Entry metadata where applicable; it is not an additional Chronicle Relationship Type.

Superseding Occurrences

A later Chronicle Entry may supersede or materially replace the historical significance or representation of an earlier Entry while preserving both records.

Supersedes Superseded By

Directional Relationship Types

Direction should be explicit when the meaning changes depending on which object is the source and which is the target.

Precedes ↔ Follows
Supersedes ↔ Superseded By
Corrects ↔ Corrected By

Directional relationships should not rely on display order or unstated assumptions.

Non-Directional Relationships

Some relationships may be symmetric or intentionally broad.

Related To

Broad relationships should be used conservatively so they do not become a substitute for more precise semantics.

Authority Boundary

CHR Entry → authoritative reference → SC-CERT object
CHR Entry → related_to → SREG Entry where materially relevant
CHR Entry → references → Atlas or other subject object where appropriate
CHR Entry → approved Relationship → another CHR Entry

None of these relationships transfer authority to Chronicle over the referenced object.

Relationship ≠ Authority

A relationship does not make Chronicle authoritative for the target object.

Relationship ≠ Causation

Temporal sequence or association must not be interpreted as causation unless causation is independently established and represented by an approved relationship type.

Relationship ≠ Ownership

Connecting an Entry to another record does not imply ownership, custody, control, or institutional responsibility.

Relationship ≠ Equivalence

Two connected records should not be treated as interchangeable merely because they reference the same Occurrence or authoritative object.

Relationship Record Structure

Every structured Relationship should conceptually identify:

Source Object · Relationship Type · Target Object · Target Identifier · Target System · Direction where applicable · Supporting rationale or context when needed

Relationship to Source Records

Source Record relationships should preserve the distinction between source origin, Evidence role, and authoritative reference.

Source relationship says where information is connected.
Evidence relationship says how material bears on the Entry.
Authority relationship identifies which system owns the referenced object.

Relationship to Registry Entries

Registry Entries should remain authoritative Registry objects. Chronicle may reference them when registration itself is historically meaningful or when a Registry Entry provides an authoritative catalog relationship to the object preserved in Chronicle.

Registry catalogs. Chronicle preserves history.

Chronicle-to-Chronicle Relationships

Distinct Chronicle Entries may form historical sequences without being merged. Each Entry preserves one qualifying Occurrence and retains its own identifier.

Originating System

Originating System should identify where the underlying authoritative action, object, or record originated when that information is relevant.

Originating System should remain structured metadata or a governed relationship, not part of the Chronicle Entry identifier.

Supersession

Supersession should be used only when a later object or Entry formally or materially replaces an earlier one for a defined institutional purpose.

Supersedes does not mean deletes.
Superseded records remain historically preserved.

Relationship Validation

Chronicle Validation confirms that:

Relationship Type is approved · Direction is valid · Target exists or is properly referenced · Source and target are not improperly duplicated · Authority is not implied incorrectly · Relationship is permitted by the applicable schema or Event-Type Profile

Relationship Principle

Relationships should make Chronicle history more understandable without making Chronicle more authoritative than it is.
