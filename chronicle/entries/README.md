# Chronicle Entries

## Purpose

Entries are the primary records within Chronicle.

An entry documents an event, observation, action, decision, publication, claim, milestone, or other occurrence that is deemed worthy of historical preservation.

Entries serve as the foundation of the Chronicle record.

They provide context, establish chronology, and create a structured history that can be reviewed, verified, corrected, and expanded over time.

---

## What Is an Entry?

An entry is a timestamped historical record.

Entries may describe:

* Events
* Decisions
* Announcements
* Publications
* Discoveries
* Milestones
* Corrections
* Incidents
* Investigations
* Observations
* Historical context

An entry may be simple or complex depending on the information available.

---

## Core Principles

### History First

Chronicle records what occurred.

Interpretation may evolve, but the historical record should remain preserved.

### Time Matters

Every entry should include temporal context whenever possible.

Questions such as:

* What happened?
* When did it happen?
* When was it recorded?

are fundamental to the Chronicle model.

### Evidence Matters

Entries should be supported by sources and evidence whenever available.

However, Chronicle may record incomplete information if historical significance warrants preservation.

### Transparency Matters

Entries should clearly distinguish between:

* Verified information
* Unverified information
* Opinion
* Interpretation
* Observation
* Evidence

---

## Entry Components

A Chronicle entry may include:

### Identifier

A unique identifier for the entry.

### Title

A concise description of the subject.

### Summary

A brief explanation of the entry.

### Timestamp

The date and time associated with the event or record.

### Source References

References to supporting sources.

### Evidence References

References to supporting evidence.

### Verification Status

Current confidence level associated with the entry.

### Tags

Optional categorization metadata.

### Related Records

Links to associated Chronicle records.

---

## Entry Types

Chronicle may support multiple entry categories.

### Event Entry

Documents a specific occurrence.

Examples:

* System launch
* Public announcement
* Organizational milestone

### Publication Entry

Documents the release of information.

Examples:

* Reports
* Specifications
* Research
* Statements

### Observation Entry

Records observed information.

Examples:

* Market observations
* Environmental observations
* Operational observations

### Investigation Entry

Documents an ongoing inquiry.

Examples:

* Incident review
* Fact-finding effort
* Historical research

### Correction Entry

Documents modifications to existing records.

### Reference Entry

Provides historical context without introducing new events.

---

## Entry Lifecycle

1. Entry created
2. Sources attached
3. Evidence attached
4. Verification performed
5. Publication completed
6. Corrections applied as necessary
7. Historical preservation maintained

Entries may evolve over time while preserving their historical lineage.

---

## Relationships

Entries may connect to:

* Sources
* Evidence
* Verification records
* Corrections
* Other entries
* Registry records
* Certifier attestations

These relationships create a broader historical network that allows events to be understood within context.

---

## Preservation

Chronicle favors preservation over deletion.

Entries should remain accessible whenever practical, even when:

* Updated
* Corrected
* Superseded
* Reclassified

The historical record benefits from visibility into how understanding changes over time.

---

## Future Development

Future Chronicle implementations may support:

* Immutable entry histories
* Digital signatures
* Attestations
* Version tracking
* Distributed storage
* Public verification mechanisms
* Cross-system historical linking
