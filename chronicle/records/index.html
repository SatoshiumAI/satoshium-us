Chronicle · Records

Satoshium Chronicle Records

Record is an umbrella term for structured information maintained within Chronicle. The canonical historical-preservation object is the Chronicle Entry, while supporting Chronicle records preserve evidence, verification, corrections, provenance, relationships, and other operational context.

Not every referenced object becomes a Chronicle-owned record. Chronicle may maintain its own supporting records while referencing authoritative objects owned by Certifier, Registry, Atlas, Anchor, Beacon, Attestor, Navigator, and other Suite systems.

Canonical Entry

The Chronicle Entry is the canonical record object through which Chronicle preserves a qualifying historical occurrence. Supporting records exist to strengthen, explain, validate, correct, relate, or preserve that Entry.

Traceability

Chronicle records should preserve traceable relationships among Entries, sources, evidence, provenance, verification activities, corrections, versions, and authoritative Suite references so historical context remains reconstructable.

Transparency

Chronicle records should remain reviewable and understandable, including their provenance, version lineage, correction history, relationship to authoritative records, and applicable verification or validation state.

Authority Boundaries

Chronicle records are authoritative only within Chronicle’s own institutional scope. Referenced Certification Packages, SREGs, Integrity References, Trust Statements, Discovery Signals, Workflow Definitions, Atlas records, and other Suite objects remain authoritative within their originating systems.

Relationships

Chronicle records may connect with one another and with authoritative external Suite objects through stable references. Relationships preserve context without transferring ownership or authority between institutions.

Continuity

Chronicle records support historical continuity by preserving how Entries, evidence, relationships, verification states, corrections, and versions evolved over time.

Suite Alignment

Chronicle Records follow Satoshium Suite standards for stable objects, canonical terminology, structured schemas, durable identifiers, version preservation, validation-ready records, and documented repeatable procedures.

Suite Interoperability requires reference-based design: Chronicle references authoritative objects maintained by other Suite institutions rather than duplicating, replacing, or reinterpreting them.

One canonical Chronicle object · Supporting Chronicle records · Referenced external authoritative objects

Record Categories

Chronicle uses one canonical historical-preservation object together with supporting record classes that perform distinct functions. These supporting classes should not be confused with separate canonical Chronicle objects.

Chronicle Entry · Supporting Source Records · Evidence Records · Verification Records · Correction Records · Relationship / Provenance / Version Records

Chronicle-Owned vs. Referenced Records

Chronicle should distinguish records it creates and governs from records it only references.

Chronicle-Owned → Chronicle Entry, Chronicle correction, verification, provenance, relationship, version, or other Chronicle support records
Referenced → Certification Package, SREG, Integrity Reference, Discovery Signal, Trust Statement, Workflow Definition, Atlas record, or other authoritative external object

A reference to an external record does not make that record part of Chronicle’s authority or lifecycle.

Record Lifecycle

Chronicle-owned records may evolve according to documented, repeatable lifecycle, validation, correction, and versioning rules while preserving prior substantive states where required.

Created → Structured → Related → Verified / Validated → Published or Applied → Maintained → Corrected / Versioned → Preserved

No Redundant Canonical Layer

Chronicle should not create a second canonical “Record” object above or beside the Chronicle Entry. “Record” is an architectural umbrella term, not a competing historical object.

Production historical preservation should remain centered on the Chronicle Entry. Supporting records exist only where they provide a distinct operational function.

Record Philosophy

Chronicle favors durable, version-aware preservation over silent deletion whenever practical. Chronicle-owned records provide value not only because they capture information, but because their provenance, relationships, correction history, and lineage reveal how the historical record was maintained over time.

The goal is not to accumulate records for their own sake. The goal is to support coherent, reviewable Chronicle Entries and durable historical continuity.
