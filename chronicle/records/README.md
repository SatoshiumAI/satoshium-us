# Chronicle Records

## Purpose

Records are the fundamental units of preservation within Chronicle.

They provide the structure through which historical information is organized, stored, referenced, reviewed, and maintained over time.

Every entry, source, evidence item, verification record, and correction ultimately exists as a record within the Chronicle system.

Records establish continuity between events, evidence, and historical context.

---

## What Is a Record?

A record is a preserved unit of information.

Records may represent:

* Historical events
* Chronicle entries
* Evidence
* Sources
* Verification results
* Corrections
* Publications
* Observations
* Decisions
* Supporting documentation

A record may stand alone or exist as part of a larger historical narrative.

---

## Core Principles

### Preservation

Records exist to preserve information.

Once created, records should remain available whenever practical.

### Traceability

Records should maintain identifiable relationships to associated records.

Future reviewers should be able to understand how records connect to one another.

### Transparency

The status, origin, and history of a record should remain visible.

### Accountability

Records should support historical review and examination.

The objective is not merely storage, but responsible preservation.

---

## Record Components

Chronicle records may include:

### Identifier

A unique reference associated with the record.

### Record Type

The category of information represented by the record.

Examples:

* Entry
* Source
* Evidence
* Verification
* Correction

### Title

A concise description of the record.

### Description

Additional context regarding the record's purpose or contents.

### Timestamp

Temporal information associated with the record.

### Relationships

References to related records.

### Status

The current state of the record.

Examples:

* Draft
* Active
* Archived
* Superseded
* Corrected

### Metadata

Optional information that assists with organization and retrieval.

---

## Record Categories

### Entry Records

Document historical events, observations, decisions, or publications.

### Source Records

Document the origin of information.

### Evidence Records

Document supporting materials associated with claims or events.

### Verification Records

Document review and confidence assessments.

### Correction Records

Document updates to existing records.

### Reference Records

Provide supporting historical context.

---

## Record Relationships

Chronicle records are designed to function as a connected historical system.

Examples:

An entry may reference:

* Multiple sources
* Multiple evidence records
* Verification records
* Correction records

A source may support:

* Multiple entries
* Multiple evidence items

A correction may affect:

* One or many records

These relationships help preserve historical context and improve traceability.

---

## Record Lifecycle

1. Record created
2. Metadata assigned
3. Relationships established
4. Review performed
5. Publication completed
6. Corrections applied as necessary
7. Historical preservation maintained

Records may evolve while preserving their historical lineage.

---

## Record Integrity

Chronicle seeks to preserve confidence in records through:

* Documentation
* Review processes
* Verification procedures
* Transparent corrections
* Historical traceability

Future implementations may include additional mechanisms such as cryptographic integrity verification.

---

## Preservation Philosophy

Chronicle favors preservation over deletion.

Historical records provide value not only because they capture what is known today, but because they reveal how understanding changes over time.

When practical:

* Records remain accessible
* Corrections remain visible
* Historical relationships remain intact
* Prior versions remain traceable

---

## Relationship to Chronicle

Chronicle is built upon records.

Entries tell the story.

Sources identify origins.

Evidence supports claims.

Verification evaluates confidence.

Corrections improve accuracy.

Records provide the structure that connects them all.
