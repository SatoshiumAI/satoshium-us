# Chronicle Purpose

## Overview

Satoshium Chronicle exists to preserve qualifying historical occurrences through canonical Chronicle Entries.

A Chronicle Entry is the structured historical-preservation object created and maintained by Chronicle. It represents a qualifying occurrence and preserves the temporal context, relationships, provenance, evidence references, authoritative record references, and historical continuity needed for future review.

Chronicle does not exist to replace the authority of Certifier, Registry, Atlas, Anchor, Beacon, Attestor, Navigator, or other Satoshium Suite systems.

It exists to preserve its own historical representation of qualifying occurrences while referencing the authoritative records, sources, and evidence that establish or contextualize them.

The objective is not universal certainty.

The objective is durable, transparent, institutionally bounded historical preservation.

---

## Suite Alignment

Chronicle operates within the Satoshium Suite architecture.

Its purpose and implementation should remain aligned with Suite-wide expectations for:

* Stable objects
* Canonical terminology
* Structured schemas
* Durable identifiers and references
* Reference-based interoperability
* Provenance and traceability
* Evidence handling
* Validation-ready records
* Version preservation
* Documented and repeatable procedures
* Clear institutional authority boundaries

Chronicle should not create a competing object model, evidence model, or authority structure where the Suite already defines one.

---

## Canonical Object

Chronicle Entry is the canonical object of Chronicle.

The historical occurrence is what happened.

The Chronicle Entry is Chronicle's structured preservation record representing that qualifying occurrence.

Conceptually:

> Occurrence → Preservation Eligibility → Chronicle Entry

Events, publications, decisions, milestones, observations, governance changes, anchor actions, attestations, releases, and other developments may become the subject of Chronicle preservation.

They do not become separate competing canonical Chronicle objects.

They are represented through Chronicle Entries and classified through Event Type or other controlled values.

---

## Why Chronicle Exists

Institutional memory can fragment.

Digital records can move, change, disappear, become difficult to interpret, or lose their original context.

As the Satoshium Suite grows:

* Decisions accumulate
* Systems evolve
* Milestones occur
* Records are created
* Relationships form
* Evidence emerges
* Corrections occur
* Interpretations change
* Historical significance may become clearer over time

Without a durable historical-preservation layer, important context can be lost even when the underlying authoritative records still exist.

Chronicle exists to reduce that loss.

It provides a structured framework for preserving qualifying historical occurrences in a way that can be reviewed, verified, corrected, versioned, published, related, and preserved over time.

## Mission

Chronicle seeks to:

* Preserve qualifying historical occurrences
* Create and maintain canonical Chronicle Entries
* Preserve temporal context
* Maintain durable references to authoritative Suite records
* Preserve relationships among historical occurrences
* Preserve provenance and source context
* Associate evidence with Chronicle Entries where relevant
* Support Chronicle verification

* Preserve institutional memory
* Maintain long-term historical continuity

Chronicle does not seek to preserve every Suite action.

Selection is governed by Preservation Eligibility.

## Preservation Eligibility

An occurrence does not become a Chronicle Entry merely because it happened.

Chronicle applies Preservation Eligibility to determine whether the occurrence belongs in the historical record.

Preservation Eligibility asks:

> Should Chronicle preserve this occurrence?

Eligibility may be established through:

* An approved Event Type or preservation class
* Historical Significance
* Other Chronicle preservation rules adopted through the institutional architecture

Preservation Eligibility is distinct from:

* Authority
* Evidence quality
* Verification confidence
* Publication status
* Operational importance within another Suite system

This keeps Chronicle distinct from logs, registries, ledgers, operational databases, and source systems.

## Historical Significance

Historical Significance describes why an occurrence matters to the continuing history of Satoshium.

Potential factors may include:

* Institutional change
* Lifecycle significance
* First or last occurrence
* Major milestone
* Material architectural change
* Governance change
* Relationship significance
* Historical continuity value
* Evidentiary importance
* Long-term interpretive importance

Historical Significance may provide a principal basis for Preservation Eligibility.

It does not replace the formal eligibility mechanism.

## Core Philosophy

### Preserve Before History Is Lost

Chronicle should preserve qualifying occurrences while their context, references, provenance, and relationships remain recoverable.

Preservation may occur contemporaneously or retrospectively.

### Context Matters

Historical meaning rarely exists in isolation.

Chronicle seeks to preserve enough surrounding context to explain:

* What happened
* When it happened
* Which system or record established it
* Why it mattered
* Which related records existed
* Which evidence and sources were available
* How it related to earlier or later history

### Authority Boundaries Matter

Chronicle does not treat transparency as a substitute for institutional authority.

Each Suite system remains authoritative for its own objects and determinations.

Chronicle is authoritative for its own historical-preservation record.

Reference-based interoperability allows Chronicle to preserve history without duplicating or replacing the systems that produced the underlying records or actions.

### Evidence Matters

Evidence may support, challenge, contradict, clarify, or contextualize a Chronicle Entry.

Evidence should remain distinguishable from:

* Authoritative records
* Sources
* Provenance
* Verification results
* Interpretation

Chronicle should follow applicable Suite evidence standards rather than inventing a separate Chronicle evidence model.

### Provenance Matters

Future reviewers should be able to understand where Chronicle's information came from and how it entered the record.

Provenance supports:

* Traceability
* Reviewability
* Relationship integrity
* Evidence integrity
* Historical accountability

### Corrections Are Part of Chronicle History

Chronicle may correct its own preservation record.

Substantive corrections should remain traceable through versioning or other preserved lineage.

Chronicle does not use corrections to alter authoritative objects owned by another Suite system.

Chronicle may preserve qualifying occurrences involving:

### Certification Events

Historically preservable certification actions or lifecycle transitions established by Certifier.

### Registry Milestones

Historically significant Registry developments or lifecycle occurrences.

### Governance Changes

Material changes in rules, institutional responsibilities, standards, or governance.

### Public Releases

Major public launches, publications, or releases with historical significance.

### Anchor Events

Qualifying occurrences associated with Anchor and Integrity References.

### Attestation Events

Qualifying occurrences associated with Attestor and Trust Statements.

### Beacon Events

Qualifying discovery or signaling milestones associated with Beacon.

### Navigator Events

Qualifying workflow or orchestration milestones associated with Navigator.

### Atlas Developments

Historically significant Atlas research, source-intelligence, jurisdiction, or data developments.

### Institutional Milestones

Firsts, lasts, major thresholds, launches, transitions, or other significant moments in Satoshium's development.

### Other Approved Occurrences

Additional occurrence classes may be preserved when they satisfy Chronicle Preservation Eligibility and applicable Event-Type rules.

Chronicle does not preserve these as separate canonical object types.

Each preserved occurrence is represented through a Chronicle Entry.

## Reference-Based Interoperability

Chronicle preserves history through durable references to authoritative Suite objects.

A Chronicle Entry may reference:

* Certification Packages
* SREG Registry Entries
* Integrity References
* Discovery Signals
* Discovery Metadata
* Trust Statements
* Workflow Definitions
* Atlas records
* Public archival records
* Evidence records
* Other Chronicle Entries

Chronicle should not duplicate another Suite system's authoritative object merely to make an Entry self-contained.

References should remain stable, reviewable, and version-aware where appropriate.

## Evidence and Context

Chronicle Entries are strengthened when authoritative references, sources, evidence, provenance, and historical context remain connected.

Chronicle should preserve enough information for future reviewers to understand:

* What information was available
* Where it came from
* How it related to the occurrence
* Which evidence supported or challenged the historical representation
* What limitations existed
* How Chronicle's understanding changed over time

Evidence does not replace the authority of an originating Suite system.

Chronicle emphasizes transparent historical recordkeeping.

Transparency includes:

* Visible provenance
* Clear authoritative references
* Explicit relationships
* Evidence limitations
* Verification state
* Validation state
* Correction history
* Version lineage
* Publication history

Transparency does not mean Chronicle becomes the final authority for every fact it records.

## Accountability

Historical preservation supports accountability by documenting:

* What occurred
* When it occurred
* Which authority established the occurrence
* Which records were available
* Which evidence was available
* Which context was preserved
* How Chronicle represented the occurrence
* How that representation later changed

Chronicle accountability applies to Chronicle's own preservation practices.

It does not convert Chronicle into the authority responsible for every underlying event.

---

## Verification

Chronicle verification reviews Chronicle's own historical representation.

Verification may examine:

* Authoritative reference existence
* Identifier consistency
* Event-date consistency
* Originating-system consistency
* Source integrity
* Evidence linkage
* Provenance completeness
* Relationship consistency
* Internal consistency

Verification does not re-adjudicate determinations owned by another Suite system.

---

## Validation

Validation determines whether a Chronicle Entry conforms to Chronicle's structural and procedural requirements.

Validation may include:

* Identifier integrity
* Schema conformance
* Required-field checks
* Controlled-value conformance
* Authoritative reference checks
* Relationship integrity
* Provenance requirements
* Version linkage
* Publication readiness

Verification and Validation are separate functions.

---

## Corrections and Versioning

Chronicle may correct or version its own Entries when errors, new evidence, changed references, improved sourcing, or additional historical context justify change.

Substantive prior states should remain traceable.

Chronicle should not silently overwrite material historical content.

If another Suite system changes its authoritative object, Chronicle may preserve that later occurrence and update its own references according to Chronicle rules.

Chronicle does not modify the originating authoritative object.

---

## Historical Continuity

Chronicle should preserve relationships among occurrences so history can be understood as a connected sequence rather than isolated records.

Historical continuity may depend on:

* Temporal order
* Related Suite objects
* Institutional milestones
* Lifecycle transitions
* Governance changes
* Version history
* Correction history
* Related Chronicle Entries

Chronicle should distinguish relationship from causation.

Connected events should not automatically be described as causally linked without adequate basis.

---

## Long-Term and Retrospective Memory

Historical importance may become clear only with time.

Chronicle may preserve an occurrence retrospectively when later context establishes Preservation Eligibility.

When this happens, Chronicle should distinguish:

* Event Date
* Chronicle Entry Creation Date
* Publication Date
* Later correction or version dates

Retrospective preservation should never rewrite the date of the underlying occurrence.

Chronicle therefore supports both contemporaneous preservation and long-horizon institutional memory.

---

## Historical Preservation Is Not Activity Logging

Chronicle is not a universal activity log.

Routine Suite activity may be operationally important without requiring independent Chronicle preservation.

Examples may include:

* Routine status checks
* Internal metadata updates
* Background processes
* Minor administrative actions
* Routine validation operations
* Ordinary system logs

Such activity should become Chronicle history only when it independently satisfies Preservation Eligibility.

Conceptually:

> Logs preserve activity.  
> Chronicle preserves history.

---

## Public Historical Discovery

Published Chronicle Entries should ultimately support public historical discovery.

The `/chronicle/entries/` collection is intended to serve as the public production index for Chronicle Entries.

A future Timeline may organize published Entries chronologically.

Timeline should remain downstream of Chronicle Entries rather than becoming a separate canonical record system.
