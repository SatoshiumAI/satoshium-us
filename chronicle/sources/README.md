# Chronicle Sources

## Purpose

Sources identify the origin of information preserved within Chronicle.

They provide the foundation for attribution, traceability, verification, and historical review.

A source helps answer a fundamental question:

> Where did this information come from?

By preserving source information alongside entries, evidence, verification records, and corrections, Chronicle supports a transparent and auditable historical record.

---

## What Is a Source?

A source is the origin of information referenced by Chronicle.

Sources may provide:

* Facts
* Claims
* Statements
* Observations
* Records
* Context
* Evidence

A source does not automatically establish truth.

Instead, it establishes origin.

Understanding where information originates is essential for evaluating reliability, significance, and historical context.

---

## Core Principles

### Attribution

Information should be traceable to its origin whenever possible.

Chronicle seeks to preserve attribution so future reviewers can evaluate information independently.

### Transparency

Sources should be clearly identified and documented.

Reviewers should understand:

* Who created the source
* When it was created
* Where it originated
* How it relates to Chronicle records

### Preservation

Sources should remain accessible whenever practical.

Historical understanding benefits when original materials remain available for future review.

### Independence

Sources should be preserved even when their claims are disputed.

Recording a source does not imply endorsement.

Recording a source preserves historical context.

## Source and Evidence

Sources and evidence are related but distinct.

A source answers:

> Where did the information originate?

Evidence answers:

> What supports or challenges a claim?

Examples:

A published report may be a source.

Specific facts, images, tables, or records contained within that report may be evidence.

One source may produce multiple evidence records.

Multiple sources may contribute evidence supporting a single entry.

---

## Source and Verification

Verification evaluates confidence in information.

Sources contribute to verification by helping reviewers assess:

* Origin
* Reliability
* Consistency
* Corroboration
* Historical context

Verification does not replace sources.

Sources provide the information that verification evaluates.

## Source Relationships

Sources may relate to:

* Chronicle entries
* Evidence records

* Correction records
* Other source records

These relationships help establish a broader historical context.

---

## Preservation Philosophy

Chronicle favors preserving source information whenever practical.

Sources may become:

* Unavailable
* Archived
* Superseded
* Disputed

Even when this occurs, preserving source metadata remains valuable.

Historical understanding often depends upon knowing what information existed at a particular point in time.

---

## Challenges

Sources are not always perfect.

Potential challenges include:

* Missing information
* Incomplete records
* Bias
* Errors
* Conflicting accounts
* Limited availability

Chronicle seeks to document these realities transparently rather than conceal them.
