Chronicle · Sources

Satoshium Chronicle Sources

Sources identify where information originated. Within Chronicle, Source Records support canonical Chronicle Entries by preserving attribution, location, temporal context, provenance, limitations, archival state, and traceable relationships to the information being used.

Understanding origin is essential to historical review, but source identity alone does not establish truth or institutional authority. Chronicle preserves where information came from while keeping Source, Evidence, Provenance, and authoritative Suite records as distinct concepts.

Role Within Chronicle

A Source Record is a supporting Chronicle-owned record. It is not the canonical historical-preservation object. The canonical Chronicle object remains the Chronicle Entry.

Chronicle Entry → Source Relationship → Source Record

Source Records exist to preserve origin, attribution, provenance, archival context, limitations, and traceable relationships needed to support Chronicle Entries.

Attribution

Source Records help establish who created, published, distributed, recorded, or otherwise originated information referenced by a Chronicle Entry.

Provenance

Source identity and provenance are related but distinct. Source identifies where information came from; provenance documents how that information was discovered, accessed, captured, transferred, preserved, and entered Chronicle.

Context

Sources may provide historical context that helps explain an occurrence, decision, publication, record, or claim. Chronicle preserves that relationship without treating the source itself as the historical occurrence.

Traceability

Chronicle should maintain durable links among Chronicle Entries, Source Records, Evidence Records, provenance, authoritative Suite references, archival copies, corrections, and versions so information can be independently reviewed.

Verification

Chronicle verification may examine source existence, attribution, publication date, archival consistency, provenance, limitations, and corroboration. It does not convert a source into Chronicle authority.

Preservation

Chronicle should preserve durable source references, archival context, provenance, integrity information, and limitations whenever practical, even when the original source later becomes unavailable.

Authoritative Sources and Authority Boundaries

Some sources are authoritative objects maintained by another Suite system. Examples may include a Certification Package, SREG Registry Entry, Integrity Reference, Discovery Signal, Trust Statement, Workflow Definition, or Atlas record.

Chronicle may reference those objects as sources, but their authority comes from the originating system. A Chronicle Source Record does not recreate, supersede, or transfer that authority.

Source Categories

Chronicle may reference or structure many forms of source information. Source Type and Source Role should be controlled independently so a source can be classified by what it is and by the role it plays in historical review.

Webpages · Documents · Publications · Archives · Databases · Public Records · Institutional Records · Repositories · Datasets · Statements · Interviews · Other Approved Sources

Source Type and Source Role

Source Type describes what the source is. Source Role describes how the source functions in relation to a Chronicle Entry or Evidence Record.

Type → webpage, document, archive, database, institutional record, repository…
Role → primary, secondary, contextual, archival, authoritative, corroborating…

Final values should be governed through Chronicle Controlled Values rather than embedded informally in page text.

Sources and Evidence

Sources and evidence serve different roles within Chronicle.

A Source identifies where information came from. Evidence is material that bears on a Chronicle Entry or claim. Provenance explains how that information or evidence originated, moved, and entered Chronicle.

Source = Where information came from.
Evidence = What bears on the Entry or claim.
Provenance = How the information originated, moved, and entered Chronicle.

Verification, Validation, and Preservation State

These functions remain distinct. Verification reviews source support, consistency, attribution, provenance, and limitations. Validation determines whether the Source Record conforms to Chronicle structure and rules. Preservation State describes the continuing availability or archival condition of the source.

Verification ≠ Validation ≠ Preservation State

Source Philosophy

Recording a source does not imply endorsement, truth, or authority. Chronicle preserves source context to improve traceability, transparency, verification, archival continuity, and historical understanding.

The goal is not to turn every source into evidence or every source into an authority. The goal is to preserve origin and the context necessary to understand how information entered the Chronicle record.
