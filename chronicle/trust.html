Chronicle Trust Model

Chronicle does not ask users to trust historical records merely because they are published. It builds reviewable historical trust through Preservation Eligibility, authoritative references, Sources, Evidence, Provenance, Verification, Validation, Corrections, Versioning, and transparent preservation.

Chronicle trust is institutional and bounded: Chronicle is authoritative for its own Chronicle Entries and preservation state, while referenced Suite systems remain authoritative for the underlying objects and determinations they own.

Trust Boundary

The canonical Chronicle object is the Chronicle Entry. Trust in a Chronicle Entry comes from the transparency and traceability of Chronicle's preservation process, not from Chronicle absorbing the authority of another Suite system.

Occurrence → Preservation Eligibility → Chronicle Entry → Reviewable Historical Trust

Preservation Eligibility

Chronicle does not preserve every activity. An Occurrence must qualify for preservation before Chronicle creates a canonical Entry. This keeps Chronicle selective and distinguishes historical preservation from ordinary activity logging.

Admission

Authoritative References

Chronicle Entries should reference the authoritative Suite objects or institutional records that establish the underlying action, state, or occurrence whenever such authority exists.

Authority

Sources and Evidence

Sources identify where information came from. Evidence describes material that bears on the Entry or claim. Chronicle preserves those relationships without treating all Sources or Evidence as equally authoritative.

Support

Provenance

Provenance explains how information, Sources, Evidence, and authoritative references originated, moved, were accessed, and entered Chronicle. Trust improves when that path remains visible.

Traceability

Verification

Verification reviews Chronicle's own historical representation for consistency, support, provenance, relationships, temporal integrity, and known limitations. It does not re-adjudicate another system's authoritative determination.

Review

Validation

Validation determines whether a Chronicle-owned record conforms to applicable schemas, identifiers, controlled values, relationship rules, provenance requirements, versioning rules, and publication requirements.

Conformance

Corrections and Versions

Historical trust requires visible change history. Substantive corrections should preserve prior states, correction rationale, resulting versions, and historical lineage rather than silently replacing earlier Chronicle records.

Lineage

Preservation and Continuity

Chronicle trust depends on durable preservation of Entries, references, relationships, Provenance, limitations, corrections, and versions so future reviewers can reconstruct how the historical record evolved.

Continuity

Suite Authority Model

Chronicle participates in Suite trust through reference-based interoperability. Each system retains authority for its own objects.

Certifier → Certification Package and certification determinations
Registry → SREG Registry Entry and Registry lifecycle
Anchor → Integrity Reference
Beacon → Discovery Signal / Discovery Metadata
Attestor → Trust Statement / attestation
Navigator → Workflow Definition / orchestration
Atlas → its own source intelligence, jurisdiction data, evidence, metadata, and records
Chronicle → Chronicle Entry and Chronicle historical-preservation state

Reference does not transfer authority.

Trust Is Not a Score

Chronicle should not reduce historical trust to a single universal confidence number. Historical review may involve authoritative references, strong Evidence, conflicting Sources, incomplete Provenance, later Corrections, or retrospective context.

Trust should remain inspectable through the structure of the Chronicle Entry and its supporting records rather than hidden behind an opaque score.

Trust and Historical Limitations

A Chronicle Entry may preserve incomplete, disputed, contradictory, unavailable, or retrospectively understood information. Those limitations should remain visible. Preserving uncertainty can strengthen historical trust when the uncertainty itself is honestly documented.

Transparency about limitations is part of trust.

Trust Model Role

The Chronicle Trust Model explains why a Chronicle Entry can remain reviewable across time. Trust is constructed through clear admission rules, stable identity, authoritative references, transparent Sources and Evidence, Provenance, Verification, Validation, visible Corrections, Version lineage, and durable preservation.

Trust is not declared. It is made reviewable through structure, authority, provenance, evidence, validation, correction lineage, and preservation.
