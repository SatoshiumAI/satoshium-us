Beacon · Certification Signals

Satoshium Beacon Certification Signals

Certification Signals define the certification-related discovery signals published by Beacon.

Beacon does not certify. Beacon publishes signals that help the world discover certification-related information.

Purpose

Certification Signals provide a common discovery model for surfacing certification-related information without transferring authority away from Certifier or Registry.

Core Distinction

Certification status belongs to Certifier. Registry status belongs to Registry. Discovery signals belong to Beacon.

Discovery Signal

A discovery signal is a surfaced indicator that points users, tools, or future systems toward certification-related records, events, or metadata.

Not Authority

A Beacon signal does not create certification authority, modify Registry status, preserve history, or prove integrity. It points back to systems that own those responsibilities.

Signal Types

Beacon may publish several certification-related signal types so users and systems can discover relevant certification states.

Certified → Expired → Revoked → Updated → Pending

Certified

A Certified signal indicates that Beacon has surfaced information pointing to a certification outcome or certified record maintained by the responsible authority.

Expired

An Expired signal indicates that Beacon has surfaced information suggesting a certification may have reached the end of its active period according to its authoritative source.

Revoked

A Revoked signal indicates that Beacon has surfaced information pointing to a certification revocation, withdrawal, or invalidation recorded by an authoritative system.

Updated

An Updated signal indicates that Beacon has surfaced information showing that certification-related records, metadata, references, or status values may have changed.

Pending

A Pending signal indicates that Beacon has surfaced information related to a certification process, registration process, review process, or record that has not yet reached a final authoritative state.

Future Signals

Future signals may include renewed, suspended, superseded, challenged, verified, anchored, attested, corrected, or other discovery-relevant states.

What a Signal Contains

A certification signal should preserve enough context to help users discover the relevant certification information while still pointing back to the authoritative system.

Signal Type · Source System · Related Certification · Related Registry Entry · Related Event · Public Reference · Signal Date · Last Observed

Relationship to Certifier

Certifier remains the authority for certification outcomes, certification status, evidence review, scoring, and certification artifacts.

Beacon may publish a Certified signal. Certifier determines whether something is certified.

Relationship to Registry

Registry remains the public catalog for certified records and SREG Registry Entries. Beacon may surface Registry references to help users discover those cataloged records.

Beacon may surface a Registry Entry. Registry owns the catalog.

Signal Interpretation

Signals are indicators, not conclusions. Users and systems should interpret Beacon signals by following their references to the authoritative records, events, anchors, attestations, or source systems that support them.

A signal helps something be found. It does not replace the thing it points to.

Signal Philosophy

Beacon helps the world discover certification-related information without becoming the certification authority. Its role is to publish signals, preserve references, and improve visibility.
