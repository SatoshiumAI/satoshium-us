Beacon · Sources

Sources

Sources provide the origin, context, and attribution behind the information surfaced through Beacon.

Discovery becomes more valuable when users can understand where information originated and how it connects to other records and signals.

Atlas Sources

Jurisdiction intelligence records, media records, profiles, and related information originating from Atlas.

Registry Sources

Structured records, metadata, identifiers, relationships, and registered information maintained by Registry.

Chronicle Sources

Historical entries, evidence, timelines, events, and preserved historical context maintained by Chronicle.

Anchor Sources

Identity-related records, claims, governance references, and reputation information associated with Anchor.

Attestor Sources

Attestations, trust-related records, confidence assessments, and validation information maintained by Attestor.

External Sources

Government records, research publications, public datasets, media organizations, and other third-party references.

Why Sources Matter

Information without attribution is difficult to evaluate.

Source → Signal → Discovery → Result

Sources help users understand where information originated and provide context for future investigation.

Source Principles

Attribution
Transparency
Traceability
Accessibility
Neutrality

Beacon seeks to preserve source visibility whenever practical throughout the discovery process.

Source Philosophy

Beacon does not replace sources.

Its purpose is to help users discover, organize, and navigate information while preserving its connection to the original source.

Discovery should illuminate origins, not obscure them.
