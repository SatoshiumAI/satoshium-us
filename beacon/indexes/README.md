# Indexes

## Purpose

Indexes are Beacon-owned discovery structures that organize references to information so users and Suite workflows can locate relevant Discovery Signals, sources, canonical objects, and relationships more efficiently.

Beacon uses indexes to reduce information friction across the Satoshium Suite and external information environments while preserving attribution, provenance, traceability, stable references, discovery context, and institutional authority.

Indexes improve discoverability.

They do not replace the information they reference.

---

## What Is an Index?

An index is an organized discovery structure that helps Beacon locate, classify, connect, and navigate references to information.

An index may organize references by:

* Topic
* Jurisdiction
* Source
* Suite institution
* Canonical-object type
* Discovery Signal type
* Historical context
* Relationship
* Time
* Status
* Other discovery-relevant classifications

Beacon may maintain an index without becoming authoritative for the objects represented within it.

---

## Why Indexes Matter

Information becomes harder to discover as its volume and complexity increase.

Indexes provide structure that can help:

* Reduce discovery friction
* Improve navigation
* Support classification
* Surface relationships
* Preserve source pathways
* Connect Discovery Signals to sources
* Locate institution-owned canonical objects
* Support cross-institution discovery
* Scale discovery as information grows

Indexing makes information easier to find without changing who owns or governs that information.

---

## Index Authority Boundary

Beacon may index references to institution-owned canonical objects without duplicating those objects or assuming their authority.

Beacon owns its:

* Index structures
* Discovery Signals
* Discovery metadata
* Discovery relationships and classifications

The originating Suite institution remains authoritative for its canonical object.

**Reference does not transfer authority.**

An index entry should therefore function as a discovery reference rather than as a replacement for the authoritative object.

---

## Indexing Canonical Objects

Beacon indexes may reference canonical objects maintained throughout the Suite:

* **Atlas → Authoritative Intelligence**
* **Certifier → Certification Package**
* **Registry → SREG**
* **Chronicle → Chronicle Entry**
* **Anchor → Integrity Reference**
* **Attestor → Trust Statement**

Navigator may define or orchestrate workflows that use Beacon indexes for discovery.

Beacon remains responsible for the discovery structures and metadata it publishes.

## Potential Index Types

### Jurisdiction Indexes

Organize discovery references associated with jurisdictions, geographic scope, or Atlas authoritative intelligence.

---

### Source Indexes

Organize references according to source institution, external provider, publication, dataset, or other source classification.

---

### Discovery Signal Indexes

Organize Beacon-owned Discovery Signals by signal type, subject, source, status, relationship, or other discovery metadata.

---

### Canonical-Object Indexes

Provide structured discovery references to canonical objects maintained by Suite institutions.

These indexes preserve the canonical identifier and originating institution rather than duplicating institutional authority.

---

### Certification Package Indexes

Organize discovery references to Certification Packages maintained by Certifier.

Certifier remains authoritative for certification and verification.

---

### SREG Indexes

Organize discovery references to SREG records maintained by Registry.

Registry remains authoritative for the registered record and its lifecycle.

---

### Chronicle Entry Indexes

Organize discovery references to Chronicle Entries, qualifying Occurrences, and related historical context.

Chronicle remains authoritative for its historical record.

---

### Integrity Reference Indexes

Organize discovery references to Integrity References and related anchoring relationships.

Anchor remains authoritative for its Integrity References.

---

### Trust Statement Indexes

Organize discovery references to Trust Statements and related trust context.

Attestor remains authoritative for its Trust Statements.

---

### External Source Indexes

Organize references to information originating outside the Satoshium Suite.

External discovery does not convert an external source into a Suite institution or its information into a Suite-authoritative object.

* Canonical-object type
* Subject
* Classification
* Discovery Signal relationship
* Provenance
* Relevant status
* Discovery context
* Relationships

Specific index schemas may evolve as Beacon advances toward production.

---

## Classification

Indexes may classify discovery references according to characteristics useful for navigation and retrieval.

Classification should support discovery without redefining the authoritative meaning of the referenced object.

Beacon classification is discovery metadata.

It does not replace institutional metadata maintained by the authoritative source.

---

## Relationships

Indexes may help reveal relationships between:

* Discovery Signals
* Sources
* Canonical objects
* Jurisdictions
* Certification Packages
* SREG records
* Chronicle Entries
* Integrity References
* Trust Statements
* External information

Relationships should preserve stable identifiers, provenance, and institutional authority whenever available.

---

## Relationship to Discovery

Indexes support Beacon discovery by organizing references that can be searched, filtered, compared, or connected.

A simplified relationship may be represented as:

```text
Source / Canonical Object
          ↓
   Indexed Reference
          ↓
       Discovery
          ↓
Discovery Signal / Metadata
          ↓
Result / Referenced Source
```

The index supports discovery.

The source remains authoritative.

---

## Relationship to Queries

Queries may use indexes to narrow or structure discovery.

Examples may include filtering by:

* Jurisdiction
* Institution
* Source type
* Canonical-object type
* Signal type
* Timeframe
* Relationship
* Status

A query may guide index-based discovery without making Beacon responsible for workflow orchestration.

Navigator retains responsibility for workflow definition and orchestration.

---

## Relationship to Results

Beacon results may use indexes to identify and present relevant Discovery Signals, sources, canonical objects, or relationships.

The presence of an indexed object within a result does not make the result authoritative for that object.

Results should preserve the path back to the authoritative source whenever practical.

---

## Relationship to Navigator

Navigator may define or orchestrate workflows that rely on Beacon indexes.

For example, a workflow may instruct Beacon to locate objects of a particular institution, type, jurisdiction, status, or relationship.

Navigator orchestrates the workflow.

Beacon performs discovery through its indexes and other discovery structures.

---

## Index Principles

### Organization

Indexes should make discovery structures understandable and usable.

### Discoverability

Relevant information should become easier to locate.

### Attribution

Sources and originating institutions should remain identifiable.

### Provenance

Indexed references should preserve sufficient origin and context for review.

### Traceability

Indexes should support navigation back to sources and canonical objects.

### Stable References

Canonical identifiers and durable source references should be preserved when available.

### Classification

Organization should assist discovery without redefining institutional meaning.

### Scalability

Index structures should support increasing information volume and relationship complexity.

### Neutrality

Indexing should not imply endorsement, certification, integrity, or trust.

### Authority Preservation

Beacon indexes references without assuming authority over the objects referenced.

---

## Index Philosophy

Information becomes more useful when it can be discovered, organized, and explored efficiently.

Beacon indexes the reference, not the authority.

```text
Index the reference.
Preserve the identifier.
Retain the provenance.
Keep the source visible.
```

**Reference does not transfer authority.**

## Long-Term Vision

As Beacon evolves, indexes may become a foundational discovery mechanism connecting Discovery Signals, sources, authoritative intelligence, Certification Packages, SREG records, Chronicle Entries, Integrity References, Trust Statements, and external information.

Indexes can help Beacon scale discovery without collapsing institutional boundaries.

Beacon organizes discovery.

The originating institution preserves authority.
