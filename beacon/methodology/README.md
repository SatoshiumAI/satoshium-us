## Governing Principle

> **Discovery becomes institutional only through a preserved and reviewable process.**

## Canonical Methodology

Beacon's governed methodology is:

```text
Observe
→ Identify
→ Establish Source & Provenance
→ Assess Relevance
→ Construct
→ Create
→ Validate & Review
→ Determine State
→ Decide Publication
→ Represent
→ Maintain
```

Creation is explicit because a canonical Discovery Signal must exist, receive its permanent BEAC identifier, and enter `Draft · Unpublished` before object-level Validation.

## Methodology Stages

1. **Observe** — encounter potentially relevant information, an object, condition, event, or relationship.
2. **Identify** — determine whether the observation presents a distinct candidate discovery.
3. **Establish Source & Provenance** — preserve what was observed and how it was encountered.
4. **Assess Relevance** — determine whether the candidate belongs within Beacon's Discovery & Signals role.
5. **Construct** — assemble the candidate Discovery Signal according to the Entry Model.
6. **Create** — create the canonical object, assign its BEAC identifier, and enter Draft.
7. **Validate & Review** — evaluate conformance, provenance, authority boundaries, references, and relationships.
8. **Determine State** — apply the governed lifecycle state.
9. **Decide Publication** — determine whether the signal is eligible and approved for public release.
10. **Represent** — expose a Published signal through Beacon Records and its individual Beacon Record.
11. **Maintain** — preserve history and govern re-observation, correction, versioning, supersession, resolution, or withdrawal.

## Stop Conditions

Not every observation becomes a Discovery Signal.

A candidate may stop, defer, or require further review when:

- relevance is insufficient
- the source cannot be meaningfully identified
- provenance is inadequate
- the candidate duplicates an existing discovery without new institutional meaning
- Beacon lacks a legitimate basis to represent it

## Authority Boundary

Throughout the methodology:

```text
Observe a source ≠ own the source
Reference an object ≠ inherit its authority
Publish a Discovery Signal ≠ certify the source assertion
```

> **Reference does not transfer authority.**

## Human Judgment and Automation

Institutional judgment may remain necessary for relevance, provenance sufficiency, relationship meaning, restrictions, and publication suitability.

Future automation may assist execution without replacing Beacon's governed architecture.

```text
Automation may execute process.
Architecture governs process.
```
