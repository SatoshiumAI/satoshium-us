# Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

## What is Beacon?

Beacon is the Satoshium Suite institution for **Discovery & Signals**.

Its purpose is to help users and Suite workflows locate, surface, organize, and explore relevant information while preserving source attribution, provenance, traceability, context, and institutional authority.

Beacon performs discovery and publishes Beacon-owned **Discovery Signals** and discovery metadata.

---

## What does Beacon do?

Beacon assists with:

* Discovery
* Discovery Signal publication
* Discovery metadata
* Information navigation
* Source exploration
* Query support
* Indexing
* Result presentation
* Relationship discovery
* Canonical-object discovery

Beacon helps users and workflows locate information that may be relevant to a question, topic, jurisdiction, event, canonical object, investigation, or workflow objective.

---

## Does Beacon verify information?

No.

Certifier owns certification and verification authority.

Beacon may discover and reference verified or certified information, but discovery and verification remain separate institutional functions.

---

## Does Beacon certify information?

No.

Certifier owns **Certification Packages** and the Suite's certification function.

Beacon does not issue Certification Packages, approvals, or endorsements.

---

## Does Beacon create records?

Beacon creates and owns its own Discovery Signals and discovery metadata.

It does **not** create or assume authority over Registry records merely by discovering them.

Registry owns **SREG** records and their lifecycle.

---

## Does Beacon own the records or objects it surfaces?

No.

Beacon may reference institution-owned canonical objects for discovery without duplicating them or assuming their authority.

The originating institution remains authoritative for its canonical object.

**Reference does not transfer authority.**

---

## Does Beacon preserve history?

Beacon does not own the Suite's historical record.

Chronicle owns **Chronicle Entries** and preserves qualifying historical Occurrences.

Beacon may discover and reference Chronicle Entries and historical context while Chronicle retains historical authority.

---

## Does Beacon manage identities?

No.

The current Suite architecture does not assign identity management to Beacon or Anchor.

Anchor owns **Integrity References** and the Suite's integrity-preservation function.

Beacon may discover and reference Integrity References without assuming Anchor's authority.

---

## Does Beacon establish trust?

No.

Attestor owns **Trust Statements** and the Suite's trust-assessment function.

Beacon may discover Trust Statements or publish trust-related Discovery Signals, but it does not independently determine trust.

---

## What is a Discovery Signal?

A Discovery Signal is a Beacon-owned object documenting an observation, event, change, indicator, update, or relationship identified through discovery as potentially relevant.

A Discovery Signal may direct attention to a source or canonical object.

It does not inherit the authority of what it references.

---

## What is discovery?

Discovery is Beacon's process of locating and surfacing information that may be relevant to a user, query, investigation, or Navigator-defined workflow.

Discovery should preserve the path to the source and the authority boundary of referenced information.

---

## What is a source?

A source is the origin of information used or referenced during discovery.

Sources may include:

* Atlas authoritative intelligence
* Certifier Certification Packages
* Registry SREG records
* Chronicle Entries
* Anchor Integrity References
* Attestor Trust Statements
* Government publications
* Research materials
* Public datasets
* Other documented external references

External discovery does not make an external source a Suite institution or convert its information into a Suite-authoritative object.

---

## What is an index?

An index is a Beacon-owned discovery structure used to organize references and improve discoverability.

Indexes may organize Discovery Signals, sources, canonical-object references, and relationships.

Beacon indexes the reference, not the authority.

---

## What are queries?

Queries express discovery intent and context.

A query may represent a search, request, investigation, exploration, or discovery objective.

Beacon may receive queries directly or participate in a Navigator-defined workflow.

Navigator retains responsibility for workflow definition and orchestration.

---

## What are results?

Results are Beacon presentations of information surfaced through discovery.

Results may include:

* Discovery Signals
* Discovery metadata
* Source references
* Canonical-object references
* Historical context
* Jurisdiction information
* Integrity References
* Trust Statements
* Relationships
* External references

A result does not become authoritative merely because it references authoritative information.

---

## How does Beacon relate to Navigator?

Navigator owns **Workflow Definition / Orchestration**.

Beacon owns the discovery function and its Discovery Signals and discovery metadata.

Navigator may define or orchestrate a workflow requiring discovery.

Beacon performs that discovery and returns its Beacon-specific outputs.

**Navigator orchestrates. Beacon discovers and signals.**

---

## How does Beacon relate to Atlas?

Atlas owns **Authoritative Intelligence**.

Beacon may discover and reference Atlas intelligence while preserving Atlas as the authoritative institution for that information.

---

## How does Beacon fit into the Satoshium Suite?

The canonical institutional model is:

* **Atlas → Authoritative Intelligence**
* **Navigator → Workflow Definition / Orchestration**
* **Beacon → Discovery Signal / Metadata**
* **Certifier → Certification Package**
* **Registry → SREG**
* **Chronicle → Chronicle Entry**
* **Anchor → Integrity Reference**
* **Attestor → Trust Statement**

Each institution owns its canonical responsibilities and objects.

Interoperability connects them without collapsing their authority boundaries.

---

## Is Beacon a search engine?

Not necessarily.

Beacon may incorporate search functionality, but Beacon is broader than search alone.

Its institutional scope includes discovery, Discovery Signals, discovery metadata, indexing, source visibility, relationship discovery, result presentation, and navigation to authoritative information.

---

## Does discovery mean information is true?

No.

Discovery indicates that information may be relevant.

A Beacon result or Discovery Signal does not independently establish:

* Accuracy
* Verification
* Certification
* Registration
* Historical authority
* Integrity
* Trustworthiness
* Endorsement

Those questions remain subject to the appropriate evidence, review process, source, or Suite institution.

---

## Can Beacon discover external information?

Yes.

Beacon may discover information outside the Satoshium Suite.

External sources should remain attributable and traceable whenever practical.

Their discovery by Beacon does not make them Suite institutions or Suite-authoritative objects.

---

## Is Beacon complete?

No.

Beacon is undergoing Suite alignment and production preparation ahead of its originally planned November 2026 development window.

Its operational schemas, discovery methods, signal structures, indexing mechanisms, result presentation, and workflow interfaces may continue to evolve.

Beacon should not be considered operational solely because its documentation or architecture is complete. Operational status requires the architecture to be proven through actual institutional use.

## Governing Principle

Beacon makes information discoverable while preserving the path to its source.

```text
Discover the information.
Publish the signal.
Preserve the source.
Retain the authority boundary.
```

**Reference does not transfer authority.**
