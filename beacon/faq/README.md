# Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

## What is Beacon?

Beacon is the discovery layer of the Satoshium Suite.

Its purpose is to help users locate, surface, organize, and explore relevant information across the Satoshium ecosystem.

Beacon helps users find signals.

---

## What does Beacon do?

Beacon assists with:

* Discovery
* Signal identification
* Information navigation
* Source exploration
* Query support
* Result organization
* Information visibility

Beacon helps users locate information that may be relevant to a particular question, topic, jurisdiction, event, or record.

---

## Does Beacon verify information?

No.

Verification is the responsibility of Certifier.

Beacon may help users discover information, but discovery and verification are separate functions.

---

## Does Beacon certify information?

No.

Certification activities belong to Certifier.

Beacon does not issue certifications, ratings, approvals, or endorsements.

---

## Does Beacon create records?

No.

Registry is responsible for maintaining structured records.

Beacon may help users discover records, but it does not create or manage them.

---

## Does Beacon preserve history?

No.

Chronicle preserves historical records and events.

Beacon may help users discover historical information maintained by Chronicle.

---

## Does Beacon manage identities?

No.

Anchor provides identity-related structures.

Beacon may help users discover identity-related information but does not create or manage identities.

---

## Does Beacon establish trust?

No.

Attestor is responsible for trust-related assessments and attestations.

Beacon helps users find information but does not determine whether information should be trusted.

---

## What is a signal?

A signal is an observation, event, update, record, source, or data point that may be relevant to a user, query, or area of interest.

Signals may vary in importance, relevance, confidence, and visibility.

---

## What is discovery?

Discovery is the process of locating and surfacing relevant information.

Discovery helps transform large collections of information into navigable information environments.

---

## What is a source?

A source is the origin of information.

Sources may include:

* Public records
* Government publications
* Research materials
* News organizations
* Registry records
* Chronicle entries
* Certified materials
* Other documented references

---

## What is an index?

An index is an organizational structure used to improve discovery.

Indexes help users locate information more efficiently.

---

## What are queries?

Queries represent user intent.

A query may be a search, request, investigation, exploration, or discovery action used to locate relevant information.

---

## What are results?

Results are the outputs generated through discovery processes.

Results may include:

* Records
* Sources
* Signals
* Historical entries
* Jurisdictions
* Media references
* Identity references
* Related information

---

## How does Beacon relate to Navigator?

Navigator provides query and exploration capabilities.

Beacon provides discovery capabilities.

Navigator asks questions.

Beacon helps locate the information needed to answer them.

---

## How does Beacon relate to Atlas?

Atlas provides jurisdictional and media information.

Beacon helps users discover and navigate Atlas content.

---

## Is Beacon a search engine?

Not necessarily.

Beacon may incorporate search functionality, but Beacon is broader than search alone.

Beacon includes discovery, organization, indexing, signal identification, and information navigation.

---

## Is Beacon complete?

No.

Beacon is currently under development.

Its standards, schemas, discovery methods, interoperability rules, and operational capabilities may evolve over time.
