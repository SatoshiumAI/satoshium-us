# Satoshium Beacon

## Overview

**Satoshium Beacon** is the Satoshium Suite institution for **Discovery & Signals**.

Beacon helps users, applications, and workflows locate, surface, organize, interpret, and trace relevant information across the Satoshium Suite and attributable external sources.

Its canonical responsibility centers on:

* **Discovery Signals**
* **Discovery Metadata**

Beacon may reference canonical objects maintained by other Suite institutions, but it does not acquire their authority.

**Reference does not transfer authority.**

---

## Mission

Beacon's mission is simple:

```text
Help users find the signal.
Preserve the path to the source.
```

Beacon exists to make relevant information easier to discover while preserving:

* Attribution
* Provenance
* Traceability
* Discovery context
* Stable references
* Relationships
* Institutional ownership
* Authority boundaries

Discovery improves visibility.

It does not redefine authority.

---

Beacon occupies a distinct institutional position within the Satoshium Suite.

```text

```

Each institution retains authority over its own canonical responsibilities and objects.

Beacon connects discovery to those objects without collapsing institutional boundaries.

## Canonical Responsibility

Beacon owns its own discovery-layer objects and structures.

These include:

* Discovery Signals
* Discovery Metadata
* Beacon indexes
* Discovery presentations and results
* Beacon source references
* Discovery relationships
* Discovery context

Beacon may point to information maintained elsewhere.

The referenced source remains authoritative for its own information.

## Discovery Signals

A **Discovery Signal** is a Beacon-owned object documenting an observation, event, change, indicator, update, relationship, or other discovery that may warrant attention.

A Discovery Signal may reference:

* Atlas Authoritative Intelligence
* Certification Packages
* SREG records
* Chronicle Entries
* Integrity References
* Trust Statements
* External sources
* Other Discovery Signals

A Discovery Signal is not automatically a conclusion.

It does not independently establish:

* Certification
* Registration
* Historical authority
* Integrity
* Trust
* Verification
* Authoritative intelligence

It identifies relevance and preserves a discoverable path to supporting context or authority.

---

It may preserve:

* Discovery Identifier
* Signal Type
* Subject

* Canonical Object Type
* Canonical Object Identifier
* Provenance
* Attribution
* Discovery Context
* Relevant Status

Discovery Metadata helps users and systems understand what was discovered, where it came from, how it relates to other information, and where authority remains.

## Discovery

Beacon discovery is the process of locating, surfacing, organizing, and presenting potentially relevant information.

Discovery may begin through:

* A direct query
* A Navigator-defined workflow
* A source observation
* An institutional event
* A relationship
* A change in relevant status
* An external information source
* Another governed discovery context

A generalized flow may be represented as:

```text
Workflow / Query
      ↓
Beacon Discovery
      ↓
Discovery Signal / Metadata
      ↓
Result / Referenced Source or Canonical Object
      ↓
Review / Navigation / Continued Workflow
```

This is an interoperability model, not a mandatory linear sequence for every Beacon activity.

* Questions
* Objectives
* Investigations
* Search requests
* Filters
* Research contexts
* Workflow inputs

## Results

Beacon Results are presentations of information surfaced through discovery.

Results may include:

* Discovery Signals
* Sources
* Canonical-object references
* Relationships
* Historical references
* Integrity-related information
* Trust-related information
* Composite discovery contexts

A result is not automatically the authoritative endpoint.

Where an authoritative canonical object exists, Beacon should preserve the path to it.

---

## Trust Boundary

Beacon may discover and surface trust-related information.

Beacon does not issue Trust Statements.

**Attestor** owns Trust Statements and trust-assessment authority.

Beacon may publish a Discovery Signal associated with trust-related information or reference an Attestor Trust Statement without becoming the trust authority.

---

## Certification Signals

Certification Signals are Beacon-owned Discovery Signals identifying certification-related information observed from authoritative sources.

Examples may include:

* Certified
* Expired
* Revoked
* Updated
* Pending

These signal types describe what Beacon observed.

They do not independently create the certification state.

**Certifier** retains authority for Certification Packages and certification status.

**Registry** retains authority for SREG records.
