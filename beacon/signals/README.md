# Signals

## Purpose

Signals are observations, events, changes, indicators, records, updates, or relationships that may warrant attention.

They represent information that has become visible through the discovery process.

Beacon exists to help users find signals.

---

## What Is a Signal?

A signal is information that may be relevant.

Signals may emerge from:

* Records
* Events
* Publications
* Jurisdictions
* Historical developments
* Identity activity
* Trust-related activity
* Research
* Emerging trends

A signal is not a conclusion.

A signal indicates that information may deserve further exploration.

---

## Why Signals Matter

Modern information environments contain more information than any individual can reasonably process.

Not all information is equally useful.

Signals help identify information that may be worthy of attention.

Discovery helps surface those signals.

---

## Signal Philosophy

A signal is not truth.

A signal is not proof.

A signal is not verification.

A signal is not trust.

A signal is an indication that something may be relevant.

Beacon seeks to improve visibility, not determine conclusions.

---

## Types of Signals

### Information Signals

New or updated information that may be relevant.

Examples:

* Publications
* Reports
* Research
* Announcements

---

### Jurisdiction Signals

Developments associated with jurisdictions.

Examples:

* Regulatory changes
* Policy updates
* Legislative activity
* Economic developments

---

### Historical Signals

Events that may influence historical understanding.

Examples:

* Historical discoveries
* Record updates
* New evidence
* Historical milestones

---

### Identity Signals

Information associated with entities or identities.

Examples:

* Identity creation
* Identity updates
* Governance activity
* Reputation activity

---

### Registry Signals

Information associated with registered records.

Examples:

* Record creation
* Record modification
* Record archival
* Record correction

---

### Trust Signals

Information related to attestations, confidence, or trust assessments.

Examples:

* New attestations
* Validation activity
* Trust assessments
* Confidence updates

---

### Relationship Signals

Connections identified between records, sources, events, or entities.

Examples:

* Shared sources
* Shared records
* Historical relationships
* Jurisdiction relationships

---

## Signal Lifecycle

Signals may move through several stages.

### Identification

Information becomes visible.

### Discovery

Beacon surfaces the signal.

### Investigation

Users explore the signal.

### Evaluation

Additional context may be gathered.

### Resolution

The signal becomes understood, archived, superseded, or incorporated into future knowledge.

---

## Relationship to Discovery

Discovery exists to help locate signals.

A simplified Beacon process may be represented as:

```text
Query
  ↓
Discovery
  ↓
Signal
```

Signals represent one of the primary outputs of discovery.

---

## Relationship to Sources

Signals should remain connected to their sources whenever possible.

Users should be able to understand:

* Where information originated
* Which sources support the signal
* How the signal was identified

Source visibility remains a core Beacon principle.

---

## Relationship to Results

Signals may contribute to discovery results.

Multiple signals may appear within a single result.

A single signal may contribute to multiple results.

---

## Relationship to Verification

Signals are not verification.

The existence of a signal does not imply:

* Accuracy
* Truth
* Certification
* Trustworthiness

Verification remains the responsibility of Certifier.

Trust-related assessments remain the responsibility of Attestor.

---

## Guiding Principles

### Visibility

Potentially relevant information should be easier to find.

### Transparency

Signal origins should remain visible.

### Attribution

Sources should remain identifiable.

### Traceability

Signals should support future review.

### Neutrality

Signals should support exploration without imposing conclusions.

---

## Long-Term Vision

As Beacon evolves, signals may become one of the primary mechanisms through which users discover emerging developments across the Satoshium ecosystem.

Signals help users focus attention.

Discovery helps users find those signals.

---

## Status

Beacon signal standards are currently under development.

This document defines conceptual principles rather than finalized operational specifications.
