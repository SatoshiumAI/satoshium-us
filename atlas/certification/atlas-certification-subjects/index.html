🧭 Atlas Certification Subjects

Atlas Certification Subjects define the canonical Satoshium Atlas objects that may be submitted for certification under the Satoshium Suite.

This page distinguishes certification subjects from supporting artifacts. Complete Atlas intelligence records may become certification subjects; evidence, metadata, signals, trust dimensions, and change logs support certification but are not normally certified as isolated objects.

Purpose

The Atlas Certification Subjects page identifies which Atlas objects may serve as the primary subject of a certification evaluation. It prevents supporting artifacts from being confused with the complete Atlas records they support.

The Subjects page answers one primary question: what Atlas object is being certified?

Subjects Role

Certification Subjects sit beneath the Framework, Profile, and Scope. The Framework explains how Atlas participates in certification; the Profile identifies evaluated characteristics; the Scope defines the boundary; Subjects identify the actual objects eligible for certification.

Subject Principle

Atlas certification applies to complete intelligence records rather than isolated supporting files. Supporting artifacts provide evidence, context, traceability, and review material for the subject, but they do not ordinarily become independent certification subjects.

The certification subject is the record being evaluated. Supporting artifacts explain and substantiate that record.

Primary Certification Subjects

Primary certification subjects are complete Atlas record objects that may be evaluated, certified, and referenced throughout the Satoshium Suite.

Jurisdiction Intelligence Engine Records

A Jurisdiction Intelligence Engine record is the primary Atlas certification subject. It represents a complete jurisdiction intelligence package with supporting evidence, metadata, trust dimensions, signals, and change history.

Country Records

Country records may serve as certification subjects when they represent complete, canonical Atlas jurisdiction records for a nation-state or country-level jurisdiction.

State Records

State records may serve as certification subjects when they represent complete, canonical Atlas jurisdiction records for a U.S. state or comparable subnational jurisdiction.

Future Atlas Record Families

Future Atlas record families may become certification subjects if they are defined as complete canonical records with evidence, metadata, traceability, and reproducible review material.

Future candidates may include corridor records, regional records, institutional records, or dataset records if Atlas formally defines them.

Supporting Certification Artifacts

Supporting artifacts are reviewed as part of certification but are not usually the certification subject. They help Certifier evaluate the record, reconstruct the reasoning, and preserve traceability.

Evidence

Evidence supports the certification subject by documenting source material, substantiating claims, and preserving the basis for review.

Metadata

Metadata supports identification, classification, versioning, update state, repository structure, and machine-readable record context.

Signals

Signals support evaluation by identifying evidence-derived indicators used within the Atlas record and certification review process.

Trust Dimensions

Trust dimensions support evaluation by organizing the trust-related qualities examined within the Atlas record.

Change Logs

Change logs support certification by preserving update history, revision context, and certification-relevant record changes.

Builder Mode & Profile Files

Builder mode, profile, and related files support the complete record by documenting record construction, organization, and review context.

Subjects Include

  • Complete Atlas jurisdiction records
  • Canonical JIE record packages
  • Country-level Atlas records
  • State-level Atlas records
  • Future Atlas record families formally defined as certification subjects

Subjects Exclude

  • Standalone evidence files
  • Standalone metadata files
  • Standalone signal files
  • Standalone trust dimension files
  • Standalone change logs
  • Registry, Chronicle, Anchor, Beacon, Attestor, or Navigator records

Subject Rules

Atlas certification subjects must preserve the distinction between complete records and their supporting materials.

  • A certification subject must be a complete Atlas record or formally defined Atlas record family.
  • Supporting artifacts may be evaluated but are not normally certified independently.
  • Certification subjects must be identifiable, traceable, and reproducible.
  • Certifier evaluates the subject using the Atlas Certification Profile and Scope.
  • Operational Suite outputs remain owned by their respective systems.
Subject discipline prevents the Suite from certifying fragments when the authoritative object is the complete Atlas record.

Subject Versioning

Certification subjects may evolve as Atlas expands. New subject families should be documented before they become eligible for certification, and certification packages should identify the subject type evaluated at the time of certification.

Subjects Philosophy

Atlas certifies complete intelligence records rather than isolated fragments. Supporting artifacts make those records reviewable, but the certification subject remains the complete canonical Atlas object.

Certification subjects define what may be certified. Supporting artifacts explain why certification can be trusted.
Satoshium is being built slowly, in public, and with architectural discipline.
