# 📊 Atlas Comparison Surfaces
## Cross-Jurisdiction Structural Interpretability Layer

The **Atlas Comparison Layer** provides structured, evidence-derived views across jurisdictions to reveal infrastructure topology patterns, governance posture continuity, research ecosystem clustering, and compute-environment alignment.

Comparison surfaces do not rank jurisdictions.

Comparison surfaces do not prescribe deployment decisions.

Comparison surfaces expose **cross-state structural relationships derived strictly from signals**.

---

## 🎯 Purpose

The comparison layer exists to:

- enable cross-jurisdiction signal interpretability
- preserve normalization consistency across states
- surface structural topology similarities
- identify corridor emergence conditions
- support future routing-awareness layers for Aegis
- provide machine-readable comparison matrices

Comparison surfaces transform jurisdiction packages into **interpretable regional signal structures**.

---

## 🧱 Comparison Model

Comparison views are derived strictly from:

signals.md
trust-dimensions.md
metadata.md

They must never introduce new signals.

They must never modify jurisdiction packages.

They must never create rankings.
