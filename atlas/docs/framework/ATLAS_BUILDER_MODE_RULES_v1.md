# ATLAS Builder Mode Rules v1

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the interpretation rules for `builder-mode.md` in Atlas jurisdiction packages.

`builder-mode.md` translates interpretive posture into deployment-facing context.

`builder-mode.md` supports infrastructure siting reasoning.

`builder-mode.md` does not introduce classification authority.

`builder-mode.md` does not modify topology placement.

`builder-mode.md` does not promote lifecycle stage.

`builder-mode.md` does not replace evidence authority.

Builder Mode is the deployment-facing interpretation layer.

It exists to describe siting posture without altering canonical package authority.

## 2. Builder Authority Boundary

`builder-mode.md` may:
- describe deployment readiness posture
- describe infrastructure compatibility signals
- describe adjacency-supported siting logic
- describe corridor continuity access pathways
- describe constraint-aware deployment conditions

`builder-mode.md` must not:
- assign anchor classification
- assign corridor attachment
- modify topology trio
- override interpretation boundary language
- replace lifecycle status

Builder Mode is an applied interpretation layer.

It may translate posture into siting relevance.

It may not create new canonical authority.

## 3. Builder Inputs

`builder-mode.md` may synthesize only from:
- `profile.md`
- `signals.md`
- `trust-dimensions.md`
- `evidence.md` deployment-relevant excerpts
- `metadata.md` lifecycle posture

`builder-mode.md` must not introduce external siting assumptions without signal or evidence grounding.

If a deployment-facing conclusion cannot be traced to canonical package materials, it does not belong in `builder-mode.md`.

## 4. Deployment Readiness Interpretation

`builder-mode.md` may describe:
- energy availability posture
- research infrastructure accessibility
- transport continuity availability
- adjacency-supported participation pathways
- federal overlay alignment

Deployment readiness descriptions must remain evidence- or signal-grounded.

`builder-mode.md` must not imply deployment completion where infrastructure remains pipeline-stage.

Readiness posture may be directional, conditional, or bounded.

It may not be narrated as completed capacity when the canonical package does not support that claim.

## 5. Signal Translation Rules for Builders

`builder-mode.md` may translate:
- pipeline signals -> near-term readiness indicators
- capacity signals -> throughput expansion relevance
- transition signals -> classification trajectory awareness
- adjacency signals -> continuity-supported siting access

`builder-mode.md` must not:
- convert signals into corridor attachment
- convert signals into anchor equivalence
- treat signals as deployment authorization

Signals may inform siting posture.

They may not independently justify topology or deployment authority.

## 6. Adjacency Interpretation for Builders

`builder-mode.md` may:
- describe adjacency-supported corridor access
- describe neighboring anchor proximity relevance
- describe infrastructure dependency pathways

`builder-mode.md` must not:
- treat adjacency as anchor presence
- treat adjacency as corridor authority
- replace topology trio classification

Adjacency remains continuity support, not deployment equivalence.

Builder Mode may explain why adjacency matters for siting.

It may not collapse adjacency into direct placement authority.

## 7. Federal Overlay Deployment Interpretation

`builder-mode.md` may:
- describe federal infrastructure alignment relevance
- describe interstate transmission participation pathways
- describe research facility access implications
- describe defense infrastructure continuity relevance

`builder-mode.md` must preserve federal reliance visibility.

Federal overlays must not be treated as anchor placement authority.

Federal relevance may strengthen siting interpretation.

It does not erase dependence, scope limits, or topology boundaries.

## 8. Capacity Interpretation Rules

`builder-mode.md` may interpret:
- grid expansion signals
- transport throughput signals
- water availability signals
- research throughput signals

Capacity interpretation must remain signal- or evidence-grounded.

Capacity signals must not be treated as deployment guarantees.

Capacity posture may indicate readiness relevance, but it must remain conditional when canonical package materials remain conditional.

## 9. Constraint Propagation Rules

`builder-mode.md` must preserve visibility of:
- deployment exclusions
- policy constraints
- infrastructure gaps
- resource limitations
- adjacency dependence
- federal reliance

Constraint visibility must remain intact after deployment-oriented synthesis.

If builder-facing readability weakens or hides canonical constraints, the synthesis is non-compliant.

## 10. Corridor Continuity Interpretation Limits

`builder-mode.md` may:
- describe corridor accessibility pathways
- describe multi-jurisdiction siting participation logic
- describe expansion-edge participation posture

`builder-mode.md` must not:
- assign corridor origin authority
- assign terminus authority
- override topology trio

Corridor continuity does not imply corridor control.

Deployment-facing interpretation may describe access and continuity.

It may not assign stronger corridor role than the canonical package supports.

## 11. Lifecycle Interaction Rules

`builder-mode.md` may:
- describe maturity posture implications for deployment timing
- describe normalization completeness relevance
- describe classification stability implications

`builder-mode.md` must not:
- promote lifecycle stage
- infer lifecycle advancement
- replace metadata lifecycle declaration

Lifecycle state may shape builder timing posture.

It may not be rewritten by Builder Mode.

## 12. Builder–Profile Relationship

`builder-mode.md` extends profile interpretation into deployment-facing context.

`builder-mode.md` must not:
- reinterpret profile classification posture
- introduce alternate topology meaning
- replace profile synthesis boundaries

`builder-mode.md` remains downstream from `profile.md`.

Profile describes how a jurisdiction reads.

Builder Mode describes what that posture means for siting-relevant interpretation.

## 13. Renderer Interaction Rules

The renderer may:
- compress builder-mode summaries
- surface deployment posture highlights
- group constraint signals for siting visibility

The renderer must not:
- strengthen classification claims
- remove constraint visibility
- introduce deployment claims not present in canonical sources

Renderer normalization may improve readability.

It may not increase builder authority beyond the canonical builder contract.

## 14. Builder Compliance Definition

A jurisdiction package is builder-mode compliant when all of the following are true:
- deployment posture remains canonically grounded
- signals are not elevated to classification authority
- adjacency remains continuity support only
- constraint visibility is preserved
- federal reliance remains visible
- topology trio is preserved

Non-compliance occurs when any of the following are true:
- builder-mode introduces classification authority
- builder-mode upgrades adjacency to anchor equivalence
- builder-mode replaces lifecycle status
- builder-mode suppresses deployment constraints
- builder-mode converts signals into corridor attachment
