# ATLAS Trust Dimensions Schema v1

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the trust-dimensions schema for Atlas jurisdiction packages.

Trust dimensions describe interpretation stability.

Trust dimensions clarify confidence posture without scoring.

Trust dimensions preserve constraint visibility.

Trust dimensions do not replace evidence authority.

Trust dimensions do not modify topology classification.

Trust dimensions do not change lifecycle status.

Trust dimensions translate structured evidence and signals into interpretive posture while preserving authority boundaries and visible limits.

## 2. Trust Authority Boundary

Trust dimensions may:
- describe interpretation stability
- clarify classification support strength
- explain constraint visibility
- identify boundary dependence
- explain adjacency reliance

Trust dimensions must not:
- assign numeric confidence
- rank jurisdictions
- override evidence
- modify topology classification
- promote lifecycle status

Trust is interpretive.

It is not evidentiary authority.

It is not topology authority.

It is not lifecycle authority.

## 3. Trust Interpretation Model Overview

Trust is composed of the following interpretive dimensions:
- evidence support basis
- constraint visibility
- boundary clarity
- adjacency dependence
- federal reliance where applicable
- interpretation continuity

These dimensions describe how stable, bounded, and intelligible a jurisdiction reading is within Atlas.

Trust expresses interpretive posture.

It does not express capability evaluation, numeric confidence, or rank ordering.

## 4. Evidence Support Basis

Evidence support basis describes how strongly classification depends on direct infrastructure presence versus indirect support structures.

Possible support-basis types include:
- direct anchor infrastructure
- adjacent infrastructure continuity
- federal overlay support
- policy-based attachment
- research participation structures

Support basis clarifies interpretation strength without altering classification authority.

It explains what kinds of support the reading rests upon.

It does not redefine the canonical topology contract.

## 5. Constraint Visibility Dimension

Constraint visibility describes how clearly classification limits are documented.

Examples may include:
- deployment absence visibility
- policy exclusions
- infrastructure gaps
- resource constraints
- regulatory limitations

Constraint visibility strengthens interpretation reliability.

Hidden constraints weaken interpretive clarity.

When a constraint materially affects interpretation, it should remain visible in trust language rather than being buried in upstream files alone.

## 6. Boundary Clarity Dimension

Boundary clarity describes how explicitly interpretation limits are defined.

Boundary clarity may include:
- scope narrowing language
- adjacency limits
- non-establishment statements
- classification exclusions
- federal reliance statements

Clear boundaries improve interpretive stability.

Boundary clarity does not mean stronger classification.

It means the limits of interpretation are easier to understand and govern.

## 7. Adjacency Dependence Dimension

Adjacency dependence describes how strongly classification relies on neighboring infrastructure continuity.

Adjacency dependence may be:
- minimal
- supporting
- structural
- primary continuity basis

Adjacency dependence must remain interpretation-boundary constrained.

Adjacency dependence does not imply anchor equivalence.

This dimension explains dependence, not authority elevation.

## 8. Federal Reliance Dimension

Federal reliance describes classification dependence on national infrastructure participation.

Examples may include:
- federal grid corridors
- national research facilities
- defense infrastructure continuity
- interstate transmission designation

Federal reliance strengthens continuity interpretation when it is evidence-grounded.

It must remain visible as reliance rather than independent authority.

Federal relevance does not erase scope limits or adjacency dependence.

## 9. Interpretation Continuity Dimension

Interpretation continuity describes stability of classification across adjacent jurisdictions or corridor segments.

Continuity indicators may include:
- consistent corridor participation
- multi-state infrastructure alignment
- stable cross-border attachment logic
- federal overlay consistency

Interpretation continuity strengthens topology confidence in the interpretive sense.

It does not change topology fields.

It explains how coherent a jurisdiction reading remains across its surrounding structural environment.

## 10. Trust Interaction with Signals

Trust dimensions may:
- interpret signals
- bound signals
- clarify signal reliability

Trust dimensions must not:
- replace signals
- elevate signals to evidence
- convert signals into classification authority

Trust consumes signals as interpretive inputs.

It does not rewrite their authority level.

## 11. Trust Interaction with Topology Classification

Trust dimensions may:
- clarify classification support strength
- explain completion-layer interpretation stability
- describe adjacency dependence

Trust dimensions must not:
- change topology trio fields
- override completion-layer semantics
- introduce new topology roles

Trust explains the stability of topology reading.

It does not alter the canonical topology contract.

## 12. Trust Interaction with Lifecycle Status

Trust dimensions may:
- clarify maturity stability
- explain normalization completeness
- describe classification confidence posture

Trust dimensions must not:
- promote lifecycle stage
- replace metadata status
- infer lifecycle advancement

Lifecycle state remains governed by metadata status and lifecycle rules, not by trust narration.

## 13. Trust Visibility Requirements

Trust dimensions must appear in:
- `trust-dimensions.md`

Trust dimensions may also appear in:
- `profile.md` synthesis summaries
- `change-log.md` normalization explanations
- renderer helper-layer visibility lifts where permitted

Trust dimensions must remain visible where they materially affect interpretation posture.

Renderer compression may reorganize trust presentation, but it may not remove trust conditions that materially shape jurisdiction interpretation.

## 14. Trust Compliance Definition

A jurisdiction package is trust-compliant when all of the following are true:
- trust dimensions remain evidence-grounded
- constraint visibility is preserved
- boundary clarity is documented
- adjacency dependence is visible where applicable
- federal reliance is visible where applicable
- trust language avoids scoring

Non-compliance occurs when any of the following are true:
- trust introduces ranking
- trust modifies topology classification
- trust replaces evidence authority
- trust promotes lifecycle status
- trust obscures interpretation boundaries
