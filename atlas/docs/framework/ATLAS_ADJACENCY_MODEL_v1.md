# ATLAS Adjacency Model v1

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the adjacency interpretation model for Atlas jurisdiction packages.

Adjacency allows jurisdictions to participate in corridor topology without direct anchor infrastructure.

Adjacency is structural participation.

It is not classification equivalence.

Adjacency must remain bounded by interpretation boundaries.

Adjacency must never replace the topology trio.

It exists to explain indirect corridor participation without inflating topology authority.

## 2. Adjacency Definition

Adjacency exists when corridor continuity passes through neighboring jurisdictions that host anchor infrastructure or other corridor-stabilizing infrastructure.

Adjacent participation may occur through:
- shared grid infrastructure
- shared research infrastructure
- transport-linked continuity
- federal corridor overlays
- pipeline continuity
- interconnection dependencies

Adjacency must be evidence-grounded.

Signals alone cannot establish adjacency classification.

Signals may help explain adjacency behavior, but the existence of adjacency participation must remain tied to canonical evidence or governed normalization logic.

## 3. Anchor vs Adjacency Distinction

Anchor participation means direct infrastructure presence that stabilizes or originates corridor structure within the jurisdiction itself.

Adjacency participation means continuity participation through neighboring anchor jurisdictions or neighboring corridor-stabilizing structures.

Adjacency must not imply:
- origin authority
- deployment authority
- corridor control authority
- topology equivalence with anchor placement

Adjacency is a bounded continuity relation.

It may support valid participation in Atlas topology, but it does not grant anchor-grade authority.

## 4. Adjacency Eligibility Conditions

Adjacency classification requires at least one of the following:
- documented infrastructure continuity
- documented grid interdependence
- documented transport continuity
- documented research attachment
- documented statutory corridor designation
- documented federal overlay participation

Adjacency must appear in:
- `evidence.md`

or

- `change-log.md` normalization logic grounded in evidence

Adjacency may be reinforced by `signals.md`, but it may not be created there independently.

## 5. Adjacency Completion-Layer Semantics

Adjacency participation may support completion-layer roles such as:
- connector
- bridge
- support-node
- expansion-edge

Adjacency must not independently justify:
- anchor classification
- terminus classification

Adjacency informs topology role.

It does not elevate topology authority beyond the limits of the interpreted participation pathway.

## 6. Multi-Jurisdiction Corridor Continuity

Corridors may traverse multiple jurisdictions without anchor infrastructure in each jurisdiction.

Adjacent jurisdictions may:
- preserve corridor continuity
- enable corridor traversal
- host supporting infrastructure dependencies

These jurisdictions remain valid topology participants through adjacency when the participation pathway is evidence-grounded and interpretation-boundary constrained.

Atlas may therefore recognize corridor continuity across multi-state chains without asserting anchor infrastructure in every participating state.

## 7. Federal Overlay Adjacency

Federal infrastructure may create adjacency-based participation across jurisdictions.

Examples may include:
- federal grid corridors
- national research facilities
- defense infrastructure corridors
- interstate transmission designations

Federal overlays must remain evidence-documented.

Federal overlays do not automatically imply anchor status.

A federal overlay may justify adjacency, dependence, or continuity participation.

It may not, by itself, collapse the distinction between adjacency and anchor placement.

## 8. Cross-Border Adjacency

International adjacency may support corridor participation.

Examples may include:
- cross-border transmission
- binational research corridors
- transport continuity infrastructure
- pipeline corridors

Cross-border adjacency must remain constrained by interpretation boundary language.

Adjacency does not imply cross-border topology authority equivalence.

Cross-border participation may be structurally relevant without granting direct authority over the neighboring jurisdiction’s topology role.

## 9. Adjacency Interpretation Boundaries

Adjacency classification must specify limits such as:
- deployment absence
- policy absence
- anchor absence
- infrastructure dependence
- federal reliance

These limits must appear in interpretation boundary language.

Renderer surfaces must preserve adjacency limits.

Adjacency without visible limits is incomplete and risks overstatement.

Interpretation boundary language is therefore required whenever adjacency materially affects topology reading.

## 10. Renderer Adjacency Responsibilities

The renderer must:
- surface adjacency explicitly
- preserve interpretation boundary language
- avoid promoting adjacency to anchor equivalence
- avoid synthesizing anchor roles from adjacency participation
- avoid merging adjacency into primary topology placement implicitly

The renderer may display adjacency as supporting topology continuity.

The renderer may use helper-layer visibility lifts where governed by renderer conventions.

The renderer must not convert adjacency into stronger topology authority than the canonical package supports.

## 11. Lifecycle Interaction with Adjacency

Adjacency authority varies by lifecycle stage as follows:
- Draft -> provisional adjacency
- Evidence-Attached -> evidence-grounded adjacency
- Corridor-Attached -> topology-visible adjacency
- Complete -> stable adjacency interpretation
- Canonical -> adjacency baseline usable for corridor continuity reasoning
- Superseded -> historical adjacency only
- Deprecated -> inactive adjacency

Lifecycle affects adjacency authority.

It does not redefine adjacency structure.

A package may preserve the same adjacency relationship while its lifecycle status changes.

What changes is the strength with which Atlas may rely on that adjacency in active interpretation.

## 12. Secondary Placement Through Adjacency

Adjacency may support:
- secondary corridor placement interpretation
- multi-foundation participation documentation
- cross-corridor linkage explanation

Adjacency must not override:
- the primary topology trio
- classification source authority
- interpretation boundary constraints

When secondary placement is documented through adjacency, it remains secondary.

It may inform interpretation.

It may not replace primary canonical topology attachment unless a future schema explicitly authorizes multi-primary classification.

## 13. Adjacency Visibility Requirements

Adjacency participation must be visible through one or more of the following:
- `evidence.md`
- `signals.md` absence-oriented or non-establishment framing where applicable
- `change-log.md` normalization explanation
- renderer helper-layer visibility lifts where permitted

Adjacency must not remain implicit when it materially affects topology reading.

If Atlas relies on adjacency to explain participation, that reliance must be visibly documented.

## 14. Adjacency Compliance Definition

A jurisdiction package is adjacency-compliant when all of the following are true:
- adjacency participation is evidence-grounded
- interpretation boundary is documented
- completion-layer semantics are consistent with the adjacency role
- no anchor equivalence is implied
- renderer surfaces preserve adjacency limits

Non-compliance occurs when any of the following are true:
- adjacency is inferred without documentation
- adjacency is treated as anchor placement
- adjacency overrides the topology trio
- adjacency is used as a corridor classification replacement
