# ATLAS Foundation Types v1

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the infrastructure foundation taxonomy used by Atlas.

Foundations classify infrastructure participation domains.

Foundations support corridor interpretation clarity.

Foundations preserve multi-domain topology visibility.

Foundations do not assign corridor roles independently.

Foundations do not replace topology trio classification.

Foundations describe infrastructure domains that support corridor participation.

They do not independently create topology authority.

## 2. Foundation Definition

A foundation is an infrastructure domain supporting corridor continuity and jurisdiction participation.

Foundations describe infrastructure type, not topology strength.

Foundation classification supports interpretation clarity across corridor systems.

Foundation presence does not independently establish anchor placement.

Foundations answer the question:

What infrastructure domain is materially supporting this jurisdiction’s corridor participation?

They do not answer:
- what role the jurisdiction occupies in the corridor
- whether the jurisdiction is an anchor
- whether the jurisdiction is mature or canonical

## 3. Canonical Foundation Types

Atlas recognizes the following foundation types:
- energy
- compute
- research
- transport
- federal-overlay
- water, where materially relevant

Each foundation type must remain explicitly identifiable where present.

Foundation types must not be merged implicitly.

A jurisdiction may participate in more than one foundation domain, but each participating foundation must remain distinguishable in canonical interpretation.

## 4. Energy Foundation Definition

Energy foundation participation may include:
- transmission infrastructure
- generation infrastructure continuity
- grid reinforcement corridors
- interstate transmission designation
- pipeline continuity where energy-relevant

Energy foundation presence supports corridor stabilization reasoning.

Energy participation does not independently assign anchor role.

Energy foundation describes domain support.

It does not independently determine completion-layer semantics or corridor authority.

## 5. Compute Foundation Definition

Compute foundation participation may include:
- data-center siting infrastructure
- hyperscale deployment corridors
- interconnection capacity alignment
- digital backbone continuity
- regional compute cluster structures

Compute foundation presence supports digital corridor continuity interpretation.

Compute presence alone does not imply energy corridor participation.

Compute foundation remains a distinct domain even when it materially depends on energy or transmission conditions.

## 6. Research Foundation Definition

Research foundation participation may include:
- national laboratories
- research university infrastructure
- federal research facilities
- innovation cluster continuity
- research throughput alignment

Research participation supports knowledge corridor continuity.

Research presence does not independently assign anchor placement.

Research foundation identifies the domain through which corridor participation is supported.

It does not, by itself, create stronger topology status.

## 7. Transport Foundation Definition

Transport foundation participation may include:
- freight rail corridors
- interstate logistics corridors
- port throughput infrastructure
- aviation infrastructure continuity
- multimodal freight alignment

Transport foundations support physical infrastructure continuity reasoning.

Transport presence does not independently establish corridor origin authority.

Transport foundation is a domain descriptor, not a role assignment.

## 8. Federal Overlay Foundation Definition

Federal overlay participation may include:
- interstate transmission designation
- national laboratory networks
- defense infrastructure continuity
- federal compute infrastructure
- federal logistics systems

Federal overlays strengthen corridor continuity interpretation.

Federal overlays must remain visible as reliance structures.

They do not independently assign anchor placement authority.

Federal overlay foundation describes dependence on nationally structured infrastructure domains.

It must remain visible as overlay reliance rather than local autonomous topology strength.

## 9. Water Foundation Definition (Optional)

Water foundation participation may include:
- cooling-water availability structures
- major basin continuity
- industrial water throughput infrastructure
- reservoir-dependent deployment alignment

Water foundation participation must remain evidence-grounded.

Water participation supports deployment interpretation.

It does not independently establish corridor placement authority.

Water foundation is optional in schema v1 and should be used only when materially relevant to the jurisdiction’s corridor participation or deployment posture.

## 10. Cross-Foundation Participation Rules

Jurisdictions may participate in multiple foundations simultaneously.

Cross-foundation participation must:
- remain explicitly documented
- preserve topology trio interpretation
- preserve corridor-role clarity
- avoid foundation blending

Each foundation must remain independently visible.

Cross-foundation participation may enrich interpretation.

It may not dissolve domain boundaries or create a synthetic mixed-foundation role without explicit canonical support.

## 11. Foundation Stacking Semantics

Foundation stacking occurs when multiple infrastructure domains support corridor participation simultaneously.

Examples may include:
- energy + compute
- compute + research
- energy + transport
- research + federal overlay

Stacked foundations strengthen interpretation continuity.

Stacking must not imply anchor equivalence automatically.

Stacking indicates multiple relevant domain supports.

It does not independently upgrade topology authority or lifecycle maturity.

## 12. Foundation Conflict Resolution Rules

If foundation signals appear to suggest conflicting corridor interpretations:
- the topology trio governs placement authority
- completion-layer semantics govern participation strength
- foundation types clarify infrastructure domain only

Foundation classification must not override canonical topology interpretation.

If multiple foundation domains are relevant, the package must preserve visibility rather than force a false singular interpretation that distorts topology authority.

## 13. Foundation Visibility Requirements

Foundation participation must appear in:
- `evidence.md`
- `signals.md` where pipeline-stage participation is relevant
- `trust-dimensions.md` where interpretively relevant
- `profile.md` synthesis summaries where applicable
- renderer helper-layer visibility lifts where permitted

Foundation participation must not remain implicit when materially relevant.

If a foundation domain materially supports topology, continuity, or builder posture, it should be visible somewhere in canonical package interpretation.

## 14. Foundation Interaction with Corridor Roles

Foundations describe infrastructure domains supporting corridor roles.

Foundations must not:
- assign anchor status
- assign transit status
- assign terminus status
- assign support-node status
- assign expansion-edge status independently

Corridor roles remain governed by topology schema and corridor ontology.

Foundation domains explain what kind of infrastructure matters.

They do not decide the jurisdiction’s corridor role.

## 15. Foundation Interaction with Lifecycle Status

Foundation participation authority varies by lifecycle stage:
- Draft -> exploratory foundation participation
- Evidence-Attached -> bounded foundation interpretation
- Corridor-Attached -> topology-visible foundation participation
- Complete -> stable foundation placement
- Canonical -> baseline foundation continuity reference
- Superseded -> historical foundation participation
- Deprecated -> inactive foundation participation

Lifecycle stage affects interpretation authority, not foundation definition.

A foundation domain remains the same kind of infrastructure domain regardless of lifecycle maturity.

What changes is how strongly Atlas may rely on the package’s interpretation of that foundation participation.

## 16. Foundation Compliance Definition

A jurisdiction package is foundation-compliant when all of the following are true:
- foundation participation remains evidence-grounded
- cross-foundation participation remains visible
- foundation stacking remains explicit
- foundation roles do not override topology trio
- foundation reliance remains interpretation-boundary constrained

Non-compliance occurs when any of the following are true:
- foundation participation implies anchor authority
- foundation stacking obscures dominant placement
- foundation blending removes domain clarity
- foundation signals replace evidence
- foundation roles override topology schema
