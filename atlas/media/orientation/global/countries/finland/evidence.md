# Finland — Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- The Digital and Population Data Services Agency states that it maintains the Population Information System, develops solutions for electronic identification, and maintains centralised support services for e-services, placing Finland inside a nationally coordinated digital-governance stack.
- Bank of Finland materials state that the Bank of Finland is Finland’s monetary authority and part of the Eurosystem, and that its tasks include maintaining stable and efficient payment systems, clearing interbank payments, and issuing euro cash in Finland.
- CSC materials state that Finland hosts the LUMI EuroHPC supercomputer in Kajaani and operates the Funet research and education network with connectivity to Nordic and European research-network structures.
- Fingrid states that Finland is part of the Nordic synchronous area and documents direct grid connections with Sweden, Norway and Estonia through AC and HVDC interconnection infrastructure.
- Traficom, FICIX and Nokia materials document Finnish communications governance, internet exchange, .fi domain administration, spectrum coordination, and telecommunications standards participation without assigning telecom routing authority.
- European Commission, CBSS and Nordic-cooperation materials place Finland inside euro-area, Baltic Sea, Nordic and TEN-T institutional structures without assigning corridor authority or topology role.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- The Digital and Population Data Services Agency states that it maintains the Population Information System, develops solutions for electronic identification, and maintains centralised support services for e-services including the Suomi.fi Web Service, Suomi.fi Messages and Suomi.fi e-Authorization.
- Suomi.fi states that its web service compiles information, instructions and services in one address and that, after identification, users can communicate with organisations, grant and request mandates, and check registered data.
- Suomi.fi states that Suomi.fi e-Identification is a strong identification service for logging into Finnish public-administration e-services and that it is used where the identity of the user must be verified.
- Suomi.fi materials state that the Finnish Authenticator service allows a foreign user to register a foreigner’s identifier and use the application for login to Finnish e-services.
- Suomi.fi materials state that eIDAS identification tokens from other European countries can be used in Finnish e-services once they have been approved by the European Union for cross-border identification.
- Suomi.fi for Service Developers states that the Suomi.fi Data Exchange Layer is a uniform and secure solution for managing data transfer related to electronic transactions between organisations and that it supports information sharing in public services.

## Financial Infrastructure and CBDC / Digital Euro Participation

- Bank of Finland materials state that the Bank of Finland is Finland’s monetary authority and part of the Eurosystem and that its tasks include maintaining stable and efficient payment systems, clearing interbank payments, and issuing banknotes and coins into circulation.
- Bank of Finland states that helping maintain the reliability and efficiency of the payment system and the overall financial system is one of its core tasks and describes payment as a critical activity for the functioning of society in Finland.
- Bank of Finland states that it provides the Eurosystem’s common TARGET services to Nordic financial sector entities and that these services include TARGET2, TIPS instant payment settlement and T2S securities settlement.
- Bank of Finland states that the reliable functioning of TARGET services supports the smooth flow of cash, securities and collateral across Europe, and that the Bank participated in continued TARGET development and contingency preparation.
- Bank of Finland states that one of its strategic priorities is to further the resilience of and alternatives for European retail payments, including preparatory work for a digital euro and the introduction in Finland of an instant payment service based on European standards.
- Bank of Finland states that the digital euro remains at the planning stage, that no issuance decision has yet been taken, and that the Eurosystem moved to a preparation phase in October 2023.

## Research Network and Compute Infrastructure

- CSC states that Funet is designed, built and optimised for the needs of science and research and that it enables cost-effective data connections for research and education, including access to supercomputer resources and large-volume data transfer.
- CSC states that Funet connects Finnish researchers and learners to national and international research networks and the internet via NORDUnet and GÉANT.
- CSC states that LUMI is a European supercomputer located in CSC’s data center in Kajaani, owned by the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking and hosted by a consortium of 11 European countries including Finland.
- CSC states that LUMI serves Finnish and European researchers as well as companies and reserves part of the available capacity for consortium countries and part for EuroHPC users across Europe.
- CSC states that its Kajaani data center hosts national supercomputers and the European LUMI system, and that the site supports world-class data management and computing environments for science.
- VTT states that a quantum-computing connection was built by VTT, CSC and Aalto University within the Finnish Quantum-Computing Infrastructure and that pilot access was opened for Finnish universities and research organisations, linking the HELMI quantum computer and LUMI.

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- FICIX states that it is Finland’s biggest internet exchange point, organised as a membership-based, not-for-profit and neutral association.
- FICIX states that it operates five exchange sites in Espoo, Helsinki and Oulu, that the sites include root-server infrastructure, and that the switching platform supports 10G, 100G and 400G port speeds.
- FICIX states that it is part of national critical infrastructure and frames its operations around resilience and usability.
- Traficom materials surfaced via official site search results state that .fi is the country-code top-level domain for Finland and that it is administered by Traficom under national legislation.
- CSC states that its Kajaani data-center environment combines renewable electricity, year-round air and water cooling, waste-heat recovery into district heating, and high-capacity Funet connectivity.
- CSC states that a broader data-center ecosystem has emerged in the Kajaani region around the research environment, industrial-grid access, available data-center space and connectivity.

## Telecommunications and Standards Infrastructure

- Nokia states that its Oulu site combines Bell Labs radio research, Mobile Networks R&D and factory operations under one roof and describes the site as one of the leading radio-research hotspots in the world.
- Nokia states that the Oulu Bell Labs and Nokia Standards teams hold key expertise in 5G standardization, air interface, massive MIMO, radio protocols, AI/ML and end-to-end systems, and that the site collaborates with the University of Oulu including the national 6G flagship programme.
- Nokia states that it leads Hexa-X-II, the second phase of the European 6G flagship initiative funded through the European Commission’s Smart Networks and Services Joint Undertaking.
- Traficom states that radio frequencies are a finite resource of major social and economic significance and that international agreement is required because frequencies do not stop at national borders.
- Traficom states that it promotes efficient and flexible use of radio frequencies through frequency planning and radio licensing to support new services and innovations.
- Traficom materials surfaced via official site search results state that Finland is closely involved in European and international 6G spectrum work.

## Energy and Grid Integration Infrastructure

- Fingrid states that it is Finland’s transmission system operator and that Finland is part of the Nordic synchronous area together with Sweden, Norway and eastern Denmark.
- Fingrid states that Finland’s main grid is connected to Sweden by two 400 kV AC connections in northern Finland and to Norway by a 220 kV AC connection from Inari.
- Fingrid states that Finland also operates HVDC interconnections including Fenno-Skan 1 and Fenno-Skan 2 to Sweden and EstLink 1 and EstLink 2 to Estonia.
- Fingrid states that it is jointly building the Aurora Line with Svenska kraftnät as a new 400 kV transmission line between Finland and Sweden and that the project increases cross-border capacity and improves security of supply.
- Fingrid states that the Aurora Line supports the integration of renewable energy sources in the Nordics and Baltic Sea area and has Project of Common Interest status under EU energy-market integration objectives.
- Fingrid materials place Finland’s grid operations inside Nordic system-operation arrangements and broader Baltic Sea regional interconnection rather than a purely standalone national grid.

## Cybersecurity and Civil Preparedness Coordination

- NCSC-FI materials surfaced via official site search results state that the National Cyber Security Centre Finland develops and monitors the operational reliability and security of communications networks and services and provides situational awareness of cyber security.
- NCSC-FI materials surfaced via official site search results state that it provides central-government actors and critical-infrastructure providers with guidance on information-security matters.
- NCSC-FI’s service materials list situation awareness and network management, assessment and guidance, monitoring and incident response, CERT activity, regulation and supervision, and national coordination functions.
- NCSC-FI states that its National Coordination Centre creates preconditions for Finnish cyber-security operators to participate in international research and development, brings together public, private and third-sector actors, and helps stakeholders participate in EU-funded cross-border projects.
- National Emergency Supply Agency materials surfaced via official site search results state that Finland’s security-of-supply system is intended to safeguard energy, transport, warehousing and distribution systems, technical systems, health services and other basic economic functions in serious disruptions and emergencies.
- National Emergency Supply Agency materials surfaced via official site search results state that NESA and the National Emergency Supply Organisation integrate the objectives and interests of society and the business community in preparedness work.

## Advanced Technology and Industrial Infrastructure

- VTT states that Finland opened a quantum computer for research use through cooperation among VTT, CSC and Aalto University within the Finnish Quantum-Computing Infrastructure, with access initially directed to Finnish universities and research organisations.
- VTT states that the HELMI-LUMI connection is intended to build practical know-how in quantum computing and to prepare for future large-scale quantum-computing infrastructure in Finland.
- CSC states that LUMI supports data-intensive scientific and industrial uses including deep-learning and other artificial-intelligence workloads.
- CSC states that Kajaani’s data-center environment combines supercomputing, data management, renewable power, free cooling, waste-heat use and industrial-grid infrastructure in a single compute-hosting environment.
- Nokia states that Finland’s Oulu site combines radio research, development and manufacturing, tying advanced telecommunications standards work to domestic industrial and R&D infrastructure.

## EU, Nordic, and Baltic Corridor Integration

- European Commission materials state that Finland has been a euro-area member since 1 January 1999, placing Finnish monetary infrastructure inside the euro-area institutional framework.
- Suomi.fi materials state that approved identification tokens from other European countries can be used in Finnish e-services, documenting digital-identity interoperability through the eIDAS framework.
- CSC states that Funet connects to NORDUnet and GÉANT, and that LUMI is operated through a multinational EuroHPC consortium, linking Finnish research infrastructure directly to Nordic and European cooperation structures.
- CBSS states that Finland is one of the member states of the Council of the Baltic Sea States.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- Bank of Finland and European Commission materials place Finnish settlement and currency infrastructure inside the euro-area and Eurosystem framework rather than a standalone national monetary architecture.
- Suomi.fi and eIDAS materials place parts of Finland’s digital-identity interoperability inside EU mutual-recognition and cross-border token frameworks rather than a fully self-contained domestic identity environment.
- Fingrid materials place Finland’s electricity-transmission system within the Nordic synchronous area and connect it materially to Swedish, Norwegian and Estonian interconnectors, indicating dependence on Nordic-Baltic market coupling and cross-border balancing structures.
- The current source set documents CSC, Funet, LUMI, Kajaani data-center capacity and FICIX exchange infrastructure, but it does not document an independent Finnish sovereign hyperscale compute stack.
- The current source set documents strong telecommunications standards participation, quantum-computing collaboration and EuroHPC compute infrastructure, but it does not document a complete Finnish semiconductor fabrication stack.
- The current source set documents key infrastructure across northern AC links and Baltic submarine/HVDC links, indicating that part of Finland’s infrastructure coordination occurs through long cross-border northern and maritime connection surfaces without supporting any readiness or ranking judgment.

## Summary

The current source set documents Finland as a Nordic digital-governance, research-network, EuroHPC compute, telecommunications-standards, cybersecurity, and Nordic-Baltic grid-integration jurisdiction embedded within EU, Nordic and Baltic institutional systems. The evidence places DVV and Suomi.fi, the Bank of Finland, CSC and Funet, LUMI, Traficom and NCSC-FI, FICIX, Fingrid, Nokia and related cross-border coordination frameworks inside a nationally administered but highly interconnected infrastructure environment, without supporting readiness tiers, routing authority, or sovereign-stack claims beyond the cited sources.
