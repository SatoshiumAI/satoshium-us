Bitcoin / Digital Asset Policy

New Hampshire enacted HB 302 in May 2025, becoming the first US state to authorize a strategic Bitcoin reserve. The legislation permits the state treasurer to allocate up to 5% of qualifying public funds into Bitcoin and precious metals. The corridor's digital asset posture is structured around this forward-leaning institutional anchor, layered beneath the NH Banking Department's money transmitter licensing framework, which governs qualifying digital asset businesses operating within the corridor.

Privacy / Data Handling

New Hampshire's consumer data privacy surface is governed by the New Hampshire Privacy Act (HB 1121), which took effect January 1, 2025. The Act is enforced exclusively by the state Attorney General without a private right of action. Threshold conditions — 35,000 or more NH residents' data, or 10,000 or more where more than 25% of gross revenue derives from data sales — determine operator applicability. The enforcement posture is active but moderate relative to the CPPA-administered California surface.

Biometrics / Identity

New Hampshire does not have a standalone biometric privacy statute as of April 2026. Biometric identifiers are classified as sensitive data under the NH Privacy Act, activating heightened handling and data protection assessment requirements for qualifying controllers. The corridor operates without facial recognition bans at the state or municipal level, and the structural posture does not reflect the surveillance-skeptical orientation found in California's biometric environment. Identity-adjacent deployments face NHPA sensitive data provisions as the operative compliance surface.

Energy / Mining / Compute Posture

Bitcoin mining operates within New Hampshire's legal framework without prohibition or specific regulatory targeting as of April 2026. The corridor participates in ISO-NE, the regional transmission organization governing New England electricity markets, which conditions electricity costs at moderate-to-elevated levels relative to the continental US average. No proof-of-work specific regulation has been proposed in the New Hampshire legislature. Portsmouth and Pease corridor industrial surfaces provide logistics interface for compute-adjacent deployment without the environmental regulatory posture that conditions California's unfavorable structural profile.
