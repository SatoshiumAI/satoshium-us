Bitcoin / Digital Asset Policy

Oregon does not maintain a state-specific digital asset licensing framework as of 2026. No statewide prohibition on digital asset payment or Bitcoin usage has been enacted, and the posture remains permissive relative to state-level digital asset frameworks in adjacent jurisdictions.

Privacy / Data Handling

Oregon Consumer Privacy Act (OCPA, SB 619) took effect July 1, 2024, establishing consumer data rights, sensitive data protections, and opt-out mechanisms for targeted advertising and data sales across qualifying data controllers. The Oregon Attorney General holds exclusive enforcement authority through existing AG infrastructure rather than a dedicated privacy agency. The enforcement posture is developing relative to California's CPPA framework, but the operative compliance surface is active and maturing.

Enforcement profile: Attorney General enforcement with civil penalty exposure. OCPA applies to controllers processing data of 100,000 or more Oregon consumers, or 25,000 or more consumers where more than 25% of gross revenue derives from data sales. Builder implication: Products and services collecting Oregon consumer data at qualifying thresholds interface with OCPA obligations from July 2024 forward. Biometric and sensitive data categories carry elevated handling, consent, and purpose limitation requirements consistent with the broader state privacy regime trajectory.

Biometrics / Identity

Oregon's biometric governance surface operates through OCPA's sensitive data classification framework. Biometric data qualifies as sensitive personal data under OCPA, triggering consent-based processing requirements and consumer opt-out rights. Oregon has not enacted a standalone biometric-specific law as of 2026, but the OCPA framework conditions the deployment environment for identity-sensitive systems, and the legislative trajectory may introduce additional biometric-specific surfaces in the 2026–2027 session cycle.
