AI Policy

Maryland's AI governance posture is evolving through targeted legislative instruments rather than a comprehensive state enforcement framework. HB 1333 established employer disclosure requirements for automated employment decision tools in 2024. The Joint Committee on Artificial Intelligence in Maryland (JCAI) coordinates legislative oversight. Federal proximity to NIST standardization surfaces in Gaithersburg and defense-intelligence coordination environments at Ft. Bitcoin / Digital Asset Policy

Maryland operates under the Money Transmitter Act for digital asset businesses, administered through the Office of Financial Regulation (OFR). The proximity of this corridor to federal regulatory surfaces — including FinCEN, SEC, and CFTC coordination interfaces in the Washington-adjacent zone — conditions the compliance environment for operators deploying within Maryland's financial services layers.

Privacy / Data Handling

Maryland's Maryland Online Data Privacy Act (MODPA), enacted as HB 567 in 2024 and effective October 1, 2025, establishes a comprehensive consumer data privacy regime enforced through the Office of the Attorney General. MODPA covers controllers and processors engaging with Maryland consumer data and includes heightened protections for sensitive data categories. Enforcement is now active. The regime conditions data-handling architecture for any operator collecting qualifying Maryland resident data at scale.

Enforcement profile: AG-led enforcement with a 60-day cure period through January 1, 2027; programmatic compliance expectations around consumer rights workflows, data protection assessments, and sensitive data processing controls.
Builder implication: operators collecting Maryland consumer data are subject to MODPA regardless of base of operations. The sensitive data classification under MODPA covers biometric, precise geolocation, health, and financial data — each carrying distinct processing restrictions.
