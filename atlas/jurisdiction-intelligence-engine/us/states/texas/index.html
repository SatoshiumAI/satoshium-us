AI Policy
Legislation · Executive Action · Advisory Posture
Permissive · Light Touch → ▼

Texas has taken a deliberately hands-off approach to AI regulation, positioning itself as the anti-California on tech governance. Governor Abbott's 2023 Executive Order GA-44 established a state AI advisory council to explore AI use in government while explicitly avoiding premature restriction. The Texas AI Advisory Council, housed under the Department of Information Resources (DIR), produced its first report in 2024 recommending voluntary frameworks rather than mandates. The 2025 legislative session produced no binding AI-specific statutes — a signal that Texas is intentionally preserving a low-friction build environment. AI procurement in state agencies remains governed by existing risk management frameworks, not AI-specific rules.

Bitcoin / Digital Asset Policy
Strategic Reserve · HB 1576 · Mining Rights · Taxation
Pro-Bitcoin · National Leader ↓↓ ▼

Texas has emerged as the most Bitcoin-friendly state legislature in the US. Senate Bill 21, signed into law in June 2025, established a Texas Strategic Bitcoin Reserve — authorizing the state comptroller to accept Bitcoin donations and hold them in a dedicated reserve fund for a minimum of five years. This makes Texas one of the first US states to formally hold Bitcoin as a state asset. The Texas Virtual Currency Bill (HB 1576, 2023) established a legal framework for virtual currency operations, defining custody, exchange, and payment activities. Texas has no state capital gains tax, creating a structurally favorable environment for Bitcoin holders, traders, and institutional operators. The Texas Blockchain Council remains one of the most organized and effective industry advocacy bodies in any US state.

Privacy / Data Handling
TDPSA · AG Enforcement · 30-Day Cure · No Private Action
Moderate · Emerging → ▼

The Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA) took effect July 1, 2024, placing Texas among the growing cohort of states with comprehensive privacy law. Enforced by the Texas Attorney General — not a dedicated privacy agency — the TDPSA covers controllers and processors that conduct business in Texas or produce products/services consumed by Texas residents. Key provisions include consumer rights to access, correct, delete, and opt out of data sales and targeted advertising. Critically, there is no private right of action; enforcement rests solely with the AG. Businesses have a 30-day cure period before the AG may bring action, making the TDPSA substantially less aggressive than California's CCPA/CPRA regime. Penalty ceiling is $7,500 per violation — significantly lower compliance pressure than the California model.

Biometrics / Identity
CUBI · Private Right of Action · $25K Per Violation
CUBI · Notable Risk ↑ ▼

Texas's biometric law — the Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act (CUBI) — stands as one of the most consequential state biometric statutes alongside Illinois's BIPA. Enacted in 2009 and enforced by the AG (not through private right of action in all circumstances), CUBI prohibits commercial entities from capturing biometric identifiers — including fingerprints, retina scans, hand geometry, and facial geometry — without informed consent. Penalties reach $25,000 per intentional or knowing violation and $1,000 per negligent violation. Unlike the TDPSA, CUBI lacks a cure period. In 2022, Facebook (Meta) settled a Texas CUBI action for $650 million. CUBI is increasingly the primary biometric compliance concern for Texas-operating tech companies.

Education / Public Sector AI
TEA Guidance · UT System · State Procurement · Workforce AI
Open · Cautious Adoption → ▼

Texas's public sector AI posture is pragmatic and increasingly active. The Texas Education Agency (TEA) issued AI guidance for K-12 schools in 2024, providing a flexible framework rather than restrictive mandates — focusing on academic integrity, responsible use, and staff training rather than prohibition. The University of Texas system has developed AI research initiatives, including UT Austin's Machine Learning Lab and newly formed AI policy centers. State agency procurement rules do not include AI-specific attestation requirements (unlike California's EO N-5-26), giving vendors significant operational latitude. Governor Abbott's DIR-based advisory approach continues to position AI as an economic opportunity rather than a governance challenge. Texas A&M's AI research programs and UT Austin's proximity to the Austin tech corridor strengthen the state's applied AI pipeline.

Open Source / Developer Climate
Austin Tech Hub · No Income Tax · Low Overhead · Corporate HQ Density
Best-in-Class · Favorable ↓ ▼

Austin has established itself as the second most important US technology hub after Silicon Valley — and by many measures, the most founder-friendly. Tesla, Oracle, Apple (second campus), Dell, SpaceX, and dozens of mid-market tech companies have relocated or expanded Texas operations since 2020, drawing a dense developer talent pool. Critically, Texas imposes no state income tax — a structural advantage that compounds for high-earning engineers and founders. The state's regulatory philosophy minimizes compliance overhead for software companies, with no AI-specific disclosure mandates, no ADMT risk assessments, and no dataset reporting requirements. The Texas Open Source community is vibrant but informally organized compared to California's government-sponsored code.ca.gov initiative. GitHub, Stack Overflow, and major conference activity increasingly reflects Austin's growing developer gravity.

Energy / Mining / Compute Posture
ERCOT Grid · #1 Mining State · Cheap Power · Demand Response
Mining Favorable · Best-in-Class ↓↓ ▼

Texas is the undisputed US leader in Bitcoin mining, hosting an estimated 25–30% of US hash rate. The ERCOT deregulated electricity market creates a uniquely favorable environment: abundant renewable energy capacity (wind and solar), competitive wholesale pricing, and demand response programs that allow miners to curtail operations during grid stress events — earning incentive payments while reducing grid strain. Industrial electricity rates in West Texas can fall below $0.02/kWh during off-peak periods, among the lowest in the world. Major mining operations including Riot Platforms, Core Scientific, and Marathon Digital have established large-scale Texas facilities. The state has no mining-specific restrictions and has actively courted miners through favorable permitting and grid access. AI data center expansion is also accelerating: Oracle, Microsoft, and Google have announced multi-billion dollar Texas compute investments targeting the state's energy advantages.
