## Jurisdiction Summary

The canonical package records Montana as a Northern Plains Sparse-Node jurisdiction and canonical member of the three-state Northern Plains Sparse-Node Corridor cluster with North Dakota and South Dakota, under the jurisdiction lens "Northern plains sparse-node continuity within northern interior completion." The evidence layer is retained from corridor continuity between adjacent Atlas states — North Dakota, South Dakota, Wyoming, and Idaho — with all citations at metadata depth, rather than from independent state-sourced research. The signals layer reflects four structural coordination signals: a northern interior continuity signal through retained placement on the Northern Interior Completion Layer with visible adjacency to North Dakota, South Dakota, and Idaho; a northern plains-to-mountain transition signal through retained placement between Dakota-facing sparse-node structure and Idaho-facing mountain-transition structure; an institutional adjacency signal across the Dakota, Wyoming, and Idaho-facing corridor frame; and an east-west interior continuity signal through retained placement between Dakota-facing and Idaho-facing structure, with no independent establishment of north-south interior continuity toward a Canada-facing frame, freight or logistics continuity, rail visibility, energy-grid participation, BEAD coordination, or inland-waterway continuity.
