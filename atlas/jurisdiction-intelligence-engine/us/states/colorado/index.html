AI Policy

Colorado's AI governance surface activated in February 2026 with the enforcement of SB 24-205, the Colorado Artificial Intelligence Act. The framework establishes accountability obligations for developers and deployers of high-risk AI systems, coordinating through the Attorney General's enforcement authority. The Rocky Mountain Coordination Corridor also interfaces with federal AI governance surfaces through research alignment environments and defense-adjacent procurement channels.

Bitcoin / Digital Asset Policy

Colorado's digital asset posture is among the more permissive in the western US, anchored by a Digital Token Act safe harbor framework and a broadly supportive executive posture. The Division of Securities within DORA administers money transmitter and digital asset oversight. Colorado was an early state to enable digital asset payments for qualifying government transactions through third-party processing infrastructure, establishing a constructive interface between digital asset operations and public-sector surfaces.

Privacy / Data Handling

Colorado's privacy enforcement surface operates through the Colorado Privacy Act (CPA), administered under Attorney General enforcement authority. The CPA activated in July 2023 and introduced a universal opt-out mechanism requirement effective July 2024. The framework covers a broad range of data processing activities and conditions obligations on both controllers and processors operating within or targeting Colorado residents. Risk assessment requirements apply to high-risk processing activities, including those intersecting with the corridor's federal-adjacent data surfaces.

Builder implication: any product or service collecting qualifying data from Colorado residents must align with CPA controller and processor obligations. Sensitive data categories — including biometrics, health data, and precise geolocation — require opt-in consent under the CPA framework.
