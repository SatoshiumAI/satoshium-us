# United Kingdom — Signals Layer

## Scope

This file records evidence-derived structural signals for the United Kingdom based solely on `evidence.md`.

It does not assign trust posture, rankings, routing meaning, corridor justification, topology interpretation, or comparative status.

## Cryptoasset Regulatory Perimeter Expansion Signals

_Derived from `evidence.md` section: Digital Asset Regulatory Structure._

- The FCA AML/CTF cryptoasset registration regime establishes a formal registration and supervisory perimeter for in-scope cryptoasset businesses operating in the UK.
- The documented requirement that already-authorised firms must also obtain cryptoasset registration for in-scope cryptoasset activity indicates perimeter attachment alongside existing regulated-status pathways.
- HM Treasury policy materials document a phased expansion path for bringing cryptoasset activities into the financial services regulatory perimeter.
- HM Treasury regulated-activities materials identify planned perimeter treatment for cryptoasset trading-platform activity, admissions and disclosures, and market-abuse provisions.
- HM Treasury materials also document planned perimeter treatment for fiat-referenced stablecoin issuance and related payments treatment.

## Digital Securities Sandbox Deployment Signals

_Derived from `evidence.md` section: Digital Securities and Tokenization Infrastructure._

- The Digital Securities Sandbox has a statutory activation route through the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 and the related Digital Securities Sandbox regulations.
- The DSS operates as a live regulated environment for digital-securities activity including issuance, trading, settlement, notary, maintenance, and, where applicable, trading-venue operation.
- The Bank of England and the FCA jointly operate the DSS and publish joint operational guidance, indicating shared sandbox governance.
- HM Treasury and Bank of England materials document a formal testing pathway for digital securities and developing-technology use in financial market infrastructure.

## Financial Market Infrastructure Modernization Signals

_Derived from `evidence.md` section: Financial Compute Infrastructure Environment._

- The Bank of England supervises recognised payment systems, central securities depositories, and central counterparties within the UK financial market infrastructure perimeter.
- The RTGS Renewal Programme documents a transition path for renewed RTGS and CHAPS modernization.
- The RTGS Renewal Programme includes ISO 20022 migration within the settlement-layer modernization path.
- Bank supervision materials document CCP and CSD infrastructure as part of the national clearing, settlement, and recording architecture.
- The documented UK CCP supervisory perimeter includes LCH Ltd and ICE Clear Europe.

## AI Governance Coordination Signals

_Derived from `evidence.md` section: Artificial Intelligence Governance Structure._

- The UK AI governance model is structured around existing sector regulators rather than a single cross-economy AI regulator.
- Government AI-regulation materials retain cross-sector regulatory principles for implementation through sector regulators.
- DSIT maintains a coordination function that supports regulators with AI risk analysis, capability-building, and governance coherence.
- The AI Safety Institute provides an institutional public-interest role focused on advanced AI safety.
- The Alan Turing Institute provides a national institutional coordination surface for data science and AI.

## Regulatory Coordination Architecture Signals

_Derived from `evidence.md` section: National Digital Strategy Signals (Structural Only)._

- The Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum provides a standing coordination structure linking the CMA, ICO, Ofcom, and FCA.
- The DRCF indicates an established cross-regulator mechanism for digital-regulation work planning and cooperation.

## Open Banking / Smart Data Infrastructure Signals

_Derived from `evidence.md` sections: National Digital Strategy Signals (Structural Only); Research and Advanced Compute Corridors._

- Open Banking Limited is documented as the implementation entity that built the UK Open Banking Standard.
- Open Banking Limited's recorded transition planning toward a future entity under JROC recommendations indicates continuing governance development around open-banking infrastructure.
- The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 enables new Smart Data schemes, including Open Banking-type structures.
- The statutory Smart Data framework indicates an expansion path for programmable financial-data infrastructure beyond the initial open-banking implementation surface.

## Compute Investment Framework Signals

_Derived from `evidence.md` section: Research and Advanced Compute Corridors._

- The UK Compute Roadmap establishes a national compute-investment framework for a public compute ecosystem.
- The roadmap documents planned public compute investment through 2030, indicating an institutional compute-capacity planning structure.
