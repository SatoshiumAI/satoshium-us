# Brazil - Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- Governo Digital states that Brazil’s digital identity layer includes the Conta GOV.BR and the Carteira de Identidade Nacional (CIN), with GOV.BR serving as a secure digital identification account and the CIN using CPF as a single national identification number.
- Governo Digital states that Conecta gov.br promotes automatic and secure interoperability between government systems so citizens do not need to re-present information the government already holds, placing Brazil inside a formal federal data-sharing and service-simplification framework.
- Banco Central do Brasil materials surfaced via official site search results state that the STR is Brazil’s real-time gross settlement system operated by the central bank, while other Banco Central materials state that Pix is a payment arrangement instituted by the central bank and that SPI was created as the instant-payments infrastructure.
- Ministério da Gestão, Serpro, Dataprev, and Ministério da Saúde materials document a public-sector data infrastructure environment that includes the Nuvem de Governo, Conecta gov.br, the Infraestrutura Nacional de Dados, and the RNDS health-data interoperability platform.
- NIC.br materials document a nationally administered internet-governance and registry environment spanning .br domain operations, IP and ASN allocation, CERT.br, and IX.br infrastructure, while IX.br publishes a broad multi-city exchange footprint.
- RNP, LNCC, Telebras, Anatel, ONS, Ministério de Portos e Aeroportos, GRU Airport, and CTIR Gov materials document national-scale research networking, HPC, fiber backbone, 5G rollout, power-system coordination, logistics throughput, and cyber-incident coordination structures.
- MERCOSUL materials place Brazil inside a regional digital and connectivity agenda that includes digital-signature recognition, connectivity projects, roaming coordination, data-policy alignment, and joint digital-security coordination mechanisms.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- Governo Digital states that digital identity in Brazil is built around a set of functionalities, attributes, and services that confirm uniquely and securely who is performing a digital transaction.
- Governo Digital states that the Conta GOV.BR is a digital identification that proves, in digital environments, that the person is who they claim to be and that it can be used to access public digital services securely.
- Governo Digital states that the Carteira de Identidade Nacional uses CPF as a unique number with a national standard, increases identification security, improves administrative records, reduces fraud, and also has a digital version.
- Conecta gov.br states that automatic and secure interoperability between government systems is a citizen right guaranteed by Lei 13.726/2018 and Lei 14.129/2021, and that the program is intended to reduce form-filling, certificate issuance, document uploads, and repeated in-person interactions across agencies.
- Conecta gov.br states that it forms part of the Infraestrutura Nacional de Dados (IND), which is defined as a set of rules, policies, architectures, standards, tools, and information assets intended to promote strategic use of data across the federal executive.
- Official GOV.BR service materials surfaced via official site search results state that electronic signature services allow a user to sign a digital document using their gov.br account.
- The current source set therefore documents a nationally coordinated identity, interoperability, and digital-service environment centered on GOV.BR credentials, CIN, legal interoperability rights, and shared federal data infrastructure.

## Financial Infrastructure and Settlement Systems

- Banco Central do Brasil materials surfaced via official site search results state that the STR is Brazil’s real-time gross settlement system for funds transfers and that it is provided and operated by Banco Central do Brasil.
- Banco Central do Brasil materials surfaced via official site search results state that Pix is a payment arrangement instituted by the central bank that governs the provision of payment services related to instant-payment transactions.
- Banco Central do Brasil materials surfaced via official site search results also identify SPI as the Sistema de Pagamentos Instantâneos and document statistics for movements processed through postings in payment accounts maintained by participants at the central bank.
- Official Banco Central notice materials surfaced via official site search results state that Circular no. 4.027 of 12 June 2020 instituted the SPI and the Conta Pagamentos Instantâneos (Conta PI) and approved its regulation.
- The current source set therefore documents a central-bank-operated settlement environment combining a national RTGS core with a central-bank-instituted instant-payments arrangement and dedicated instant-payments infrastructure.
- The current source set does not support broader claims about international settlement authority beyond the Brazil-centered payment-system perimeter documented by the cited Banco Central materials.

## Data Infrastructure and Digital Continuity

- Conecta gov.br states that the platform promotes automatic and secure exchange of information between systems so that citizens do not have to re-submit information the government already possesses.
- Conecta gov.br states that this interoperability environment has produced large-scale transaction volume and accumulated economic savings, and that it operates as part of the broader Infraestrutura Nacional de Dados.
- Ministério da Gestão states that the Nuvem de Governo is a technological infrastructure for data storage under the direct responsibility of the federal public administration, operated with Serpro and Dataprev, with data remaining in national territory.
- Ministério da Gestão further states that the Nuvem de Governo assures security, sovereignty, legal conformity, integration, and interoperability between public databases, and is also intended to preserve access to state systems in cases of natural disasters or attacks on infrastructure.
- Ministério da Saúde states that the Rede Nacional de Dados em Saúde (RNDS) is the official interoperability platform of the ministry, establishes national infrastructure for secure and standardized sharing of health data, and connects public and private health institutions.
- Ministério da Saúde further states that RNDS enables continuity of care across different states, municipalities, care settings, and public and private systems, while following LGPD-based privacy and security requirements.
- Governo Digital materials state that the Portal Brasileiro de Dados Abertos is the central point for searching and accessing public data in Brazil, and that open-data publication supports transparency, accountability, and reuse.
- The current source set therefore documents a layered public-sector data infrastructure that combines federal interoperability rails, sovereign-cloud-style public administration storage, open-data publication, and sector-specific national data exchange in health.

## Research Network and Compute Infrastructure

- RNP states that it has Pontos de Presença (PoPs) in all 27 states, documenting a nationwide institutional research-network presence.
- RNP also states that its connectivity infrastructure is robust, covers the whole country, and has global integration, and that the network is composed of the Rede Ipê backbone together with PoPs and Pontos de Agregação.
- RNP states that the services environment includes identity, connectivity, collaboration, security, and e-science functions for higher education, research, health, cultural, and innovation institutions.
- RNP states that CAFe provides federated authentication infrastructure for Brazilian teaching and research institutions, while eduroam in Brazil reaches thousands of access points and connected institutions.
- LNCC states that the Santos Dumont supercomputer is located at the LNCC headquarters in Petrópolis and acts as the Tier-0 central node of SINAPAD.
- LNCC states that SDumont has installed processing capacity on the order of 20 petaflop/s, uses a hybrid configuration of CPU, GPU, GraceHopper, and AMD APU nodes, and provides aggregated storage on the order of 4 petabytes.
- The current source set therefore documents a national research-network backbone with federated identity services and a named national high-performance-computing anchor, while not documenting a broader sovereign compute estate across the full economy.

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- NIC.br states that it is the non-profit entity responsible for operation of the .br domain, for IP-number distribution, and for Autonomous System registration in Brazil, implementing CGI.br decisions and projects.
- NIC.br states that Registro.br is the NIC.br department responsible for registering and maintaining domain names under .br and for distributing IPv4, IPv6, and ASN resources in the country.
- NIC.br states that more than 5.4 million .br domains were registered in the first half of 2025.
- NIC.br states that IX.br is one of the constituent parts of its infrastructure program, alongside Registro.br and CERT.br, and frames these activities as support for and improvement of Brazil’s network infrastructure.
- IX.br publishes a broad list of current exchange localities including major metropolitan and regional centers across the country, documenting a distributed multi-city exchange presence rather than a single-node exchange environment.
- The current source set therefore directly documents national registry governance, numbering-resource allocation, and a broad IX footprint more clearly than broader claims about global exchange centrality.

## Telecommunications and Digital Infrastructure

- Anatel states that 5G is the most recent technological standard for mobile services and that the agency is working toward its full implementation in Brazil.
- Anatel states that 5G in Brazil is intended to deliver high data rates, low latency, security, and reliability, and describes the technology as opening a wide range of industrial, automation, and service-development uses.
- Anatel news materials surfaced via official site search results state that 5G implementation in Brazil began in July 2022 after the 2021 spectrum auction.
- Anatel news materials surfaced via official site search results also state that, by July 2024, 4,302 municipalities had been cleared for use of the 3.5 GHz band, covering about 192 million Brazilians and representing 90.20% of the population.
- Anatel materials surfaced via official site search results state that the 5G auction also included commitments for fiber transport networks in the North through the Programa Amazônia Integrada e Sustentável (PAIS) and for construction of a private communications network for the federal public administration.
- Telebras materials surfaced via official site search results state that Telebras operates one of the country’s most modern networks with more than 30,000 kilometers of optical fiber across the national territory, and that its satellite solutions operate in synergy with a high-capacity national backbone.
- The current source set therefore documents a telecom environment built around regulated nationwide 5G rollout, significant public backbone infrastructure, and explicit expansion obligations for northern fiber transport and federal administrative communications.

## Energy and Industrial Infrastructure

- ONS materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Operador Nacional do Sistema Elétrico is responsible for coordinating and controlling the operation of generation and transmission installations in the Sistema Interligado Nacional (SIN) and for planning operation of the country’s isolated systems.
- ONS materials surfaced via official site search results state that the SIN is constituted by four subsystems: South, Southeast/Central-West, Northeast, and most of the North region.
- ONS materials surfaced via official site search results further state that interconnection through the transmission grid enables transfer of energy between subsystems.
- ONS open-data materials surfaced via official site search results publish international interchange data between the SIN and other countries, documenting a live cross-border power-exchange interface.
- Ministério do Desenvolvimento, Indústria, Comércio e Serviços materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Programa Brasil Semicondutores is intended to strengthen the semiconductor research, innovation, and productive ecosystem and is linked to a broader industrial policy that seeks to consolidate robust technological infrastructure in the country.
- The current source set therefore documents nationally coordinated electric-grid operations and a policy-level industrial push in semiconductors, while not documenting a fully sovereign semiconductor fabrication stack through the cited sources.

## Logistics and Global Connectivity Infrastructure

- Ministério de Portos e Aeroportos states that the Port of Santos ended 2025 with a historic record of 186.4 million tonnes of cargo, consolidating itself as the main logistics hub in the country and one of the world’s most relevant port complexes.
- The same ministry states that Santos connects Brazil to major international markets, especially through dry bulk, liquid bulk, and containerized cargo flows.
- Ministério de Portos e Aeroportos states that the planned Tecon Santos 10 concession is expected to raise the port’s installed container capacity by about 50%, documenting a major continuing expansion surface.
- GRU Airport materials surfaced via official site search results state that São Paulo/Guarulhos is the leader in cargo transportation in Brazil, handling 38% of imports and exports per year, and that the airport links domestic and international route networks through dozens of airlines and routes.
- The current source set therefore documents both maritime and air-logistics anchors with strong international throughput and expansion capacity, without extending that evidence into broader routing-role claims beyond the cited institutional materials.

## Cybersecurity and National Coordination

- CTIR Gov states that it is part of the Department of Cyber Security within the information-security structure of the Presidency and that it functions as a Computer Security Incident Response Team.
- CTIR Gov states that it is classified as a national-coordination-responsibility CSIRT and that its objective is to coordinate and integrate actions for management of computer incidents across organs and entities of the federal public administration.
- CTIR Gov states that its remit includes preventing, monitoring, analyzing, and mitigating information-security incidents, promoting scientific and technological exchange, helping establish incident-management guidelines, and creating cyber-threat-intelligence processes to support public policy and decision-making.
- NIC.br states that CERT.br is a national last-resort incident-response group whose mission is to raise security levels and incident-handling capacity across networks connected to the Brazilian Internet.
- NIC.br also states that CERT.br works on incident handling, security awareness, assistance in establishment of new CSIRTs in Brazil, and situational awareness about internet threats.
- The current source set therefore documents a dual cyber-coordination surface combining federal-administration incident governance through CTIR Gov and broader internet-incident response and capability-building through CERT.br.

- MERCOSUL materials surfaced via official site search results state that the agreement on mutual recognition of digital signatures expands the digital frontier for electronic documents and transactions.
- The same MERCOSUL materials surfaced via official site search results identify use cases including exchange of fiscal and customs documents, contract signing between firms in different member countries, product traceability, and automatic recognition of electronic documents generated from certificates issued within official national infrastructures.
- ONS open-data materials surfaced via official site search results document live international interchange data between Brazil’s SIN and other countries.
- Ministério de Portos e Aeroportos materials and GRU Airport materials document maritime and aviation interfaces that connect Brazil to major global cargo and route networks.
- RNP states that its connectivity has global integration, placing the academic-network layer inside wider international collaboration structures.
- The current source set therefore places Brazil within regional digital-governance integration, cross-border energy exchange, international logistics flows, and global research-network connectivity.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- Banco Central do Brasil materials document STR, Pix, and SPI as nationally centered payment and settlement infrastructure, but the current source set does not support claims about broader international monetary or settlement centrality beyond the Brazilian perimeter.
- Ministério da Gestão states that the Nuvem de Governo is operated by public entities and keeps data in national territory, but it also states that the available cloud services include solutions from AWS, Google, Huawei, and Oracle, meaning the cited environment is not documented as a wholly isolated sovereign hyperscale stack.
- RNP and LNCC document a strong national academic-network and HPC surface, but the current source set does not document a comprehensive sovereign compute estate spanning the broader economy.
- NIC.br and IX.br directly document registry and exchange infrastructure, but the current source set does not provide a basis for broader claims of global exchange centrality beyond the distributed national IX footprint.
- Anatel and Telebras document large-scale telecom rollout and backbone infrastructure, but the cited materials describe a mixed regulatory, public, and multi-operator environment rather than a single self-contained telecom stack.
- MDIC semiconductor materials document industrial-policy intent and ecosystem development, but the current source set does not document a sovereign semiconductor fabrication stack in operation.
- These constraints describe the boundaries of the cited evidence only and should not be treated as weakness judgments.

## Summary

- The current source set documents Brazil as a federally coordinated digital-governance jurisdiction built around GOV.BR identity, CIN, Conecta gov.br interoperability, and shared legal and technical data infrastructure.
- It documents Brazil as a central-bank-operated settlement environment with STR, Pix, and SPI under Banco Central do Brasil.
- It documents Brazil as a public-sector data and continuity environment with the Nuvem de Governo, open-data infrastructure, and the RNDS health-data interoperability platform.
- It documents Brazil as a national research-network and compute jurisdiction through RNP’s countrywide backbone and the Santos Dumont Tier-0 supercomputer.
- It documents Brazil as a registry, exchange, telecom, energy, logistics, and cyber-coordination jurisdiction through NIC.br, IX.br, Anatel, Telebras, ONS, Porto de Santos, GRU Airport, CTIR Gov, and CERT.br.
- It also places Brazil inside MERCOSUL digital-integration structures and wider international energy, logistics, and research-network links.
- The cited evidence supports these infrastructure characterizations without assigning readiness tiers, corridor authority, or broader Atlas interpretation beyond the institutional materials.
