# Turkey — Trust Dimensions

## Trust Interpretation Scope

- This file records trust-dimension characteristics for Turkey derived strictly from `evidence.md` and `signals.md`.
- These dimensions describe operational continuity, interoperability, coordination, concentration, resilience, dependency, and persistence characteristics only.
- No scores, rankings, readiness levels, or Atlas surface assignments are made in this file.
- All dimensions are renderer-safe structural interpretations bounded to the documented evidence and signal layers.

## Administrative Continuity Characteristics

- The evidence and signals support a trust dimension of Ankara-centered administrative continuity through the concentration of nationally visible coordination functions in transport, energy, communications regulation, disaster management, and payment infrastructure.
- e-Devlet supports a trust dimension of centralized service entry with distributed execution rather than fully fragmented outward-facing administration.
- e-Devlet’s documented access to information, documents, and application processes supports a trust dimension of shared-service administrative persistence rather than isolated agency-by-agency digital channels.
- BTK’s trust-service regulation supports a trust dimension of administrative continuity reinforced by formal credential and electronic-signature coordination rather than loosely connected access methods.
- The interaction among e-Devlet access, electronic-signature infrastructure, payment rails, and sectoral regulators supports a trust dimension of recurring administrative continuity through connected public-service layers rather than a standalone portal.
- The overall pattern supports a trust dimension of centralized coordination with distributed national execution, without implying a complete inventory of all Turkish administrative systems.

## Payment & Financial Coordination Characteristics

- The evidence and signals support a trust dimension of centrally coordinated payment continuity through the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye’s operation of EFT and FAST.
- EFT supports a trust dimension of domestic settlement persistence through a common national payment rail rather than fragmented institution-specific arrangements.
- FAST supports a trust dimension of instant-payment continuity extending into 24/7 operation rather than remaining limited to standard banking-hour cycles.
- FAST’s integration with banks and payment or electronic-money institutions supports a trust dimension of layered payment coordination across multiple institution types rather than a narrow bank-to-bank transfer environment.
- The coexistence of EFT and FAST supports a trust dimension of coordinated interaction between non-instant and instant payment layers inside one national payment structure.
- Borsa İstanbul supports a trust dimension of Istanbul-centered financial-market concentration within the wider national financial system rather than a fully dispersed capital-market environment.
- The overall pattern supports a trust dimension of payment continuity through central-bank-operated settlement layers and Istanbul-centered market concentration rather than through a purely decentralized financial structure.

## Cross-System Stability Characteristics

- The evidence and signals support a trust dimension of Ankara-centered administrative concentration with distributed national execution. This pattern appears across transport administration, energy coordination, communications regulation, disaster management, and payment infrastructure while operational systems remain territorially distributed.
- A second recurring stability characteristic is Istanbul-centered commercial, financial, and airport concentration within a wider national continuity structure. This pattern appears through Borsa İstanbul, Istanbul Airport, Sabiha Gökçen, Bosphorus-crossing Marmaray operations, and limited digital-adjacent concentration signals.
- A third recurring stability characteristic is distributed territorial continuity through layered infrastructure. Rail, roads, airports, ports, electricity transmission, gas transmission, public-service access, cyber notification, and disaster-response structures all extend beyond one metropolitan core.
- A fourth recurring stability characteristic is Bosphorus-crossing continuity as operational infrastructure rather than symbolic geography. Marmaray makes cross-strait rail continuity visible as part of routine national transport function.
- A fifth recurring stability characteristic is interoperability as a continuity mechanism, visible across FIR coordination, electricity interconnection, gas interconnection, GÉANT linkage, and transport-system interfaces.
- A sixth recurring stability characteristic is energy interconnection supporting continuity. TEİAŞ and BOTAŞ both show centrally coordinated national systems whose routine operation includes border-facing or maritime-linked interconnection layers.
- A seventh recurring stability characteristic is central-bank payment coordination coupled to layered digital public-service continuity. EFT, FAST, e-Devlet, trust services, and regulated digital access rails show interaction between financial and administrative coordination layers.
- An eighth recurring stability characteristic is research-network and cyber-coordination support for broader continuity. ULAKNET, ULAKBİM, and USOM show that Turkey’s digital continuity depends not only on service gateways and regulators but also on separate knowledge-network and cyber-notification structures.
- Taken together, the documented layers support a trust dimension of continuity sustained through overlapping transport, energy, payment, digital, administrative, research-network, and disaster-response systems rather than through a single corridor or single platform.

## Dependency & Constraint Characteristics

- The evidence and signals support a trust dimension of payment continuity dependency on central-bank-operated rails, especially EFT for domestic settlement continuity and FAST for instant-payment persistence.
- Energy continuity shows explicit dependency on TEİAŞ dispatch coordination, electricity interconnection lines, BOTAŞ gas-transmission coordination, multiple gas-entry routes, storage sites, and LNG or FSRU assets rather than exclusively internal pipeline flows.
- Administrative and digital continuity depend materially on shared-service components such as e-Devlet, regulated electronic-signature services, TRABİS naming administration, and wider sectoral coordination rather than fully separate institutional stacks.
- Telecommunications and data continuity depend partly on interaction among BTK regulation, trust-service providers, TRABİS, and the separate ULAKNET backbone rather than on one fully visible commercial network topology.
- Several nationally visible coordination functions remain concentrated in Ankara, while financial-market and major-airport functions remain visibly concentrated in Istanbul, creating structural concentration dependencies even though transport, energy, emergency, and research systems are territorially distributed.
- Public observability remains bounded across commercial peering topology, private backbone architecture, data-center geography, detailed rail operations, some port-operating detail, redundancy engineering, and internal continuity procedures, which limits how fully these characteristics can be described from open sources.
- These dimensions therefore describe observable continuity and dependency patterns only, not a complete map of Turkish operational infrastructure conditions.
