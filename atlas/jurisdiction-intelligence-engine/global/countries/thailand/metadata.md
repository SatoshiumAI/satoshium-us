# Thailand — Metadata Layer

## Metadata Scope

- This file records metadata for Thailand derived strictly from `evidence.md`, `signals.md`, and `trust-dimensions.md`.
- This metadata is structural only and describes classification, topology, continuity, interoperability, infrastructure orientation, and dependency characteristics.
- No rankings, scores, readiness levels, or Atlas surface assignments are made in this file.
- All metadata statements are renderer-safe and bounded to prior Atlas layers.

## Jurisdiction Type

- Sovereign mainland Southeast Asian nation-state.
- Bangkok-centered operational environment.
- Mixed industrial and service infrastructure environment.
- Multimodal logistics coordination environment.
- Regionally interoperable ASEAN-linked and mainland-connected system.

## Geographic Orientation

- Mainland Southeast Asia positioning with overland borders to Myanmar, Lao PDR, Cambodia, and Malaysia.
- Dual maritime orientation through the Gulf of Thailand and the Andaman Sea.
- Bangkok-centered inland coordination structure with strong eastern seaboard extension.
- Mainland continuity environment combining overland linkage, maritime access, and aviation concentration.
- Bangkok to eastern seaboard axis as the densest visible concentration zone in the public infrastructure record.

## Infrastructure Orientation

- Multimodal continuity structure spanning aviation, metropolitan rail, commuter rail, power transmission, payments, telecommunications, and cloud-linked data infrastructure.
- Bangkok-centered concentration across administrative, transport, exchange, and payment layers.
- Eastern seaboard industrial and logistics coupling through airport, rail, cargo, port-adjacent, and data-center-linked infrastructure.
- Layered digital, payment, and logistics interaction rather than single-sector infrastructure dependence.
- Metropolitan-first continuity structure with secondary extension into the Bangkok to Chonburi corridor.

## Administrative Coordination Characteristics

- Shared transaction-governance structures through ETDA across government-to-government, government-to-business, and government-to-citizen environments.
- Distributed administrative interoperability through linked governance, identity-exchange, and federation layers.
- Public and private coordination linkage across transaction oversight and identity-service interaction.
- Administrative continuity shaped by reusable digital coordination components rather than fully isolated agency environments.

## Identity & Service Characteristics

- Identity-verification continuity through NDID-supported verification, consented data exchange, eKYC, eConsent, and electronic signatures.
- Linked identity and service coordination through NDID readiness to connect to the ThaID application.
- Reusable consent and verification structures supporting repeated transaction and service-access interaction.
- Federation-oriented identity continuity through ThaiIDF in the education and research environment.
- Distributed service-access continuity across identity, consent, verification, and sector-specific federation layers.
