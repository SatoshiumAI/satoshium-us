# Chile - Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- Secretaría de Gobierno Digital materials state that Chile’s digital-government mandate includes cross-sector coordination, strategic technology use, and the development and operation of shared platforms and services for interoperability and digital identity, placing Chile inside a documented state-led digital-transformation environment.
- Gobierno Digital materials also state that the Chilean state aims for the public sector to surpass the OECD average in digital transformation by 2030.
- ChileAtiende states that ClaveÚnica is a digital identity that enables online access to multiple state services and benefits, documenting a national access credential for public digital services.
- Banco Central de Chile materials surfaced via official site search results state that the central bank is mandated to ensure the normal functioning of payments, acts as regulator of payment systems, and operates the Sistema de Liquidación Bruta en Tiempo Real (LBTR), placing Chile inside a documented central-bank-operated settlement environment.
- SUBTEL, MinCiencia, REUNA, NLHPC, NIC Chile, Empresa Portuaria San Antonio, Nuevo Pudahuel, and related materials document national infrastructure across fiber, 5G, data centers, research networking, domain governance, maritime logistics, and air cargo.
- CSIRT Nacional materials surfaced via official site search results state that Article 24 of Law No. 21.663 creates the national incident-response team within the Agencia Nacional de Ciberseguridad, documenting an institutional cyber-coordination structure.
- Pacific Alliance materials place Chile inside a formal regional integration bloc oriented toward free mobility of goods, services, capital, and people, with explicit projection toward the Asia-Pacific region.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- Gobierno Digital states that its mandate is to coordinate and advise state bodies on the strategic use of digital technologies, public data, and information, and to develop and operate shared platforms and services including interoperability and digital identity.
- Gobierno Digital states that it works across three lines of action: establishing cross-cutting digital-transformation policies and rules, developing and operating enabling shared platforms, and coordinating institutions in implementation.
- Gobierno Digital’s services and platforms catalogue identifies ClaveÚnica as a centralized digital-authentication service made available to public institutions to facilitate secure citizen access to digital services and platforms.
- The same catalogue identifies CasillaÚnica as a shared service for official electronic notifications to people and companies through a unique digital address, DocDigital as the platform for digital processing and exchange of official communications between state bodies, and CPAT as the official catalogue for identifying and characterizing administrative procedures and other state transactions.
- Gobierno Digital states that PISEE 1 has operated centrally since late 2009 as a services-integration platform intended to foster interoperability through a single service bus, while PISEE 2 is presented as a decentralized state platform for exchanging data, documents, and case files between public institutions.
- Gobierno Digital states that PISEE 2 is tied to the Ley de Transformación Digital del Estado and is intended to reduce connection times between agencies while protecting cybersecurity and supporting state digitalization.
- ChileAtiende states that ClaveÚnica can be requested through Registro Civil offices or videoconference and that it enables online access to diverse state services and benefits.
- Gobierno Digital states that Law No. 21.180 requires the full administrative cycle of covered state bodies to be carried out electronically, including official communications, notifications, applications, electronic files, digitization of paper submissions, and interoperability between institutions.
- Gobierno Digital further states that state bodies must provide and properly use electronic platforms for electronic records and that those platforms must meet security, interoperability, interconnection, and cybersecurity standards.

## Financial Infrastructure and Settlement Systems

- Banco Central de Chile materials surfaced via official site search results state that one of the mandates established in the central bank’s constitutional organic law is to ensure the normal functioning of payments.
- The same Banco Central materials state that the central bank fulfills that mandate by acting as regulator of payment systems and as operator of the Sistema de Liquidación Bruta en Tiempo Real (LBTR).
- Banco Central materials surfaced via official site search results state that Chile’s high-value payment systems are composed of the LBTR system administered by Banco Central de Chile and the clearing-house system.
- Banco Central materials surfaced via official site search results also state that Chile’s low-value payment systems use a variety of instruments including cash, checks, credit and debit cards, and electronic transfers.
- Banco Central’s payment-systems report materials surfaced via official site search results state that the number of card payments and electronic transfers in Chile has more than quadrupled over the last decade, and attributes this in part to continued modernization of the regulatory framework.
- Banco Central announcement materials surfaced via official site search results state that the bank updated its regulation on issuance and operation of payment cards in 2024 by modifying Chapter III.J of its Compendium of Financial Standards to adapt regulation to changes in the retail-payments industry.
- The current source set therefore documents a central-bank-led payment and settlement environment with an RTGS core and a broad retail-payments layer, without supporting broader claims about international settlement authority beyond the cited institutional materials.

## Data Infrastructure and Digital Continuity

- Gobierno Digital materials document a state-operated shared-platform environment that includes ClaveÚnica, CasillaÚnica, CPAT, DocDigital, and PISEE, providing evidence of centralized and federated digital-service components used across the public sector.
- Gobierno Digital states that PISEE 1 provides a single service bus for institutional integration and that PISEE 2 is designed for decentralized exchange of data, documents, and case files between public institutions.
- Gobierno Digital states that Law No. 21.180 requires administrative procedures to move to electronic formats and requires public electronic platforms to comply with security, interoperability, interconnection, and cybersecurity standards.
- Gobierno Digital’s open-data platform description states that DatosGob functions as the state’s open-data repository, where institutions publish data in open formats under a centralized and organized catalogue intended for reuse.
- MinCiencia states that Chile has characteristics positioning it as an attractive pole for data-center development, including abundant low-cost renewable energy, 62,000 kilometers of fiber-optic infrastructure, connections to a network of 69,000 kilometers of submarine cables, and rapid growth in data-center capacity from 35 MW in 2013 to 198 MW in 2023.
- MinCiencia states that the Plan Nacional de Data Centers was created to accelerate investment, consolidate Chile as a technological hub in Latin America, and guide sustainable data-center development with collaborative governance.
- Google Cloud states that its Santiago cloud region is operational, describing it as Google Cloud’s first region in Chile and second in South America.
- AWS states that it announced plans to launch an AWS South America (Chile) Region by the end of 2026 with three Availability Zones, and also documents earlier Chile infrastructure presence including a CloudFront edge location in 2019, an AWS Ground Station antenna location in Punta Arenas in 2021, and AWS Outposts in Chile.
- The current source set therefore documents a growing public-sector data layer, local and planned hyperscale-provider presence, and a national policy push around data-center development, but it does not document a wholly sovereign hyperscale compute stack independent of global providers.

## Research Network and Compute Infrastructure

- REUNA states that its national high-speed academic network extends from Arica to Punta Arenas and privately connects all member institutions to facilitate traffic exchange among them.
- REUNA states that the network is administered by its operations center and supported 24 hours a day throughout the year.
- REUNA states that it offers international connectivity to member institutions through RedCLARA and AmLight, placing Chile’s academic-network layer inside wider regional and global research-network structures.
- REUNA materials surfaced via official site search results also state that the organization has direct connections in Chile, Brazil, and the United States through alliances with RedCLARA, AmLight, and PIT Chile to improve access to public-cloud services for research and education institutions.
- NLHPC states that its Guacolda-Leftraru Epu supercomputer is a high-performance computing infrastructure based on distributed-memory architecture.
- NLHPC states that Guacolda-Leftraru Epu integrates processing capacity, InfiniBand NDR interconnection, and IBM ESS 3200 storage, and that the infrastructure totals 769 Tflops with 9,956 compute cores distributed across 91 compute nodes.
- NLHPC states that the Guacolda cluster entered operation in 2019 and that Leftraru Epu joined in 2024 to increase national compute capacity.
- The current source set therefore documents a national research-network backbone and a named national HPC facility, while not documenting a broader sovereign compute estate beyond the cited academic and research infrastructure.

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- NIC Chile Registry states that the .CL registry is operated by NIC Chile Registry and explains the distinction between the registry, which manages the database of domain names under the top-level domain and publishes the zone file, and registrars authorized to market domain names.
- NIC Chile materials surfaced via official site search results state that NIC Chile, a center of the Faculty of Physical and Mathematical Sciences of the University of Chile, administers the .CL domain-name registry that identifies Chile on the Internet and is responsible for its secure and stable operation.
- NIC Chile DNSSEC materials surfaced via official site search results state that NIC Chile officially signed .CL in 2010 and included the DNSSEC key for the .CL zone in the DNS root in 2011.
- NIC Chile DNSSEC implementation materials surfaced via official site search results state that each .CL domain administrator implementing DNSSEC must create a public key and send it to NIC Chile, which signs the key and creates a chain of trust from .CL to the domain name.
- The current source set directly documents .CL registry governance and DNSSEC continuity controls more clearly than broader internet-exchange topology, and does not provide a comparably strong institutional basis for wider national exchange claims.

## Telecommunications and Digital Infrastructure

- SUBTEL states that Chile is advancing fiber deployment across all regions and identified named projects including Fibra Óptica Nacional, Fibra Óptica Tarapacá, Fibra Óptica Complejos Fronterizos, Fibra Óptica Austral, and last-mile projects.
- SUBTEL stated in 2023 that by 2024, 340 of Chile’s 345 communes would be connected by fiber optic infrastructure and that solutions were being advanced for the remaining five communes.
- Fibra Óptica Austral project materials state that the project’s main objective is to develop an open-access, non-discriminatory high-capacity telecommunications infrastructure for southern Chile.
- The same project materials state that, once complete, the southern network would become the world’s southernmost node of global optical-cable infrastructure.
- SUBTEL statistics published in 2026 state that Chile exceeded 10 million 5G connections, that 84.1% of connected households with fixed internet already had fiber, and that fixed-fiber accesses surpassed 4.05 million.
- MinCiencia states that Chile has 62,000 kilometers of fiber-optic infrastructure, links to 69,000 kilometers of submarine cables, and 3.8 million 5G-connected devices, framing those assets as part of Chile’s attractiveness for digital infrastructure and data centers.
- The current source set therefore documents a telecom environment built around extensive fiber rollout, open-access southern backbone development, mobile 5G growth, and submarine-cable linkage.

## Energy and Grid Integration Infrastructure

- Coordinador Eléctrico Nacional materials surfaced via official site search results describe the Coordinador as a technical and independent body responsible for coordinating and operating the Sistema Eléctrico Nacional (SEN), while guaranteeing open access.
- Coordinador materials surfaced via official site search results state that the SEN covers almost the entirety of Chilean territory, from Arica y Parinacota in the north to the island of Chiloé in the south.
- Additional Coordinador materials surfaced via official site search results describe the operator’s system as extending more than 3,100 kilometers in length.
- MinCiencia states that Chile has abundant low-cost renewable energy, and presents that energy base as one of the factors attracting data-center development.
- Ministerio de Energía materials surfaced via official site search results define non-conventional renewable energies in Chile as including wind, small hydroelectric, biomass, biogas, geothermal, solar, and marine energy.
- The current source set therefore documents a nationally coordinated grid operator and a broad renewable-energy base, while not supporting more specific claims here about cross-border interconnection or wholesale-market structure beyond the cited materials.

## Logistics and Global Connectivity Infrastructure

- Empresa Portuaria San Antonio states that the San Antonio port system operates under a concession model supervised by the state port company and integrates infrastructure, terminals, and vessel and cargo-transfer services.
- Empresa Portuaria San Antonio states that Puerto San Antonio has 1,886 linear meters of quay and that cargo operations are handled through concessioned terminals including San Antonio Terminal Internacional, DP World San Antonio, Puerto Panul, Terquim San Antonio, and QC Policarpo Toro.
- The same source states that Puerto San Antonio serves 67% of the container market of Chile’s central macrozone, whose hinterland represents 59% of national GDP and about 63% of the country’s population.
- Empresa Portuaria San Antonio also states that the port maintained 21 regular and non-regular shipping services and handled 1,025 vessels in 2025, connecting with major ports in Asia, Europe, North America, South America, Africa, and Oceania.
- Nuevo Pudahuel states that Santiago Airport became FedEx’s main logistics hub in Latin America in 2024 and describes Santiago as a cargo-logistics hub for the Southern Cone.
- Nuevo Pudahuel states that Santiago Airport handled 425,860 tons of cargo in 2023 and projected 435,754 tons for 2024, with 94.3% of that total expected to be international cargo.
- The current source set therefore documents maritime and air-cargo infrastructure with strong Pacific and hemispheric connectivity, without extending that evidence into broader routing-role claims.

## Cybersecurity and Digital Resilience Coordination

- CSIRT Nacional materials surfaced via official site search results state that Article 24 of Law No. 21.663 creates the Equipo Nacional de Respuesta a Incidentes de Seguridad Informática, CSIRT Nacional, within the Agencia Nacional de Ciberseguridad.
- The same CSIRT Nacional materials surfaced via official site search results state that this team must respond to cybersecurity incidents with significant effects in the country and coordinate state administration bodies when incidents affect their ability to continue operating.
- CSIRT Nacional incident-guidance materials surfaced via official site search results instruct affected organizations to notify CSIRT Nacional, collect information, resolve the problem, recover system operations, and communicate again with leadership and users, documenting an operational incident-response workflow.
- Additional CSIRT Nacional service materials surfaced via official site search results describe periodic active scanning intended to identify Internet-exposed resources and detect vulnerabilities preventively in compliance with Law No. 21.663.
- Gobierno Digital states that state electronic platforms used for digital administrative procedures must meet cybersecurity standards, linking Chile’s digital-administration layer to explicit cyber requirements.
- The current source set therefore documents Chile’s cyber-coordination layer more clearly through legal mandate, incident response, and preventive scanning services than through publicly detailed national certification structures.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- Banco Central de Chile materials surfaced via official site search results document domestic regulation and operation of payment systems, but the current source set does not support broader claims about international monetary or settlement centrality beyond the Chilean payment-system perimeter.
- Google Cloud and AWS materials document operational and planned hyperscale-provider presence in Chile, while MinCiencia’s data-center plan documents state support for the sector; however, the cited infrastructure remains substantially tied to global providers rather than a documented sovereign hyperscale stack.
- REUNA and NLHPC document academic-network and high-performance-compute infrastructure, but the current source set documents a narrower research-compute estate than a fully sovereign national compute stack spanning multiple sectors.
- NIC Chile materials directly document .CL registry governance and DNSSEC continuity, but the current source set does not provide equally strong institutional evidence for broader claims about carrier-neutral national internet-exchange centrality.
- Coordinador and energy materials document a national grid operator and renewable-energy base, but the current source set does not establish broader claims about cross-border grid authority or export-energy corridor status.
- The current source set does not document a sovereign semiconductor fabrication stack in Chile.
- These limitations describe only the boundaries of the currently cited evidence and should not be treated as negative judgments.

## Summary

- The current source set documents Chile as a state-coordinated digital-governance jurisdiction with shared interoperability, identity, notification, and electronic-procedure platforms anchored by Gobierno Digital and ClaveÚnica.
- It documents Chile as a central-bank-led payment and settlement environment with Banco Central de Chile regulating payment systems and operating the LBTR high-value settlement core.
- It documents Chile as a telecommunications and data-infrastructure environment with deep fiber expansion, southern open-access backbone development, strong 5G growth, expanding data-center policy support, an operational Google Cloud region, and a planned AWS region.
- It documents Chile as a research and connectivity jurisdiction with a national academic backbone, international research-network links, and a named national HPC facility.
- It documents Chile as a registry-governance, logistics, cyber-coordination, and Pacific-integration jurisdiction through NIC Chile, CSIRT Nacional, Puerto San Antonio, Santiago Airport, and Pacific Alliance structures.
- The cited evidence supports these infrastructure characterizations without assigning readiness tiers, corridor authority, or wider Atlas interpretation beyond the institutional materials.
