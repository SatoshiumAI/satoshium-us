## Institutional Continuity Dimension

- The source layers indicate institutional continuity supported by coordinated digital-administration structures, interoperable public-sector systems, legally mandated electronic procedures, and layered administrative coordination mechanisms rather than fragmented agency-by-agency modernization.
- Gobierno Digital indicates continuity through a standing state coordination mandate spanning digital transformation policy, shared platforms, interoperability, and digital identity systems.
- Law No. 21.180 indicates continuity through legally mandated electronic procedures, electronic records, interoperability requirements, and cybersecurity obligations attached to administrative modernization.
- ClaveÚnica, CasillaÚnica, DocDigital, CPAT, and PISEE indicate continuity across identity, official communications, procedure management, and institutional exchange rather than isolated functional systems.
- Banco Central de Chile, Coordinador Eléctrico Nacional, NIC Chile, REUNA, NLHPC, CSIRT Nacional, and Chile’s logistics operators indicate continuity across settlement governance, grid coordination, namespace administration, research-network interconnection, scientific compute infrastructure, cyber coordination, and Pacific-facing freight systems.
- Pacific Alliance participation and repeated cross-border research, logistics, and cloud-provider attachments indicate that Chile’s institutional continuity also operates within wider Latin American, Pacific, and globally connected frameworks rather than within a closed domestic perimeter.
- The documented trust characteristic is institutional continuity carried through layered public coordination mechanisms, legal digital-administration requirements, and repeated attachment to wider regional and global systems.
