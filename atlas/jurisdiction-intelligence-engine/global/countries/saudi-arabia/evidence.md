# Saudi Arabia - Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- Vision 2030 and the National Industrial Development and Logistics Program document Saudi Arabia as an industrial-development and logistics coordination environment, while Aramco, National Grid SA, Mawani, GACA, and GCCIA materials document large-scale energy, transmission, port, aviation, and regional interconnection infrastructure.
- my.gov.sa, SDAIA, and National Information Center materials document a digital-government environment built around national platforms, digital identity, government-cloud and data-platform functions, and shared digital-enablement infrastructure.
- Saudi Central Bank (SAMA), Saudi Payments, and SAMA Rulebook materials document a central-bank and payment-system coordination surface spanning SARIE, instant payments, mada, SADAD, and related regulatory structures.
- CST, stc, center3, and SaudiNIC materials document a telecommunications and digital-infrastructure environment that includes sector regulation, international internet gateway services, internet-exchange points, carrier and landing-station connectivity, and .sa registry governance.
- SDAIA, the National Strategy for Data & AI, NDMO materials, and my.gov.sa digital-transformation materials document a data and AI governance coordination layer tied to national data governance, digital identity, and government data platforms.
- Mawani, GACA, Riyadh Airports Company, Vision 2030, GCCIA, OPEC, and Aramco materials place Saudi Arabia inside regional and global shipping, aviation, grid, and energy-market coordination structures without making broader positional claims beyond the cited institutional evidence.
- National Cybersecurity Authority materials document a national cybersecurity coordination surface for state and critical-infrastructure protection.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- SDAIA official materials surfaced via official site search results describe SDAIA as the competent authority in the Kingdom concerned with data and AI, and other SDAIA materials describe it as the national reference body for regulating the data and AI sector.
- SDAIA materials surfaced via official site search results state that the National Information Center is organizationally linked to SDAIA and is responsible for developing, hosting, and operating the National Data Bank and the national foresight platforms, and is also the operator of the government cloud(s), the Government Secure Network, and the host of the Government Service Bus.
- my.gov.sa states that GOV.SA is the national reference for digital-government information and services in the Kingdom and provides single-point access to services across government ministries and departments.
- my.gov.sa service-channel materials identify Absher as the official eService portal for individuals and business of the Ministry of Interior.
- my.gov.sa National Profile materials describe the National Single Sign-On initiative and digital identity as a national initiative provided by the Ministry of Interior to manage and issue digital identity for citizens and residents, and state that it aims to provide unified access on a national level.
- my.gov.sa Nafath materials describe Nafath as part of the eID ecosystem and state that the Nafath mobile application enables users to create their digital identity securely via smartphones and receive verification requests from platforms and applications.
- my.gov.sa and CST materials document Tawakkalna as an active national digital-service surface. my.gov.sa news materials state that Tawakkalna integrates more than 1,000 government services, while CST’s Saudi Internet Report 2023 lists Nafath, Absher, and Tawakkalna Services among the most downloaded e-government applications in 2023.
- my.gov.sa Digital Transformation Key Enablers materials describe a digital-government support layer that includes electronic identification, trust services, secure payments, e-notifications, e-invoicing, interoperability frameworks, and the National Data Bank.
- SDAIA’s NDMO materials surfaced via official site search results describe a national data-governance framework spanning data classification, data sharing, data privacy, freedom of information, and open data, and SDAIA regulations materials describe NDMO as the national regulator and reference body for data management and governance.

## Financial Infrastructure and Payment Systems

- SAMA describes itself as the Saudi Central Bank, and SAMA Rulebook materials state that it is the legislative body responsible for exercising regulatory and supervisory control over banks and money exchangers and for issuing rules and overseeing compliance in the sector.
- SAMA materials state that SARIE is a state-of-the-art payment and settlement system that links all the banks in the Kingdom and provides a mechanism for safely and efficiently exchanging funds-transfer and direct-debit messages.
- SAMA Rulebook materials describe payment-systems and payment-services-provider rules and identify rules and instructions for operating the Saudi Fast Payment System (SARIE), documenting an explicit central-bank rulebook layer around national payment rails.
- Saudi Payments states in The Payments Report 2020 that it operates the back-end infrastructure of four platforms, mada, SADAD, SARIE, and Esal, documenting institutional operation across core domestic payment rails.
- my.gov.sa Digital Transformation Key Enablers materials describe SADAD as an online system that provides payment and tax services for individuals, businesses, and the public sector and enables payment of electricity, water, telecommunications, and government fees.
- SAMA news materials state that the instant-payment system sarie credits eligible transactions instantly to recipient accounts and includes a quick-transfer service, while SAMA Rulebook materials describe the February 2021 launch of the Instant Payment System as a fundamental building block toward the Kingdom’s electronic-payment goals.
- SAMA materials on CBDC experimentation state that Project Aber was an initiative conducted in collaboration with the Central Bank of the UAE to examine whether distributed-ledger technology could contribute to seamless cross-border payments.
- The current source set documents domestic payment-rail coordination, instant-payment capability, and cross-border experimentation, but does not support broader claims about independent global-settlement authority beyond the cited institutional materials.

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- Current official operator materials document Saudi Arabia’s exchange and gateway layer more clearly through stc’s Saudi Arabian Internet Gateway and center3’s published internet-exchange footprint than through the legacy SAIX label.
- stc states that approved ISPs can obtain international internet backbone services through the Saudi Arabian Internet Gateway (IGW), providing direct access to international bandwidth, content filtering, and national internet backbone access.
- stc’s wholesale connectivity materials state that its International Internet Connectivity service provides national operators in the Kingdom and international operators with connectivity to the world’s internet hubs, using national transmission, cable-station access, international circuits, and Jeddah IGW and landing-station infrastructure.
- center3 describes itself as a regional digital-infrastructure hub offering data centers, submarine cables, and internet exchanges, and its global-network materials list internet exchanges in Jeddah, Riyadh, and Dammam.
- SaudiNIC regulation materials state that SaudiNIC is the registration authority for Saudi top-level domains, is responsible for registration services for Saudi TLDs, formulates and implements rules and procedures, manages and operates the registries, and licenses registrars.
- SaudiNIC regulation materials further identify the Saudi Latin ccTLD .sa and the Saudi Arabic TLD السعودية as Saudi TLDs managed by SaudiNIC.
- SaudiNIC’s public statistics page documents an institutional registry-statistics surface that includes total registered domains, domains with DNSSEC activated, and registration statistics since 1995.

- my.gov.sa materials document national digital-service uptake through GOV.SA, Nafath, Absher, and Tawakkalna, showing telecom and digital-service infrastructure operating in conjunction rather than as isolated systems.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- SAMA, Project Aber, GCCIA, OPEC, Aramco, stc, and Mawani materials place major Saudi financial, grid, energy-export, telecom, and logistics systems inside cross-border regional and global coordination structures rather than a wholly isolated national infrastructure environment.
- Google Cloud’s Dammam-region access materials show region-specific access and billing conditions, including CNTXT-based reseller and invoiced-billing requirements, indicating controlled access terms rather than a universally open regional cloud surface.
- Official materials document domestic payment rails, registry governance, national data-governance frameworks, and government-cloud functions, but the current source set does not document a fully self-contained sovereign semiconductor-fabrication stack.
- Official materials document government data platforms, commercial cloud regions, data centers, and research supercomputing, but the current source set does not document a fully self-contained sovereign hyperscale compute stack independent of global provider and partner participation.
- Official internet-infrastructure materials more directly document stc gateway infrastructure and center3 internet exchanges than a currently retrievable official SAIX-branded surface.
- Research and compute materials directly document KAUST and KACST, but the current source set provides less direct institutional detail for a comprehensive national research-network backbone spanning all regions.
- SaudiNIC, SAMA, NIC, CST, NCA, and my.gov.sa materials document strong national governance and service layers, but those layers are presented inside broader interoperability, operator, licensing, and standards environments rather than as evidence of a fully closed national stack.

## Summary

Saudi Arabia is documented in the current source set as a energy and industrial coordination jurisdiction with institutionally visible oil, gas, transmission, grid, renewable-energy, and export infrastructure; a digital-government infrastructure environment built around GOV.SA, SDAIA, NIC, Absher, Nafath, Tawakkalna, and the National Data Bank; a central-bank and payment-system coordination jurisdiction structured through SAMA, Saudi Payments, SARIE, mada, SADAD, and instant-payment infrastructure; a data and AI governance environment tied to SDAIA, NDMO, national data-governance frameworks, government-cloud functions, and institutionally documented commercial cloud and data-center buildout; a research and compute environment anchored by KAUST and KACST; a telecommunications and registry environment documented through CST, stc, center3, and SaudiNIC; a logistics and global energy integration participant across ports, airports, aviation strategy, and export infrastructure; a cybersecurity coordination jurisdiction through NCA and national cyber controls; and a GCC and global institutional participant through GCC grid interconnection, Project Aber, OPEC membership, and wider cross-border infrastructure interfaces.
