## Cross-System Structural Patterns

- The strongest recurring pattern is Warsaw-centered administrative concentration with distributed national execution. This appears across public-service access, payment coordination, and aviation concentration while rail, road, port, energy, cyber, and research systems remain territorially distributed.
- A second recurring pattern is distributed territorial continuity through layered infrastructure. Rail management, motorway continuity, Baltic ports, electricity transmission, gas transmission, crisis coordination, and research-network functions all extend beyond Warsaw and operate at national scale.
- A third recurring pattern is interoperability as a continuity mechanism. TEN-T participation, Baltic Sea-Adriatic corridor inclusion, TARGET-NBP, Euro Elixir, electricity interconnections, Baltic Pipe, eIDAS framing, and GÉANT connectivity all show continuity reinforced through structured external coupling.
- A fourth recurring pattern is layered identity-and-service continuity. Profil Zaufany, ePUAP, and mObywatel show shared identity, communication, application, and access layers supporting administrative continuity rather than isolated service channels.
- A fifth recurring pattern is energy interconnection supporting continuity. PSE and GAZ-SYSTEM both show centralized national operators whose routine operating model includes cross-border or Baltic-facing interfaces.
- A sixth recurring pattern is research-network and cyber-coordination support for broader continuity. NASK, CERT Polska, and PIONIER indicate that digital continuity depends not only on public-service portals but also on institutional cyber coordination and a separate research backbone.
- Overall, the evidence supports a Warsaw-centered but nationally distributed operating model in which transport, energy, payments, digital services, crisis coordination, and research networking reinforce one another without reducing Polish continuity to a single corridor or system.
