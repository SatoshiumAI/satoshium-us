2.Evidence Layer

The change-log records that evidence.md established the documented institutional and infrastructure anchors for the Poland jurisdiction package across administrative governance, identity, payments, telecommunications, transport, energy, EU and Central European interoperability, disaster-response, and connectivity surfaces.

Geographic and regional position

The evidence layer records Poland as a sovereign state in Central Europe with Baltic Sea access and land borders with Germany, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Lithuania, Belarus, Ukraine, and Russia through the Kaliningrad region, combining a large land-connected national territory with northern maritime interfaces, making corridor management, domestic continuity, and border-facing infrastructure operationally significant. European Union materials are recorded as identifying Warsaw as the capital, confirming Poland's EU membership since 1 May 2004 and Schengen membership since 21 December 2007, and describing a state structure divided into 16 voivodeships, placing Polish transport, payment, energy, and digital-service systems inside wider European interoperability frameworks rather than a purely domestic operating environment.

Transportation and logistics infrastructure

The evidence layer records PKP Polskie Linie Kolejowe as the manager of the national railway network, with PKP Intercity as the leader of the passenger-transport market operating 505 trains daily, linking city centers and tourist centers, and enabling cross-European travel, with cross-border services such as the Baltic Express linking the Polish Baltic coast through major inland nodes to Prague. Road continuity is recorded through GDDKiA's nationwide structure of national roads, motorways, expressways, and regional branches across the voivodeships, with continued operating relevance of the A1, A2, and A4 motorway axes inside the TEN-T framework. Maritime continuity is recorded as concentrated at the Baltic coast, with Port Gdańsk divided into an Inner Port and an Outer Port on Gdańsk Bay with deepwater terminals handling the largest vessels through the Danish Straits and regular shipping links across the Baltic Sea, North Sea, the Americas, and Asia, and Port Gdynia as a universal port specializing in general cargo, containers, and ro-ro traffic with multimodal hinterland connections and short-sea and ferry links. Warsaw Chopin Airport is recorded as the principal public aviation node organized around arrivals, departures, transfers, and passenger services.

Energy and industrial structure

The evidence layer records PSE as performing the duties of the transmission system operator using its own highest-voltage transmission grid, with published network-scale figures as of 31 December 2025 including 309 lines totaling 16,520 km, 112 extra-high-voltage substations, and the under-sea 450 kV DC connection between Poland and Sweden. PSE is recorded as contributing to security of electricity supply, maintaining interconnections with other power systems, managing congestion on interconnections, balancing the power system, preparing defence and restoration plans, and ensuring long-term ability of the system to meet domestic and cross-border transmission demand, with responsibility for balancing the Polish Power System and ensuring nationwide electricity delivery through the transmission layer into local distribution. Gas transmission is recorded through GAZ-SYSTEM as responsible for natural-gas transmission across Poland and operator of major gas pipelines, as controller of the President Lech Kaczyński LNG Terminal in Świnoujście, with Baltic Pipe as a strategic infrastructure project creating a new gas-supply corridor between Norway, Denmark, and Poland.

Digital and telecommunications infrastructure

The evidence layer records NASK as a national research institute specializing in cybersecurity, operating research divisions and specialized departments focused on cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, and cloud computing, with work for public institutions including secure document-management systems for government agencies and cybersecurity training for public officials. CERT Polska is recorded as operating within NASK as the first Polish computer emergency response team, with incident handling, active response to direct threats, cooperation with national and international CERT teams, security research and development, malware analysis, monitoring and threat-correlation tooling, annual cybersecurity reporting, and participation in FIRST and TF-CSIRT. PIONIER is recorded as a research-network consortium with 8,504 km of fiber-optic lines including domestic and foreign segments, high-capacity DWDM transmission, connection of Polish scientific users to European research networks through GÉANT, and access to the global Internet. PLIX-specific descriptive topology is recorded as not stably retrievable in public form during compilation, so no detailed PLIX topology or capacity claims appear in the evidence layer.

Financial and payment infrastructure

The evidence layer records Narodowy Bank Polski as operator of the wholesale payment systems SORBNET3 and TARGET-NBP, in which accounts of authorized financial institutions are maintained in Polish zloty and euro respectively, with the Governor of NBP overseeing the functioning of payment schemes and NBP granting authorizations for new payment systems and major rules changes. KIR is recorded as providing the main domestic clearing layer, with systems launched for interbank transactions providing the technological foundations for electronic banking in Poland, and Elixir, Euro Elixir, and Express Elixir identified as active transaction-handling systems, with Express Elixir's instant-payment service processed in real time and guaranteed uninterrupted availability 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, including public holidays. The evidence supports a layered payment structure in which Warsaw-centered central-bank functions, wholesale settlement rails, and KIR-operated interbank clearing systems interact with domestic zloty payments and euro-facing interoperability.

Government and administrative technology structure

The evidence layer records Profil Zaufany as a means of electronic identification that enables users to confirm their identity online and sign documents electronically when handling official matters, usable for access to multiple public systems including ZUS, the patient account, tax services, and ePUAP. ePUAP is recorded as a nationwide ICT platform enabling communication between citizens and entrepreneurs and public entities and among public-administration entities, allowing public bodies to provide services electronically without each institution building a separate system, with a single account usable across many public entities. mObywatel is recorded as a separate mobile and web access layer providing digital documents, electronic forms and applications, payments to public-administration entities, access to mailboxes for contact with public administration, vehicle-and-driver services, and case history. The evidence supports a layered identity-and-service environment rather than a set of isolated agency portals.

EU and Central European interoperability infrastructure

The evidence layer records Poland's infrastructure as deeply connected to European interoperability frameworks, with the European Commission describing TEN-T as the framework for a coherent multimodal network and the Baltic Sea-Adriatic Sea corridor placing Polish Baltic ports, Warsaw, and inland Polish nodes directly inside a cross-border corridor structure reaching Czechia, Slovakia, Austria, Hungary, Slovenia, Croatia, and Italy. Energy interoperability is recorded through PSE's responsibilities for maintaining interconnections with other power systems, cross-border transmission demand, and congestion management on interconnections, and through Baltic Pipe linking Norway, Denmark, and Poland alongside GAZ-SYSTEM's wider regional gas flows. Payment interoperability is recorded through NBP's TARGET-NBP operation and KIR's Euro Elixir and other euro-facing payment services, while digital interoperability is recorded through the European Commission's eIDAS framework for mutual recognition of national eID schemes and PIONIER's connection to GÉANT.

Disaster resilience, cybersecurity, and operational coordination

The evidence layer records the Government Centre for Security as a supraministerial structure created to optimize and standardize threat perception across government departments, conduct comprehensive risk analysis, coordinate information flow on threats, launch crisis-management procedures at the national level, support critical-infrastructure protection, organize trainings and exercises, and serve as a national center for crisis management under the Prime Minister. CERT Polska is recorded as providing the principal publicly documented cyber-response layer through registration and handling of network-security incidents, active response to direct threats, cooperation with national and international CERT teams, and development of detection and analysis tooling. CERT Polska operating within NASK is recorded as placing Poland's cybersecurity coordination as part of a broader national digital-infrastructure and research environment, with RCB and CERT Polska together supporting a combination of crisis-management coordination, critical-infrastructure planning, incident response, and national-to-international cooperation.

Regional and international connectivity

The evidence layer records regional and international connectivity as layered across rail, road, maritime, aviation, electricity, gas, payments, and research networks, with Germany-, Czech-, and Slovakia-facing continuity visible in road and rail corridor structures, the Baltic Express to Prague, and TEN-T and Baltic Sea-Adriatic corridor documentation, and Lithuania-facing and broader Baltic connectivity visible through the maritime layer at Gdańsk and Gdynia. Maritime connectivity is recorded as concentrated on the Baltic coast with direct regular links from Port Gdańsk to Baltic, North Sea, intercontinental, and Asian shipping networks and Port Gdynia's multimodal general-cargo, container, and ro-ro specialization, while Warsaw Chopin Airport provides the main visible aviation interface. Electricity connectivity is recorded through PSE's interconnections and the Sweden link, gas connectivity through LNG infrastructure and Baltic Pipe, and research-network connectivity through PIONIER's connection to GÉANT and European scientific networks.

The current source set documents Poland as a Warsaw-centered administrative and payment environment supported by nationally distributed transport, energy, digital, and continuity operators, with Warsaw the clearest concentration point for government technology, central-bank payment infrastructure, and the most visible aviation gateway, while rail management, long-distance passenger rail, motorways, Baltic port infrastructure, electricity transmission, gas transmission, research networking, and crisis-management functions are documented as nationwide or corridor-based systems.
