## Jurisdiction Normalization Summary

- Kenya normalized as a Nairobi-centered administrative environment with distributed county continuity rather than a single-node administrative model.
- It normalized as a payment-settlement interoperability environment through CBK continuity, National Payments System continuity, KEPSS continuity, RTGS continuity, and visible regional payment-system interfaces rather than a fragmented payment model.
- It normalized as a telecommunications continuity environment through Communications Authority continuity, `.ke` naming governance, KeNIC continuity, and KIXP continuity rather than a purely operator-defined communications environment.
- It normalized as an electricity-coordination environment through KETRACO continuity, transmission continuity, interconnector continuity, dispatch-planning visibility where evidenced, and Kenya Power electricity-service continuity rather than a detached electricity model.
- It normalized as a maritime-support continuity environment through Kenya Ports Authority continuity and Port of Mombasa continuity rather than a port-isolated or gateway-framed model.
- It normalized as a research-network-supported continuity environment through KENET continuity, academic-network continuity, institutional-connectivity continuity, international research-network continuity, and eduroam-related continuity rather than a commercial-connectivity-only environment.
- It normalized as a regional interconnection continuity environment through payment, electricity, exchange, aviation, maritime, and research-network linkage rather than geopolitical framing.
- It normalized as a bounded-observability environment in which continuity is visible across multiple public layers while deeper private, cyber-operational, logistics, and commercial topology remains only partly visible.
