# New Zealand — Evidence Layer

## Scope & Methodology

This evidence layer is derived from publicly visible sources only, principally official New Zealand government portals, regulators, public operators, public institutional websites, and stable official-site search-visible records where direct extraction was limited by dynamic rendering, bot protection, thin output, redirects, or inconsistent page exposure. It documents observable administrative structures, identity and public-service systems, payment and settlement rails, communications and domain-management layers, electricity-grid structures, transportation systems, aviation and port administration, emergency-coordination surfaces, cyber and data-governance mechanisms, research-network signals, and distributed territorial continuity environments visible in New Zealand.

The interpretation here is intentionally operational and infrastructure-oriented. It does not rank New Zealand, assign trust or readiness scores, infer hidden capability, infer surveillance capability, infer military or intelligence capability, create geopolitical interpretation, create tourism interpretation, create island-nation mythology, create remote-country interpretation, create disaster-resilience interpretation, create clean-green national-brand interpretation, create agricultural-export interpretation, create innovation interpretation, create economic-power interpretation, or create cultural-identity narratives. Public evidence is treated as bounded and incomplete by default, and wording remains renderer-safe by limiting claims to what public sources directly expose.

## Administrative & Public-Service Infrastructure

Publicly visible administrative infrastructure in New Zealand presents as a Wellington-centered administrative environment with distributed public-service and local-authority continuity layers. Public Service Commission Te Kawa Mataaho materials state that the Public Service includes departments, departmental agencies, and interdepartmental executive boards, and that the Commission leads the public sector under the Public Service Act 2020 while providing leadership and oversight of the Public Service system. The same public-service material maintains an up-to-date list of central government organisations. Govt.nz publicly exposes a cross-government service portal organised around operational topics including passports, citizenship and identity, tax, education, work, health, housing, family, and consumer matters. Department of Internal Affairs public-site records further expose that DIA provides services including passports, citizenship, birth, death and marriage registration, local government, internet safety, anti-spam, and support services to the executive.

Territorial continuity is also directly visible. Local Government Commission material states that local government in New Zealand is comprised of 78 local authorities, namely 11 regional councils and 67 territorial authorities, and distinguishes regional-council functions from territorial-authority functions. Auckland Council public materials expose a standing local-board layer through named local-board areas and associated plans, reports, members, and contact surfaces. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a Wellington-centered administrative environment with distributed regional, territorial, and service-delivery continuity rather than a single capital-office layer.

## Identity & Digital-Service Infrastructure

New Zealand’s publicly visible identity layer is anchored by the Department of Internal Affairs, RealMe, and public civil-registration services. DIA public-site records expose passports, citizenship, and birth, death and marriage registration as standing public functions. RealMe publicly states that one login can be used to access online services used with government agencies, including Inland Revenue, visa, company-detail, and student-support interactions. RealMe also publicly states that its verified-identity function can be used in situations where counterparties need to know who they are dealing with, including opening a bank account and enrolling to vote. This is direct public evidence of an identity and authentication layer spanning multiple service environments.

A second visible layer exists through civil-registration access. Births, Deaths and Marriages Online publicly states that the site is for people with a verified RealMe account or approved organisations to order certificates and printouts, and exposes non-historical record-ordering workflows through that access layer. Govt.nz also publicly exposes passports, citizenship and identity as a service category. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as an identity and digital-service continuity environment with visible document, registration, authentication, and public-service access structures, without inferring surveillance or non-public verification capability.

## Payment & Financial Infrastructure

New Zealand’s publicly visible payment infrastructure is organized around the Reserve Bank of New Zealand and industry clearing-system governance. Official Reserve Bank records visible through official-site search results state that the Exchange Settlement Account System (ESAS) is New Zealand’s principal high-value payment system, used by banks and other financial organisations to settle transactions in real time. The same official records state that RBNZ has provided ESAS as a real-time gross settlement system since 1998 and has established a legal and contractual environment intended to ensure settlement finality and irrevocability at the point of settlement. Additional RBNZ official records state that the Bank oversees, operates, regulates, and supervises core payment systems and is the sole regulator of pure payments systems, while sharing financial-market-infrastructure oversight with the Financial Markets Authority.

A second visible layer exists through Payments NZ. Payments NZ publicly states that it does not own or operate payments infrastructure, but manages clearing systems that govern how payments are exchanged and settled between financial institutions in Aotearoa New Zealand. Payments NZ publicly exposes the Bulk Electronic Clearing System, Consumer Electronic Clearing System, High Value Clearing System, and Settlement Before Interchange. Official-site search-visible Payments NZ records further state that Settlement Before Interchange is the payment settlement system used by BECS and CECS participants, and that HVCS governs large payments that cannot be reversed, including same-day cleared payments used where confirmation is required within the business day. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a payment-settlement interoperability environment with central-bank settlement infrastructure, industry-governed clearing systems, and visible retail and high-value settlement mechanisms, without converting those layers into financial-center or fintech narratives.

## Telecommunications & Connectivity Infrastructure

Telecommunications in New Zealand are publicly visible as a regulated communications environment linked to domain and exchange continuity layers. Commerce Commission materials publicly state that its role in regulating broadband services has revolved around Chorus’ copper-lines network, including setting wholesale prices for access to that network, and that legislative changes provide for regulation of ultra-fast broadband fibre networks as copper use declines. National Infrastructure Funding and Financing material on fibre publicly identifies the ultra-fast broadband delivery partners as Chorus, Northpower, Enable, and Tuatahi First Fibre. This is direct public evidence of a standing regulatory and programme-delivery layer for fixed-network infrastructure.

A second visible layer exists through .nz administration and peering exchanges. Official-site search-visible InternetNZ records state that InternetNZ is the home of .nz and that it operates the registry for .nz, keeping the definitive register of .nz domain names through the InternetNZ Registry System and related registry surfaces. Vital publicly states that it operates two peering exchanges in New Zealand, the Auckland Peering Exchange (APE) and the Wellington Internet Exchange (WIX), and that these exchanges provide a common meeting place where ISPs and other organisations can connect and exchange traffic. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a telecom-domain-exchange continuity environment with visible regulation, broadband programme actors, domain administration, registry continuity, and peering-exchange infrastructure, while private carrier topology and private peering remain only partly visible.

## Electricity & Energy Infrastructure

New Zealand’s publicly visible electricity environment is organized through Transpower and the Electricity Authority. Transpower publicly states that it holds a unique position in the New Zealand energy sector as both National Grid owner and System Operator. Transpower’s System Operator materials further state that it operates the wholesale electricity market and the power system, and manages system security while running the power system so homes and businesses can access electricity every second of every day. This is direct public evidence of a national grid and system-operation layer.

A second visible layer exists through market governance and participant structures. The Electricity Authority publicly states that it is New Zealand’s electricity regulator and that it sets and enforces the Electricity Industry Participation Code 2010, described as rules governing nearly every aspect of New Zealand’s electricity industry. Official-site search-visible Electricity Authority material further exposes a wholesale market with generation companies, retailers, gentailers, distribution and network companies, and other industry participants, and describes the system operator function as coordinating electricity supply and demand in real time to avoid fluctuations in frequency and disruption of supply. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as an electricity-grid continuity environment with visible grid ownership, system operation, market-rule administration, and multi-party industry participation, while internal contingency design, reserve structure, and private commercial arrangements remain only partly visible.

## Transportation Infrastructure

New Zealand’s publicly visible transport environment is organized through NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi, KiwiRail, and inter-island ferry links. Official-site search-visible NZTA records state that the agency’s primary function is to promote an affordable, integrated, safe, responsive and sustainable land transport system. NZTA’s roads-and-rail public materials expose state-highway management, toll-road administration, roads-and-rail surfaces, and the Highways Information Portal. Search-visible NZTA records further state that the state highway network comprises almost 11,000 kilometres of road, with identifiable North Island and South Island totals, and that it links to nearly 83,000 kilometres of local roads. NZTA public records also state that it works with transport partners throughout the country in planning, operating, and maintaining the state highway network.

Rail and ferry continuity are also publicly visible. Official-site search-visible KiwiRail records state that KiwiRail owns and maintains the national rail network infrastructure. KiwiRail’s Interislander materials publicly state that the Interislander ferry service is an extension of State Highway 1 and the Main Trunk Line across Cook Strait, linking road and rail networks between New Zealand’s North and South islands. NZTA’s public-facing navigation also exposes walking, cycling, public-transport, and roads-and-rail surfaces. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a road-rail-ferry continuity environment with visible state-highway administration, rail-network ownership, and inter-island transport linkage rather than a single-mode transport system.

## Aviation Infrastructure

New Zealand’s publicly visible aviation infrastructure is organized through the Civil Aviation Authority, Airways New Zealand, and multiple airport nodes. Official-site search-visible Civil Aviation Authority materials state that Part 71 empowers the Director to designate and classify airspace for aviation purposes in New Zealand’s domestic airspace and in airspace over the high seas for which New Zealand has accepted responsibility. Official CAA airspace materials also expose the Auckland Oceanic Flight Information Region and controlled and special-use airspace categories. This is direct public evidence of a standing aviation-regulatory and airspace-administration layer.

A second visible layer exists through air navigation services and airport operations. Airways publicly states that it is New Zealand’s air navigation service provider, safely managing more than 500,000 flights and providing air traffic management services. Wellington Airport, Christchurch Airport, and Queenstown Airport publicly expose passenger-information, airport-map, arrivals-and-departures, facilities, and contact surfaces. Auckland Airport’s official public and corporate surfaces are publicly visible through official-site records, though some direct extraction was limited at review time. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a distributed aviation-node continuity environment with visible regulation, air-navigation services, and multiple named airport surfaces including Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, and Queenstown, without converting those nodes into tourism-gateway or aviation-hub narratives.

## Maritime & Port Infrastructure

New Zealand’s publicly visible maritime infrastructure is organized through Maritime New Zealand, port-company surfaces, and ferry operations. Official-site search-visible Maritime New Zealand records state that Maritime NZ is the national regulatory, compliance and response agency for the safety, security, and environmental protection of coastal and inland waterways. Additional official-site records expose staff and operational presence across regional offices, a Wellington head office, a Marine Pollution Response Centre in Auckland, and Rescue Coordination Centre New Zealand in Lower Hutt. This is direct public evidence of a standing maritime regulatory and response layer.

Port continuity is also publicly visible through port-company surfaces. Publicly visible official sites expose Ports of Auckland, Port of Tauranga, CentrePort Wellington, Lyttelton Port Company, and Port Otago as operating port entities. Port of Tauranga publicly describes itself as an international freight gateway and exposes port-service material. CentrePort publicly describes itself as a port and logistics business serving central New Zealand and publicly exposes road and rail connections in its service description. Port Otago and Lyttelton Port Company publicly expose operating port-company surfaces, and KiwiRail’s Interislander materials provide direct evidence of standing ferry continuity across Cook Strait. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a maritime and port continuity environment with visible maritime regulation, distributed port-company surfaces, and ferry-linked continuity, while terminal-level commercial topology and detailed berth operations remain only partly visible.

## Disaster-Response & Emergency Coordination Infrastructure

New Zealand’s publicly visible emergency-coordination environment is organized through the National Emergency Management Agency, Civil Defence Emergency Management structures, public alerting, and hazard-information systems. Official-site search-visible NEMA records state that NEMA is the Government lead for emergency management and that it manages the National Crisis Management Centre. NEMA public materials also expose the Coordinated Incident Management System (CIMS) as New Zealand’s official framework for coordinated incident management across responding agencies. Additional official-site records state that 16 Civil Defence Emergency Management Groups have been formed across New Zealand as committees of elected councillors from councils within regional boundaries. This is direct public evidence of a layered emergency-management and coordination environment.

A second visible layer exists through public warning and monitoring systems. Get Ready public materials state that Emergency Mobile Alerts are broadcast to all capable phones from targeted cell towers. GeoNet publicly states that it was established to build and operate a modern geological-hazard monitoring system in New Zealand and that it comprises a network of geophysical instruments, automated software applications, and skilled staff to detect, analyse, and respond to earthquakes, volcanic activity, large landslides, tsunami, and slow deformation. MetService publicly states that it is New Zealand’s designated National Meteorological Service and publicly exposes its warning and advisory responsibilities. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as an emergency coordination environment with visible national leadership, regional CDEM structures, mobile alerting, geophysical monitoring, and meteorological warning functions, without converting those layers into disaster-resilience narratives.

## Cybersecurity & Data Infrastructure

New Zealand’s publicly visible cyber and data-governance layer is organized through the National Cyber Security Centre, digital-government leadership structures, and the Office of the Privacy Commissioner. Official-site search-visible NCSC records state that the National Cyber Security Centre responds to cyber security threats in New Zealand with authoritative information and advice, and that the NCSC is New Zealand’s Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT NZ). Additional official-site records expose telecommunications-network-security regulatory functions and public standards and advice surfaces. This is direct public evidence of a standing public cyber-incident and cyber-governance layer.

A second visible layer exists through digital-system leadership and privacy oversight. Official-site search-visible Digital Government records state that the Public Service Commissioner appoints system leads to develop and improve specific subject areas of government, and that Paul James is the Government Chief Digital Officer while also serving as Secretary for the Department of Internal Affairs. Digital Government leadership materials also expose the Digital Executive Board as supporting the digitisation and integration of public services. The Office of the Privacy Commissioner publicly states that section 17 of the Privacy Act 2020 sets out the Commissioner’s functions, including investigating complaints, receiving reports of notifiable privacy breaches, monitoring agencies, enforcing compliance with the Privacy Act, examining draft legislation, and monitoring technology impacts on privacy. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a cyber and data-governance environment with visible incident-response, digital-system leadership, and privacy-regulation structures, while deeper defensive tooling, intelligence capability, surveillance architecture, and non-public cyber operations remain outside public observability.

## Research & Education Network Infrastructure

New Zealand’s publicly visible research-and-education connectivity layer is anchored by REANNZ. REANNZ publicly states that it operates New Zealand’s national research and education network, and that people operating that network are part of REANNZ’s public-facing organisational surface. Official-site search-visible REANNZ records further describe REANNZ as designing, building, and operating New Zealand’s advanced national research and education network and as providing pathways and connections for global research and science collaboration. This is direct public evidence of a standing research-network environment.

A second visible layer exists through member-facing and international connectivity functions. Official-site search-visible REANNZ materials state that REANNZ is a Crown-owned company and membership organisation delivering eResearch infrastructure, connectivity, and support, and that federation members include universities and Crown Research Institutes using secure single sign-on access to research services and online resources. Additional official-site records state that REANNZ seamlessly connects with more than 120 networks globally and expose international network-update material. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a research-network-supported environment with visible national-network operations, institutional participation, identity-federation surfaces, and international research-network links, while deeper topology, routing design, and institution-specific dependency structures remain only partly visible.

## Regional & International Connectivity Infrastructure

New Zealand’s outward-facing connectivity is publicly visible across payments, domain and peering systems, aviation, maritime surfaces, and research networking. Reserve Bank official records expose ESAS as the principal high-value settlement system and describe the Bank’s role in overseeing, operating, regulating, and supervising core payment systems. Payments NZ publicly exposes interbank clearing-system governance across BECS, CECS, HVCS, and Settlement Before Interchange. InternetNZ and registry records publicly expose .nz domain administration and the definitive register for .nz names, while Vital publicly exposes APE and WIX as shared exchange points for participating networks. These are direct public indicators of payment, naming, and interconnection continuity.

Additional outward-facing layers are visible through aviation, maritime, and research-network systems. CAA airspace materials expose the Auckland Oceanic Flight Information Region and New Zealand-administered airspace responsibilities. Airways publicly exposes air-navigation management across New Zealand airspace. Public port-company surfaces expose distributed maritime nodes, and REANNZ publicly exposes global research-network connectivity. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a regional and international interconnection environment through specific payment, domain, exchange, aviation, maritime, and research-network systems, without converting those interfaces into geopolitical, island-nation, remote-country, trade, or strategic-region narratives.

## Distributed Territorial Continuity Infrastructure

Public evidence supports normalization of New Zealand as both a Wellington-centered and territorially distributed continuity environment. Wellington concentration is visible through the Public Service Commission, central-government organisation listings, NEMA, Maritime New Zealand, and digital-government leadership structures. Auckland concentration is visible through Auckland Council’s publicly exposed local-board layer, Auckland peering infrastructure, Auckland Airport public surfaces, the Marine Pollution Response Centre identified in Maritime New Zealand records, and major port and telecommunications surfaces. This establishes visible administrative and infrastructure concentration in more than one metropolitan node.

At the same time, continuity is not confined to Wellington or Auckland. Local Government Commission material exposes 11 regional councils and 67 territorial authorities across the country. NZTA records expose state-highway totals across both the North Island and South Island and their linkage to local-road networks. KiwiRail publicly links North Island and South Island road and rail networks through the Interislander service. Publicly visible airport, port, and emergency-management surfaces extend across Wellington, Auckland, Christchurch, Queenstown, Tauranga, Lyttelton, Otago, CentrePort, GeoNet, MetService, and 16 regional CDEM group structures. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of New Zealand as a distributed territorial continuity environment with overlapping administrative, transport, port, airport, digital, and emergency layers across both main islands and multiple regional nodes.

## Structural Constraints & Evidence Boundaries

This file is public-source-bound. Private-network visibility is incomplete across telecommunications carriers, enterprise networks, banking relationships, government-contractor environments, airport systems, and port systems. Cyber-operational visibility is incomplete beyond the public existence of NCSC, CERT NZ, digital-government leadership surfaces, Privacy Commissioner functions, and published public guidance and reporting mechanisms. Commercial-topology visibility is incomplete for private carrier arrangements, retail and wholesale telecom dependencies, private peering, bank-to-bank dependency mapping, port-terminal commercial structures, freight-routing arrangements, and backend service-provider relationships. Infrastructure-dependency visibility is incomplete for power-system contingencies, telecom-power interdependence, cloud-service dependencies, airport and port interdependencies, and internal government service dependencies. Operational visibility is incomplete for non-public continuity plans, reserve arrangements, incident playbooks, failover designs, and institution-specific capacity management.

Telecom and internet visibility is incomplete beyond public regulatory material, InternetNZ registry-facing surfaces, and publicly exposed APE and WIX information. Port and terminal commercial visibility is incomplete beyond port-company and regulator-facing surfaces. Payment-provider topology visibility is incomplete beyond the public existence of ESAS, RBNZ oversight roles, and Payments NZ clearing-system governance. Research-network topology visibility is incomplete beyond public REANNZ descriptions, federation surfaces, and internationally facing summaries. Rural-service topology visibility is incomplete across broadband, local transport, and local service-delivery networks. Several relevant official pages were dynamically rendered, bot-protected, thinly exposed, or inconsistently retrievable at review time, including parts of RBNZ, InternetNZ, NZTA, Digital Government, Maritime New Zealand, NCSC, and some airport surfaces. Bounded observability therefore applies throughout. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. Hidden-capability inference is prohibited. Geopolitical inference is prohibited. Tourism and national-brand inference are prohibited. Disaster-resilience inference is prohibited. This evidence layer does not infer undocumented resilience, undisclosed surveillance architecture, non-public cyber capability, non-public infrastructure dependencies, or deeper commercial or interconnection structure beyond what public sources directly expose.
