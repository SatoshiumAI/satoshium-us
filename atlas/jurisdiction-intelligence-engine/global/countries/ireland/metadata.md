# Ireland — Metadata Layer

## Metadata Scope

- This file records metadata for Ireland derived strictly from `evidence.md`, `signals.md`, and `trust-dimensions.md`.
- This metadata is structural only and describes classification, topology, continuity, interoperability, infrastructure orientation, concentration, and dependency characteristics.
- No rankings, scores, readiness levels, or Atlas surface assignments are made in this file.
- All metadata statements are renderer-safe and bounded to prior Atlas layers.

## Jurisdiction Type

- Sovereign European nation-state.
- Island-state operational environment.
- Dublin-centered operational environment.
- EU-interoperable infrastructure environment.
- Transatlantic connectivity environment.
- UK-adjacent continuity environment.

## Geographic Orientation

- Atlantic-facing island-state geography.
- Dublin concentration as the densest visible operational coordination zone in the public infrastructure record.
- EU-member territorial and regulatory environment.
- Northern Ireland adjacency through bounded documented operational interfaces.
- Maritime and aviation dependence for external movement and continuity.
- Cross-border interaction corridors shaped by electricity, gas, rail, payments, and digital interoperability rather than extensive land-border infrastructure.

## Infrastructure Orientation

- Multimodal continuity structure spanning ports, airports, road networks, rail systems, energy networks, telecommunications, government platforms, and interoperable payments.
- Dublin-centered concentration across freight, aviation, rail orientation, exchange infrastructure, data-centre presence, and multiple administrative and digital layers.
- Maritime and aviation continuity structure supporting island-state movement and external linkage.
- Cable, exchange, and interconnection orientation across digital and energy infrastructure.
- Layered digital, payment, logistics, and energy coordination rather than single-sector dependence.
- Distributed national support around a dominant capital-region operating environment.

## Administrative Coordination Characteristics

- gov.ie-centered public-service access environment.
- Build to Share administrative architecture through Government Cloud, Government Networks, gov.ie, and the Government Data Centre programme.
- Inter-agency digital coordination shaped by Connecting Government 2030 principles including digital-by-default delivery, cloud-first adoption, stronger data reuse, and the once-only principle.
- Shared administrative platform structure rather than fully isolated department-facing service environments.
- Distributed service delivery supported by common digital and network infrastructure layers.

## Identity & Service Characteristics

- MyGovID-linked public-service access environment.
- PSC-linked service continuity through SAFE registration, Public Services Card issuance, and MyGovID verification.
- Reusable identity coordination across multiple public-service access points.
- Linked credential and service-access structure rather than separate identity and service-entry layers.
- Identity-enabled continuity bounded to documented administrative access and verification functions.

## Payment & Financial Characteristics

- SEPA-interoperable euro-payment environment.
- TARGET-participating settlement environment.
- TIPS-participating instant-payment environment.
- Central Bank-coordinated payment and settlement structure.
- EU-linked payment interoperability across retail payments, high-value payments, securities settlement, collateral movement, and instant payments.
- Euro-payment continuity reinforced through Eurosystem participation rather than a closed domestic payment perimeter.

## Telecommunications Characteristics

- Dublin interconnection concentration through INEX switching centres, Dublin-area Equinix facilities, Aqua Comms routes, and HEAnet's Dublin Core Ring.
- INEX participation as a neutral domestic exchange layer with dominant Dublin presence and secondary Cork support.
- Aqua Comms connectivity across Irish Sea and transatlantic cable systems.
- National Broadband Plan expansion through combined commercial rollout and state intervention in non-commercial areas.
- Cable, cloud, exchange, and backbone-network interaction within a concentrated metropolitan compute environment.
- Metropolitan compute concentration combined with nationally distributed broadband-extension activity.

## Transportation & Logistics Characteristics

- Dublin-centered multimodal coordination across port, airport, rail, and road layers.
- Airport, rail, port, and road synchronization centered on the capital-region logistics environment.
- Maritime-supported continuity through Dublin Port, Rosslare Europort, Port of Cork, and Shannon Foynes.
- Aviation-supported continuity through a dominant Dublin Airport gateway with Cork Airport as secondary support.
- Distributed port-support structures around a dominant Dublin freight concentration.
- Island-state logistics orientation sustained through layered maritime, aviation, rail, and road systems rather than extensive overland continuity corridors.

## Energy & Industrial Characteristics

- SEM-participating electricity environment.
- Interconnector-supported continuity through East West, Greenlink, and Celtic interconnection structures.
- Electricity and gas integration across national transmission, gas supply, and generation-support layers.
- EirGrid-coordinated electricity system and market-operation environment.
- ESB infrastructure support through national transmission assets and cross-border/cross-channel connectivity.
- External energy linkage structures connecting Ireland with Northern Ireland, Great Britain, and continental Europe.

## EU, UK & Cross-Border Characteristics

- EU interoperability through SEPA, TARGET services, TIPS participation, and EU-linked digital policy alignment.
- All-island electricity coordination through the Single Electricity Market.
- SONI and EirGrid coordination through joint SEMO operation.
- Dublin-Belfast transport continuity through the Enterprise rail service.
- Cross-border infrastructure interfaces across electricity, gas, rail, and bounded operational continuity layers.
- UK-adjacent continuity through Great Britain-linked electricity interconnectors and gas pipeline structures.
- Interoperability beyond jurisdiction boundaries as a recurring continuity mechanism rather than a single-border dependency.

## Disaster-Response & Continuity Characteristics

- NECG-coordinated whole-of-government continuity environment.
- Met Éireann warning systems providing tiered national weather-alert support.
- Flood-management continuity through OPW-led flood information, mapping, and planning layers.
- Cyber-response coordination through NCSC and CSIRT-IE structures.
- Multi-agency synchronization across transport, fuel, ports, emergency services, health, agriculture, and weather-linked disruption contexts.
- Operational continuity supported by warning, coordination, cyber, and flood-management layers rather than isolated incident channels.

## Data Infrastructure Characteristics

- Dublin compute concentration through data-centre, exchange, cable, and backbone-network co-location.
- Exchange, cable, cloud, and government-platform continuity as overlapping data-infrastructure layers.
- Government cloud infrastructure and shared public-service data infrastructure through Build to Share components.
- Public and private infrastructure interaction across government platforms, commercial colocation, domestic exchange points, and submarine cable systems.
- Transatlantic data continuity through Aqua Comms cable participation and Dublin-centered interconnection infrastructure.
- Concentrated but multi-layered data continuity environment.

## Research & Knowledge-Network Characteristics

- HEAnet participation as the national education and research network layer.
- Research-network interoperability through links to peer research and education networks in Europe, the USA, and wider international communities.
- Academic-network continuity through island-wide backbone coverage and national network persistence.
- International knowledge-network linkage as a distinct public-sector network layer alongside commercial telecommunications infrastructure.

## Regional & International Connectivity Characteristics

- EU integration across payments, digital-policy alignment, and electricity interoperability structures.
- UK connectivity through gas pipelines, electricity interconnectors, and Dublin-Belfast rail continuity.
- Transatlantic cable participation through Aqua Comms systems connected to Dublin.
- Aviation and logistics interoperability through primary and secondary airports together with multiple outward-facing ports.
- Energy interconnection continuity through UK-facing and France-facing electricity links and gas-network dependency on external interfaces.
- Payment-system interoperability through SEPA, TARGET services, and TIPS participation.

## Cross-System Characteristics

- Dublin concentration with distributed national support is the dominant recurring topology pattern.
- Island-state continuity is maintained through layered maritime, aviation, digital, payment, gas, and electricity systems.
- Interoperability functions as continuity support across payments, electricity markets, digital administration, and external network linkage.
- Maritime and aviation continuity compensate for the limited role of land-border infrastructure in wider national movement and access.
- Energy, payment, digital, and logistics layers interact as overlapping continuity systems rather than fully separate infrastructure domains.
- Concentration-with-distribution patterns are visible through a dominant Dublin core combined with regional ports, Cork exchange support, Cork Airport, and multiple external interconnection paths.

## Dependency Characteristics

- Dublin concentration dependencies are visible across freight, aviation, rail orientation, exchange infrastructure, compute presence, and administrative platform concentration.
- Maritime and aviation continuity dependencies remain central to external movement and broader national continuity.
- Energy interconnection dependencies are visible across electricity interconnectors, SEM-linked coordination, and gas-system linkage to Great Britain and Northern Ireland.
- External connectivity dependencies extend across submarine cables, payment interoperability, gas infrastructure, and cross-jurisdiction energy systems.
- Bounded observability remains a standing characteristic across private networks, back-office systems, emergency procedures, commercial data-centre operations, and parts of national infrastructure visibility.

## Structural Constraints

- Incomplete infrastructure visibility remains a standing constraint in the public source record.
- Bounded operational observability limits full visibility into real-time transport, energy, telecom, payment, cyber, and administrative operating conditions.
- Uneven regional visibility is present because public documentation shows strong Dublin concentration and selected regional nodes without providing uniform detail across all regions.
- Incomplete public operational access limits visibility into private backbone arrangements, commercial facility operations, emergency procedures, and some government back-office systems.
- Metadata therefore describes observable structural characteristics only and not a complete operating inventory.
