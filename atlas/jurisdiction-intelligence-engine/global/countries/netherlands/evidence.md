# Netherlands — Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- The Port of Rotterdam Authority states that the port of Rotterdam is the largest port in Europe and that it manages, operates and develops the Rotterdam port and industrial area while ensuring safe and expedient shipping services.
- AMS-IX states that its Amsterdam platform is one of the largest hubs for internet traffic in the world and that the exchange operates as a neutral member-based association.
- ASML states that its global headquarters and biggest R&D and manufacturing site are in Veldhoven, in the Brainport Eindhoven region, and that it provides the lithography hardware, software and services used to mass produce patterns on silicon.
- European Commission rail-deployment materials identify the Netherlands within the Rhine-Alpine, North Sea-Mediterranean and North Sea-Baltic corridors, with documented routes starting from Amsterdam and Rotterdam toward Germany and Belgium.
- Benelux Union treaty materials document a formal regional cooperation framework in which the Benelux countries cooperate with other EU member states and regional groupings.

## Economic and Infrastructure Coordination Anchors

- The Port of Rotterdam Authority states that it manages, operates and develops the Rotterdam port and industrial area and is responsible for safe and expedient shipping services.
- AMS-IX states that it houses infrastructure in several secure data centres and offers identical services across multiple Amsterdam-area points of presence, including Amsterdam, Schiphol-Rijk, Haarlem and Rotterdam-area facilities.
- Official Schiphol cargo materials surfaced via official site search results state that Schiphol handled 1.5 million tons of cargo in 2024 and maintained a position among Europe’s top five cargo hubs.

- Gasunie states that the Hydrogen Network Netherlands will connect five industrial clusters to each other, to other countries, and to hydrogen storage and import locations, using mainly existing and partly new pipelines.

- ASML’s Veldhoven materials place the site in Brainport Eindhoven and state that more than 200 companies on Eindhoven’s High Tech Campus are responsible for nearly 40% of Dutch patent applications.

## Digital Governance and Public Infrastructure

- DigiD states that it allows users to identify themselves online with the government, educational institutes, healthcare institutions and pension funds, using login methods including the DigiD app, SMS verification, username and password, and identity-card-based login for particularly privacy-sensitive data.
- DigiD states that the service is provided by Logius, which ensures the availability, correct operation, continuity and security of DigiD.
- Government.nl states that DigiD and eHerkenning are the Dutch national eID schemes recognised under eIDAS and that recognised Dutch eIDs can be used with public service providers in other EEA countries, while recognised foreign eIDs can be used with Dutch public services.
- data.overheid.nl identifies itself as the data register of the Dutch government, and official site materials state that it provides an overview of open and shared data published by the Dutch government and supports government organisations in opening data.
- NCSC materials state that the National Cyber Security Centre gives direction in the cyber domain, anticipates threats, connects partners and strengthens the cybersecurity system, while acting simultaneously as implementation coordinator, knowledge and expertise centre, and national and sectoral CSIRT.
- Government.nl eIDAS materials document EU interoperability structures through eIDAS recognition, European login pathways and cross-border use of recognised Dutch and foreign eIDs.

## Financial and Regulatory Coordination Environment

- Government.nl states that the Dutch financial sector is well-positioned in Europe, that the Netherlands has traditionally been a desirable location for major financial institutions, and that Dutch knowledge, expertise, connections and business cultures form a link between the eurozone and other financial centres in Europe and beyond.
- The same Government.nl materials state that the Ministry of Finance works to make the Netherlands a world financial centre and identifies support structures including the Innovation Room and Holland Financial Centre for institutions that wish to locate in the Netherlands.
- ECB materials on the international role of the euro state that, in early 2021, equity trading of EU-listed corporates saw an abrupt relocation from the City of London mainly to Amsterdam.
- ECB clearing materials state that, in the first three years after Brexit, euro-area CCPs achieved a modest increase in market share across euro-denominated clearing services, while ECB speeches also note that significant euro-denominated derivatives clearing remained concentrated outside the EU.
- DNB states that it is the Dutch central bank, prudential supervisor and resolution authority.
- DNB materials state that the ECB supervises all large banks in Europe and that DNB contributes to European banking supervision; DNB also states that it works in the European Single Resolution Mechanism.
- AFM materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Dutch Authority for the Financial Markets supervises the conduct of the entire financial market sector and carries out conduct-of-business supervision within EU financial-market frameworks.
- DNB states that the InnovationHub is a joint information desk of DNB, AFM and ACM for firms with questions about supervision and regulation relating to innovative financial products and services, and that it does not function as a regulatory sandbox that waives legal requirements.
- AFM Brexit materials surfaced via official site search results state that British financial institutions without an EU licence may no longer provide services in the Netherlands from 1 January 2021 onward and that firms seeking to continue services in the Netherlands need authorisation within an EU Member State.

## Energy and Climate Coordination Platforms

- TenneT materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Dutch high-voltage grid is connected through interconnectors with Germany, Belgium, Great Britain and Norway.
- TenneT states that, as grid operator for the Netherlands and a large part of Germany, it owns and manages 25,000 kilometres of high-voltage cables.
- TenneT Programme 2030 materials state that the Dutch offshore-wind roadmap provides for more than 11 GW of offshore wind farms to be built and connected to land by 2030.
- TenneT offshore materials surfaced via official site search results state that offshore production capacity is planned to grow from 4.7 GW to 23 GW by 2033.
- Gasunie states that the Hydrogen Network Netherlands connects industrial clusters with each other, with hydrogen storage facilities, production sites and surrounding countries, primarily using existing pipelines.
- Port of Rotterdam and Gasunie materials state that the first section of the national hydrogen network starts in Rotterdam, linking the Second Maasvlakte and Pernis.
- TenneT states that the COBRAcable has interconnected the Dutch and Danish high-voltage grids since 2019 through a 700 MW submarine cable around 325 kilometres long.
- Government.nl states that global warming effects are increasingly evident in the Netherlands, that sea-level rise and extreme weather create particular risks because much of the country lies below sea level, and that the Delta Programme coordinates flood safety, freshwater security and spatial adaptation in a low-lying delta environment.

## Semiconductor and Advanced Technology Anchors

- ASML states that it is an innovation leader in the semiconductor industry and that it provides chipmakers with the hardware, software and services needed to mass produce patterns on silicon through lithography.
- ASML states that its Veldhoven headquarters is its biggest R&D and manufacturing site and houses global corporate functions together with the European ASML Global Support Center.
- ASML’s EUV materials state that work to industrialize EUV lithography began in 1994 with a semiconductor-industry coalition including ASML and that EUV development and production facilities were opened at the Veldhoven headquarters in 2009.
- ASML states that its headquarters, located in Veldhoven, comprises over half of all ASML employees.
- ASML’s Veldhoven materials place the site in Brainport Eindhoven and describe the region as a high-tech manufacturing and design hotspot with more than 200 companies on Eindhoven’s High Tech Campus.
- Government.nl published a parliamentary letter in 2023 on Dutch efforts to achieve a strong semiconductor ecosystem in geopolitically challenging times, documenting national policy attention to the semiconductor base.
- European Commission materials state that the European Chips Act entered into force on 21 September 2023 to reinforce the semiconductor ecosystem in the EU, ensure supply-chain resilience and reduce external dependencies.

## Connectivity and Data Infrastructure

- AMS-IX states that its Amsterdam platform has been operating at the core of the internet for 30 years and is one of the largest hubs for internet traffic in the world.
- AMS-IX states that connecting to its Amsterdam platform allows internet service providers, telecom companies and cloud providers to route global IP traffic in an efficient, fast, secure, stable and cost-effective way.
- AMS-IX colocation materials document a distributed Amsterdam-area exchange footprint across multiple points of presence, including Equinix AM1/2, AM3, AM5, AM6 and AM7, Digital Realty sites, NorthC Amsterdam, Nikhef, Haarlem and Rotterdam-area facilities.
- Google Cloud states that its Netherlands region, europe-west4, is located in Eemshaven and that Dedicated Interconnect services from that region are accessible from Equinix Amsterdam AM3 and AM5.

- The documented connectivity surface combines AMS-IX exchange infrastructure, Amsterdam-area colocation sites and a Google Cloud region in the Netherlands.

## Multilateral and Legal Coordination Participation

- Government.nl states that promoting the development of the international legal order is a permanent objective of Dutch foreign policy and that the Netherlands hosts a large number of international legal bodies.
- ICJ materials surfaced via official site search results state that the seat of the International Court of Justice is at the Peace Palace in The Hague and that the Court has had its seat there since 1946.
- Government.nl states that the Netherlands is the host State for the International Criminal Court, and ICC materials state that the Court’s headquarters are in The Hague, the Netherlands.
- PCA materials state that the Peace Palace was constructed to serve as the headquarters of the Permanent Court of Arbitration and that the PCA’s headquarters and main hearing venue remain there.
- Europol states that it is headquartered in The Hague, the Netherlands, and its contact materials identify its visitor address on Eisenhowerlaan in The Hague.
- Eurojust states that it is the European Union Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation and that it operates as a hub based in The Hague, the Netherlands, where national judicial authorities work together on serious organised cross-border crime.

## Cross-Border Corridor Integration

- Benelux Union treaty materials state that the Benelux countries cooperate with other EU member states and regional groupings, providing a formal cross-border coordination framework linking the Netherlands with Belgium and Luxembourg.
- European Commission materials on the Netherlands state that the Rhine-Alpine corridor starts from the Amsterdam and Rotterdam harbours and runs south-east toward Germany, including Düsseldorf and Cologne.
- The same European Commission materials state that the North Sea-Mediterranean corridor starts from Amsterdam and runs toward Belgium via Antwerp.
- European Commission corridor materials state that the Rhine-Alpine corridor crosses the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany, Switzerland and Italy and connects the North Sea ports of Rotterdam and Antwerp with major economic centres in the Rhine-Ruhr and Rhine-Main-Neckar regions.
- These corridor materials document Dutch gateway links with Germany, Belgium and onward continental freight routes.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- The official source set used here documents Dutch exchange infrastructure, commercial cloud-region presence, public digital identity systems and EU-linked digital frameworks, but does not identify an independent Dutch sovereign hyperscale compute stack.
- The same source set repeatedly places Dutch digital identity, transport, semiconductor and financial-supervisory structures inside EU frameworks including eIDAS, ECB-led banking supervision, EU financial-market rules, TEN-T corridor planning and the European Chips Act.
- Port of Rotterdam and Schiphol materials document strong dependence on external maritime and air-logistics gateways; the source set therefore records structural exposure to external trade flows, while not providing a separate Dutch institutional metric for maritime chokepoint volatility.
- Government.nl states that much of the Netherlands lies below sea level, that the country is a low-lying delta prone to flooding, and that sea-level rise, extreme rainfall, drought and freshwater pressure are ongoing structural climate risks.
- TenneT and Gasunie materials document that electricity interconnection and hydrogen-network development are tied to surrounding countries and cross-border infrastructure during the energy transition.

## Summary

The current source set documents the Netherlands as a European logistics, semiconductor, digital exchange and legal-institutional coordination jurisdiction. The evidence places Rotterdam, Amsterdam, Veldhoven and The Hague within linked maritime, transport, semiconductor, digital-governance, financial-supervisory and multilateral legal structures that operate through Benelux, EU and wider cross-border infrastructure systems.
