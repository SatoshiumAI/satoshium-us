# Vietnam - Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- The National Public Service Portal states that it connects and provides information on administrative procedures and online public services, supports implementation, monitoring, evaluation, and handling of feedback and recommendations nationwide, and allows online fee payment through banks and payment intermediaries, documenting a nationally integrated public-service delivery environment.
- Ministry of Science and Technology materials state that people can create personal digital signatures directly through the Ministry of Public Security’s VNeID application and use them on public-service portals, documenting an identity-linked digital-signing layer tied to state service access.
- State Bank of Vietnam materials state that, under SBV direction, NAPAS is developing the national retail payment infrastructure across cards, accounts, QR codes, e-wallets, and mobile money, while separate SBV materials document ongoing cross-border QR payment links with Singapore and previously established links with Thailand, Cambodia, and Lao PDR.
- Government digital-infrastructure planning materials state that by 2025 high-tech parks, concentrated IT parks, and research, development, and innovation centres are to have internet access at a minimum speed of 1 Gbps, while additional international telecommunications cable lines, national data centres, and green-standard large-scale data centres are to be developed.
- Ministry materials also state that Vietnam licensed commercial 5G deployment in April 2024, that Viettel and VNPT received 5G business licences, and that the national strategy expects 5G coverage across all cities, provinces, industrial parks, ports, and international airports by 2025.
- VNNIC states that the .VN country-code top-level domain is managed by VNNIC and that it handles policies and procedures for Internet address and AS number management, while VNNIC DNS materials document a national DNS estate using IPv6, DNSSEC, and Anycast.
- MOST materials surfaced via official site search results state that VinaREN had 55 member networks across 11 provinces and cities and was supporting telemedicine, distance learning, research collaboration, and weather and disaster-data exchange, documenting a national research-network layer.
- EVN materials state that the National Load Dispatch Center operates the national power system, while the seaport master plan states that Vietnam is developing international gateway ports and modern maritime infrastructure for large-volume and long-route freight movement.
- Cybersecurity materials state that VNCERT/CC coordinates nationwide incident response, that the National Cyber Security Centre supervises cyberspace, and that the Cybersecurity Emergency Response Center monitors and operates related technical systems and databases.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- The National Public Service Portal states that it connects and provides information on administrative procedures and online public services, supports implementation, supervision, evaluation, and handling of citizen and organisational feedback nationwide, and serves as a unified public-service access point at www.dichvucong.gov.vn.
- The same portal states that users can register one account for login, track the handling of administrative procedures, reuse previously stored information and documents, and query integrated data held in connected systems such as business registration, tax, and insurance databases.
- The portal also states that users can carry out procedures across multiple provinces and cities with one declaration and pay fees and charges online using bank accounts and payment intermediary accounts.
- Ministry of Science and Technology materials state that, as of October 28, users can create personal digital signatures directly through the Ministry of Public Security’s VNeID application.
- The same materials state that VNeID level 2 users can register for VNPT SmartCA signatures on the app with identities already verified by the Ministry of Public Security, eliminating the need to submit physical documents.
- The VNeID integration materials further state that the signature layer extends across a broad application estate, including the National Public Service Portal, the Ministry of Public Security’s Public Service Portal, tax systems, the State Treasury, provincial and municipal portals, and applications in banking, finance, insurance, education, and healthcare.
- State Bank of Vietnam materials quote the Deputy Prime Minister stating that Scheme 06 on development of applications for the population database, personal identification, and e-KYC is part of the national digital-transformation framework for 2022-2025 with a vision to 2030.
- Digital-infrastructure strategy materials state that digital-signature adoption among the adult population is targeted to rise from over 50 per cent by 2025 to over 70 per cent by 2030.
- The current source set therefore documents a digital-governance environment built around a national public-service portal, integrated administrative and database access, VNeID-linked digital signatures, and the use of population-data and identification infrastructure in broader digital transformation.

## Financial Infrastructure and Payment Systems

- State Bank of Vietnam materials state that, under SBV direction, NAPAS has been coordinating with member institutions and partners to develop the national retail payment infrastructure.
- The same NAPAS materials state that this infrastructure provides domestic and international financial-switching services through cards, accounts, QR codes, e-wallets, and mobile money.
- NAPAS materials further state that transaction volumes and values on the NAPAS system increased year on year in 2023.
- The same materials describe high-volume transaction throughput and high-availability service levels across ATM/POS switching, online card payments, and NAPAS 247 fast-transfer services.
- NAPAS materials also state that public-service payment services were integrated across ministries, localities, and issuing organisations including banks and finance companies.
- State Bank of Vietnam materials state that NAPAS and Singapore’s NETS committed to implement cross-border QR-code payment connections between Vietnam and Singapore.
- The same cross-border payment materials state that NAPAS had already established QR payment connections with Thailand, Cambodia, and Lao PDR, while actively coordinating with additional countries including China, the Republic of Korea, and Japan.
- National Public Service Portal materials state that bank accounts and payment-intermediary accounts can be used to pay administrative fees and public-service charges online.
- State Bank of Vietnam materials on cashless-payment development state that the Government is implementing the national strategy on cashless payment promotion for 2021-2025 together with broader financial-inclusion and digital-data programs.
- The current source set therefore documents a payment environment centered on SBV-directed retail-payment infrastructure through NAPAS, high-volume domestic switching and fast-transfer rails, public-service payment integration, and regional QR cross-border expansion.
- The currently accessible source set does not provide equally direct English-language documentation for Vietnam’s full high-value central-bank settlement architecture, so this section remains anchored to the retail and interoperability layers directly evidenced here.

## Data Infrastructure and Digital Continuity

- Government ICT-infrastructure planning materials state that large-scale data centres are to be formed following green standards and energy-zone plans.
- The same planning materials state that national data centres are to be established and deployed to serve national databases and other shared databases according to law, and that at least three national multi-purpose data centres together with regional multi-purpose data centres are to be formed by 2025.
- The planning materials also state that all Government agencies are to use the cloud-computing ecosystem for digital-government services and that 70 per cent of Vietnamese businesses are to use services provided by domestic businesses.
- Digital-infrastructure strategy materials state that Vietnam aims to operate at least two new international undersea fibre-optic cables by 2025 and six new such cables by 2030.
- The same strategy materials state that Vietnam aims to form AI Data Centres and develop new data centres meeting international green standards by 2025, while building Hyperscale Data Centres and Digital Hubs by 2030.
- Ministry materials on the Tan Thuan CMC Data Centre state that the facility represents large-scale commercial data-centre and cloud-computing infrastructure investment in Vietnam.
- Those same materials state that the Tan Thuan site is CMC’s third data centre and is intended to support digital-transformation services for Government, city authorities, and domestic and foreign clients.
- The current source set therefore documents a mixed public and commercial continuity environment that combines planned national data-centre and cloud infrastructure, international cable expansion, and commercially deployed large-scale data-centre facilities.
- The same source set does not document a full nationwide inventory of all public and private data-centre capacity and should not be read as an exhaustive map of Vietnam’s compute estate.

## Research Network and Scientific Infrastructure

- MOST materials surfaced via official site search results state that, since 2009, VinaREN has supported international-scale telemedicine sessions, network-based learning, remote thesis presentation, and participation in global computing-network activities.
- The same materials state that VinaREN also served weather forecasting and natural-disaster warning functions by enabling Vietnamese hydro-meteorological organisations to connect to and download data from hydro-meteorology data centres in the United States, Canada, and the Asia-Pacific region.
- Those MOST materials further state that VinaREN had 55 member networks including research institutes, universities, hospitals, and major information centres across 11 provinces and cities.
- Additional MOST materials surfaced via official site search results state that Vietnam was one of five Asian countries benefiting from EC support under TEIN2 and that MOST approved and supported the TEIN2-VN project to develop the National Research and Education Network (VinaREN) in 2006.
- The current source set therefore documents a national research-and-education network with international linkage, scientific collaboration functions, and institutional membership spanning research, university, medical, and data-oriented organisations.
- Because direct extraction from some legacy MOST and VinaREN pages was unreliable in the current session, this section remains carefully limited to what official-site search surfaces clearly describe.

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- VNNIC states that .VN is the country-code top-level domain of Vietnam and that it is managed by the Vietnam Internet Network Information Center.
- VNNIC also states that its public service environment includes policies, regulations, and procedures for Internet address and AS number management.
- VNNIC DNS materials state that the Vietnam National Domain Name Server System operated by VNNIC consists of eight server clusters, including six domestic clusters in Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, and Da Nang, and two international clusters, with more than 100 points of presence across major cities on five continents.
- The same DNS materials state that the system employs IPv6, DNSSEC, and Anycast so users can resolve .vn domain names over both IPv4 and IPv6 by connecting to the nearest authoritative DNS server.
- VNNIC materials surfaced via official site search results list internet statistics, DNS query statistics, reports on Vietnam internet resources, and VNIX member and IP-member surfaces, documenting an institutional reporting and exchange-governance layer around internet resources.
- VNNIC report materials surfaced via official site search results state that from 2020 VNNIC deployed DNS root-server clusters in Vietnam connected to VNIX, with the stated aim of reducing dependence on international connections and increasing access speed to local-domain services.
- The current source set therefore documents a nationally administered ccTLD, address and AS-number governance surface, an authoritative DNS estate with domestic and international clusters, and a VNIX-linked exchange and reporting environment.
- The current source set does not provide a full contemporary peering census or complete real-time exchange-traffic map and should not be interpreted as such.

## Telecommunications and Connectivity Infrastructure

- Ministry materials state that the ministry officially licensed deployment of 5G services on April 11, 2024 and that Viettel and VNPT received business licences and spectrum-use permissions.
- The same materials state that the two providers committed to deploying telecommunications networks and providing service no later than one year after licensing, together with substantial additional station investment within two years of being licensed.
- Those 5G materials state that service rollout would begin in urban district areas and then expand further, while also targeting industrial parks and smart factories where low latency and high density are relevant.
- Separate ministry materials on the 5G spectrum auctions state that Viettel won the B1 band and VNPT won the C2 band, marking Vietnam’s first successful spectrum allocation in the radio-frequency field in 15 years.
- The ministry also states that 2G shutdown efforts were being accelerated, that network operators had been told to stop subscriptions of new 2G-only phones, and that a large 2G-only subscriber base remained as of late 2023.
- ICT-infrastructure planning materials state that by 2025 all high-tech parks, concentrated IT parks, and research, development, and innovation centres are to have internet access at a minimum speed of 1 Gbps.
- The same planning materials state that Vietnam is to deploy and invest in two to four additional international telecommunications cable lines by 2025.
- Digital-infrastructure strategy materials state that 5G coverage is expected to reach all cities, provinces, high-tech zones, research and development centres, industrial parks, stations, ports, and international airports by 2025, and 99 per cent of the population by 2030.
- Ministry IPv6 materials state that Vietnam targeted substantial additional IPv6 adoption in 2024, that IPv6 usage had already reached majority levels by the end of the prior year, that important national Internet infrastructure had been operating with IPv6, and that broadband subscriber usage was already at national scale.
- The current source set therefore documents a connectivity posture combining commercial 5G rollout, spectrum allocation, 2G sunset planning, high-capacity fixed connectivity targets, new international cable expansion, and large-scale IPv6 adoption.

## Energy and Grid Coordination Infrastructure

- EVN materials state that the National Load Dispatch Center was established in 1994 with the core task of commanding and operating the power production, transmission, and distribution system in the national power system.
- The same materials state that this role is intended to ensure the national power system operates continuously, reliably, and safely while maintaining technically and economically coordinated operations.
- EVN materials also identify the May 1994 energization of the North-South 500 kV transmission line as a historical milestone in unification of the power system across the country.
- The same account states that, following later restructuring, the National Load Dispatch Center became a unified dispatch organisation with national and regional levels and responsibility for directly operating major power plants and high-voltage transmission infrastructure.
- The current source set therefore documents a nationally coordinated grid-operations and dispatch layer with long-standing institutional continuity.
- The cited materials support grid-governance and system-integration claims without establishing broader claims of industrial or energy primacy.

## Logistics and Maritime Connectivity Infrastructure

- The seaport master plan states that seaports are an important part of Vietnam’s maritime and socio-economic infrastructure and are treated as strategic national infrastructure within the plan.
- The same plan states that Vietnam aims to develop coordinated maritime infrastructure that ensures safety and security, effectively connects transport modes, and supports maritime transport in large-volume freight and international freight movement.
- The plan further states that Vietnam will focus on developing international gateway ports capable of receiving large-tonnage ships travelling on long sea routes.
- The same document also states that Vietnam will promote application of fourth-industrial-revolution technologies in construction, management, and exploitation toward green seaports, energy saving, and efficient shoreline and water-surface resource use.
- ICT-infrastructure planning materials state that the postal network is to be developed with sufficient backup capacity to ensure an intact and uninterrupted supply chain in emergencies, and that three to five regional postal centres are to be built by 2030.
- Digital-infrastructure strategy materials state that 5G coverage is expected to reach ports and international airports by 2025, documenting explicit telecommunications buildout to transport and logistics nodes.
- The current source set therefore documents a maritime and logistics environment centered on international gateway-port development, modernization and digitization goals, and linkage between transport nodes and national digital infrastructure.
- The cited materials do not provide a complete multimodal throughput map across ports, airports, rail, and inland logistics systems.

## Cybersecurity and Digital Resilience Coordination

- Ministry materials state that the Cybersecurity Emergency Response Center was established in Hanoi with branches in Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang.
- The same materials state that the center was formed through reorganisation of the Vietnam Computer Emergency Response Center and the Vietnam Information Security Testing Center.
- Those materials state that the center is tasked with coordinating responses to security incidents and verifying information security nationwide; monitoring and operating related data systems, databases, and technical systems; and preventing spam emails and messages.
- The same materials also state that the center is responsible for testing and evaluating information safety for hardware, software, information systems, and information-safety management and operation systems in accordance with law.
- Ministry cybersecurity materials state that Vietnam was placed among the higher-scoring countries assessed by ITU in the 2024 Global Cybersecurity Index.
- Official ministry materials surfaced via official site search results state that VNCERT, now VNCERT/CC, functions as the focal unit coordinating emergency support throughout the nation and that the National Cyber Security Centre supervises cyberspace.
- The same search-result materials also indicate that supervision centers monitor key national infrastructure under the Ministry of Public Security and Ministry of National Defence.
- The current source set therefore documents a cyber-resilience environment combining national incident response, national cyberspace supervision, system monitoring, and security testing and evaluation functions.
- The source set does not provide equally detailed evidence for all-sector certification regimes or the full private-sector cybersecurity governance landscape.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- The accessible State Bank of Vietnam source set clearly documents NAPAS retail-payment infrastructure, public-service payment integration, and QR-based cross-border links, but it does not provide equally direct current English-language visibility into the full high-value interbank settlement stack.
- VinaREN evidence is supported by official MOST search-result surfaces, but direct extraction from some legacy MOST and VinaREN pages was unreliable in the current session, so claims here remain tightly bounded to what those official surfaces clearly describe.
- VNNIC materials clearly document .VN management, Internet address and AS-number governance, DNS infrastructure, and VNIX-linked reporting surfaces, but the current source set does not provide a complete real-time national peering or exchange-membership map.
- Data-infrastructure materials document formal planning targets and selected commercial-facility buildout, but they do not amount to a full inventory of all sovereign, private, or foreign-operated compute capacity in Vietnam.
- Telecommunications materials document licensing, strategy targets, IPv6 adoption, and some cable-expansion goals, but they do not independently provide a complete market-share or nationwide service-quality view across all operators.
- Cybersecurity materials document VNCERT/CC, NCSC, and the Cybersecurity Emergency Response Center, but do not provide equally detailed evidence for all-sector certification, audit, and resilience structures.
- Logistics materials document seaport and postal planning, but the current source set does not provide a full multimodal throughput and corridor map across maritime, air, rail, and inland logistics systems.
- These constraints describe limits of the current cited evidence only and should not be treated as negative judgments about Vietnam.

## Summary

- The current source set documents Vietnam as a digital-governance jurisdiction built around the National Public Service Portal, population-data and e-KYC programs, and VNeID-linked digital-signature infrastructure.
- It documents a retail-payment environment directed by the State Bank of Vietnam through NAPAS, with fast-transfer, QR, public-service-payment, and cross-border QR connectivity layers.
- It documents planned and emerging data infrastructure through national data-centre policy, cloud-adoption targets, undersea-cable expansion, and commercial data-centre buildout.
- It documents research-network, DNS, registry, IPv6, and connectivity infrastructure through VinaREN, VNNIC, 5G licensing, and broadband and cable-planning materials.
- It documents nationally coordinated grid operations, seaport-planning and modernization structures, and national cyber-response structures through EVN, the seaport master plan, VNCERT/CC, NCSC, and the Cybersecurity Emergency Response Center.
- The cited evidence supports these infrastructure characterizations without assigning readiness tiers, corridor status, routing authority, or broader Atlas interpretation beyond the institutional materials.
