# Switzerland — Evidence Layer

## Scope

This file records only country-level structural anchors documented for Switzerland that are sufficient to support later Atlas signal derivation.

It does not perform a complete canton-by-canton infrastructure survey and does not assign trust posture, routing role, coordination tier, investment rank, or Atlas surfaces.

## Federal governance and cantonal distribution

Switzerland has documented multi-level federal governance distributed across the Confederation, cantons, and communes.

The current Switzerland package preserves evidence of:

- a federal system operating across three political levels: the Confederation, the cantons, and the communes
- a documented count of 26 cantons within the Swiss federal structure
- direct-democracy mechanisms that allow citizens to vote on specific issues at different political levels
- a governance model that keeps powers as close as possible to communes and delegates powers upward only when necessary
- a seven-member Federal Council operating as a collegial executive body whose decisions are made by consensus

These materials document a distributed governance environment with formal subnational variation and consensus-oriented federal structure.

## Data-protection and information-governance framework

Switzerland has documented national privacy legislation and an independent federal supervisory authority for data protection and freedom of information.

The current Switzerland package preserves evidence of:

- the new Federal Act on Data Protection taking effect for Swiss companies from 1 September 2023
- legislative updates that improved the processing of personal data and granted new rights to affected persons
- explicit inclusion of privacy-by-design and privacy-by-default principles in the current implementation guidance
- treatment of genetic and biometric data as sensitive data in the current implementation guidance
- the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner as an independent authority responsible for data protection and freedom-of-information matters
- the FDPIC role in supervising whether federal bodies and private companies process personal data lawfully
- the FDPIC role in mediating access-to-document disputes under the Freedom of Information Act
- explicit FDPIC guidance that the Federal Data Protection Act is directly applicable to AI-supported data processing

These materials document an active federal privacy and information-governance framework rather than an absence of regulatory structure.

## Regulated digital-asset and financial-market infrastructure environment

Switzerland has documented legal and supervisory structures for distributed-ledger-based financial-market activity.

The current Switzerland package preserves evidence of:

- a FINMA licensing regime for DLT trading facilities under Article 73a ff. and related provisions
- a requirement that a DLT trading facility be structured as a legal entity under Swiss law with its registered office and head office in Switzerland
- FINMA responsibility for licensing, supervision, and change approvals for DLT trading facilities
- the DLT Act entering into force in Switzerland on 1 August 2021 as the legal basis for the new market-infrastructure category
- FINMA licensing of BX Digital as the first DLT trading facility in Switzerland in 2025
- FINMA documentation that the DLT framework supports innovation without compromising stability and security for market participants
- SIX Digital Exchange approval to operate integrated trading, settlement, and custody infrastructure for digital securities in a fully regulated environment
- continued digital-bond issuance activity on SDX exceeding CHF 1 billion on-platform by 2024
- Project Helvetia Phase III settlement activity in Swiss-franc wholesale central-bank digital currency within the current SDX source set

These materials document a regulated digital-asset market-infrastructure environment with visible supervisory and exchange-layer implementation.

## International Financial Governance Hosting

Switzerland has documented hosting presence for major international monetary and financial-governance institutions, especially in Basel.

The current Switzerland package preserves evidence of:

- the Bank for International Settlements having its head office in Basel, Switzerland
- BIS headquarters operations at Centralbahnplatz 2 in Basel
- the BIS role as an institution owned by 63 central banks representing economies accounting for about 95% of world GDP
- the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision operating through the BIS as the primary global standard setter for the prudential regulation of banks
- the Basel Committee providing a forum for regular cooperation on banking supervisory matters across 45 members from 28 jurisdictions
- the Financial Stability Board being established as a not-for-profit association under Swiss law
- the Financial Stability Board Secretariat being located in Basel, Switzerland and hosted by the BIS
- formal concentration in Basel of international standard-setting and financial-stability coordination functions rather than only domestic Swiss banking institutions

These materials document international institutional hosting and financial-governance concentration in Basel without assigning routing authority or topology interpretation.

## National transmission-grid backbone and cross-border electricity coordination

Switzerland has documented national transmission-grid ownership and real-time operating visibility through Swissgrid.

The current Switzerland package preserves evidence of:

- Swissgrid ownership of the Swiss transmission grid
- a transmission network of more than 6,700 kilometres operating at 380 and 220 kilovolts
- a documented footprint of 147 substations within the transmission-grid layer
- Swissgrid responsibility for regular maintenance, upgrades, and appropriate expansions to keep the grid available
- real-time operational monitoring across around 40,000 metering points and roughly 10,000 measured values processed within seconds
- explicit Swissgrid responsibility for balancing supply and demand around the clock
- documented coordination with European partners because electricity flows cross national borders

These materials document a nationally operated grid backbone with cross-border electricity coordination rather than an isolated domestic-only grid structure.

## Rail, freight, and transalpine corridor infrastructure

Switzerland has documented dense rail infrastructure and transalpine freight programs tied to national and cross-border logistics.

The current Switzerland package preserves evidence of:

- SBB Infrastructure describing itself as the backbone of Switzerland with operational control of the railways
- around 10,000 SBB Infrastructure staff supporting daily passenger and goods movement
- SBB company reporting daily transport volumes above one million passengers and substantial daily freight tonnage
- around 600 sidings connecting manufacturers and logistics centres directly to the SBB rail network
- SBB reporting that almost 90% of conventional rail goods transport uses these sidings
- Federal Office of Transport documentation that the New Rail Link through the Alps comprises the Gotthard, Lötschberg, and Ceneri base tunnels
- federal documentation that the NRLA shortens travel times and promotes rail freight
- federal documentation that Switzerland built the NRLA and 4-metre corridor to allow more freight trains to travel under easier conditions on the Gotthard route
- SBB reporting that, after the Gotthard Base Tunnel timetable change, up to four freight trains per hour in each direction could operate and that the tunnel marked a major step toward a flat rail route through the Alps for international transit traffic

These materials document strong rail-logistics continuity and transalpine freight-corridor relevance in the current source set.

Switzerland has documented neutral internet-exchange infrastructure for local traffic exchange.

The current Switzerland package preserves evidence of:

- SwissIX describing itself as the largest Internet Exchange Point in Switzerland in terms of port capacity and bandwidth
- SwissIX operating as a non-profit association

These materials document local interconnection presence and neutral exchange infrastructure without establishing a broader European backbone or routing-dominance claim.

## Research, AI, and high-performance compute infrastructure

Switzerland has documented nationally visible AI, research, and high-performance compute infrastructure anchored by ETH Zurich, EPFL, and CSCS.

The current Switzerland package preserves evidence of:

- the ETH AI Center serving as ETH Zurich's central hub for artificial intelligence across foundations, applications, and implications
- the Swiss AI Initiative as a large open-science and open-source effort for AI foundation models launched through the ETH AI Center and EPFL AI Center partnership
- the Swiss National AI Institute launched by ETH Zurich and EPFL to steer national-level research, education, and innovation in trustworthy and open AI
- Swiss National AI Institute plans for a national foundation model supported by supercomputer infrastructure of more than 10,000 next-generation AI GPUs
- CSCS Alps operating as a general-purpose compute and data research infrastructure open to researchers in Switzerland and internationally
- Alps support for dedicated virtual clusters including weather forecasting, user-lab work, and machine-learning and AI workloads
- Alps geo-distribution across CSCS in Lugano, EPFL in Lausanne, PSI in Villigen for data archiving, and ECMWF in Bologna for meteorological-data access
- Alps sustained peak performance of 434.9 petaflops and BF16 performance of 10 exaflops in the current CSCS system specification

These materials document a multisite national research and compute environment with explicit AI and data-infrastructure relevance.
