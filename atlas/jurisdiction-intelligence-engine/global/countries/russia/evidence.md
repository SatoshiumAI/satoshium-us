# Russia — Evidence Layer

## Scope & Methodology

This evidence layer is derived from publicly visible sources only, principally official Russian government portals, regulators, public operators, public institutional websites, and stable official-site search-visible records where direct extraction was limited by dynamic rendering, translation gaps, or retrieval instability. It documents observable administrative structures, digital public-service and identity-enablement surfaces, payment and settlement rails, communications and domain-management structures, electricity-grid institutions, transport and aviation administration, maritime-port surfaces, emergency-management institutions, data-governance supervision, research-and-education networking, and territorially distributed continuity visible in Russia.

The interpretation here is intentionally operational and infrastructure-oriented. It does not rank Russia, assign readiness or trust scores, infer hidden capability, infer surveillance capability, infer military or intelligence posture, create geopolitical interpretation, create sanctions interpretation, or convert public infrastructure into strategic-power, sovereignty, or state-capacity narratives beyond what the sources directly expose. Public evidence is treated as bounded and incomplete by default, and wording remains renderer-safe by limiting claims to what public sources directly expose.

## Administrative & Public-Service Infrastructure

Publicly visible state infrastructure in Russia presents as a Moscow-centered federal administrative environment with distributed territorial governance. Russian Government materials publicly describe the Government’s structure as including the cabinet, deputy prime ministers, federal ministers, and executive bodies including federal ministries, departments, agencies, services, and oversight bodies. Federation Council materials separately expose Russia’s federal constituent-entity structure through region-level listings. This is direct public evidence of a multi-level federal administrative environment rather than a purely capital-only service structure.

Public service continuity is also visibly layered across federal institutions and territorial units. Government directories expose ministries and agencies at the federal level, while official regional directories expose constituent-entity public bodies. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a Moscow-centered but territorially distributed administrative environment with standing federal ministries and visible regional public-service layers.

## Identity & Digital-Service Infrastructure

Russia’s publicly visible digital-service layer is anchored by the unified public-services portal and related identity-authentication infrastructure. Catalogue materials for the Common Government Services Portal describe the portal of public services, officially the unified portal of state and municipal services, as a federal state information system that provides information on state and municipal services provided by authorities of the Russian Federation. Ministry of Digital Development materials further state that e-government tools are designed to reduce administrative barriers and the need to personally visit state institutions, and that citizens can apply for approvals and permits over the Internet.

A second visible layer exists through the Unified System of Identification and Authentication (ESIA). Official Ministry materials publicly expose ESIA as a dedicated state information system, and related Ministry records describe simplified identification flows redirecting users to ESIA authentication surfaces. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a digital public-service environment with a unified services portal and a standing government identity-authentication layer, without inferring broader undocumented national identity-verification or monitoring architecture.

## Payment & Financial Infrastructure

Russia’s publicly visible payment infrastructure is organized around the Bank of Russia. Bank of Russia materials state that as of 1 January 2026 the national payment system comprised 31 payment systems and 353 money transfer operators, and that the Bank of Russia ensures the stability and smooth functioning of the national payment system while providing the necessary infrastructure for cashless settlements in the Russian Federation. This is direct public evidence of a central-bank-coordinated payment and settlement environment.

A second visible layer exists through named payment rails and nationally operated payment instruments. Bank of Russia materials state that the Bank established NSPK in 2014, that NSPK launched Mir cards and processes domestic payments made in Russia with cards of international payment systems, and that more than 476.5 million Mir cards had been issued as of 1 January 2026. The Bank further states that as of 1 May 2026 there were 226 banks using SBP and that the system had processed 49.1 billion transactions worth 259.7 trillion rubles. Separate Bank of Russia materials describe the Faster Payments System as enabling instant 24/7 transfers using only a mobile phone number, and the National Payment System Department describes the Bank of Russia payment system as the main channel for settlements in rubles between Russian banks while also referencing express transfers, the Faster Payments System, and the Financial Messaging System. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a payment-settlement interoperability environment with a central-bank-run payment system, a domestic card-processing environment, instant-transfer infrastructure, and interbank settlement rails, without converting those rails into financial-power or sanctions-resilience narratives.

## Telecommunications & Connectivity Infrastructure

Telecommunications in Russia are publicly visible as a state-regulated communications environment. Ministry of Digital Development materials state that the ministry is responsible for state policy and legal regulation in information technologies, telecommunications including radio-spectrum usage and conversion, and postal services. Separate Ministry materials state that one of the important goals in ICT is to create conditions for the spread of modern communication networks in all localities of the country and to introduce quality standards for telecommunications services and Internet access. This is direct public evidence of a centrally administered but territorially distributed telecom-policy environment.

A second visible layer exists through country-code domain administration. The Coordination Center for TLD .RU/.РФ publicly states that it is the national registry and administrator of the .RU and .РФ domains, that it develops registration rules, accredits registrars, supports the technological development of the domains’ infrastructure, and works to guarantee the integrity, continuity, stability, sustainability, and security of the national Russian internet segment’s operations. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a telecom-domain continuity environment with visible communications policy, country-code domain administration, registrar accreditation, and explicit continuity language around DNS infrastructure, while private backbone topology, carrier-to-carrier commercial arrangements, and enterprise-network design remain only partly visible.

## Electricity & Energy Infrastructure

Russia’s publicly visible electricity environment is organized through very large grid institutions. Rosseti materials state that the group ensures power supply across 82 regions, operates 2.56 million kilometers of power transmission lines, and has 581,000 substations with 877 GVA of transformer capacity. Rosseti further states that more than 80 percent of all electric power generated in the country is transmitted through the group’s grids. This is direct public evidence of a very large territorially distributed grid environment.

A second visible layer exists through the formal structure of the Unified Energy System. System Operator materials publicly describe the Russian power-system complex as consisting of the Unified Energy System of Russia together with five technologically isolated territorial power systems, and separate system descriptions publicly identify large pooled regional systems such as the Unified Power System of the East. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as an electricity continuity environment with a unified high-level grid framework, large regional operating segments, and very substantial transmission infrastructure, while fuller balancing practice, reserve structure, fuel dependence, and non-public contingency arrangements remain only partly visible.

## Transportation Infrastructure

Russia’s publicly visible transport environment is organized through the Ministry of Transport. Ministry materials state that the ministry is a federal executive authority responsible for policy and legal regulation in civil aviation, use of airspace, sea and inland water transport, rail, road, urban and industrial electric transport, road infrastructure, transport security, aircraft registration, and traffic management in organizational and legal terms. This is direct public evidence of a nationally coordinated multimodal transport environment.

A second visible layer exists through Russian Railways. Official Russian Railways materials describe the company as the national freight and passenger rail carrier and the owner of common-use railway infrastructure. The same materials state that Russian Railways accounts for 46 percent of the freight turnover of the entire Russian transport system, including pipelines. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a transport environment with visible ministry coordination and a very large publicly visible rail backbone embedded inside a broader multimodal transport structure.

## Aviation Infrastructure

Russia’s publicly visible aviation environment is organized through both the Ministry of Transport and the Federal Air Transport Agency. Ministry materials explicitly include civil aviation and the use of airspace within the ministry’s scope. Rosaviation’s official resources publicly expose regulation, management, contacts, accreditation certificates for laboratories and testing centers, certification centers, and aviation laws and advisory materials. This is direct public evidence of a standing aviation-administration and certification environment.

Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as an aviation environment with a visible federal ministry remit, a dedicated air-transport agency, public contact and correspondence channels, and openly surfaced regulatory and certification materials, while fuller airport-operating dependencies, airline commercial arrangements, and non-public air-navigation resilience measures remain only partly visible.

## Maritime & Port Infrastructure

Russia’s maritime environment is publicly visible through both ministry remit and port-operator surfaces. Ministry of Transport materials explicitly include sea and inland water transport within federal transport regulation. Rosmorport materials publicly list the seaports of the Russian Federation in which the enterprise operates, exposing activity across Far Eastern, Caspian, and Azov-Black Sea basin branches and naming ports such as Vladivostok, Vostochny, Nakhodka, Astrakhan, Olya, Makhachkala, Novorossiysk, Rostov-on-Don, Tamany, Tuapse, and others. This is direct public evidence of a multi-basin port environment rather than a single-coast maritime structure.

Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a distributed maritime continuity environment with multiple basin-level port surfaces and public operator visibility across numerous named seaports, while terminal-level commercial arrangements, private logistics dependencies, and fuller river-sea integration details remain only partly visible.

## Disaster-Response & Emergency Coordination Infrastructure

Russia’s public emergency-coordination layer is visible through EMERCOM. EMERCOM materials state that the ministry develops or presents draft laws and regulations concerning civil defence, protection of the population and territories against emergency situations, fire protection, safety on water bodies, and the operation of the Single State Disaster Management System. This is direct public evidence of a formal nationwide emergency-management and civil-defence institution.

A second visible layer exists through crisis-management structures and territorial offices. EMERCOM structure materials expose a Main Department of the National Crisis Management Center, a Civil Defence and Population Protection Department, a Rescue Units Department, a Fire Main Office, an Aviation and Aircraft Rescue Technologies Office, and an Information Technologies and Communications Department. Separate EMERCOM materials publicly expose contacts for Main Offices in the subjects of the Russian Federation and lists of single helplines. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a disaster-response and emergency-coordination environment with a central ministry, a crisis-management center, specialized rescue and fire functions, and territorially distributed subject-level coordination surfaces.

## Cybersecurity & Data Infrastructure

Russia’s publicly visible cyber and data-governance layer is anchored by personal-data law and communications-supervision institutions. Roskomnadzor’s English personal-data portal states that pursuant to Article 23 of Federal Law No. 152-FZ of 27 July 2006 On Personal Data, the competent authority for protecting the rights of personal-data subjects in the Russian Federation is a federal executive authority performing control and supervision in information technology and telecommunications. This is direct public evidence of a formal state data-protection and supervisory layer.

A second visible layer exists through published legislative materials. Roskomnadzor’s legislation portal publicly exposes federal laws and government regulations relating to personal-data protection, including materials on biometric personal-data media and requirements for protection within personal-data information systems. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a cyber and data-governance environment with visible supervisory authority and legal-regulatory structures around personal data and information systems, while deeper cyber-operational tooling, enforcement practice, and non-public defensive or offensive capability remain outside public observability.

## Research & Education Network Infrastructure

Russia’s publicly visible research-and-education networking layer is anchored by RUNNet. RUNNet materials publicly describe the Russian University Network as a major federal research and education network operating since 1994. Separate RUNNet network materials publicly describe developed data centers in Moscow and St. Petersburg hosting server and network equipment supporting basic RUNNet services and national and regional research and educational Internet projects. This is direct public evidence of a standing academic-network environment.

A second visible layer exists through the services RUNNet publicly describes. RUNNet states that it provides telecommunications connectivity for high-performance computing resources of Russian universities and provides access to official websites of educational authorities, federal educational resources, universities, research institutes, and educational organizations of various levels. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a research-network-supported environment with federal academic networking, data-center support, university and research connectivity, and public-sector educational web-service support.

## Regional & International Connectivity Infrastructure

Russia’s regional and international connectivity is publicly visible through payments, rail, domains, research networking, and ports. Bank of Russia materials publicly describe national payment infrastructure with named interoperable rails. The Coordination Center for TLD .RU/.РФ publicly states that it represents the national registry in international and regional Internet organizations. RUNNet publicly supports research and educational Internet projects and high-performance university connectivity. These are direct outward-facing indicators across financial, internet-governance, and academic-network surfaces.

Additional outward-facing continuity is visible through transport and maritime interfaces. Russian Railways publicly describes terminal and storage services linked to regular container trains and international land freight linkages, while Rosmorport publicly lists seaports across multiple basins from the Far East to the Caspian and Azov-Black Sea systems. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a regional and international connectivity environment through named port, rail, internet-governance, academic-network, and payment interfaces, without converting those interfaces into geopolitical or strategic-corridor narratives.

## Distributed Territorial Continuity Infrastructure

Public evidence supports normalization of Russia as both a Moscow-centered and territorially distributed continuity environment. Administrative concentration is visible through the Russian Government, federal ministries, the Bank of Russia, the Ministry of Digital Development, EMERCOM, the Ministry of Transport, and Roskomnadzor-facing data-governance surfaces. This establishes a clear concentration of policy and administrative authority around Moscow-centered federal institutions.

At the same time, continuity is not confined to a single administrative node. Rosseti publicly states that its companies ensure power supply in 82 regions. EMERCOM exposes Main Offices and helplines across the subjects of the Russian Federation. Digital-policy materials explicitly describe the goal of extending modern communication networks to all localities of the country. Rosmorport exposes named seaports across multiple maritime basins. RUNNet describes data-center and networking support beyond a single location. Public evidence therefore supports normalization of Russia as a distributed territorial continuity environment with overlapping administrative, payment, telecom, energy, transport, emergency, maritime, and research-network systems extending well beyond the capital core.

## Structural Constraints & Evidence Boundaries

This file is public-source-bound. Telecom visibility is incomplete, especially for backbone routes, inter-operator commercial arrangements, private enterprise connectivity, traffic-engineering practice, and non-public network management. Private-network visibility is incomplete across banking, government-contractor, logistics, port, airport, and enterprise environments. Cyber-operational visibility is incomplete beyond public laws, portals, supervisory descriptions, and published regulatory materials. Commercial-topology visibility is incomplete for terminal operations, rail-freight dependencies, airport-system dependencies, private data-center use, and bank-to-bank backend arrangements. Infrastructure-dependency visibility is incomplete for reserve-power arrangements, fuel logistics, private cloud dependence, and non-public continuity planning.

Several relevant official pages were dynamically rendered, translation-limited, intermittently unstable, or more accessible through official-site search-visible text than direct extraction at retrieval time, including some Russian Government, Ministry, railway, RUNNet, and Roskomnadzor pages. Bounded observability therefore applies throughout. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. Hidden-capability inference is prohibited. Geopolitical inference is prohibited. This evidence layer does not infer undocumented resilience, undisclosed surveillance architecture, non-public cyber capability, or deeper commercial or interconnection structure beyond what public-source materials directly expose.
