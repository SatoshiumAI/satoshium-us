# Estonia - Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- RIA materials state that Estonia’s state information system includes X-tee as a secure Internet-based data-exchange environment and the eesti.ee state portal as a national environment for secure access to state-related information and services, supporting Estonia’s position as a digital-governance and data-interoperability coordination jurisdiction.
- RIA materials state that the State Authentication Service and State SSO allow institutions to support ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID, and European Union notified eID, placing Estonia inside a documented digital-identity and cross-border e-service interoperability environment.
- Eesti Pank states that it is the central bank of the Republic of Estonia and a member of the European System of Central Banks and the Eurosystem, while European Commission materials state that Estonia joined the euro area on 1 January 2011.
- Eesti Pank states that it manages the Estonian component of the TARGET payment system, including TIPS, and provides cash-leg settlement services for T2S-linked securities settlement.
- RIA cyber-security materials state that, under the Cybersecurity Act, the relevant national cyber authority is RIA’s Cyber Security Centre, NCSC-EE, and that CERT-EE operates as the national CERT.
- ETAIS, NORDUnet, Estonian Internet Foundation, Elering, Nord Pool, and related official materials place Estonia inside Baltic and European research-network, naming, payment, cyber, and grid-integration structures without assigning corridor authority or topology role.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- RIA states that X-tee is a technological and organizational environment enabling secure Internet-based data exchange between information systems, and that members can use data services from other members on the basis of agreements.
- RIA states that X-tee uses authentication, multi-level authorisation, high-level log processing, and encrypted and signed data traffic, and that both private-sector and public-sector entities can join X-tee.
- RIA states that Estonia’s electronic identity environment uses a single electronic identity across multiple carriers, including ID-cards, residence cards, diplomatic IDs, Mobile-ID, digital ID, and an e-Resident’s digital ID.
- RIA states that ID-cards and Mobile-ID can be used for authentication in most public- and private-sector e-services, and that eID supports authentication, digital signatures, encryption, and related access functions.
- RIA states that the State Authentication Service (TARA) and State SSO (GovSSO) support ID-card, Mobile-ID, Smart-ID, and European Union notified eID through the eIDAS-node infrastructure in public online services.
- RIA also states that e-service providers should note that Smart-ID identification does not allow differentiation between Estonian residents and non-residents in the way an e-resident certificate does.
- RIA states that the eesti.ee state portal offers private individuals and businesses secure and convenient access to state-related information and services, and after secure login provides personalised access to registry data, document-validity information, national-mailbox messages, prescriptions, real-estate and vehicle data, company data, licences, and authorisation-management tools.
- e-Residency programme materials surfaced via official site search results state that e-Residency is a government-issued digital identity for non-residents that provides access to Estonia’s e-services and business environment.

## Financial Infrastructure and Settlement Systems

- Eesti Pank states that it is the central bank of the Republic of Estonia, a member of the European System of Central Banks, and responsible for implementing Eurosystem decisions in Estonia after euro adoption.
- European Commission materials state that Estonia joined the euro area on 1 January 2011 and that Estonian kroon banknotes and coins can be exchanged for euros at Eesti Pank for an unlimited period of time.
- Eesti Pank states that payment and settlement systems important for the Estonian economy include card-payment systems, interbank payment systems, and the securities settlement system.
- Eesti Pank states that, as a member of the ESCB, it manages the Estonian component of the TARGET payment system, which includes TIPS, and is a part owner of the TARGET2-Securities platform together with other Eurosystem central banks.
- Eesti Pank states that T2 is the real-time gross settlement system for euro payments between central banks and commercial banks, while TIPS is the Eurosystem TARGET service for settling instant payments in accounts opened at the central bank.
- Eesti Pank states that banks offering instant-payment services in Estonia joined TIPS in 2021.
- Eesti Pank states that T2S is the Eurosystem’s securities-settlement platform and that the Bank of Estonia is responsible for cash-leg settlement services for T2S transactions and technical support for connected market participants.
- Eesti Pank states that Nasdaq CSD operates the Estonian securities settlement system alongside Latvian and Lithuanian systems under separate national jurisdictions, while T2S-eligible securities are settled on the Eurosystem platform in central bank money.
- Eesti Pank states that it participated with the European Central Bank and other euro-area central banks in technical experimentation around a possible digital euro, including an experiment in which payments in digital money were tested between people with digital identities from Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, and Spain.
- Eesti Pank states that the decision to introduce the digital euro has not yet been taken and depends on European Union legislation before consideration by the Governing Council of the European Central Bank.

## Data Infrastructure and Digital Continuity

- RIA states that X-tee is an interoperable ecosystem for data exchange in which one member describes shared data and other members can use that data based on an agreement, rather than through a single central database.
- RIA states that X-tee’s security model includes authentication, multi-level authorisation, log processing, and encrypted and signed data traffic.
- RIA states that, when very specific and sensitive data are exchanged through X-tee, the principle followed is that the data owner controls the data throughout the whole process and that the X-Road technology offers secure data exchange rather than taking over ownership of the data.
- RIA states that the eesti.ee portal provides logged-in users with visibility into data held in various registries and includes a data tracker for operations performed with their data, as well as a consent service for sharing data with private companies.
- Brussels MFA materials surfaced via official site search results state that Luxembourg hosts Estonia’s data embassy and frame it as part of Estonia’s e-state cooperation with Luxembourg.
- The current source set therefore documents Estonia’s data continuity posture through distributed data-exchange architecture, registry-linked service delivery, and a documented cross-border data-embassy arrangement, without supporting broader claims beyond the cited continuity structures.

## Research Network and Compute Infrastructure

- HARNO materials surfaced via official site search results state that the agency develops and manages the Estonian Education and Research Data Communication Network EENet and represents Estonia in international organisations uniting academic data-communication networks.
- NORDUnet states that Estonian NREN EEnet operates an optical network connection between Tallinn and Helsinki, allowing direct connection between NORDUnet and EEnet.
- NORDUnet states that, through that connection, it provides global network peering access to EEnet and optical network capacity between EEnet and the GÉANT network.
- ETAIS states that the Estonian Scientific Computing Infrastructure is a pan-Estonian unified e-infrastructure offering cloud services, classical scientific computing, hosting, and management services for scientific web applications.
- ETAIS states that it provides computing resources, storage space, training, user support, and data services including secure storage, backup, and handling.
- ETAIS states that users can train models on local clusters and on the LUMI supercomputer in Finland, and that ETAIS also provides high-performance computing, e-infrastructure, and artificial-intelligence services.

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- Estonian Internet Foundation materials surfaced via official site search results state that the foundation handles management of Estonia’s top-level domain .ee and its sub-domains.
- Estonian Internet Foundation states that ICANN approved its re-delegation application to become the sponsoring organisation of the .ee domain name and that it has fulfilled the duties of administration of the .ee top domain since 2010.
- Estonian Internet Foundation states that DNSSEC has been available in the .ee zone since January 2014 and that .ee uses a registry-registrar model in which DNSKEY information is forwarded from registrars to the registry.
- Estonian Internet Foundation statistics materials publish the number of DNSSEC-protected .ee domains and the percentage of DNSSEC domains.
- The current source set directly documents .ee registry governance and DNS continuity controls more clearly than broader exchange or peering infrastructure, and does not provide a comparably strong institutional basis for wider internet-exchange claims.

## Telecommunications and Digital Infrastructure

- RIA states that the eesti.ee state portal integrates and mediates official information and personalised e-services to both individuals and businesses.
- RIA states that, after secure login with ID-card, Smart-ID, Mobile-ID, or EU eID, the eesti.ee portal provides users with access to a broad set of state-related digital services and data views.
- RIA states that GovSSO allows institutions to add both national and European Union cross-border authentication methods with session management to e-services, while TARA provides a common authentication service for public online services.
- RIA states that the software for GovSSO and TARA is open source and available in the e-government building-blocks repository.
- RIA states that X-tee is built around platform independence, multilateralism, availability, and standardisation, documenting a coordinated digital infrastructure layer for service interoperability across systems.
- The current source set documents integrated digital-service, identity, and interoperability infrastructure directly, while providing less direct institutional detail on standalone telecom-routing or broadband-governance claims.

## Energy and Grid Integration Infrastructure

- Elering materials surfaced via official site search results state that Elering is Estonia’s independent electricity and gas transmission system operator and that its main task is to ensure high-quality energy supply to Estonian consumers.
- Elering states that it signed the framework agreement with Fingrid for construction of the EstLink 2 direct-current connection, later became co-owner of EstLink 1 with Fingrid, and entered into a contract to establish the third Estonian-Latvian power connection.
- Elering states that the Baltic States submitted an application in 2018 to connect to the Continental European power system.
- Elering states that the cornerstone of the Estonian-Finnish gas connection Balticconnector was laid in 2018.
- Elering states that Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania synchronised their electricity systems with the Continental Europe Synchronous Area on 9 February 2025, with support from the European Union and ENTSO-E.
- Nord Pool states that Estonia constitutes one bidding area in the day-ahead power market.

## Cybersecurity and Digital Resilience Coordination

- RIA states that, in Estonia, the national central cyber authority for the purposes of the NIS framework is RIA, more specifically its Cyber Security Centre, NCSC-EE.
- RIA states that the tasks of the Cyber Security Centre include 24/7 cyberspace monitoring, cyber-incident response, protection of critical infrastructure, cyberspace analysis and prevention, surveillance, creation of information-security standards, cyber-crisis management and exercises, and coordination of research and development.
- RIA states that CERT-EE is Estonia’s national CERT and that it has one team member on duty 24/7.
- RIA states that CERT-EE deals with incidents in AS8240 and AS56588 and in most state and municipal networks in the .ee domain, serves as the single point of contact for foreign CERTs and CSIRTs, and coordinates activities in elevated situations.
- RIA states that CERT-EE is a department of the Cyber Security Centre NCSC-EE and lists affiliations including FIRST, GÉANT, Trusted Introducer, and the CSIRT Network established under the NIS Directive.
- CCDCOE materials surfaced via official site search results state that the NATO Cooperative Cyber Defence Centre of Excellence operates in Tallinn, Estonia, and conducts major cyber-defence exercise activity there.

## EU and Baltic Corridor Integration

- RIA states that TARA and GovSSO support European Union notified eID and that the purpose of eIDAS is to facilitate the cross-border use of e-services.
- RIA states that users can log in to the eesti.ee state portal with EU eID, placing part of Estonia’s public-service access layer inside the European cross-border identification framework.
- European Commission and Eesti Pank materials place Estonia’s currency and central-bank functions inside the euro area, the European System of Central Banks, and the Eurosystem.
- NORDUnet states that EEnet connects from Tallinn to Helsinki and receives optical capacity onward to the GÉANT network, placing Estonian research-network infrastructure inside wider Nordic and European academic-network structures.
- Elering states that Baltic synchronisation with the Continental Europe Synchronous Area was carried out in cooperation with Latvian, Lithuanian, Polish, and wider Continental European transmission-system actors, with support from the European Union and ENTSO-E.
- Brussels MFA materials surfaced via official site search results state that Luxembourg hosts Estonia’s data embassy as part of e-state cooperation with Luxembourg.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- European Commission and Eesti Pank materials place Estonia’s currency and central-bank settlement functions inside the euro-area and Eurosystem framework rather than a standalone national monetary architecture.
- Eesti Pank materials place Estonia’s large-value, instant-payment, and securities-settlement infrastructure inside TARGET, TIPS, and T2S structures and Baltic CSD arrangements rather than a wholly separate domestic settlement stack.
- RIA materials place part of Estonia’s public-service authentication and interoperability surface inside European Union notified eID and eIDAS-node structures rather than a fully self-contained national cross-border identity framework.
- Brussels MFA materials surfaced via official site search results place part of Estonia’s digital continuity model in cross-border hosting through the Luxembourg data-embassy arrangement.
- HARNO, NORDUnet, and ETAIS materials document research-network connectivity and scientific compute infrastructure, but the current source set does not document an independent sovereign hyperscale compute stack.
- The current source set documents scientific computing and digital-service infrastructure, but it does not document a domestic semiconductor fabrication stack.
- Elering and Nord Pool materials place Estonia’s electricity and gas infrastructure within Baltic and wider European interconnection, synchronisation, and market-coupling systems.
- Estonian Internet Foundation materials document .ee governance and DNS continuity controls, but the current source set does not provide equally strong institutional evidence for broader national internet-exchange authority claims.

## Summary

The current source set documents Estonia as a digital-governance and data-interoperability coordination jurisdiction built around RIA-operated identity, authentication, portal, and data-exchange infrastructure; a Eurosystem settlement participant through Eesti Pank and TARGET services; a data-continuity and cyber-coordination environment with documented cross-border resilience structures; and a Baltic and European integration participant across research networking, energy synchronisation, and EU eID and euro-area frameworks. The evidence places Estonia inside nationally administered but strongly European-connected systems without supporting readiness tiers, routing authority, or broader Atlas interpretation beyond the cited institutional materials.
