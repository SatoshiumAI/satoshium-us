# Denmark — Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- The Agency for Digital Government states that Denmark operates a common public digital infrastructure that includes the National Citizen Portal, MitID, NemLog-in, Digital Post, and cross-border eID access through the Danish eID Gateway.
- Danmarks Nationalbank states that the Danish payments infrastructure processes and settles approximately DKK 732 billion on an average banking day in 2024 through the core IT systems of the financial infrastructure.
- Danmarks Nationalbank materials state that Danish payment and securities settlement are being consolidated on TARGET Services, including T2, TIPS, and T2S, and that the Danish krone is being connected directly to pan-European payment infrastructure.
- DeiC states that it connects researchers to advanced resources and secure knowledge sharing through reliable networks and services, while its research-network and HPC materials describe nationally coordinated network and compute infrastructure for Danish research.
- DIX states that the Danish Internet Exchange Point is operated in Denmark with four points of presence, while Punktum dk identifies itself as administrator for .dk and states that .dk has been managed by DIFO and Punktum dk since 1999.
- Energinet materials surfaced via official site search results state that Energinet is an independent state-owned company that owns and operates Danish energy infrastructure and that it expands and renovates Danish electricity and gas transmission grids and international interconnections.
- CFCS materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Centre for Cyber Security is Denmark’s national IT security authority, Network Security Service and National Centre of Excellence within cyber security.
- The current source set places Denmark inside EU, Nordic, and Baltic cross-border digital, payment, research-network, and energy-interconnection structures without assigning corridor authority or readiness ranking.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- The Agency for Digital Government states that borger.dk is the Danish public sector’s common portal and single point of access to information about public authorities and their online services.
- The Agency for Digital Government states that borger.dk contains around 800 information pages and showcases about 2,000 online self-services across the public sector, regardless of whether the service is offered by a national agency or a municipality.
- The Agency for Digital Government states that MitID is Denmark’s digital ID and is used to access public self-service solutions and online banking.
- The Agency for Digital Government states that MitID was developed through a collaboration between the public sector and the country’s financial institutions so that one identity solution can be used across public and private self-service solutions.
- The Agency for Digital Government states that NemLog-in is a log-on solution for authority self-service solutions across municipalities, regions and government, and that its single-sign-on function allows users to identify themselves once across multiple public self-service solutions.
- The Agency for Digital Government states that Digital Post is part of Denmark’s cross-governmental digital service infrastructure and enables secure digital communication between public authorities, citizens and businesses.
- The Agency for Digital Government states that the Danish eIDAS infrastructure, called the eID Gateway, was developed to give EU citizens and EU companies with an eID access to the Danish public digital infrastructure.
- The Agency for Digital Government also states that citizens with MitID can use digital self-service solutions in other EU/EEA countries through the Danish eID Gateway infrastructure.

## Financial Infrastructure and Payment Systems

- Danmarks Nationalbank states that the Danish payments infrastructure is a network of systems enabling consumers, companies and financial institutions to exchange payments and conduct securities transactions.
- Danmarks Nationalbank states that, on an average banking day in 2024, payments amounting to approximately DKK 732 billion are processed and settled through the core IT systems of the Danish financial infrastructure.
- Danmarks Nationalbank states that instant payments in Denmark are managed in TIPS, which is part of the European Central Bank’s TARGET Services, and that instant transfers can be made 24 hours a day, every day of the year.
- Danmarks Nationalbank states that it oversees TARGET DKK for large, time-critical payments, TARGET Services including T2, T2S and TIPS, the retail-clearing systems, ES-CPH settlement, and major payment instruments including Dankort, Betalingsservice and credit transfers.
- Danmarks Nationalbank states that, in March 2024, it signed the accession agreement for the Danish krone to join T2 and TIPS, and that with migration in 2025 payments and securities transactions in Danish kroner will be consolidated on TARGET Services.
- Danmarks Nationalbank states that the Danish krone will be the first non-euro area currency to handle both payments and securities transactions on TARGET Services.
- Danmarks Nationalbank states that, in January 2024, it decided to join a project with the European Central Bank and Sveriges Riksbank to introduce settlement of cross-currency instant payments in TIPS.
- Danmarks Nationalbank states that Denmark is closely linked to the euro area through the fixed exchange rate policy, payment systems and financial conditions, and that it is monitoring developments around a possible digital euro.

## Research Network, Security, and Compute Infrastructure

- DeiC states that it connects researchers to advanced resources and ensures secure knowledge sharing through reliable networks and services.
- DeiC states that universities, research institutions, regions, other public institutions and Danish companies with a significant research element can join the Danish Research Network.
- DeiC states that the research network functions as the internet provider for universities and research institutions and is based on two central routers in Ørestaden and Lyngby, both connected to corresponding routers from NORDUnet.
- DeiC states that each university has a connection to each of the two central routers and that the connections are routed differently across the country to ensure redundancy.
- DeiC states that the universities and DeiC collaborate on the establishment and operation of the national HPC landscape, while DeiC coordinates access and the universities handle operations and development.
- DeiC states that Danish researchers can access national facilities including DeiC Interactive HPC and DeiC Throughput HPC, and that Danish participation in EuroHPC gives access to European HPC facilities including LUMI in Finland.
- DeiC states that EuroCC Denmark helps businesses, public authorities and academia gain access to high-performance computers in Denmark and across Europe.
- DeiC Security / DKCERT states that it handles the security of the research network operated by DeiC, publishes warnings and information about risks and emerging security issues, and collaborates with public and private sectors, the research and education community, and international partners.
- Novo Nordisk Foundation materials state that the Danish Centre for AI Innovation (DCAI) was established together with EIFO and NVIDIA.
- Novo Nordisk Foundation materials state that Gefion, launched in October 2024, is Denmark’s first AI-ready supercomputer.
- Novo Nordisk Foundation materials state that researchers from Denmark’s public and private sectors can access Gefion as a state-of-the-art NVIDIA AI supercomputer for large-scale AI projects.
- Novo Nordisk Foundation materials state that Gefion is a public-private partnership infrastructure owned and operated through DCAI.
- Novo Nordisk Foundation materials state that the supercomputer is intended to accelerate research and innovation in fields including healthcare, life sciences, green transition and quantum computing.
- Novo Nordisk Foundation materials state that Gefion will have a high level of security, support Danish data sovereignty, and support projects involving sensitive data.

## Internet Exchange and Naming Infrastructure

- DIX states that the Danish Internet Exchange Point is a facility operated by i2 with four points of presence located at DTU in Lyngby, Interxion in Ballerup, Fuzion in Skanderborg, and GlobalConnect in Tåstrup.
- DIX states that the sites are interconnected so that participants at one site can peer with participants at the other sites or with the available route servers.
- DIX states that its purpose is to alleviate the exchange of data traffic between networks that constitute the Danish part of the global internet structure.
- DIX states that it was established in May 1994 and has most Danish internet providers connected.
- Punktum dk identifies itself as administrator for .dk.
- Punktum dk states that since 1999 all .dk domain names have been managed by Danish Internet Forum (DIFO) and Punktum dk.
- Punktum dk states that this administration created a strong digital infrastructure and frames the .dk domain as a safe and secure place to be.

## Energy, Grid, and Offshore Interconnection Infrastructure

- Energinet materials surfaced via official site search results state that Energinet is an independent state-owned company that owns and operates Danish energy infrastructure.
- The same Energinet materials state that Energinet ensures high security of supply in the electricity and gas sectors in Denmark and contributes to the green transition.
- Energinet infrastructure-project materials surfaced via official site search results state that Energinet expands and renovates the Danish electricity and gas transmission grids and international interconnections.
- Energinet materials surfaced via official site search results state that Energinet is the transmission system operator for both electricity and gas in Denmark and is responsible for operating and developing the backbone of Danish electricity and gas supply.
- Energinet materials surfaced via official site search results state that, as part of the Danish Energy Island project, 3 GW of offshore wind farms are to be established south of Bornholm in the Danish sector of the Baltic Sea, with generated power exported to Zealand and Germany.
- Energinet materials surfaced via official site search results state that Energinet and Germany’s 50Hertz entered into cooperation on a cable connection via Bornholm as a future energy island.
- Energinet materials surfaced via official site search results also state that Energinet and 50Hertz started tendering for key technologies for Bornholm Energy Island intended to bring 3 GW of green power to Danish and German consumers.
- Energinet DataHub materials surfaced via official site search results state that DataHub is a self-contained IT system owned and operated by Energinet.

- Danmarks Nationalbank states that Danish payments and securities settlement are being consolidated on the pan-European TARGET Services platform and that Denmark participates in TIPS-based cross-currency instant-payment work with the ECB and Sveriges Riksbank.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- Agency for Digital Government materials place parts of Denmark’s digital-identity interoperability inside the eIDAS and EU/EEA cross-border framework rather than a fully self-contained national identity environment.
- Danmarks Nationalbank materials place Danish payment and settlement infrastructure inside TARGET Services and TIPS linkages with the Eurosystem rather than a wholly separate standalone settlement stack.
- Danmarks Nationalbank materials state that a digital euro remains a possible future instrument under development at the European level and that no issuance decision has yet been taken.
- Energinet materials place major parts of Danish grid expansion and offshore-energy development inside cross-border interconnection structures involving Germany, Nordic balancing arrangements, and Baltic Sea export routes.
- DeiC and Novo Nordisk Foundation materials document national research-network, HPC and AI-compute capability, but the current source set does not document an independent Danish sovereign hyperscale compute stack.
- The current source set documents digital-governance, payments, research-network, exchange, naming, cyber, energy and AI-compute infrastructure, but it does not provide an official basis for assigning corridor authority, topology role, or institutional readiness beyond the cited materials.

## Summary

The current source set documents Denmark as a Nordic-Baltic connectivity jurisdiction with common public digital-governance coordination infrastructure, cross-sector identity infrastructure, pan-European payment-system integration, nationally coordinated research-network and compute infrastructure, domestic exchange and .dk naming administration, state-owned energy-grid and offshore interconnection infrastructure, national cyber-coordination functions, and emerging AI-compute capacity. The evidence places the Agency for Digital Government, Danmarks Nationalbank, DeiC, DIX, Punktum dk, Energinet, CFCS and DCAI-related institutional structures inside a nationally administered but strongly cross-border-connected environment without supporting readiness tiers, routing authority, or topology claims beyond the cited sources.
