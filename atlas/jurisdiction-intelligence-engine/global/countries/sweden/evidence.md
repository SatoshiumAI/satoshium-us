# Sweden — Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- DIGG states that it is responsible for Ena, the name of Sweden’s digital infrastructure, and that it coordinates e-identification, e-signatures, public-data access, and cross-border digital-government programs.
- Svenska kraftnät states that it maintains and develops the Swedish national grid, balances production and consumption, and operates a transmission system with international connections.
- Netnod states that it is a neutral and independent internet infrastructure organisation based in Sweden and that it operates the largest internet exchange in the Nordics alongside DNS, time, transport, and root-server services.
- Ericsson materials place the company’s global headquarters in Stockholm and document Swedish participation in telecommunications standardisation work through ETSI and 3GPP-related leadership materials.
- Sunet states that it operates, maintains, and develops Sweden’s university data network and provides national research-network, identity, and IT-security services linked to higher education and research.
- Government, Nordic-cooperation, CBSS, and Trafikverket materials place Sweden inside EU, Nordic, Baltic Sea, and ScanMed corridor coordination structures without assigning corridor authority or topology role.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- DIGG states that it is responsible for Ena, Sweden’s digital infrastructure, and that its work includes leading and coordinating a joint digital infrastructure for information exchange.
- DIGG states that it coordinates e-identification and e-signatures nationally and internationally.
- DIGG states that it is responsible for the Swedish eIDAS single point of contact, Sweden’s participation in the eIDAS Cooperation Network, and Sweden Connect, which includes the Swedish eIDAS node, the Technical Framework, and the General Register for secure eID transactions within Sweden.
- DIGG states that eID is used to verify identity online when using services, contacting authorities, and interacting with banks and other digital services.
- DIGG states that it is responsible for Min Myndighetspost and that dataportal.se indexes public and private datasets for broader reuse.
- DIGG states that it is the national coordinator for the DIGITAL programme and for the Single Digital Gateway, linking Swedish digital public-service coordination to EU interoperability structures.

## Financial Infrastructure and CBDC Exploration

- Riksbank materials state that the Riksbank is investigating the e-krona as a digital complement to cash and that the technical e-krona project was finalised in 2023.
- The same Riksbank materials state that the e-krona would complement rather than replace cash and that future work is focused on monitoring international CBDC developments, including work on a digital euro.
- Riksbank materials surfaced via official site search results state that the RIX system consists of RIX-RTGS for large-value and aggregated payments and RIX-INST for instant payments.
- Riksbank materials surfaced via official site search results state that RIX-INST settles instant payments between banks in real time, 24 hours a day and all year round in central bank money, using the Eurosystem’s TIPS platform.
- Riksbank materials surfaced via official site search results state that clearing organisations such as Bankgirot aggregate large numbers of account payments that are then exchanged in RIX.
- Riksbank materials surfaced via official site search results state that, from March 2024, Swish payments moved to settlement through RIX-INST.

## Research Network and Compute Infrastructure

- Sunet states that it operates, maintains, and develops Sweden’s university data network and serves higher-education institutions and other public organisations linked to research, higher education, and culture.
- Sunet states that it provides identity infrastructure, enhanced IT-security services, and international access to other e-infrastructures and associated resources.
- Sunet states that Sunet CD has 400 Gbit/s transmission capacity, fully encrypted data communication, and redundancy for Swedish higher-education institutions and affiliated cultural organisations.
- Sunet states that SunetC provides nationwide access to the internet and global research networks and can provide dedicated point-to-point and cross-border research-network connections.
- Sunet states that it represents Sweden in NORDUnet, the Nordic cooperation between the region’s national research and education networks.
- NAISS materials state that the National Academic Infrastructure for Supercomputing in Sweden maintains advanced computing, AI, and data services for Swedish research, is hosted by Linköping University, and is funded by the Swedish Research Council for 2023–2026.
- Linköping University and EuroHPC materials state that Linköping University, through NAISS, is hosting the EuroHPC supercomputer Arrhenius for researchers in academia and industry across Europe.

## Internet Exchange and Data Infrastructure

- Netnod states that it provides interconnection services, internet exchanges, time services, DNS services, and root-server operations.
- Netnod states that it was established in 1996 as a neutral and independent internet infrastructure organisation based in Sweden and that it runs the largest internet exchange in the Nordics.
- The Swedish Internet Foundation states that it is responsible for the Swedish top-level domain .se and the operation of the top-level domain .nu and that its charter includes ensuring a strong and secure internet infrastructure in Sweden.

## Energy and Grid Integration Infrastructure

- Svenska kraftnät states that the Swedish national grid consists of approximately 16,000 km of power lines, about 175 substations and switching stations, and connections to other countries.
- Svenska kraftnät states that it is Sweden’s electricity system operator and is responsible for balancing production and consumption in real time.
- Svenska kraftnät materials surfaced via official site search results state that the national grid transports electricity from wind, hydro, and nuclear generation into regional and local networks.
- Svenska kraftnät materials surfaced via official site search results place Sweden within the Nordic electricity-market structure, including four bidding areas and day-ahead price formation on the Nordic power exchange.
- Svenska kraftnät states that Aurora Line is a planned new 400 kV interconnector between Messaure in Sweden and Pyhänselkä in Finland, developed with Fingrid and classified as an EU Project of Common Interest.
- Svenska kraftnät states that Konti-Skan Connect is renewing the HVDC connection between Lindome in Sweden and Vester Hassing in Denmark together with Energinet.
- The Swedish Energy Agency states that Sweden’s energy system includes hydropower, wind power, solar, biomass, and nuclear-related inputs and that the system is undergoing transformation with conversion losses and changing generation shares.

## Cybersecurity and Civil Contingency Coordination

- Swedish Civil Defence and Resilience Agency materials surfaced via official site search results state that the agency is responsible for helping society prepare for major accidents, crises, and the consequences of war.
- The agency’s English materials include digital security and business-preparedness guidance within a wider civil-defence and resilience mandate.
- CERT-SE states that it is Sweden’s national CSIRT supporting Swedish society in managing and preventing IT-security incidents, with priority for essential services and critical infrastructure.
- CERT-SE states that it reacts to incidents, coordinates large-scale incident management, and supports affected organisations in mitigating and restoring function.
- CERT-SE states that it cooperates with agencies in the Swedish National Cyber Security Centre and serves as a contact point for corresponding functions in other countries.
- CERT-SE lists participation in the EU CSIRTs Network, European Government CERTs group, FIRST, TF-CSIRT/Trusted Introducer, and other national and international information-sharing forums.
- Swedish Civil Defence and Resilience Agency materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Swedish National Coordination Centre for cybersecurity research and innovation enables EU funding access for Swedish research institutes, companies, and public authorities.

## Advanced Manufacturing and Battery Infrastructure

- Northvolt states that Northvolt Ett in Skellefteå is a lithium-ion battery gigafactory in northern Sweden and describes it as Europe’s first homegrown gigafactory.
- Northvolt states that Northvolt Ett is powered by renewable electricity and that the site includes large-scale industrial battery production capacity in Sweden.
- Vinnova states that Batteries Sweden (BASE) is Sweden’s battery alliance for long-term battery research, intended to strengthen the Swedish battery research community across the entire battery value chain.
- Vinnova states that BASE links Uppsala University, Chalmers, KTH, RISE, cell manufacturers, automotive actors, material developers, software and hardware suppliers, and recycling companies.
- Government materials surfaced via official site search results state that the government instructed Business Sweden to coordinate efforts to set up large battery plants as part of the automotive industry’s green transition.
- The current source set documents battery-industrial coordination and research infrastructure, but does not document a complete domestic semiconductor fabrication stack.

## EU and Nordic Corridor Integration

- Government materials surfaced via official site search results state that Sweden is an EU member state and that the Government represents Sweden in the European Council and the Council of the European Union.
- DIGG states that it is the national coordinator for the DIGITAL programme and for the Single Digital Gateway, adding EU institutional linkage to Swedish digital-governance infrastructure.
- Nordic-cooperation materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Nordic Council of Ministers is the official body for intergovernmental cooperation in the Nordic region and that Sweden is part of that structure.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- DIGG and Government materials place Swedish digital identity, eIDAS interoperability, DIGITAL coordination, and the Single Digital Gateway inside wider EU digital-governance frameworks rather than a fully standalone national regime.
- Riksbank materials place payment modernisation in relation to the Eurosystem’s TIPS platform and continuing CBDC monitoring tied in part to broader European digital-currency developments.
- Svenska kraftnät materials document cross-border transmission links and Nordic power-market coupling, indicating that parts of Sweden’s electricity-market operation are structurally tied to neighbouring systems and shared market mechanisms.
- The current source set documents research networking, NAISS, EuroHPC participation, exchange infrastructure, and operator-hosted data-centre capacity, but does not document an independent Swedish sovereign hyperscale compute stack.
- The current source set documents battery-industrial and telecom-manufacturing coordination surfaces, but does not document a complete domestic semiconductor fabrication stack.
- The current source set documents nationally important infrastructure across a long north-south geography, including nationwide research-network redundancy and major northern industrial and energy assets, but does not provide a separate official basis for treating geography itself as an institutional ranking variable.

## Summary

The current source set documents Sweden as a Nordic digital-governance, telecommunications, research-network, low-carbon energy, and Baltic/Nordic corridor-integration jurisdiction embedded within EU and Nordic institutional systems. The evidence places Swedish digital identity coordination, payment modernisation, exchange infrastructure, research networking, high-performance computing, grid interconnection, civil-contingency cyber functions, and battery-industrial coordination inside linked national, Nordic, Baltic, and EU frameworks without assigning readiness tiers or routing authority.
