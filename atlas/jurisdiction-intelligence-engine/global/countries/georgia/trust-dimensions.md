- Electricity continuity shows explicit dependency on Georgian State Electrosystem dispatch coordination, transmission lines, substations, regional maintenance structure, and documented cross-border interconnections rather than exclusively internal local generation.
- Gas continuity shows explicit dependency on Georgian Gas Transportation Company operating coordination and on the South Caucasus Pipeline and North-South Main Gas Pipeline rather than exclusively domestic gas production.
- Administrative and digital continuity depend materially on shared-service components such as Public Service Hall, my.gov.ge, NIC.GE naming administration, ComCom-coordinated communications structure, and GRENA federation support rather than fully separate institutional stacks.
- Several nationally visible coordination functions remain concentrated in Tbilisi, creating structural concentration dependency even though transport, energy, emergency-response, and digital-service extension are territorially distributed.
- Public maritime visibility is concentrated most strongly at Poti, creating a visible Black Sea-facing continuity dependency on that publicly documented port environment rather than on an evenly exposed multi-port record.
- Public observability remains bounded across commercial exchange topology, private backbone architecture, data-center geography, Batumi port detail, some airport and railway operational detail, and current cyber-operational topology, which limits how fully these characteristics can be described from open sources.
- These dimensions therefore describe observable continuity and dependency patterns only, not a complete map of Georgian operational infrastructure conditions.
