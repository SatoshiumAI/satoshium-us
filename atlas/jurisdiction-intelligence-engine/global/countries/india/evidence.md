# India - Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- UIDAI states that Aadhaar is a 12-digit random number issued to residents of India after a verification process, that it is verifiable online in a cost-effective way, and that it may be used as a basis or primary identifier for delivery of government welfare schemes and programmes, placing India inside a large-scale state digital-identity environment.
- DigiLocker materials surfaced via official site search results state that it is a flagship Ministry of Electronics and IT initiative under Digital India and a secure cloud-based platform for storage, sharing, and verification of documents and certificates, documenting a state-backed digital-document rail.
- Digital India states that India Stack Global was launched to showcase the India Stack and its building blocks globally, and that it currently includes key platforms such as Aadhaar, UPI, API Setu, DigiLocker, UMANG, eHospital, eOffice, and eCourt.
- NPCI materials surfaced via official site search results state that UPI powers multiple bank accounts into a single mobile application and merges banking features, fund routing, and merchant payments, while RBI states that RTGS is a continuous real-time gross-settlement system and that NEFT is a nation-wide centralised payment system owned and operated by RBI.
- NIC states that it provides nationwide ICT infrastructure and services for government bodies including NICNET, NKN, National Data Centres, and the National Cloud, while NIC data-centre materials document National Data Centres at Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad, and Bhubaneswar together with 37 small data centres at state capitals.
- NIXI states that it was established for peering among ISPs so that domestic traffic is routed within the country rather than abroad, while the .IN Registry states that it is the official registry for India’s top-level domain under NIXI authority.
- CERT-In states that it is the national cyber-security agency under the Information Technology (Amendment) Act 2008, while NCIIPC materials surfaced via official site search results state that NCIIPC is the national nodal agency for protection of critical information infrastructure.
- NKN states that it is a state-of-the-art pan-India network for the knowledge community, and National Supercomputing Mission infrastructure materials state that the mission aims to create a nationally distributed supercomputing infrastructure integrated through NKN.
- PIB states that India and Singapore launched a real-time UPI-PayNow linkage for cross-border remittances, while PIB port materials state that India’s major ports handled about 855 million tonnes in FY 2024-25, documenting both cross-border digital-payments and physical-logistics interfaces.

- UIDAI states that Aadhaar number is a 12-digit random number issued to residents of India after a defined verification process.
- UIDAI states that enrolment is voluntary for residents of India, requires only minimal demographic and biometric information, and that uniqueness is achieved through demographic and biometric de-duplication.
- UIDAI states that Aadhaar is verifiable online in a cost-effective way and may be used as a basis or primary identifier for rollout of government welfare schemes and programmes to improve transparency and service delivery.
- DigiLocker materials surfaced via official site search results state that it is a flagship initiative of MeitY under Digital India and that it provides access to authentic digital documents through a citizen’s digital document wallet.
- DigiLocker materials surfaced via official site search results state that it is a secure cloud-based platform for storage, sharing, and verification of documents and certificates.
- UMANG states that it is a unified portal aggregating major citizen-centric services of central and state governments, local bodies, and statutory bodies through mobile apps and the web.
- UMANG states that its vision is to operate as a single interface for citizens to interact with government for services, schemes, documents, information, and participation, and it also publishes large-scale service and transaction counts.
- Digital India states that India Stack Global currently includes 15 key projects and platforms, including Aadhaar, UPI, API Setu, DigiLocker, UMANG, eHospital, eOffice, and eCourt, and frames India’s experience as implementation of digital transformation projects at population scale.
- The current source set therefore documents a digital-governance environment anchored by Aadhaar identity, DigiLocker document issuance and verification, UMANG service aggregation, and a broader India Stack framing for interoperable public digital platforms.

## Financial Infrastructure and Settlement Systems

- RBI states that RTGS stands for Real Time Gross Settlement and that it is a system where fund transfers are settled continuously and in real time on a transaction-by-transaction basis without netting.
- RBI states that RTGS settlement occurs in the books of RBI and that resulting payments are final and irrevocable.
- RBI states that RTGS is available on a 24x7x365 basis.
- RBI states that NEFT is a nation-wide centralised payment system owned and operated by RBI.
- RBI states that NEFT is available round the clock throughout the year, provides near-real-time secure settlement, has pan-India branch coverage, and is also available for one-way fund transfers from India to Nepal.
- NPCI materials surfaced via official site search results state that UPI is a system that powers multiple bank accounts into a single mobile application, merging several banking features, seamless fund routing, and merchant payments into one environment.
- Additional NPCI materials surfaced via official site search results state that UPI is an instant payment system developed by NPCI, an RBI-regulated entity, and that it allows instant transfer of money between bank accounts.
- PIB states that India and Singapore launched the real-time linkage between India’s UPI and Singapore’s PayNow, describing it as a cross-border P2P payment facility intended to make remittances easy, cost-effective, and real time.
- The current source set therefore documents a payment and settlement environment with a central-bank-operated RTGS and NEFT core, an NPCI-operated instant-payments layer through UPI, and at least one official cross-border real-time payment linkage.
- The current source set does not support broader claims about international monetary centrality beyond these India-centered settlement and cross-border payment interfaces.

## Data Infrastructure and Digital Continuity

- NIC states that it is closely associated with government bodies across India through establishment of nationwide ICT infrastructure and services including NICNET, NKN, National Data Centres, National Cloud, domain registration, and webcast services.
- NIC states that National Data Centres have been established at Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad, and Bhubaneswar, together with 37 small data centres at state capitals, and that these form the core of e-governance infrastructure in India.
- NIC states that the Bhubaneswar National Data Centre is cloud-enabled and that the Delhi, Pune, and Hyderabad centres were upgraded with high-speed networking, enterprise storage, load balancers, intrusion-prevention systems, and backup and storage services.
- NIC also states that national-level projects including e-Office, e-Courts, and e-Transport were hosted or enhanced through this infrastructure.
- NIC states that MeghRaj, the Government of India’s cloud initiative, is intended to accelerate delivery of e-services while optimizing government ICT spending and enabling faster development and deployment of e-governance applications.
- NIC states that MeghRaj offers multiple service models including PaaS, IaaS, SaaS, container services, and AI services.
- PIB states that a national cloud infrastructure has been established under Digital India and that MeghRaj provides secure, scalable, and elastic cloud facilities for delivery of e-governance services, while also noting that cloud services from multiple empanelled providers are part of the government cloud environment.
- DigiLocker materials surfaced via official site search results document a citizen-facing digital-document layer on top of this wider state digital infrastructure as a secure cloud-based storage, sharing, and verification platform.
- The current source set therefore documents a layered public-sector data and continuity environment combining national data centres, government cloud services, hosted national platforms, and digital-document infrastructure.
- The same source set does not document a wholly isolated sovereign hyperscale cloud stack independent of mixed government and empanelled-provider infrastructure.

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- NIXI states that it is a not-for-profit organisation established for peering among ISPs so that domestic traffic can be routed within India instead of being carried abroad, with the stated effects of reducing latency and international-bandwidth costs.
- NIXI states that it is managed and operated on a neutral basis.
- NIXI states that the Government of India delegated operation of the .IN Registry to NIXI in 2004, and that the registry operates and manages India’s .IN country-code top-level domain.
- NIXI also states that IRINN provides allocation and registration services for IP addresses and AS numbers in India.
- The .IN Registry states that it is the official .IN registry, appointed by the Government of India and operated under the authority of NIXI.
- The .IN Registry states that .IN is India’s top-level domain on the Internet and that registration procedures have been simplified so that .IN domains are available through multiple registrars.
- The current source set therefore directly documents national internet-exchange, ccTLD-registry, and numbering-resource administration functions.
- The current source set does not provide an equally strong basis for exhaustive claims about the full national exchange topology or broader global exchange centrality beyond these documented governance and exchange functions.

## Telecommunications and Digital Infrastructure

- Additional BBNL materials surfaced via official site search results state that the BharatNet / NOFN programme was intended to cover around 250,000 Gram Panchayats across roughly 6,600 blocks and 641 districts through incremental fibre deployment.
- NKN states that it is a pan-India network for the knowledge community, documenting a national institutional backbone layer.
- UMANG states that it acts as a single digital interface across central and state services, showing that national digital-service delivery sits on top of broad connectivity and application infrastructure.
- POWERGRID materials surfaced via official site search results state that the company leveraged its pan-India transmission network through optical ground wire and that its telecom subsidiary operates a pan-India overhead optic-fibre network, documenting another large-scale state-linked communications surface.
- The current source set therefore documents national middle-mile rural broadband expansion, institutional networking, and additional state-linked fibre infrastructure.
- The current source set does not provide a complete picture of India’s entire telecom market or a comprehensive nationwide 5G deployment profile through the cited materials alone.

## Cybersecurity and Critical Infrastructure Protection

- CERT-In states that it is a functional organisation of MeitY with the objective of securing Indian cyberspace.
- CERT-In states that it provides incident-prevention and response services as well as security quality-management services.
- CERT-In states that, under the Information Technology (Amendment) Act 2008, it serves as the national agency for collection, analysis, and dissemination of cyber-incident information, forecasting and alerts, emergency measures, coordination of response activities, and issuance of advisories and vulnerability notes.
- NCIIPC materials surfaced via official site search results state that NCIIPC, a unit of NTRO, was created under Section 70A of the Information Technology Act and serves as the national nodal agency for protection of Critical Information Infrastructure.
- Additional NCIIPC materials surfaced via official site search results state that NCIIPC runs a Responsible Vulnerability Disclosure Program for vulnerabilities affecting Critical Information Infrastructures.
- The current source set therefore documents a dual cyber-coordination surface combining national incident response through CERT-In and critical-infrastructure protection through NCIIPC.
- The current source set does not provide equally detailed evidence for all-sector certification and private-sector cyber-governance structures beyond these national public bodies.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- RBI and NPCI materials document RTGS, NEFT, and UPI clearly, and PIB documents the UPI-PayNow linkage, but the current source set does not establish broader claims about international monetary authority beyond these India-centered payment and remittance interfaces.
- NIC and PIB materials document National Data Centres and MeghRaj, but the cited cloud environment includes multiple empanelled providers and therefore does not document a wholly isolated sovereign hyperscale cloud stack.
- NKN and National Supercomputing Mission materials document research-network and distributed-supercomputing infrastructure, but the current source set does not document a comprehensive sovereign compute estate across all sectors.
- NIXI and the .IN Registry document exchange, registry, and numbering functions, but the current source set does not establish exhaustive claims about the full national peering topology or broader global exchange centrality.
- BharatNet, NKN, and POWERGRID search-result materials document important connectivity layers, but the current source set does not provide a complete nationwide telecom-market or 5G deployment picture.
- CERT-In and NCIIPC clearly document national cyber and critical-infrastructure functions, but the current source set does not provide comparably detailed visibility into all-sector cyber-governance, certification, or private-sector resilience structures.
- PIB port materials document major maritime throughput and expansion, but the current source set does not by itself establish a full multimodal logistics map across ports, airports, rail, and inland corridors.
- These constraints describe the limits of the cited evidence only and should not be treated as negative judgments.

## Summary

- The current source set documents India as a large-scale digital-governance jurisdiction built around Aadhaar identity, DigiLocker document infrastructure, UMANG service aggregation, and an outward-facing India Stack framework.
- It documents India as a central-bank-operated payment jurisdiction with RTGS and NEFT, layered with NPCI’s UPI instant-payments infrastructure and an official cross-border linkage with Singapore.
- It documents a public-sector data and continuity environment through NIC’s nationwide ICT estate, National Data Centres, MeghRaj national cloud services, and hosted e-governance platforms.
- It documents a national research-network and supercomputing posture through NKN and the National Supercomputing Mission.
- It documents registry, exchange, broadband, grid, cyber, and logistics functions through NIXI, the .IN Registry, BharatNet, NLDC / GRID-INDIA, CERT-In, NCIIPC, and India’s major ports.
- It also places India inside a global digital-public-infrastructure narrative and selected cross-border payment and trade interfaces.
- The cited evidence supports these infrastructure characterizations without assigning readiness tiers, routing authority, corridor status, or broader Atlas interpretation beyond the institutional materials.
