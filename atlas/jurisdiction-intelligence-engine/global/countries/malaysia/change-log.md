- Visibility limitations were retained explicitly so that incomplete public access, partial domain retrieval, and uneven infrastructure observability remained part of the canonical evidence record rather than being normalized away.

- Malaysia was normalized as a dual-region jurisdiction composed of Peninsular Malaysia and East Malaysia rather than as a land-contiguous domestic environment.
- Klang Valley was treated as the primary concentration environment because it contained the clearest documented overlap of rail, airport access, payment infrastructure, exchange participation, compute presence, and adjacent cable-linked infrastructure.
- East Malaysia was treated as a coordinated but distinct operational environment with separate port and power arrangements that still remained inside the same national continuity structure.
- Continuity was interpreted through layered systems, with maritime and aviation movement reinforced by digital administration, payment rails, exchange infrastructure, and segmented but coordinated utility systems.
- Territorial separation was handled as a structural operating condition rather than as fragmentation, weakness, or geopolitical instability.
- Geopolitical interpretation was intentionally excluded.
- The package therefore preserved a concentration-with-distribution model rather than a fragmented-territory narrative.
