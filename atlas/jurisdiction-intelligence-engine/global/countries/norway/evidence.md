# Norway — Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- Digdir states that its mission is to serve as the government’s foremost instrument for expediting and harmonizing the digitalisation of society and that it is responsible for strategic planning and further development of a comprehensive digital infrastructure for the public sector.
- Norges Bank states that Norges Bank’s settlement system comprises the core of the payment system and assesses Norway’s financial infrastructure as secure, efficient and stable.
- Statnett states that it is the transmission system operator in the Norwegian energy system and that foreign exchange capacity is an integral part of the Norwegian central grid.
- Gassco states that it operates an integrated gas-transport system linking Norwegian offshore production and processing infrastructure to receiving terminals in multiple European countries.
- Nkom states that it is responsible for consistent and reliable postal and electronic communications services across Norway, including security and resilience responsibilities for e-com networks.
- Sikt states that it is a national resource owned by the Ministry of Education and Research and that it delivers shared technology, services and infrastructure for education and research.
- Norid states that it runs the registry for Norwegian domain names under .no, while NIX states that neutral exchange points have been established across Norway to support open IP traffic exchange.
- NSM and DSB materials place Norway’s cyber-protective and emergency-communications infrastructure inside nationally organised security, resilience and preparedness institutions.
- Regjeringen and Nordic-cooperation materials place Norway inside EEA, Schengen and formal Nordic cooperation structures without assigning corridor authority or readiness rankings.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- Digdir states that its main tasks include coordinating cross-cutting digitalisation measures, strategic planning for a comprehensive public-sector digital infrastructure, preventive information-security work in the public sector, and operation and development of shared digital components and solutions.
- ID-porten documentation states that ID-porten offers authentication services from Norwegian citizens toward service providers in the Norwegian public sector.
- Digdir materials describe Altinn as both an internet portal that helps people, businesses and public agencies communicate digitally and a technical platform used by public bodies to build digital services.
- Digdir materials state that more than 94 million messages and forms were sent via Altinn in the prior year and that more than four million private persons have access to an Altinn inbox.
- Digdir states that it leads a multi-country NOBID-linked consortium piloting payments in the EU Digital Identity Wallet framework and that Norway is participating alongside other Nordic and European countries.
- Digdir states that Norway is preparing for eIDAS 2.0 and the European Digital Identity Wallet and frames that work explicitly through European and Nordic cooperation.

## Financial Infrastructure and CBDC Exploration

- Norges Bank states that banks access Norges Bank’s settlement system (NBO) via the international SWIFT network or through NBO Online.
- Norges Bank states that each participating bank has a main RTM account, a loan account and one or more LOM subaccounts, documenting a structured domestic settlement architecture.
- Norges Bank states that its legal responsibility includes promoting an efficient payment system domestically as well as vis-à-vis other countries, and that its oversight covers licensed interbank systems including NICS and the privately owned settlement bank DNB.
- Norges Bank assesses Norway’s financial infrastructure as secure and efficient, states that operations are stable and that payments can be made swiftly at low cost, and notes that the next generation settlement system is under research.
- Norges Bank states that Norway is among the countries with the highest annual use of payment cards per inhabitant and that mobile payment use at physical and online points of sale is rising.
- Norges Bank states that the CBDC project has moved into a fifth phase intended to support a recommendation on whether a CBDC should be introduced and, if so, with what design characteristics.
- Norges Bank also states that the current payment system in Norway functions well and that CBDC work should not proceed with undue haste.

## Telecommunications and Network Regulation

- Nkom states that it is subject to the Ministry of Digitalisation and Public Governance for electronic communications services and to the Ministry of Transport for postal services.
- Nkom states that its overall objective is to facilitate consistent and reliable postal and e-com services across Norway and that these services must be robust, forward-looking, high quality and affordable.
- Nkom states that it has special responsibility to ensure the security and resilience of e-com networks against both data attacks and extreme weather impacts.
- Nkom states that it plays an active role in the development of new emergency networks and mobile emergency preparedness and maintains 24/7 security rosters for cybernet emergency preparedness.
- Nkom states that it manages frequencies for mobile networks, radio and television in Norway and that it began selling 5G frequencies at auctions in 2019.

- Nkom, “About Nkom”

## Research Network, Compute, and AI Infrastructure

- Sikt states that it is a national resource for the common good owned by the Ministry of Education and Research and that its mission includes ensuring access to high-quality infrastructure and shared services, promoting data-sharing and providing robust information security and data protection.
- Sikt materials surfaced via official site search results describe the National Research and Education Network, Uninett, as a major part of the Norwegian internet connected to all Norwegian NIX points, with 10 or 100 Gbit/s connectivity and 400G planned.
- Sikt materials surfaced via official site search results state that NORDUnet is the Nordic national research and education network organisation owned by the Nordic countries and that Sikt provides GÉANT-related services to Norwegian research and higher education.
- Sikt states that Sigma2 is a subsidiary with strategic responsibility for, and operational control of, the national e-infrastructure for computer science in Norway, including HPC and research-data storage.
- Simula states that the Norwegian AI Cloud (NAIC) is an infrastructure project intended to increase access to e-infrastructure for artificial intelligence in Norway and to create a national e-infrastructure resource pool for academia, public administration and industry.
- Simula states that NAIC is funded by the Research Council of Norway and links multiple Norwegian universities, Sigma2 and research institutions.

## Internet Exchange and Naming Infrastructure

- NIX states that the University of Oslo established a set of neutral IP exchange points to support open IP traffic exchange in Norway.
- NIX states that the Norwegian Internet eXchange refers to six independent exchange points, with primary Oslo sites and regional sites in Bergen, Trondheim, Tromsø and Stavanger.
- Norid states that it runs the registry for Norwegian domain names, develops domain-name policy, and is responsible for technical operation and development of associated services.
- Norid states that it works to keep the domain name system reliable and robust and to ensure governance in an open and democratic way.
- Norid states that the service is supervised by Nkom, that Norid is a neutral non-profit organisation, and that ownership was transferred in 2021 to the Ministry of Digitalisation and Public Governance.

Sources:
- NIX, “About NIX”
- Norid, “About Norid”

## Energy, Grid, and Gas Transport Infrastructure

- Statnett states that it is the transmission system operator in the Norwegian energy system and that its mission is to ensure a reliable power supply by building, operating, maintaining and preparing the transmission grid.
- Statnett states that it coordinates production and consumption of electrical power in Norway so that there is always balance in the power system.
- Statnett states that exchange capacity with foreign countries is an integral and important part of the Norwegian central grid.
- Statnett states that Norway has lines to Sweden and Finland and subsea connections to Denmark, the Netherlands, Germany and the United Kingdom, with 17 lines or subsea connections to other countries in total.
- Statnett states that North Sea Link runs between Norway and the United Kingdom.
- Statnett states that NordLink is the first interconnector between Norway and Germany.
- Statnett materials identify NorNed as the high-voltage connection between Norway and the Netherlands.
- Statnett states that these interconnectors strengthen Norwegian supply security, support value creation and contribute to a future climate-friendly energy system.
- Gassco states that it receives gas from almost 70 fields on the Norwegian continental shelf and operates processing facilities connected to that flow.
- Gassco states that it is responsible as operator for an 8,600 kilometre pipeline network transporting Norwegian gas to continental Europe and the UK.
- Gassco states that it operates six gas receiving terminals in Europe, located in the United Kingdom, Germany, Belgium and France.

## Cybersecurity and Civil Contingency Coordination

- NSM materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Norwegian National Security Authority is Norway’s agency for national protective security.
- The same NSM materials state that the agency’s mission is to strengthen Norway’s ability to counter espionage, sabotage, terrorism and hybrid threats and that it helps protect information, systems, objects and infrastructure relevant to national security.
- NSM materials surfaced via official site search results state that NSM operates a national warning system for critical infrastructure and coordinates national efforts to handle serious cyberoperations.
- NSM materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Norwegian National Cyber Security Centre is part of NSM, that it is Norway’s national cyber security centre, and that it is home to the national CERT, NorCERT.
- NSM materials surfaced via official site search results state that NCSC handles severe computer attacks against critical infrastructure and information.
- DSB states that Nødnett is Norway’s nationwide digital critical communication network for police, fire, health services and other preparedness users.
- DSB states that Nødnett is a secure and robust network with good coverage and uptime and describes it as one of the cornerstones of Norwegian emergency preparedness.
- DSB states that the network supports cross-organisational and cross-geographical communication and that DSB manages the state’s ownership of Nødnett infrastructure, while Nkom is responsible for work on the new emergency network.

## Nordic, EEA, and Cross-Border Coordination

- Regjeringen states that the EEA Agreement has served Norway well and continues to frame important aspects of Norway’s cooperation with the EU.
- Regjeringen materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Department for European Affairs and International Trade is responsible for Norway’s relations with the European Union, including matters relating to the EEA Agreement, Schengen cooperation, justice and home affairs.
- Nordic cooperation materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Nordic Council of Ministers and the Nordic Council are the main forums for official Nordic cooperation and explicitly include Norway in that structure.
- Digdir states that Norway’s digital-wallet and NOBID-related work is being developed in close connection with European and Nordic initiatives.
- Statnett, Gassco and Sikt materials document infrastructure layers that operate through Nordic and wider European interconnection rather than in isolation.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- Digdir, Regjeringen and Nordic-cooperation materials place significant parts of Norway’s digital-identity and digital-governance trajectory inside wider EEA, EU and Nordic frameworks rather than a fully standalone national regime.
- Norges Bank materials place payment modernisation inside an already well-functioning domestic system while CBDC remains under evaluation and next-generation settlement-system work continues, rather than documenting a deployed sovereign digital-currency stack.
- Statnett and Gassco materials document electricity and gas infrastructures whose operational value depends in part on cross-border exchange, interconnection and downstream European delivery systems.
- The current source set documents Sikt, Sigma2, NIX, Norid and NAIC as meaningful national network, compute and naming surfaces, but does not document an independent Norwegian sovereign hyperscale compute stack.
- The current source set documents national cyber, registry, payment, grid, gas and emergency-communications institutions, but does not provide an official basis for ranking Norway by corridor authority, topology role or institutional readiness tier.

## Summary

The current source set documents Norway as a Nordic digital-governance, payment-system, energy-interconnection, gas-transport, research-network and resilience jurisdiction linked tightly to EEA, Nordic and wider European frameworks. The evidence places Digdir, ID-porten, Altinn, Norges Bank, Statnett, Gassco, Nkom, Sikt, Norid, NIX, NSM and Nødnett inside a nationally administered but cross-border-connected infrastructure stack, while leaving CBDC adoption, sovereign hyperscale compute, and any ranking-based topology claims unsupported by the present source set.
