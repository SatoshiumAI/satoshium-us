Trust derivation constraint: trust dimensions derive strictly from `evidence.md` and `signals.md`. This file does not introduce new infrastructure claims, assign routing authority, assign readiness tiers, rank jurisdictions, or infer deployment eligibility.

## Institutional Continuity Dimension

- The source layers indicate institutional continuity across LNG production, utilities, digital governance, payment systems, telecommunications, logistics, cybersecurity, and cross-border institutional embedding through centralized state-coordinated structures rather than fragmented operator-led continuity.
- QatarEnergy and Kahramaa indicate continuity across energy production, export-linked industrial systems, and nationally coordinated utility infrastructure.
- Mwani Qatar, Hamad International Airport, and Qatar Airways Cargo indicate continuity across maritime, aviation, and freight systems through named national infrastructure operators and logistics platforms.
- MCIT and TASMU indicate continuity across digital-governance and smart-infrastructure coordination through standing state programs and national digital platforms.
- QCB indicates continuity in central-bank-led payment-system governance, while CRA, QNBN, QIXP, and .qa registry governance indicate continuity across telecommunications regulation, domestic interconnection, and naming-layer administration.
- NCSA indicates continuity in national cybersecurity coordination through assurance and certification structures, while Qatar Foundation indicates continuity in institutionally anchored research and compute activity.
- GCC participation and broader cross-border connectivity documented in the source layers indicate that Qatar’s institutional continuity also remains embedded in repeated regional and global coordination structures rather than within a wholly closed national perimeter.
- The documented trust characteristic is institutional continuity across multiple critical infrastructure layers under centralized coordination, combined with durable embedding in wider GCC and global systems.

- The same source layers indicate domestic settlement continuity and real-time payment capability, while not supporting a claim of independent global settlement authority beyond the national framework.
- The documented trust characteristic is continuity of layered domestic payment infrastructure under central-bank coordination, combined with bounded integration into wider financial systems.

## Constraint Boundary Dimension

- The source layers indicate trust boundaries reflecting Qatar’s embedded position within global energy, cloud, financial, logistics, and connectivity systems rather than full infrastructure sovereignty.
- Dependence on global LNG markets indicates that a central part of Qatar’s economic and industrial infrastructure continuity remains tied to external market demand and cross-border export systems.
- Financial-system integration under QCB-led payment infrastructure indicates domestic settlement continuity within a wider interbank and cross-institutional financial environment rather than a detached national payment perimeter.
- Cloud-provider dependence documented through Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure region presence indicates that part of Qatar’s digital-infrastructure continuity remains attached to global provider ecosystems.
- The source layers do not document a sovereign semiconductor fabrication stack, and they do not document a fully sovereign hyperscale compute stack.
- Qatar Foundation, HBKU, and QCRI indicate meaningful institutional compute capability, but the documented compute base remains smaller and more institutionally bounded than a broad sovereign-scale national compute estate.
- Telecommunications and exchange continuity documented through CRA, QNBN, QIXP, and international-connectivity frameworks remains tied to wider global connectivity systems rather than a fully self-contained communications perimeter.
- The documented trust characteristic is continuity within a strongly coordinated national infrastructure environment whose scale and autonomy remain bounded by continued attachment to global systems.

## Trust Dimensions Summary Statement

Qatar is documented as a centralized, state-coordinated infrastructure jurisdiction anchored in LNG production and export, supported by digital-government and smart-infrastructure systems, and integrated into global energy, logistics, telecommunications, and data networks. The source layers indicate continuity across institutional coordination, digital governance, payment systems, hybrid cloud and data infrastructure, telecommunications, logistics, cybersecurity, and GCC and global institutional embedding, while also indicating trust boundaries associated with continued dependence on wider global systems.
