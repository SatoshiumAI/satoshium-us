# Indonesia - Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- Dukcapil states that Identitas Kependudukan Digital (IKD) is being positioned as a foundational digital identity for government services under Perpres No. 82 Tahun 2023, while Satu Data Indonesia states that it is the government data-governance policy for sharable data across central and regional institutions, placing Indonesia inside a large-scale archipelagic digital-governance environment with explicit identity, data, and service-integration layers.
- INA Digital states that it exists to build an integrated government digital-services ecosystem and that its mandate is tied to Perpres No. 82 Tahun 2023, while Komdigi states that SPBE is the main foundation of national digital government and that central and regional systems are being integrated within a national architecture.
- Bank Indonesia states that BI-RTGS is the national high-value transfer infrastructure, that SKNBI is the national clearing infrastructure, that BI-FAST is retail-payment infrastructure intended to become the backbone of retail payments, and that QRIS and GPN are national interoperability layers, documenting a central-bank payment-modernization and settlement-governance environment.
- Kominfo and Komdigi materials surfaced via official site search results describe Palapa Ring as a national fiber-optic network connecting 90 regencies and cities and identify ongoing BAKTI 4G expansion in underserved areas, placing Indonesia inside a telecommunications and inter-island connectivity environment shaped by archipelagic infrastructure deployment.
- Telin states that it operates more than 250,000 km of cable systems in the Asia-Pacific and that the Bifrost cable lands in Jakarta and Manado, while Telkom states that neuCentrIX Banda Aceh was opened to support the digital ecosystem in northern Sumatra, documenting both submarine-cable and maritime-connectivity infrastructure.
- Itjen Komdigi states that the National Data Center in Cikarang is being built as strategic digital infrastructure with Tier IV-aligned standards and dual power feeds, while AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Learn document Jakarta or Indonesia-based hyperscale cloud regions, placing Indonesia inside a public-sector cloud and mixed-provider digital-continuity environment.
- PANDI materials surfaced via official site search results identify PANDI as the .ID registry assigned by Kominfo decree, while APJII states that its data-center services include IIX and MMR functions for keeping domestic interconnection local, documenting an internet exchange and registry-governance environment.
- iDREN states that it is a dedicated network for Indonesian research and education institutions with connectivity onward to the global TEIN research network, while BRIN materials surfaced via official site search results document ASEAN-oriented HPC infrastructure and programs, placing Indonesia inside a research-network and scientific-infrastructure environment.
- PLN materials surfaced via official site search results state that the grid is being modernized with smart-grid solutions, advanced forecasting, and transmission-interconnection work, while Pelindo states that PTOS-M and Phinnisi are being deployed across port operations in support of the logistics ecosystem and NLE targets, documenting energy-grid coordination together with maritime logistics modernization.
- BSSN materials surfaced via official site search results identify Gov-CSIRT Indonesia as the government-sector incident-response team, while Bank Indonesia, iDREN, BRIN, Telin, and Pelindo materials place Indonesia inside ASEAN and wider cross-border payment, research, cable, and trade-connectivity interfaces.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- Dukcapil states that IKD is highly relevant to Perpres No. 82 Tahun 2023 on accelerating digital transformation and national digital-service integration.
- Dukcapil states that the government is pursuing integrated national digital services through SPBE priority applications with an emphasis on integration and interoperability.
- The same Dukcapil materials state that Digital ID, data interoperability, and digital payment are three key transformation foundations, and specifically position IKD as the foundational digital ID required across government services with personal-data protection.
- Dukcapil further states that Perpres 82/2023 provides for nine priority SPBE applications, including integrated education services, health services, social assistance, population administration integrated with digital identity, state-finance transactions, state-administration services, a public-service portal, Satu Data Indonesia, and integrated policing services.
- INA Digital states that it is working to build an integrated digital-government service ecosystem and that it operates as part of PERURI under the government mandate established in Perpres No. 82 Tahun 2023.
- INA Digital also states that it is intended to act as an accelerator for higher-quality, trusted, and efficient public digital services.
- Satu Data Indonesia states that SDI is the government data-governance policy intended to create high-quality data that is accessible and shareable across central and regional institutions, and that the policy is grounded in Perpres No. 39 Tahun 2019.
- Satu Data Indonesia states that data.go.id is the official open-data portal managed by the central SDI secretariat at Bappenas.
- Komdigi states that SPBE is the main foundation of national digital government, that roughly 27,000 applications are spread across ministries, agencies, and regions, and that the government is working to integrate them within one national architecture.
- The same Komdigi materials state that regional alignment is being pushed through national infrastructure already prepared by the central government, including the National Data Center and a national API backbone for service-data exchange.
- Kominfo materials surfaced via official site search results state that INA Digital is intended to integrate various government services in one portal and improve interoperability across ministries and agencies.
- The current source set therefore documents a digital-governance environment anchored by Dukcapil identity systems, IKD as a documented foundational digital ID layer, SPBE integration priorities, INA Digital service integration, and Satu Data Indonesia data-governance infrastructure.

## Financial Infrastructure and Payment Systems

- Bank Indonesia states that the Indonesian payment system comprises rules, institutions, and mechanisms for fund transfers, and distinguishes between high-value wholesale transactions and retail transactions.
- Bank Indonesia states that wholesale transactions in Indonesia are processed through BI-RTGS and BI-SSSS, while retail transactions are processed through SKNBI.
- Bank Indonesia states that BI-RTGS is infrastructure for electronic fund transfers settled immediately per transaction on an individual basis, that it has operated since 17 November 2000, and that it is used especially for urgent high-value transactions.
- Bank Indonesia states that BI-RTGS is designed to ensure gross settlement in real time that is final and irrevocable.
- Bank Indonesia states that BI-FAST is payment-system infrastructure provided by the central bank for retail payment transactions and that it is intended to become the backbone of retail payment infrastructure.
- Bank Indonesia states that BI-FAST is available 24/7, operates in real time at bank and customer levels, supports push and pull transactions, proxy address use, fraud detection, automatic customer notifications, and AML/CTF systems.
- Bank Indonesia states that SKNBI is the national clearing infrastructure for funds transfers and scheduled clearing, including transfer services, paper-based debit clearing, regular credit services, and regular debit services.
- Bank Indonesia states that the National Payment Gateway (GPN/NPG) is an ecosystem connecting electronic payment instruments offered by domestic banks, and that it was created to build an integrated and connected national retail payment ecosystem through interconnection and interoperability.
- Bank Indonesia states that QRIS is the national QR payment standard for use by payment-service providers in Indonesia, that one QRIS code can support multiple payment instruments, and that QRIS is intended to support both inclusion and cross-border payment connectivity.
- Bank Indonesia states that QRIS Antarnegara is an interconnection between Indonesia’s QR standard and partner-country QR standards through central-bank-initiated cooperation, with settlement through appointed cross-currency dealers in local currencies.
- The same QRIS Antarnegara materials document real-time transaction receipt and directly identify partner-country acceptance through PromptPay in Thailand, DuitNow in Malaysia, NETS in Singapore, and JPQR Global in Japan.
- The current source set therefore documents a central-bank-operated payment environment combining BI-RTGS, SKNBI, BI-FAST, GPN, QRIS, and cross-border QR linkages without supporting broader claims of global settlement authority.

## Data Infrastructure and Digital Continuity

- Itjen Komdigi states that the National Data Center (PDN) in Cikarang is a strategic project intended to provide technology infrastructure for national digital transformation, particularly for large-scale data storage and management.
- The same Itjen Komdigi materials state that PDN Cikarang is designed with SNI 8799-1:2019 strata 4, Uptime Tier IV, and ANSI TIA 942-B:2017 Rate 4 standards, with at least 25,000 processor cores, 200 TB of memory, 40 petabytes of storage, and 20 MVA of electricity from two separate sources.
- Itjen Komdigi also states that the project is financed through loans from the French government and commercial banks with domestic budget participation, and that it is being built by the Ellipse Projects consortium under a design, supply, and installation model.
- Komdigi states that SPBE integration depends on national infrastructure including the National Data Center and a national API backbone for service-data exchange.
- Satu Data Indonesia states that all government data and related institutional data can converge in the official Satu Data Indonesia portal, documenting a public data-governance and publishing layer alongside infrastructure buildout.
- AWS states that the Asia Pacific (Jakarta) Region has three Availability Zones and allows customers, including government and education organizations, to run workloads and store data locally in Indonesia.
- Google Cloud states that its Jakarta region is the company’s first cloud region in Indonesia, launched with three cloud zones, and positioned to support lower-latency access, regulatory and compliance requirements, and additional disaster-recovery options across APAC.
- Microsoft Learn lists Indonesia Central in Jakarta as an Azure public-cloud region with availability-zone support.
- The current source set therefore documents a mixed-provider digital-continuity environment that combines public-sector PDN infrastructure, service-exchange backbones, open-data governance, and locally present hyperscale cloud regions.
- The same source set does not document a wholly isolated sovereign hyperscale environment independent of mixed public, consortium, and global-provider infrastructure.

## Telecommunications and Connectivity Infrastructure

- Additional Kominfo materials surfaced via official site search results state that Palapa Ring was designed to connect Indonesia through high-speed internet and reduce the telecommunications-service gap between Java and other regions.

- The Bifrost materials also state that the system is intended to serve telecom operators, hyperscalers, cloud service providers, and data-center operators.
- The current source set therefore documents an inter-island and submarine-connectivity environment built from Palapa Ring backbone expansion, BAKTI access expansion, domestic facilities expansion through Telkom, and international cable participation through Telin.
- The cited materials support infrastructure descriptions without establishing broader routing authority.

## Logistics and Maritime Connectivity Infrastructure

- Pelindo states that PTOS-M is an integrated multi-terminal port operating system for non-container cargo, structured as a single platform supporting port operations through planning and controlling functions.
- Pelindo states that PTOS-M is integrated with customer portals, vessel-service systems, and financial-service systems, and includes online booking requests, operation planning, storage inventory, and control-and-monitoring functions.
- Pelindo states that Phinnisi is an end-to-end vessel-service operating platform developed to support cross-port connectivity and synergy across logistics-ecosystem entities.
- Pelindo states that Phinnisi uses a centralized and integrated network and is treated as one pillar in realizing the National Logistics Ecosystem (NLE).
- Pelindo states that Phinnisi had been implemented across 34 ports by the end of 2023, exceeding the NLE port-governance standardization target of 32 ports for that year.
- Kemenkeu materials surfaced via official site search results describe NLE as a government breakthrough program intended to ensure smooth movement of export-import flows and domestic goods flows, including flows within islands and between islands.
- The current source set therefore documents a maritime-logistics modernization environment combining Pelindo port-digitization systems with an NLE framework for multimodal and inter-island logistics coordination.
- The cited materials support logistics-infrastructure and modernization claims without assigning logistics dominance.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- PDN Cikarang, SPBE backbone materials, and Satu Data Indonesia document substantial public digital infrastructure, but the same source set also documents AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure regional presence as well as foreign-financed and consortium-built PDN infrastructure, indicating a mixed-provider cloud and continuity environment rather than a wholly sovereign hyperscale stack.
- PANDI and APJII document .ID registry governance and domestic exchange functions, but the current source set does not establish exhaustive national IX topology or any broader exchange centrality beyond those documented functions.
- iDREN and BRIN materials document research-network and HPC collaboration, but the current source set provides limited evidence for a broader sovereign compute estate outside those documented research and scientific environments.
- QRIS Antarnegara, TEIN connectivity, international cloud regions, and Telin submarine systems all document cross-border infrastructure linkage, indicating that several important digital functions operate through regional and global integration layers rather than isolated domestic enclosures.
- Palapa Ring, BAKTI BTS deployment, Phinnisi expansion, and NLE materials all reflect archipelagic scale and inter-island coordination demands, which means infrastructure descriptions in the current source set are inseparable from multi-island deployment complexity.
- BSSN and Gov-CSIRT materials clearly document public-sector incident-response coordination, but the current source set does not provide comparably detailed visibility into private-sector or all-sector cyber-resilience structures.
- These constraints describe the boundaries of the currently cited evidence only and should not be treated as weakness or readiness judgments.

## Summary

- The current source set documents Indonesia as a large-scale archipelagic digital-governance jurisdiction built around Dukcapil identity systems, IKD, SPBE integration priorities, INA Digital service integration, and Satu Data Indonesia data governance.
- It documents Indonesia as a central-bank payment-modernization environment with BI-RTGS, SKNBI, BI-FAST, GPN, QRIS, and QRIS Antarnegara.
- It documents inter-island and submarine connectivity through Palapa Ring, BAKTI 4G expansion materials, Telkom facility expansion, and Telin’s international cable systems.
- It documents a mixed public and provider-backed digital-continuity environment through PDN Cikarang, national API and data-governance layers, and locally present AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure regions.
- It documents registry and exchange functions through PANDI and APJII, research-network and scientific infrastructure through iDREN and BRIN-linked HPC materials, energy-grid modernization through PLN materials, maritime-logistics modernization through Pelindo and NLE materials, and public-sector cyber coordination through BSSN and Gov-CSIRT Indonesia.
- It also places Indonesia inside ASEAN and broader global integration channels through cross-border payment linkages, TEIN research connectivity, international submarine cables, and institutionally documented trade-connectivity systems.
- The cited evidence supports these infrastructure characterizations without assigning readiness tiers, routing authority, or broader interpretive status beyond the institutional materials.
