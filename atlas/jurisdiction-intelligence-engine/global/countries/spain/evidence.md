# Spain - Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- European Commission materials state that Spain has been a euro area member since 1 January 1999, placing the country inside the Eurosystem monetary and settlement environment rather than a standalone national currency system.
- European Commission rail-deployment materials state that the trans-European corridors entering the Iberian Peninsula and the Spanish rail network are the Atlantic corridor, from the French border at Irun/Hendaye toward Portugal with branches via Madrid and Algeciras, and the Mediterranean corridor, from the French border at Portbou/Cerbère along the Mediterranean coast toward Algeciras and Seville.
- The same European Commission materials state that ADIF is the national infrastructure manager in Spain, managing and maintaining the Spanish network, with part of that network belonging to the core network corridors.
- Red.es states that it is a public corporate entity attached to the Ministry for Digital Transformation and Public Function and that it develops the Digital Agenda and digital-transformation programmes for public administrations, companies, and citizens.
- INCIBE-CERT states that it is the reference security incident response centre for citizens and private-law entities in Spain, while CCN-CERT materials surfaced via official site search results place the Spanish government CERT role around public administrations, classified systems, and strategic-sector entities.
- Aena, Puertos del Estado, and ADIF materials place Spain inside a documented multi-modal infrastructure footprint spanning airport, port, and rail systems, without supporting broader Atlas interpretation beyond the cited institutional transport structures.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- Cl@ve states that it is a system aimed at unifying and simplifying citizens’ electronic access to public services and that it functions as a common platform for identification, authentication, and electronic signature.
- Cl@ve states that it complements existing access systems based on DNIe and electronic certificates and offers cloud signing with personal certificates guarded on remote servers.
- Cl@ve states that the system provides multiple access modes, including Cl@ve PIN for sporadic access, Cl@ve Permanente for habitual access, and Cl@ve Mobile for mobile-device identification flows.
- PAe identity-and-signature materials state that Spain maintains shared public-administration components including Cl@ve, the @firma product suite, the Spanish eIDAS node, Valide for online validation of signatures and certificates, and Autentica for authentication and single sign-on for internal public-administration applications.
- PAe materials state that the @firma suite provides common services and products related to electronic signature to facilitate the implementation of signature and authentication systems in the public administration.
- Cl@ve Firma materials surfaced via official site search results state that use of Cl@ve signing certificates was adapted to eIDAS requirements, including high-assurance registration requirements for recognised-signature use.

Sources:
- Cl@ve, "What is Cl@ve?"
- PAe, "Identidad y firma electrónica"
- Cl@ve Firma materials surfaced via official site search results

## Financial Infrastructure and Settlement Systems

- European Commission materials state that Spain has been a euro area member since 1 January 1999 and identify Banco de España as the national central-bank institution in that framework.
- Banco de España states that the new-generation TARGET system settles in euro in central-bank money and provides central liquidity-management services, including large-value real-time gross settlement for payments, cash payments related to securities settlement, and settlement of instant payments.
- Banco de España states that TIPS, integrated into TARGET, enables the settlement of instant-payment orders in central-bank money 24 hours a day, every day of the year.
- The current source set therefore places Spain’s monetary and payment-settlement posture inside euro-area and Eurosystem-operated infrastructure rather than a separate sovereign settlement stack.

Sources:
- European Commission, "Spain and the euro"
- Banco de España, "TARGET"
- Banco de España, "TIPS"

## Data Infrastructure and Digital Continuity

- PAe identity-and-signature materials document a shared administrative service layer built around Cl@ve, @firma, Valide, and the Spanish eIDAS node, supporting a common identification, signature, and validation posture across public-administration services.
- PAe materials state that Valide allows citizens and businesses to validate signatures and certificates online and includes a demonstrator for @firma web services.
- Spain 2024 digital-public-administration materials surfaced via official site search results state that Red SARA is Spain’s government intranet and interconnects all ministries, all autonomous communities and autonomous cities, and more than 4,000 local entities representing more than 90% of the population, with the goal of increasing collaboration and interoperability between administrations.
- Additional PAe materials surfaced via official site search results describe SARA as a communications network of the Spanish public administrations and show multiple shared services distributed through that network.
- Red.es states that it develops digital-transformation programmes for public administrations and digital public services, placing part of Spain’s continuity posture inside ministry-linked shared service and coordination structures.

Sources:
- PAe, "Identidad y firma electrónica"
- Spain 2024 Digital Public Administration Factsheet supporting document surfaced via official site search results
- SARA materials surfaced via official site search results
- Red.es, "About us"

## Research Network and Compute Infrastructure

- RedIRIS states that it is the Spanish academic and research network, provides advanced communication services to the scientific community and national universities, is funded by the Ministry of Science and Innovation, and is managed by the public corporate entity Red.es.
- RedIRIS states that it has more than 500 affiliated institutions, mainly universities and public research centres.
- Red.es states that it manages RedIRIS Nova 100 to support more than 500 universities and research institutions in Spain and their users through services including eduroam.
- Barcelona Supercomputing Center materials state that MareNostrum 5 is a pre-exascale EuroHPC supercomputer hosted at BSC-CNS with a total peak computational power of 314 PFlops.
- BSC materials also state that MareNostrum 5 includes large-scale general-purpose and accelerated partitions and multi-hundred-petabyte storage and archive capacity, documenting substantial HPC infrastructure on Spanish territory.

Sources:
- RedIRIS, "About RedIRIS"
- Red.es, "What we do"
- Barcelona Supercomputing Center, "MareNostrum 5 Technical information"

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- Red.es states that the ".es" domain contributes identity, security, and confidence to websites used by companies, professionals, brands, and citizens in Spain.
- Red.es materials surfaced via official site search results state that Red.es is the Spanish authority entrusted with assigning and managing ".es" domain names.
- Red.es also states that domain management forms part of its public digital-infrastructure mandate alongside research-network and digital-public-service programmes.
- The current source set therefore documents registry governance for the Spanish country-code namespace more clearly than it documents broader national internet-exchange authority, and it does not support stronger institutional claims about exchange-layer control beyond the cited registry functions.

Sources:
- Red.es, "What we do"
- Red.es domain-governance materials surfaced via official site search results
- Red.es, "About us"

## Telecommunications and Digital Infrastructure

- Ministry for Digital Transformation materials state that Spain closed the fixed digital divide in 2023 by guaranteeing access to broadband of at least 100 Mbps to 100% of the population.
- The same ministry materials state that 5G coverage reached 92.3% of the population in 2023 and 68.9% in rural areas, with public investment targeted at extending 5G deployment in municipalities with fewer than 10,000 inhabitants.
- Ministry materials also state that 92.5% of households and businesses had access to speeds above 1 Gbps, placing Spain above the European average on those connectivity indicators.
- Red.es states that it works on 5G spectrum initiatives, rural broadband deployment, RedIRIS Nova 100, and broader infrastructure capillarity and quality across the territory.
- Taken together, the current source set documents a ministry-linked, publicly coordinated national digital-infrastructure programme with strong broadband and 5G coverage indicators, without assigning sovereign telecom-routing authority beyond the cited public infrastructure programmes.

Sources:
- Ministry for Digital Transformation and Public Function, "In 2023 Spain closed the fixed digital divide and 5G mobile coverage reached 92% of the population"
- Red.es, "What we do"

## Energy and Grid Integration Infrastructure

- Red Eléctrica states that international interconnections are a fundamental tool to end Spain’s energy isolation and are strategic infrastructures supporting electricity systems increasingly based on renewable energy sources.
- Red Eléctrica states that the Spanish mainland electricity system is currently connected to those of France, Portugal, and Morocco.
- Red Eléctrica states that the Bay of Biscay interconnection with France is a project of common interest of the European Commission and is expected to increase exchange capacity and improve the security, stability, and quality of supply for both systems and the rest of Europe.
- Red Eléctrica also states that additional interconnection with Portugal increases exchange capacity and supports reliability, renewable integration, and European climate-plan objectives.

Sources:
- Red Eléctrica, "Interconnections"

## Logistics and Global Connectivity Infrastructure

- Aena states that its network includes 46 airports in Spain and two heliports, documenting a nationally coordinated airport system with broad territorial coverage.
- ADIF states that Spain’s railway network comprises 11,672 km, 1,448 stations, and 45 freight terminals, and that traffic is coordinated through a network of traffic-management centres and control and regulation centres.
- ADIF states that Spain has developed high-speed rail traffic management through the DaVinci platform and related control and regulation centres, documenting a nationally administered rail-operations stack.
- Puertos del Estado states that Spain is the European Union country with the longest coastline and that the state-owned port system includes 46 ports of general interest managed by 28 port authorities.
- Puertos del Estado states that it is responsible for implementation of government port policy and the coordination and efficiency control of the state-owned port system under the Ministry of Transport and Sustainable Mobility.
- The current source set therefore documents Spain as a multimodal logistics jurisdiction with institutionally coordinated airport, rail, and port infrastructure.

Sources:
- Aena, "The Aena network"
- ADIF, "Railway Network"
- Puertos del Estado, "Institutional"

## Cybersecurity and Digital Resilience Coordination

- INCIBE-CERT states that it is the reference security incident response centre for citizens and private-law entities in Spain and that it coordinates with national and international teams to improve efficiency in responding to crimes involving networks and information systems.
- INCIBE-CERT states that incident management affecting critical private-sector operators is jointly operated by INCIBE and the Cybersecurity Coordination Office of the Ministry of the Interior.
- CCN-CERT materials surfaced via official site search results state that CCN-CERT is responsible for countering cyberattacks on classified systems, public administrations, and companies and organisations in strategic sectors essential to Spanish security and the economy.
- CCN materials surfaced via official site search results also state that the National Cryptologic Centre coordinates public-administration cryptologic and information-technology security activities.
- The current source set therefore documents a split but complementary resilience structure in which public-administration and strategic-sector cyber functions are distinguished from citizen and private-law response roles.

Sources:

- CCN-CERT, "Mission and objectives" materials surfaced via official site search results
- CCN-CERT, "FAQ" materials surfaced via official site search results

## Advanced Technology and Semiconductor Infrastructure

- Ministry of Economy materials state that PERTE Chip was approved on 24 May 2022 as Spain’s strategic project for the recovery and transformation of microelectronics and semiconductors.
- The same ministry materials state that PERTE Chip aims to reinforce Spain’s design and production capacities across the semiconductor value chain, including conception, design, chip production, and revitalisation of the electronics and ICT industries.
- Ministry materials state that PERTE Chip has a €12.25 billion budget funded through the recovery plan and related instruments.
- IMB-CNM-CSIC states that it is the largest centre in Spain dedicated to research and development in micro and nano technologies for electronic applications and that it has unique capabilities in silicon technology and related materials for chip manufacturing for microsystems and integrated circuits.
- IMB-CNM-CSIC also states that it belongs to the Spanish National Research Council and is accredited as a María de Maeztu Unit of Excellence for 2024-2028.
- The current source set therefore documents an officially backed semiconductor-capacity-building strategy alongside a substantial domestic microelectronics research base, without supporting claims that Spain already operates a complete sovereign fabrication stack at leading-edge scale.

Sources:
- Ministry of Economy, "PERTE de Microelectrónica y Semiconductores"
- IMB-CNM-CSIC, "About the center"

## EU, Atlantic, and Mediterranean Corridor Integration

- European Commission materials place Spain inside the euro area and identify Banco de España within the institutional architecture of the single currency.
- PAe materials state that Spain maintains the Spanish node of the European electronic-identity recognition system through the eIDAS node.
- European Commission rail materials state that the Atlantic corridor crosses Spain, Portugal, France, and Germany and that the Mediterranean corridor crosses Spain, France, Italy, Slovenia, Croatia, and Hungary.
- European Commission materials on Spain’s rail deployment state that Spain’s trans-European rail posture is organised around both Atlantic and Mediterranean corridors and that ADIF manages the national network within that framework.
- European Commission Mediterranean-corridor materials state that, in Spain, most lines planned by 2023 were already in operation, including one cross-border section with France, and that all Spanish MED sections are planned to be operational by 2030.
- European Commission Atlantic-corridor materials state that some Spanish Atlantic-corridor lines are already in operation and that most planned Spanish ATL sections are targeted by 2030, subject to listed exceptions.
- RedIRIS and Red.es materials place Spain’s academic networking inside broader European research-network cooperation without making corridor-authority claims beyond the documented network relationships.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- European Commission and Banco de España materials place Spain’s monetary and payment-settlement functions inside euro-area, TARGET, and TIPS structures rather than a wholly independent national settlement architecture.
- PAe materials place part of Spain’s digital-identity and signature interoperability surface inside shared national components and the European eIDAS-recognition layer rather than a self-contained national cross-border identity framework.
- Spain 2024 digital-public-administration materials surfaced via official site search results place inter-administration communications and shared services inside Red SARA, a federated network spanning central, regional, and local public bodies rather than a single monolithic state stack.
- European Commission rail materials state that Spain operates both Iberian gauge and UIC gauge high-speed sections, documenting interoperability complexity inside the national rail system.
- Red Eléctrica states that interconnections are necessary to reduce Spain’s energy isolation, which directly implies continuing dependence on cross-border electricity interfaces.
- IMB-CNM-CSIC and PERTE Chip materials document research capacity and strategic industrial policy, but the current source set does not document a complete domestic leading-edge semiconductor manufacturing stack.
- Red.es materials document ".es" registry governance clearly, but the current source set does not provide equally strong institutional evidence for broader Spanish authority over internet-exchange infrastructure.
- BSC and RedIRIS materials document strong research-network and supercomputing capacity, but they do not by themselves document an entirely sovereign hyperscale cloud stack.

## Summary

The current source set documents Spain as a euro-area, ministry-coordinated digital and infrastructure jurisdiction with shared public-administration identity and signature platforms, Banco de España participation in TARGET and TIPS, Red.es and RedIRIS-linked digital and research infrastructure, substantial airport, rail, and port coordination, cross-border electricity interconnections, dual-track cyber-resilience institutions, and an officially backed semiconductor-capacity programme anchored by domestic research capability. The evidence places Spain inside strongly European-connected monetary, transport, identity, research, and energy systems while supporting only bounded claims about sovereign control, industrial depth, and continuity posture beyond the cited institutional materials.
