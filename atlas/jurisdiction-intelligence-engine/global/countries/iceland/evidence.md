# Iceland — Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- Digital Iceland states that the Icelandic government aims to make digital services the main means of communication between its agencies and the Icelandic people, and that Digital Iceland works across ministries and government agencies under the Ministry of Finance and Economic Affairs.
- Ísland.is states that it is a central public information and service hub for government agencies in Iceland and a single source of truth about government services for the public.
- Central Bank of Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Bank’s oversight of financial infrastructure is aimed at promoting security, efficiency, and efficacy of core infrastructure in the Icelandic financial system, and that the oversight basis draws on the PFMI framework.
- Farice states that it operates the FARICE-1, DANICE, and IRIS submarine cable systems and offers connectivity between Iceland, the Faeroe Islands, the UK, Ireland, mainland Europe, and North America via Greenland Connect. Míla states that its Network Operations Centre monitors connections tying Iceland with Europe and North America around the clock.
- Invest in Iceland states that Iceland has reliable power infrastructure, renewable power sources, year-round ambient cooling conditions for data centers, and redundant overseas and backhaul connectivity. Verne states that its Iceland campus uses 100% renewable energy and offers direct high-capacity connectivity to the United States and Europe.
- RHnet states that it links Icelandic universities and research institutions through a high-capacity network and serves as their gateway to international networks, including NORDUnet.
- Government of Iceland and Electronic Communications Office of Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results document a cyber security strategy and action plan, the NCC-IS coordination function, and communications-security supervision functions within the Icelandic state.
- Government of Iceland, NIIS, RHnet, Farice, and RIX materials place Iceland inside Nordic, European, and North Atlantic digital, scientific, and connectivity structures without providing a basis for assigning corridor authority or topology role.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- Digital Iceland states that Ísland.is is a central public information and service hub for government agencies in Iceland and a centralized service portal for government services.
- Digital Iceland states that government agency websites are subsites of Ísland.is.
- Digital Iceland states that My Pages allows individuals and legal entities to securely access data from government agencies in one place, while the agencies themselves handle the reception, storage, and processing of the underlying data.
- Digital Iceland states that the Digital Mailbox gives individuals and legal entities their own mailbox for government messages and personal information, and that implemented institutions use it as part of the government’s digital communication channel.
- Digital Iceland states that the authentication system supports three electronic-ID methods: electronic ID on a phone, the Auðkenni app, and an Auðkenni card.
- Digital Iceland also states that passkeys in the Ísland.is app can be used to sign into websites that rely on the Ísland.is authentication service, including My Pages and the Application system.
- Digital Iceland states that Straumurinn (X-Road) is a distributed data-exchange system without centralized data storage, that it enables secure and reliable sharing of data between information systems, and that all public institutions and municipalities in Iceland can use it.
- Digital Iceland states that Straumurinn supports publication to My Pages and use of the Ísland.is Application System, and that there are already over one hundred users of Straumurinn in Iceland.

## Financial Infrastructure and Payment Systems

- Central Bank of Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Bank’s oversight of financial market infrastructure is aimed at promoting security, efficiency, and efficacy of core infrastructure in the Icelandic financial system.
- Central Bank of Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results state that the PFMI criteria form the basis for the Bank’s oversight and operation of systemically important financial market infrastructure in Iceland.
- Central Bank of Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results state that the interbank payment system is subject to Rules no. 1030/2020 and is divided into two components: the gross settlement component (RTGS) and the retail component (EXP).
- Central Bank of Iceland payment-instrument materials surfaced via official site search results state that payment cards and electronic payment instruments are commonly used for public transactions in Icelandic krónur.
- Central Bank of Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results state that, as of 25 November 2024, the Bank has a leading role in implementing a centralized domestic infrastructure for payment requests based on account-to-account payments.
- The current source set documents central-bank oversight, domestic interbank infrastructure, and payment-modernization work, but does not provide an official basis for inferring global settlement authority.

## Data and Digital Infrastructure

- Invest in Iceland states that Iceland has one of the world’s most reliable power infrastructures and renewable power sources for data centers, and that year-round ambient cooling is possible because of local climate conditions.
- The same Invest in Iceland materials state that Iceland is connected to North America and Europe through high-bandwidth submarine fiber cables operated by Farice and that both overseas connections and backhaul are redundant.
- Verne states that its Iceland data center uses 100% renewable energy and supports high-density colocation, HPC, and high-intensity compute workloads.
- Verne states that its Iceland campus has capacity of over 140 MW and direct high-capacity connectivity to the United States and Europe.
- Digital Iceland’s service catalog documents national digital-service infrastructure that includes the Application system, Authorisation system, Service System, Digital Mailbox, Notifications, digital certificates, the Ísland.is mobile app, and the Admin system.
- Digital Iceland states that the Application system is intended to manage main application types across organizations in order to help ensure user experience across organizations.
- The current source set documents commercial colocation, renewable-energy-linked data-center infrastructure, and national digital-service platforms; it does not directly document a sovereign hyperscale cloud stack.

## Research Network and Compute Infrastructure

- RHnet states that it was formally established on 24 January 2001 to link Icelandic universities and research institutions through a high-capacity computer network and provide communications services domestically and internationally.
- RHnet states that it serves as the gateway of the Icelandic university and research community to international networks.
- RHnet states that it handles relations with NORDUnet, the collective university and research network of the Nordic countries.
- RHnet states that it is based on the principle of exclusive service to acknowledged Icelandic institutions of research and higher learning and lists connected institutions across the university and research sector.
- The current source set directly documents RHnet’s national research-network role and its Nordic/international connectivity functions more clearly than separate sovereign compute or HPC coordination infrastructure.

- RHnet, "RHnet -- Icelandic University Research Network"

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- ISNIC states that it concentrates on operating the .is country-code top-level domain and the Reykjavik Internet Exchange.
- ISNIC states that it inherited the management role for the .is ccTLD and has registered .is domains since 1988.
- ISNIC states that .is was one of the first ccTLDs to establish a completely automated registration system, in 2001.
- ISNIC states that .is domain registration is backed by a set of nameservers distributed around the world and synchronized every 20 minutes with the authoritative .is delegation records.
- RIX states that it is a public internet exchange in Reykjavík, has been operating since 1999, and was the first open internet exchange in Iceland.
- RIX states that all major Icelandic internet service providers are connected, that connection speeds include 1G, 10G, and 100G Ethernet, and that the exchange operates from three Reykjavík locations.
- RIX states that it is a service provided by Internet á Íslandi hf. (ISNIC).

## Telecommunications and Submarine Connectivity Infrastructure

- Farice states that it operates the FARICE-1 submarine cable between Iceland and Scotland with a branch to the Faroe Islands, the DANICE submarine cable between Iceland and Denmark, and the IRIS cable to Ireland.
- Farice states that it offers data services between Iceland, the Faeroe Islands, the UK, Ireland, and mainland Europe via IRIS, DANICE, and FARICE-1, and that customers can select among three submarine routes between Iceland and Europe.
- Farice states that it offers capacity between Iceland and North America on the Greenland Connect submarine route, with normal delivery in Halifax and optional locations including Boston, New York, and Montreal.
- Farice also states that, in cooperation with Tele Greenland, it can offer transatlantic circuits from Europe to North America over Greenland Connect.
- Míla states that nearly all companies and households in Iceland are connected to its access network, that its trunk network includes all urban cores in Iceland, and that its main fiber network is 1,800 km with more than 4,000 km of optical fiber in the trunk network.
- Míla states that it operates over 500 technical facilities across Iceland and that its Network Operations Centre monitors telecommunications systems and the connections tying Iceland with Europe and North America around the clock.
- Síminn states that it has built mobile networks in Iceland for nearly four decades and that its 5G network is based on Ericsson equipment.
- Nova materials surfaced via official site search results state that Nova’s network offers 5G coverage across Iceland.
- Vodafone Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results state that the company has built extensive 3G and 4G networks throughout Iceland.

## Energy and Industrial Infrastructure

- Landsvirkjun states that it operates fourteen hydropower stations, three geothermal power stations, and two wind turbines for research purposes in five operating areas in Iceland.
- Invest in Iceland states that 100% of electricity production and space heating in Iceland comes from renewable hydro or geothermal resources.
- The same Invest in Iceland materials state that Iceland’s transmission grid connecting hydro and geothermal power plants is circular and includes redundant routes intended to maximize uptime.
- Landsnet materials surfaced via official site search results state that Landsnet operates Iceland’s electricity transmission system and that the grid receives electricity directly from power stations and transmits it to distributors and power-intensive users around the country.
- Verne states that its Iceland campus is powered by hydroelectric and geothermal energy and combines that energy base with year-round free cooling.
- The current source set documents renewable-energy-powered data infrastructure and national transmission coordination functions, but does not provide an official basis for assigning energy-corridor authority.

## Logistics and Global Connectivity Infrastructure

- Keflavík Airport states that the airport is geographically well located to serve as a cargo hub between east and west.
- Keflavík Airport cargo materials list dedicated cargo airlines, operators, and handling links and direct users to cargo departures and arrivals services.
- Keflavík Airport states that its cargo masterplan includes a Diamond Gate area with new cargo aircraft apron area, cargo facilities, freight-forwarding facilities, and administrative facilities.
- Keflavík Airport states that the Diamond Gate area is designed so cargo traffic does not need to circulate through the terminal area and that its proximity to the terminal apron supports cargo split between dedicated freighters and passenger aircraft.
- Keflavík Airport destination materials state that the airport maintains direct-flight destination listings, while official flight-information materials surfaced via site search results document live arrivals and departures functions.
- The current source set documents transatlantic aviation and cargo-continuity infrastructure at Keflavík without providing an official basis for assigning global logistics dominance.

## Nordic, European, and North Atlantic Integration

- Government of Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results state that Iceland is a member of the European Economic Area and that the EEA Agreement entered into force on 1 January 1994 for the EU and the three EEA EFTA states: Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway.
- NIIS states that Iceland is a strategic member alongside Estonia and Finland, and that NIIS is responsible for the development and strategic management of X-Road and other cross-border digital-government components.
- Digital Iceland states that Straumurinn (X-Road) in Iceland began formal implementation in 2018 under a cooperation agreement with Finland and Estonia, and that Iceland is a member of NIIS.
- RHnet states that it handles relations with NORDUnet, placing Iceland’s research-network infrastructure within Nordic research-network coordination.
- Farice and Míla materials document direct telecommunications links from Iceland toward Europe and North America, while RIX documents an open Icelandic exchange environment with major national ISPs connected.
- Government of Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results also document Iceland’s presidency of the Nordic Council of Ministers in 2023, placing it within formal Nordic cooperation structures.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- Statistics Iceland materials surfaced via official site search results place Iceland’s population at 389,444 at the beginning of 2025, framing the national infrastructure base within a small-population scale.
- Farice documents three submarine routes between Iceland and Europe plus Greenland Connect capacity toward North America, while Míla documents continuous monitoring of links to Europe and North America; together these materials indicate that international telecommunications continuity depends on submarine-cable systems.
- Central Bank of Iceland materials document domestic interbank infrastructure, payment instruments, and payment-request modernization, but the current source set does not document an Icelandic global settlement role beyond those domestic and oversight functions.
- Government of Iceland, EEA, NIIS, and RHnet materials place important parts of Iceland’s digital, scientific, and market integration within cross-border European and Nordic institutional frameworks rather than a fully self-contained national stack.
- The current source set documents commercial data-center infrastructure and renewable-energy-powered colocation environments, but it does not document a sovereign semiconductor fabrication stack.
- The current source set documents RHnet and commercial high-intensity compute hosting, but it does not document a sovereign hyperscale cloud stack or extensive sovereign AI/HPC infrastructure.
- The current source set documents digital-government, payments, exchange, naming, telecom, data-center, energy, logistics, cyber, and research-network infrastructure, but it does not provide an official basis for readiness tiers, routing authority, or topology-role claims.

## Summary

The current source set documents Iceland as a North Atlantic connectivity and renewable-energy infrastructure jurisdiction combining submarine-cable systems, renewable-energy-powered digital infrastructure, digital-government coordination, domestic payment and central-bank infrastructure, research-network participation, cyber-coordination functions, and Nordic/European institutional integration. The evidence places Digital Iceland, the Central Bank of Iceland, Farice, Míla, RHnet, ISNIC, RIX, Landsvirkjun, Landsnet, Verne, Keflavík Airport, and related coordination structures inside a nationally administered but strongly cross-border-connected environment without supporting readiness tiers, routing authority, or Atlas interpretation beyond the cited materials.
