# Argentina - Evidence Layer

## Strategic Position

- Argentina.gob.ar states that Mi Argentina is the citizen’s digital profile for managing procedures, appointments, credentials, and personalized information, and describes it as an easy and secure way to access state digital services with a single session and in one place.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that Trámites a Distancia (TAD) is the platform through which people can complete procedures before national public bodies in a 100% digital manner.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that GDE is an integrated system for filing, numbering, tracking, and registering the movement of all proceedings and case files across the National Public Sector, documenting a shared state document-management core.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that Interoperar and the broader interoperability framework are designed to enable secure exchange of documents and data between public bodies while preserving the authentic source and reducing repeated submissions by citizens.
- Banco Central de la República Argentina states that the MEP is its real-time gross settlement system, while BCRA also states that Transferencias 3.0 reformulated immediate transfers into a 24/7 environment with online crediting in up to 15 seconds and interoperable QR-based payment use cases.
- ARSAT states that it has implemented the Red Federal de Fibra Óptica since 2010 to expand broadband quality and territorial reach, and that its Benavídez datacenter carries TIER III certifications for design and construction.
- NIC Argentina identifies itself as the official registry for .ar domains, while CABASE states that its IXPs are fundamental internet components operating under a cooperative model.
- Argentina.gob.ar documents a national cyber-coordination surface through the Centro Nacional de Ciberseguridad and CERT.ar, while MERCOSUR materials surfaced via official site search results place Argentina inside the bloc’s digital-agenda and mutual digital-signature-recognition structures.

## Digital Governance and Identity Infrastructure

- Argentina.gob.ar states that Mi Argentina is the citizen’s digital profile and provides a single-session access point to state digital services.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that validated Mi Argentina users can access additional state-linked credentials and services, including digital DNI, digital driver’s license, vehicle credentials, personalized procedure tracking, and single access across state services.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that identity validation for Mi Argentina can be completed in the application through a camera-based workflow, and that in-person validation through Punto Digital remains available when app-based validation is unsuccessful.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that TAD allows procedures to be initiated digitally before national public bodies and that access can be made through ARCA credentials, DNI and trámite number, ANSES social-security credentials, Mi Argentina, and NIC for non-residents.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that GDE is the integrated electronic document-management system of the National Public Sector and that TAD is one of its modules for citizen interaction with the administration.
- Decree 561/2016 states that GDE was implemented as the integrated system for case-file and document management across the National Public Sector and frames it as part of a modernization policy intended to accelerate procedures, increase transparency, facilitate access to information, and enable integration and interoperability.
- Decree 182/2019 states that Argentina’s digital-signature law establishes a federal digital-signature infrastructure and that digital signature use is mandatory for transactions of the National Public Administration, while also identifying electronic official documents, electronic case files, electronic notifications, TAD, and GDE as part of the operative environment.
- The current source set therefore documents a national digital-governance environment centered on Mi Argentina identity, TAD procedure intake, GDE document circulation, and a federal digital-signature legal framework.

## Financial Infrastructure and Settlement Systems

- Banco Central de la República Argentina states that the MEP is a real-time gross settlement system that processes bilateral transactions between participants with debit and credit entries in their accounts at the BCRA.
- Banco Central de la República Argentina states that accepted MEP transactions become irrevocable once they pass the BCRA’s validations, including verification of available funds.
- BCRA materials surfaced via official site search results state that Argentina’s payment system is structured through the MEP administered by the central bank.
- Banco Central de la República Argentina states that Transferencias 3.0 is a BCRA initiative that reformulated immediate transfers as electronic fund transfers with online crediting in up to 15 seconds between bank or virtual accounts, available every day of the year at all hours.
- Banco Central de la República Argentina states that Transferencias 3.0 includes push transfers, pull transfers, payments with transfers, and a not-yet-implemented extraction-with-transfer function.
- The same BCRA materials state that payments with transfer support interoperable QR payments, immediate settlement to bank or payment accounts, irrevocability once funds are received by the merchant, and access through either bank accounts with CBU or payment-provider accounts with CVU.
- The current source set therefore documents a central-bank-operated RTGS core together with a national immediate-transfer and interoperable digital-payments framework.
- The current source set does not support broader claims about international settlement centrality beyond the Argentina-centered perimeter described by the cited BCRA materials.

## Data Infrastructure and Digital Continuity

- Argentina.gob.ar states that interoperability is essential to public digital transformation because it enables secure exchange of documents and data between bodies, reduces repeated submissions by citizens, and preserves the authentic source without generating unnecessary replicas.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that Interoperar is a secure information-exchange platform between nodes of the National Public Administration, organized as a decentralized network of MSI nodes, and that access to shared data is controlled through validations, electronic authentication, and digital certificates.
- Argentina.gob.ar also states that the national government invites provinces and municipalities to enter cooperation agreements around interoperability, documenting an outward expansion surface beyond the federal core.
- Argentina.gob.ar news materials state that the national interoperability network comprises 17 nodes installed and configured in public bodies and integrates 167 systems and services that interoperate in different modalities.
- The same news materials state that interoperability work between the Subsecretaría de Innovación Administrativa, VUCE, and the Dirección General de Aduana enables secure and auditable exchange of LPCO information, reduces duplicated information, supports dematerialization, and enables interoperability at international scales in the trade-procedure context.
- Decree 561/2016 states that GDE is intended to accelerate procedures, increase transparency, facilitate information access, and enable system integration and interoperability across the National Public Sector.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that TAD is the platform for fully digital interactions with national public bodies, while the INDAP program is intended to strengthen the technical and governance foundations of open-data policy across the National Public Sector through metadata, licensing, high-value-data, standards, and coordinated implementation roles.
- ARSAT states that its Benavídez datacenter is certified TIER III for design and construction, providing evidence of a certified state-linked hosting and infrastructure environment.
- The current source set therefore documents a layered public-sector data environment combining interoperable exchange rails, electronic case-file and document systems, digital procedure intake, open-data policy infrastructure, and certified datacenter capacity.
- The current source set does not document a wholly sovereign national hyperscale cloud stack independent of mixed public, institutional, and service-provider infrastructure.

## Research Network and Compute Infrastructure

- Argentina.gob.ar states that the Redes Avanzadas program is intended to contribute to improvement of the national advanced network INNOVARED and to align its actions with the needs of scientific and technological institutions.
- CONICET states that a national agreement with ARSAT was signed to expand InnovaRed across the country within the national e-Ciencia program.
- CONICET states that InnovaRed had been functioning as a trunk network since 2013 and that the expansion objective was to extend connectivity across the whole country.
- CONICET states that InnovaRed connected Buenos Aires, Rosario, Córdoba, San Luis, Villa Mercedes, Mendoza, Malargüe, La Plata, Bahía Blanca, Choele Choel, Neuquén, and Bariloche at the time described.
- CONICET further states that InnovaRed enables research and development projects for 13 national science and technology organizations, 42 national public universities, four private universities, and two international facilities: the Pierre Auger Observatory and Deep Space 3.
- The current source set therefore documents a nationally coordinated research-network layer with scientific, university, and international-facility connectivity.
- The current source set does not, however, document a named national high-performance-compute anchor comparable to the dedicated HPC facilities cited in some other jurisdiction files.

## Internet Exchange and Registry Infrastructure

- NIC Argentina identifies itself as the official registry of .ar domains, documenting a nationally administered domain-registration environment.
- NIC Argentina’s homepage also exposes functions for domain search, registration, renewals, transfers, and user support, indicating an operational registry layer rather than a purely symbolic governance role.
- CABASE states that IXPs are fundamental components of the Internet through which traffic is exchanged among networks of operators, access providers, government bodies, academic entities, and others.
- CABASE states that all CABASE IXPs follow a cooperative model and that their stated aims include improving routing efficiency, improving service quality, and minimizing interconnection costs.
- The current source set therefore documents a national registry surface through NIC Argentina and a cooperative internet-exchange model through CABASE.
- The current source set does not provide a comparably strong basis for exhaustive claims about the national exchange topology or relative global exchange centrality beyond those documented registry and cooperative-IXP functions.

## Telecommunications and Digital Infrastructure

- ARSAT states that it has implemented the Red Federal de Fibra Óptica (REFEFO) since 2010, with the stated purpose of increasing the quality and reach of broadband throughout the national territory.

- CONICET states that the expansion of InnovaRed was signed together with ARSAT, showing an interface between national scientific-network development and state communications infrastructure.
- The current source set therefore documents a public-sector digital-infrastructure layer centered on federal fiber expansion and certified datacenter capacity.
- The current source set does not document the full telecommunications market or a comprehensive nationwide 5G deployment picture through the cited materials.

## Logistics and Global Connectivity Infrastructure

- Argentina.gob.ar states that Puerto Buenos Aires has annual capacity of 1.5 million TEUs, handles more than 62% of the country’s container cargo, receives approximately 1,200 ships per year, and functions as a multimodal port connected to the rest of the country through truck, rail, and river transport.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that VUCE is a single-window foreign-trade tool intended to facilitate trade, reduce time and costs, and increase transparency and controls.
- Argentina.gob.ar news materials state that interoperability work between TAD, GDE, VUCE, and the customs service supports LPCO exchange and harmonized data handling for foreign-trade procedures.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that the new Ezeiza cargo terminal was designed to optimize air-cargo processes, improve operational efficiency, and incorporate technology aligned with international standards.
- The same Ezeiza materials state that the project reduced merchandise assembly costs by 30%, doubled storage volume, and reduced truck waiting times.
- The current source set therefore documents both maritime and air-logistics anchors, together with a digital trade-facilitation layer for external trade procedures.
- The cited materials support these logistics and trade-facilitation descriptions without establishing broader routing-role claims beyond the documented throughput, capacity, and system-integration functions.

## Cybersecurity and Digital Resilience Coordination

- Argentina.gob.ar states that the Centro Nacional de Ciberseguridad designs policies intended to protect critical information infrastructures and to build and improve national capacities for prevention, detection, response, and recovery from information-security incidents.
- Argentina.gob.ar states that CERT.ar is the national computer emergency response team and that it works on the technical-administrative management of computer-security incidents in the National Public Sector.
- CERT.ar materials also document public reporting and publication functions, including incident reporting, prevention materials, good-practice guidance, and recommendations.
- The current source set therefore documents a public-sector cyber-coordination surface that combines policy design for critical information infrastructure with a named incident-response function.

## MERCOSUR and Global Institutional Integration

- MERCOSUR materials surfaced via official site search results state that the Grupo Agenda Digital del MERCOSUR (GAD) was established in 2017 with the objective of promoting development of a digital MERCOSUR through biennial action plans containing common policies and initiatives.
- MERCOSUR materials surfaced via official site search results state that one of the bloc’s digital-agenda achievements was approval of the agreement on mutual recognition of digital-signature certificates, alongside digital-policy measures such as roaming-charge elimination.
- MERCOSUR materials surfaced via official site search results state that the digital-signature-recognition agreement supports exchange of fiscal and customs documents, contract signing among companies established in different member countries, product traceability, and automatic recognition of electronic documents produced from certificates issued within official infrastructures of each country.
- Argentina.gob.ar news materials state that interoperability work linked to VUCE, TAD, and customs supports interoperability at international scales in the trade-procedure context.
- CONICET states that InnovaRed serves international facilities including Pierre Auger and Deep Space 3, while Puerto Buenos Aires and Ezeiza cargo materials document international maritime and air-cargo connectivity.
- The current source set therefore places Argentina inside regional digital-governance structures, internationalized trade-procedure interfaces, and wider scientific and logistics networks.

## Constraints and Structural Limitations

- BCRA materials document MEP and Transferencias 3.0 as domestic payment and settlement infrastructure, but the current source set does not support broader claims about international monetary or settlement centrality beyond the Argentina-centered perimeter described by those materials.
- Mi Argentina, TAD, GDE, Interoperar, INDAP, and ARSAT’s certified datacenter document a meaningful public digital infrastructure environment, but the current source set does not document a wholly sovereign national hyperscale cloud stack.
- The cited research-network materials document InnovaRed clearly, but the current source set does not document a named national high-performance-compute anchor or a broader sovereign compute estate spanning multiple sectors.
- NIC Argentina and CABASE document registry administration and a cooperative IXP model, but the current source set does not establish exhaustive claims about the full national exchange topology or broader global exchange centrality.
- CAMMESA search-result materials document SADI-centered grid coordination, but the current source set does not establish broader claims about cross-border electricity authority or energy-corridor primacy.
- The cyber sources clearly document public-sector coordination through the Centro Nacional de Ciberseguridad and CERT.ar, but do not provide comparable visibility into all-sector cyber-governance or certification architecture.
- These constraints describe the boundaries of the currently cited evidence only and should not be treated as negative judgments.

## Summary

- The current source set documents Argentina as a federally structured digital-governance jurisdiction built around Mi Argentina identity, TAD procedure intake, GDE electronic document management, and a federal digital-signature framework.
- It documents Argentina as a central-bank-operated payment jurisdiction with the MEP RTGS core and the Transferencias 3.0 immediate-transfer and interoperable QR-payment environment.
- It documents a public-sector data and continuity environment through Interoperar, GDE, TAD, INDAP, and ARSAT’s certified datacenter infrastructure.
- It documents a national research-network layer through INNOVARED and a public digital-infrastructure layer through ARSAT’s fiber backbone and datacenter assets.
- It documents registry, exchange, logistics, cyber, and grid-coordination functions through NIC Argentina, CABASE, Puerto Buenos Aires, Ezeiza cargo infrastructure, the Centro Nacional de Ciberseguridad, CERT.ar, and CAMMESA.
- It also places Argentina inside MERCOSUR digital-integration structures and wider international research and trade connectivity.
- The cited evidence supports these infrastructure characterizations without assigning readiness tiers, routing authority, corridor status, or broader Atlas interpretation beyond the institutional materials.
