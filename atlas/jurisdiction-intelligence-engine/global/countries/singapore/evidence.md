# Singapore — Evidence Layer

## AI Governance Signals

- Smart Nation records the original National AI Strategy publication in November 2019 and states that National AI Strategy 2.0 launched in 2023.
- Smart Nation states Singapore is driving broad-based AI adoption across government agencies to improve operational efficiency and service delivery.
- IMDA states A.I. Verify was launched in 2022 as an AI governance testing framework and toolkit for objective and verifiable responsible-AI testing.
- IMDA states A.I. Verify follows Singapore’s Model AI Governance Framework and was developed by IMDA and PDPC.
- IMDA states the AI Verify Foundation was launched in 2023 to develop AI testing tools through a global open-source community for companies and regulators.
- IMDA states the draft Model AI Governance Framework for Generative AI was developed by AI Verify Foundation and IMDA, expands the earlier model framework, and is intended to support trusted and interoperable governance.
- PDPC’s 2024 advisory guidelines address how organisations should be transparent when AI systems use personal data to make recommendations, predictions, or decisions.

## Privacy and Data Governance Architecture

- IMDA states PDPC was set up in 2013 to administer and enforce the Personal Data Protection Act.
- IMDA states the PDPA governs the collection, use, disclosure, and care of personal data in Singapore.
- IMDA states PDPC is part of IMDA and promotes and regulates data protection while also providing AI-governance guidance to private-sector organisations.
- Singapore Statutes Online publishes the Personal Data Protection Act 2012 as the governing statute.
- PDPC states personal data may be transferred to another country only in accordance with regulatory requirements to ensure protection comparable to the PDPA, unless exempted.
- PDPC’s AI recommendation and decision systems guidance ties AI deployment using personal data back to transparency and data-governance obligations.

## Digital Asset and Financial Technology Oversight Signals

- MAS states the Payment Services Act 2019 provides licensing and regulation for payment service providers and oversight of payment systems in Singapore, while providing regulatory certainty and consumer safeguards.
- MAS states amendments effective from 4 April 2024 expanded regulated payment services to include DPT custodial services, facilitation of DPT transmission and exchange, and certain cross-border money-transfer activities.
- MAS states those amendments also empower MAS to impose AML/CFT, user-protection, and financial-stability requirements on DPT service providers, including safeguarding customer assets.
- MAS Notice PS-N02 sets AML/CFT requirements for DPT service providers, including customer due diligence, suspicious transaction reporting, record-keeping, and internal controls.
- MAS states its stablecoin framework applies to qualifying single-currency stablecoins issued in Singapore and imposes requirements on reserve assets, capital, redemption at par, and disclosure.
- MAS states Phase 3 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2022, commenced on 30 June 2025, implements a framework for Singapore-established or incorporated digital token service providers serving outside-Singapore activity.
- MAS states Project Guardian is a cross-border sandbox bringing policymakers, regulators, and industry together to test asset tokenisation and interoperable market infrastructure.

## Compute and Digital Infrastructure Signals

- Smart Nation presents a whole-of-nation digital transformation programme oriented toward a shared digital future.
- GovTech states it is the lead government agency driving Singapore’s Smart Nation initiative and the digitalisation of public services.
- GovTech states data and AI are used inside government for evidence-based policymaking, operational efficiency, and secure adoption of large language models.
- IMDA states the Digital Connectivity Blueprint sets strategic priorities for 10 Gbps domestic connectivity, infrastructure resilience, security, green data-centre growth, and wider digital-utility adoption.
- IMDA states data centres are foundational digital infrastructure within Singapore’s digital economy.
- IMDA states the Green DC Roadmap aims to provide at least 300 MW of additional data-centre capacity in the near term, with more through green energy deployments.

## Telecom, Submarine Cable, and Interconnection Signals

- IMDA states it develops and regulates the infocomm and media sectors and operates Singapore’s telecom licensing framework.
- IMDA’s FBO framework covers operators deploying telecommunications networks, systems, and facilities, while the SBO framework covers service-based telecommunications operators.
- IMDA states the Digital Connectivity Blueprint targets capacity to enable submarine cable landings to double within the next 10 years.
- IMDA states it guides parties deploying submarine cable systems into Singapore and requires a permitting process for submarine cable works.
- SGIX describes itself as an open and neutral Internet exchange established in 2009 with IMDA support.
- SGIX states it operates peering points across Singapore’s major data centres, reinforcing Singapore’s exchange-layer interconnection role.

## Energy and Sustainability Constraints

- IMDA states data centres are power- and resource-intensive and frames sustainable data-centre growth as a core governance issue.
- IMDA’s Green DC Roadmap pairs capacity growth with sustainability measures including GMDC:2024, energy-efficiency grants, and new technical standards.
- IMDA states Singapore Standard SS 715:2025 is intended to support higher-temperature and more energy-efficient IT equipment operation in data centres.
- EMA states Singapore generates about 95% of its electricity from natural gas and imports that gas through pipelines and LNG.
- EMA states Singapore does not have the land for large solar or wind farms or fast-flowing rivers for hydro-electric power.
- MSE states Singapore’s energy transition is constrained by space limits, cloud cover, and humidity.

## Institutional Stability Signals

- The Singapore Judiciary states the Judiciary, Executive, and Legislature are three branches of the state under a separation-of-powers structure set by the Constitution.
- The Judiciary states it is impartial, interprets and enforces the law, and is responsible for ensuring equality before the law and access to justice.
- The Judiciary states the Constitution is the most fundamental law in Singapore and that all other laws must not conflict with it.
- The Judiciary states the Attorney-General brings public prosecutions independently and without influence from any of the three branches of the state.
- IMDA, PDPC, and MAS operate through standing national regulatory institutions with published mandates across infocomm and media regulation, personal data protection, and financial supervision.
- Singapore Statutes Online publishes the Constitution of the Republic of Singapore and states the service is provided by the Legislation Division of the Attorney-General’s Chambers.
